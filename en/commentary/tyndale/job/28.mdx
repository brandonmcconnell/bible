<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 28:1](/en/kjv/job/28#v1)</div>
<p>28:1-28 This section is a self-contained speech. No speaker is listed, so it could be a continuation of the preceding words. However, some consider this a poetic interlude by the author of Job that sums up the argument to this point, emphasizes the failure of human wisdom, and lays the foundation for the Lord’s speeches.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 28:5](/en/kjv/job/28#v5)</div>
<p>28:5 Miners melted rock by burning a fire in a mine for days or even weeks to melt out the ore. Some alternated fire with cold water to split the rocks.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 28:13](/en/kjv/job/28#v13)</div>
<p>28:13 Wisdom does not originate among the living. God is the source of true wisdom (28:23-28).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 28:16](/en/kjv/job/28#v16)</div>
<p>28:16-19 The metals, gemstones, and glass listed here were precious in the ancient world, but the speaker says that wisdom is much more precious than wealth.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v23" data-v="23">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 28:23](/en/kjv/job/28#v23)</div>
<p>28:23-27 When God looks throughout the whole earth (28:23), he sees his own wisdom expressed in his creation. God later showcased his wisdom for Job when he took him on a cosmic tour (chs 38–41).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v28" data-v="28">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 28:28](/en/kjv/job/28#v28)</div>
<p>28:28 fear of the Lord (Hebrew, ’adonay): In Job, this name for God appears only here, which might be one argument for understanding ch 28 as the narrator’s own poem.</p>
</div>
