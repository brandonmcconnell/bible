<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 15:1](/en/kjv/job/15#v1)</div>
<p>15:1–21:34 In this second round of speeches, Job’s friends focus on the fate of the wicked and imply that Job’s condition shows he has sinned.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 15:2](/en/kjv/job/15#v2)</div>
<p>15:2-3 You are nothing but a windbag (literally You fill your belly with the east wind): Since the east wind was hot, it might represent heated (Exod 14:21; Hos 13:15; Jon 4:8) or violent (Job 27:21; Jer 18:17) speech.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 15:6](/en/kjv/job/15#v6)</div>
<p>15:6 Your own mouth condemns you: Job feared that this would happen (9:20; see Matt 26:65).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 15:7](/en/kjv/job/15#v7)</div>
<p>15:7-8 When the Lord himself later issued a similar challenge, Job found it convicting (38:1-11).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 15:8](/en/kjv/job/15#v8)</div>
<p>15:8 The book’s readers know about God’s secret council (1:6-12; 2:1-6; see 1 Kgs 22:19-20; Ps 89:5-7), but Job and his company did not.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 15:9](/en/kjv/job/15#v9)</div>
<p>15:9-10 Aged, gray-haired men claim a monopoly on wisdom (8:8-10; 12:20; see 12:2).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 15:12](/en/kjv/job/15#v12)</div>
<p>15:12 What has weakened your vision (literally Why do your eyes blink): This sentence might be a metaphor for unbelief, or it could indicate winking like a schemer or blinking in disbelief.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 15:14](/en/kjv/job/15#v14)</div>
<p>15:14 Can any mortal be pure? Eliphaz repeated himself (4:17-19) and Job (7:17; 14:4). • anyone born of a woman: Both “mortal” and “born of woman” imply weakness.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 15:15](/en/kjv/job/15#v15)</div>
<p>15:15 The heavens, traditionally associated with purity (Exod 24:10), were not absolutely pure; they had been defiled, perhaps by rebellious angels (Job 1:6-7).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 15:17](/en/kjv/job/15#v17)</div>
<p>15:17-19 before any foreigners arrived: Eliphaz’s contempt for foreign ideas is ironic because wisdom literature has a more international flavor than is characteristic of other Old Testament writings.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 15:20](/en/kjv/job/15#v20)</div>
<p>15:20-35 The wicked also suffer everything that happened to Job (see 1:16-19)—attacks by marauders (15:21), loss of possessions (15:29), crumbled houses (15:28), and fire (15:30, 34).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v21" data-v="21">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 15:21](/en/kjv/job/15#v21)</div>
<p>15:21 Although Eliphaz generalized the terror that the wicked experience, Job had undergone similar experiences (3:25; 6:4; 9:34; 13:11, 21; 23:15; 27:20; 30:15). Bildad (18:11, 14) and Zophar (20:25) spoke of more terror to come. • The Sabeans and Chaldeans were examples of the destroyer (1:13-17), but this could refer to any destructive agent (1:18-19). Destroyers might be agents of Satan (1 Cor 10:10; Rev 9:11) or divine agents that punish wickedness (Exod 12:23; 2 Sam 24:16; 2 Chr 32:21; Acts 12:23; Heb 11:28). Eliphaz meant the latter.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v22" data-v="22">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 15:22](/en/kjv/job/15#v22)</div>
<p>15:22 for fear they will be murdered (literally he is marked for the sword): The wicked might be killed by murder or by the sword of God’s wrath.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v23" data-v="23">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 15:23](/en/kjv/job/15#v23)</div>
<p>15:23 They wander around, saying, “Where can I find bread?”: Like the wicked (15:20), Job either experienced hunger (see 15:27) or (following the Greek Old Testament) had been “appointed to be food for a vulture,” which would parallel “marked for the sword” (15:22).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v25" data-v="25">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 15:25](/en/kjv/job/15#v25)</div>
<p>15:25 Job had complained earlier that God was treating him like a formidable foe (7:19-21; 13:24).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v30" data-v="30">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 15:30](/en/kjv/job/15#v30)</div>
<p>15:30 The burning sun (literally The flame) might be the scorching sun or a flame of judgment from God (15:34, see Num 16:31-35; Ps 106:17-18; Ezek 20:47). • The breath of God might be a desert wind or a more direct theophany (a manifestation of God’s presence) that caused the burning of Job 15:34.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v34" data-v="34">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 15:34](/en/kjv/job/15#v34)</div>
<p>15:34 The flame of judgment (15:30) will burn the unjust gain of the godless.</p>
</div>

</div>
