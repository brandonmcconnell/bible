<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 33:1](/en/kjv/job/33#v1)</div>
<p>33:1 Listen: Elihu begged to be heard (33:1; 34:2, 16), which is what Job had also sought (13:6).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 33:5](/en/kjv/job/33#v5)</div>
<p>33:5 By inviting Job to make his case, Elihu acted as though he were God, or at least the arbiter that Job had been seeking (9:32-35).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 33:7](/en/kjv/job/33#v7)</div>
<p>33:7 you don’t need to be afraid of me (literally my terror shall not make you afraid): God’s terrors (6:4; 7:14; 9:34; 13:21; 23:15) had been thrown in Job’s face by Eliphaz (15:21, 24; 22:10), Bildad (18:11, 14), and Zophar (20:25). • Elihu promised not to come down hard on Job, but later he broke his word (34:7-9, 33-37).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 33:9](/en/kjv/job/33#v9)</div>
<p>33:9 You said: Job claimed to be pure and without sin, using various terms (9:21; 10:6-7; 11:4; 13:23-24; 15:14-16; 16:17; 31:1-40).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 33:11](/en/kjv/job/33#v11)</div>
<p>33:11 Job frequently complained about God’s surveillance (7:20; 10:14; 13:27); he wanted relief from having his every move scrutinized (14:16; 29:2).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 33:13](/en/kjv/job/33#v13)</div>
<p>33:13 Job’s central complaint was that God does not respond (9:16; 19:7; 23:2-7).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 33:15](/en/kjv/job/33#v15)</div>
<p>33:15 He speaks in dreams: See, e.g., Gen 15:12; 20:3; 31:24; 40:1–41:36; Dan 2:1-45; Joel 2:28; Acts 16:9-10.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 33:18](/en/kjv/job/33#v18)</div>
<p>33:18 from crossing over the river of death: This phrase may also be translated from passing &#91;i.e., dying&#93; by the sword. Both translations fit the context (cp. 36:12).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v22" data-v="22">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 33:22](/en/kjv/job/33#v22)</div>
<p>33:22 angels of death wait for them: Cp. Exod 12:23; 2 Sam 24:16; 1 Cor 10:10.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v23" data-v="23">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 33:23](/en/kjv/job/33#v23)</div>
<p>33:23 An angel from heaven would intercede, taking the role of the mediator Job longed for (see 5:1; 9:33-34; 16:18-21).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 33:24](/en/kjv/job/33#v24)</div>
<p>33:24 The ransom is ultimately found in Christ (Matt 20:28; Rom 3:24-25; 1 Tim 2:6; 1 Pet 1:18-19).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v26" data-v="26">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 33:26](/en/kjv/job/33#v26)</div>
<p>33:26 God will receive him with joy: Cp. Num 6:24-26; Ps 67:1; Acts 2:28; Jude 1:24.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v32" data-v="32">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 33:32](/en/kjv/job/33#v32)</div>
<p>33:32 For Elihu to say that he was anxious to see Job justified sounds ironic; he had entered the discussion in the first place “because Job refused to admit that he had sinned” (32:1-5).</p>
</div>

</div>
