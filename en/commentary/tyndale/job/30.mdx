<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 30:2](/en/kjv/job/30#v2)</div>
<p>30:2-3 In the past, the most honorable members of society spoke well of Job (29:11); now, the least honorable mocked him (30:1) and spit in his face (30:10). Cp. Pss 35:15; 69:12; Mark 14:65; 15:17-20.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 30:5](/en/kjv/job/30#v5)</div>
<p>30:5-6 live in frightening ravines, in caves and among the rocks: The wretched life of Job’s mockers resembled Israel’s situation under Midianite oppression (Judg 6:2), David’s life as he fled from Saul (1 Sam 22:1-2), and Elijah’s life as he awaited the Lord’s instruction (1 Kgs 17:3-8).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 30:9](/en/kjv/job/30#v9)</div>
<p>30:9 They taunt me! See also 17:6; cp. Ps 69:12; Lam 3:14, 63.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 30:10](/en/kjv/job/30#v10)</div>
<p>30:10 won’t come near me: See also 19:13-15; cp. Ps 88:8; Prov 19:7; Matt 26:56. • To spit in someone’s face was to display revulsion or contempt (Deut 25:9; Isa 50:6; Matt 26:67; 27:30; Mark 14:65).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 30:12](/en/kjv/job/30#v12)</div>
<p>30:12-14 The series of images presented here is drawn from a military advance against a fortified city. Job had already used this image for God’s attack on him (19:10-12). • The word translated traps might refer to siege ramps raised against a city’s walls.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 30:15](/en/kjv/job/30#v15)</div>
<p>30:15 terror: See also 6:4; 7:14; cp. Ps 88:15.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 30:18](/en/kjv/job/30#v18)</div>
<p>30:18 God grabs . . . the collar of my coat: The Hebrew in this verse is difficult to translate; it could mean that Job feels like he is in a chokehold and is about to be thrown into the mud (cp. 30:19).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 30:19](/en/kjv/job/30#v19)</div>
<p>30:19 dust and ashes: Earlier, Job was sitting “among the ashes” in anguish (2:8); later, he would “sit in dust and ashes to show . . . repentance” (42:6).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 30:20](/en/kjv/job/30#v20)</div>
<p>30:20-21 you don’t answer: This was Job’s frequent complaint (9:16; 19:7; 23:2-9; 31:35), which God soon answered (38:1).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v22" data-v="22">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 30:22](/en/kjv/job/30#v22)</div>
<p>30:22 Job felt tossed into the whirlwind and blown about like worthless straw or chaff (9:17; 21:18; 27:21; Ps 1:4; Isa 17:13).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v28" data-v="28">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 30:28](/en/kjv/job/30#v28)</div>
<p>30:28 Before his testing began, Job had been respected in the public square (29:7-10, 21-25); he helped others who were in need (29:11-17).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v29" data-v="29">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 30:29](/en/kjv/job/30#v29)</div>
<p>30:29 I am considered: Job might have been expressing what he thought of himself, how others viewed him, or both. • By claiming that he was a brother to jackals and a companion to owls, Job might have been describing himself as in the throes of lament (Mic 1:8). Jackals were associated with desolation or ruin (see Ps 63:10; Isa 13:22; 34:13; 35:7; Jer 9:11; 10:22; 49:33; 51:37; Lam 5:18; Ezek 13:4; Mal 1:3).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v30" data-v="30">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 30:30](/en/kjv/job/30#v30)</div>
<p>30:30 The skin of a person who lived in comfortable circumstances was protected from the sun and wind; dark skin indicated physical and social decline (Lam 4:8; 5:10).</p>
</div>

</div>
