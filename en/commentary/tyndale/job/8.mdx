<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 8:1](/en/kjv/job/8#v1)</div>
<p>8:1 Like Eliphaz, Bildad the Shuhite (see study note on 2:11) believed that Job’s sufferings were God’s retribution (8:3-6, 13; cp. 4:7-8; 5:2-3). This counselor revered the wisdom of the past (8:8-10; cp. 4:7) and addressed Job with a mixture of instruction (8:3-6, 8-10; cp. 4:12-21; 5:9-16) and encouragement (8:5-7, 20-22; cp. 4:6; 5:19-26). His point was that Job should quit blustering. He advised Job to repent and allow God’s justice to bring about restoration (8:1-7).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 8:2](/en/kjv/job/8#v2)</div>
<p>8:2 How long? Bildad’s question might be an ironic parody of Job’s question in 7:19.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 8:3](/en/kjv/job/8#v3)</div>
<p>8:3-4 According to Bildad, God does not twist justice, so the death of Job’s children proved that they had sinned against God. Bildad divided people into the blameless (Hebrew tam, 8:20a; see 1:1) and the secretly wicked (Hebrew khanep, 8:13b). He believed that they could be differentiated by watching what God did to them.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 8:7](/en/kjv/job/8#v7)</div>
<p>8:7 you will end with much: Bildad’s words were more true than he realized (42:12; cp. 5:19-26).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 8:8](/en/kjv/job/8#v8)</div>
<p>8:8-10 Bildad made the case for traditional wisdom by appealing to ancient tradition and history (Deut 4:32; Jer 18:13).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 8:9](/en/kjv/job/8#v9)</div>
<p>8:9 born but yesterday and know nothing: Bildad suggested that Job’s generation was either born too late to be acquainted with ancient wisdom or was too young to have accumulated it.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 8:11](/en/kjv/job/8#v11)</div>
<p>8:11 Papyrus reeds grow as high as ten to fifteen feet.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 8:12](/en/kjv/job/8#v12)</div>
<p>8:12-13 To forget God does not mean to have a lapse of memory but to act as if God did not exist (see Pss 10:4; 14:1; Zeph 1:12) or cannot see (see Job 22:13-17; Ps 94:7).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 8:16](/en/kjv/job/8#v16)</div>
<p>8:16-17 In this parable, a plant with roots that grow down through a pile of stones gains stability by becoming entwined with the rocks.</p>
</div>
