<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 21:2](/en/kjv/job/21#v2)</div>
<p>21:2 Job’s friends insisted that they were delivering God’s own message of comfort (15:11), but Job found no comfort in their words (16:2), so he asked for the consolation of their attentive silence (see 13:5).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 21:6](/en/kjv/job/21#v6)</div>
<p>21:6 When I think about what I am saying: Job was dismayed by his horrible task of complaining against God (13:3, 15-16, 22; 23:1-5) and discussing his terrors (6:4; 27:20; 30:15). • My body trembles: This reaction resembles that of Habakkuk (Hab 1:5; 3:16), Ezekiel (Ezek 3:14), and Daniel (Dan 8:27; 10:8-9).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 21:8](/en/kjv/job/21#v8)</div>
<p>21:8 In spite of assertions by Eliphaz (5:4), Bildad (18:19), and Zophar (20:10), Job noted that the wicked enjoy children and grandchildren, which should be the reward of the righteous (Prov 17:6) but was apparently not going to be his own reward (Job 1:18-19; but see 42:13-16).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 21:17](/en/kjv/job/21#v17)</div>
<p>21:17 light of the wicked never seems to be extinguished: Job countered Bildad (18:5-6, 18) and standard wisdom (Prov 13:9; 20:20; 24:20; see God’s remarks, Job 38:15).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 21:19](/en/kjv/job/21#v19)</div>
<p>21:19 you say: Job was quoting and rebutting his friends’ arguments. • God will punish their children: These were the words of Eliphaz (5:4) and Zophar (20:10), but Job thought God should punish the ones who sin (see Ezek 18:19-20).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v22" data-v="22">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 21:22](/en/kjv/job/21#v22)</div>
<p>21:22 The rhetorical answer is that no one can teach a lesson to God (Isa 40:13-14; 45:9; Rom 11:34; 1 Cor 2:16). • The most powerful might refer to angels or demons (1 Cor 6:3; 2 Pet 2:4; Jude 1:6; Rev 20:1-3) or, more likely, to the powerful on earth (Job 34:17-19).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 21:24](/en/kjv/job/21#v24)</div>
<p>21:24 vigorous and fit (literally the marrow of his bones &#91;is&#93; moist): Bones were thought to be the seat of health.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v25" data-v="25">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 21:25](/en/kjv/job/21#v25)</div>
<p>21:25 in bitter poverty: Job repeatedly complained about the apparent futility of existence (3:20; 7:11; 9:18; 10:1).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v26" data-v="26">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 21:26](/en/kjv/job/21#v26)</div>
<p>21:26 eaten by the same maggots: Cp. 17:14; Isa 14:11; see Job 19:26.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v28" data-v="28">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 21:28](/en/kjv/job/21#v28)</div>
<p>21:28 houses have vanished because of their sins: Job was echoing Bildad (8:22), Eliphaz (15:34), and Zophar (20:26).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v33" data-v="33">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 21:33](/en/kjv/job/21#v33)</div>
<p>21:33 the earth gives sweet repose: Job viewed death as a relief (3:17-18).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v34" data-v="34">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 21:34](/en/kjv/job/21#v34)</div>
<p>21:34 They told lies about Job (13:4) and about God (42:7).</p>
</div>

</div>
