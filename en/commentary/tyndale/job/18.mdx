<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 18:2](/en/kjv/job/18#v2)</div>
<p>18:2-3 How long before you stop talking? Bildad addressed Job in the plural, perhaps meaning “people like you.”</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 18:5](/en/kjv/job/18#v5)</div>
<p>18:5 The light of the wicked will be snuffed out could be a metaphor for a life of confused darkness (Prov 4:19); more likely, it indicates an early death (Prov 13:9; 24:20).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 18:7](/en/kjv/job/18#v7)</div>
<p>18:7 The stride that is shortened is the opposite of walking and running in the blessed life (Prov 4:12).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 18:8](/en/kjv/job/18#v8)</div>
<p>18:8-10 A series of hunting devices illustrates the biblical principle of retribution (see study note on 34:11).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 18:11](/en/kjv/job/18#v11)</div>
<p>18:11-13 Bildad applies the biblical principle of retribution (18:8-10) to Job, as do Eliphaz (15:21-23) and Zophar (20:25).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 18:13](/en/kjv/job/18#v13)</div>
<p>18:13 death devours: Isaiah later reversed the figure and saw the Lord as swallowing up death (Isa 25:8; see 1 Cor 15:54).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 18:15](/en/kjv/job/18#v15)</div>
<p>18:15 The image of burning sulfur recalls the fate of Sodom and Gomorrah (Gen 19:24) and suggests the fate of all the wicked (Ps 11:6; Rev 19:20; 21:8).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 18:17](/en/kjv/job/18#v17)</div>
<p>18:17 Being forgotten by those who come later is a familiar curse against the wicked (see 18:19; Pss 34:16; 109:13; Prov 10:7).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 18:19](/en/kjv/job/18#v19)</div>
<p>18:19 This curse of childlessness (see 18:16; Pss 109:13; Isa 14:21-22) and Bildad’s earlier cruelty (Job 8:4) show that he regarded Job’s condition as the just reward for his wickedness (1:18-19).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 18:20](/en/kjv/job/18#v20)</div>
<p>18:20 in the west . . . in the east: These phrases might refer to people from times past and times to come or to people from various places.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v21" data-v="21">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 18:21](/en/kjv/job/18#v21)</div>
<p>18:21 Bildad kept implying that Job had rejected God (15:4, 13, 25).</p>
</div>

</div>
