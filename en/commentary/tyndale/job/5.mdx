<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 5:1](/en/kjv/job/5#v1)</div>
<p>5:1 The angels (literally the holy ones), probably a reference back to 4:18, are called “holy ones” because of their proximity to God, not because they were morally perfect (15:15; Dan 4:14; 8:13; Zech 14:5).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 5:4](/en/kjv/job/5#v4)</div>
<p>5:4 The court (literally the gate) was the site of legal and commercial deliberations in which Job had participated (29:7; see Deut 21:19-21; Ruth 4:1-11).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 5:8](/en/kjv/job/5#v8)</div>
<p>5:8 To go to God meant lodging a formal appeal with God for assistance, counsel, or vindication, sometimes by way of repentance (Amos 5:4, 6) and possibly through a prophetic oracle (Gen 25:22; 1 Sam 9:9; 1 Kgs 22:8). • Job wanted to present his case to God (Job 13:3, 15, 18; 23:3-8), and later he attempted to do so (29:1–31:40).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 5:9](/en/kjv/job/5#v9)</div>
<p>5:9-16 Eliphaz’s doxology praised God as the wonder-working Creator and Sustainer of the universe (5:9-10) and as the righteous Judge who brings down the powerful and lifts up the poor (5:11-16).5:9-10 Job’s region depended upon rain rather than irrigation (see 36:27-28).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 5:13](/en/kjv/job/5#v13)</div>
<p>5:13 God traps the wise in the cleverness of their words (Prov 12:13). Paul alluded to this verse in his rejection of worldly wisdom (1 Cor 1:19).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 5:17](/en/kjv/job/5#v17)</div>
<p>5:17 the discipline of the Almighty: See Deut 8:5; Prov 3:11-12; Rev 3:19.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 5:19](/en/kjv/job/5#v19)</div>
<p>5:19-26 six disasters . . . even in the seventh: This wisdom formula reflects a sense of completeness (cp. Prov 30:15-31). • This list reflects Mosaic covenant blessings and curses (Lev 26; Deut 28). Eliphaz thought that people in right relationship with God would be free from famine (Job 5:20, 22; see Lev 26:4-5, 10; Deut 28:4-6, 8, 11-12), war (i.e., death, Job 5:20) and the destruction it causes (5:21, 22; see Lev 26:6-8; Deut 28:7, 48), and slander. They will even be at peace with wild animals (Job 5:23; see Lev 26:6) and the stones of the field. This peace might indicate fertile rather than stony fields (2 Kgs 3:19, 25; Matt 13:5), or it might symbolize wider harmony with the natural world (see Ps 91:11-12).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 5:24](/en/kjv/job/5#v24)</div>
<p>5:24 nothing will be missing: See Deut 28:11-12; cp. Job 1:6-19.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v25" data-v="25">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 5:25](/en/kjv/job/5#v25)</div>
<p>5:25 descendants . . . as plentiful as grass: See Gen 22:17; Lev 26:9; Deut 28:4, 11-12.</p>
</div>
