<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:1](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v1)</div>
<p>5:1–6:2a Right conduct in God’s household (see 3:15) relates to old and young (5:1-2), widows (5:3-16), elders (5:17-25), and slaves (6:1-2a). Proper honor within the household cuts across social boundaries.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:3](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v3)</div>
<p>5:3-16 A widow without wealth or family was alone in a world that did not provide for her. The Christian community was expected to care for such widows among its members (see Deut 10:17-19; Isa 1:17; cp. Acts 6:1-6; Jas 1:27). Some have argued that this passage suggests a religious order of widows in the first-century church at Ephesus, but there is no certain evidence of such an order in the first-century church, only of a ministry of care for community members without means.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:5](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v5)</div>
<p>5:5 The only recourse for a widow who was truly alone was hope in God (Pss 68:5; 146:9).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:6](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v6)</div>
<p>5:6 Some widows in Ephesus were living only for pleasure, more interested in receiving than in giving (see also 6:17-19; Jas 5:5). They did not need support.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:8](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v8)</div>
<p>5:8 Faith is not mere belief but a whole way of life (see Jas 2:14-26).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:9](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v9)</div>
<p>5:9 at least sixty: Young widows required a different approach (5:11-15). Young and old were customarily divided at around forty; elders would probably have been over fifty (see Titus 2:2). • was faithful to her husband (literally was the wife of one husband): The grammar is identical in 1 Tim 3:2, 12; Titus 1:6.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:10](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v10)</div>
<p>5:10 and served other believers humbly: Cp. Gen 18:4; Luke 7:44; John 13:1-17.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:11](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v11)</div>
<p>5:11-15 Young widows still in their childbearing years required a different approach. Paul was concerned that the false teachers were leading them astray.5:11-12 If Paul was concerned about remarriage to unbelievers (cp. 5:14), their previous pledge would refer to their Christian faith, which they would surrender upon entering a pagan marriage; such a marriage would probably have alienated them from Christ. It is also possible that Paul and the church recognized a special category of “sacred widowhood” (see 5:5; cp. Acts 9:36-37), entered by a vow that Paul refers to as the previous pledge.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:13](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v13)</div>
<p>5:13 Too seldom do those who are fully supported by the church invest their lives in remarkable piety (cp. 2:9-10; 5:10). Abusing the church’s support does not help them or the Good News (cp. 5:14). • talking about things they shouldn’t: The wording suggests involvement in the false teaching (5:15; cp. 2 Thes 3:11-13; Titus 1:11).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:14](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v14)</div>
<p>5:14 Paul is advising younger widows to remarry—and to marry believers (5:11-12; cp. 2:15). • not . . . say anything against them: Their actions were hurting their testimony for the Good News (see 1 Thes 4:11-12; Titus 2:8).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:15](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v15)</div>
<p>5:15 now follow Satan: They had committed apostasy, renouncing Christ, probably in connection with the false teachers (1:6-7; cp. 3:6-7; Heb 2:1) and their self-indulgence (1 Tim 5:6, 13).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:16](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v16)</div>
<p>5:16 Individual Christian households maintained their function and identity within the church (cp. 2:15; 3:15). They were not eclipsed by the church, although the household of God is primary to the identity of the believing community (see Mark 3:31-35).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:17](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v17)</div>
<p>5:17-25 Elders: As in Judaism, community leaders (see also 3:1-7; Titus 1:5-9) were usually older men who were leaders in the wider community. The letters to Timothy and Titus recognize an office of elders, as here (see also 1 Tim 4:14; Titus 1:5), but the word is also used generally for older men in the community (as in 1 Tim 5:1).5:17-18 should be respected and paid well (literally should be worthy of double honor): Cp. Gal 6:6. There is no evidence of a paid clergy at this time; the word used here is never used for a continuous salary, though it does imply something beyond a show of respect. The quotations from Scripture (Deut 25:4; Luke 10:7) suggest gifts of money.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:19](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v19)</div>
<p>5:19-21 The climate created by the false teachers may have led to spurious charges against the leaders of the community. This passage follows the outline of Deut 19:15-21. • two or three witnesses: See Deut 17:6; 19:15; Matt 18:16; 2 Cor 13:1; Heb 10:28.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:20](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v20)</div>
<p>5:20 Those who sin: This term refers either to one who is (rightly) accused or to one making a (false) accusation (Deut 19:16-19). • The others are either the other elders or the rest of the church (cp. Deut 19:20; Acts 5:1-11).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v21" data-v="21">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:21](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v21)</div>
<p>5:21 God and Christ Jesus and the highest angels constitute three witnesses (5:19). These witnesses confirm the accusation against leaders who sin (5:20). • Angels are associated with God’s judgment (Dan 7:9-10; Matt 13:49; 25:31, 41; Luke 9:26; 2 Thes 1:7).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v22" data-v="22">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:22](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v22)</div>
<p>5:22 Do not share in the sins of others: Those who commission an elder are responsible for his conduct.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v23" data-v="23">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:23](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v23)</div>
<p>5:23 Possibly the concern with purity (5:22) was being taken too far with respect to wine (see 3:3; Num 6:1-21) or food (1 Cor 10:21). Wine may have been considered medicinal.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Timothy 5:24](/en/kjv/1-timothy/5#v24)</div>
<p>5:24-25 These two proverbial statements summarize 5:17-23 and point to God’s judgment.</p>
</div>

</div>
