<div className="cm-chapter" lang="en" data-commentary="tyndale" data-book="JUD" data-chapter="7">

<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 7:1</div>
<p>6:1–8:35 Gideon’s judgeship brings to a close the first period of judges.7:1 The spring of Harod and the hill of Moreh were just east of the town of Jezreel; they guarded the pass that connects the valley of Jezreel to the fords of the Jordan near Beth-shan. Gideon’s army was located on the north slope of Mount Gilboa. The Midianite hosts were in the valley below, with the hill behind them.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 7:2</div>
<p>7:2 The Lord does not require a large force to save his people (cp. 1 Sam 14:6). A large number might even have hindered success because the element of surprise was crucial to the Lord’s battle plan. Gideon was to pare down the army to maintain godly humility, not for reasons of military strategy.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 7:3</div>
<p>7:3 The first reduction sorted out those who initially responded with enthusiasm but then had second thoughts.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 7:4</div>
<p>7:4-8 The choice of those who lapped from their hands over those who knelt was apparently arbitrary, but it enabled the Lord to reduce the size of the army.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 7:8</div>
<p>7:8 The jars (in which the men carried provisions) and trumpets borrowed from the warriors who were sent home would be used in the coming battle.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 7:10</div>
<p>7:10-15 The Lord encouraged Gideon with a third sign (6:17-21, 36-40).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 7:11</div>
<p>7:11 With the enemy camp spread out across the valley (7:1, 12), it was easy for Gideon and his servant to sneak undetected into the camp.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 7:13</div>
<p>7:13-14 Dreams and their interpretations were often discussed in Egypt and Babylonia. The Old Testament teaches that dreams are inspired by God and are best interpreted by his revelation (see Gen 41:15-16, 25; Dan 2:28). The point of this dream is clear: Gideon’s band, represented as a rounded loaf of barley bread, would come tumbling down and overturn the Midianite camp.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 7:16</div>
<p>7:16 Dividing the small band into three groups created the impression of a much larger force.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 7:17</div>
<p>7:17-20 Surprise and deception took the place of a massive army. The war cry, the sudden blast of 300 trumpets, and the torches bursting into light created the impression of a large force.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v21" data-v="21">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 7:21</div>
<p>7:21 The warriors stood in place while God fought the battle (cp. Exod 14:13; 1 Sam 12:16; Rev 19:11-16). • The Midianites’ camels (Judg 6:5) might have contributed to the panic. Camels usually provided a military advantage, but in this situation they would have been a liability. They had probably been hobbled for the night, and camels themselves are subject to panic.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v22" data-v="22">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 7:22</div>
<p>7:22 The fugitives fled in a southeasterly direction, past Beth-shan and toward the fords of the Jordan, trying to reach more friendly and favorable surroundings.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 7:24</div>
<p>7:24 The shallow crossings of the Jordan were key to cutting off the escape of an army (cp. 3:28; 12:4-6).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v25" data-v="25">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 7:25</div>
<p>7:25 Oreb (“raven”) and Zeeb (“wolf”) were military leaders, in contrast to Zebah and Zalmunna, who were kings (8:5). This capture was commemorated in the subsequent names of the rock and winepress where they were captured.</p>
</div>

</div>
