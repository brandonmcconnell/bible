<div className="cm-chapter" lang="en" data-commentary="tyndale" data-book="JUD" data-chapter="20">

<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 20:1</div>
<p>20:1 This was not the Mizpah east of the Jordan associated with Jephthah (10:17; 11:11, 29, 34), but a place in central Israel. This common name means “watchtower.”</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 20:3</div>
<p>20:3 The tribe of Benjamin had probably received the summons (19:29), so they knew that trouble was brewing.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 20:5</div>
<p>20:5 The “troublemakers” (19:22) were leading citizens (literally lords). • planning to kill me: The death of his concubine convinced the Levite that the men of Gibeah intended his death.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 20:9</div>
<p>20:9 The lots may have been the Urim and Thummim on the priest’s breastplate (Exod 28:30; cp. Josh 14:2; 1 Sam 14:41; Ezra 2:63).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 20:12</div>
<p>20:12-13 Israel recognized corporate responsibility. The leading citizens of Gibeah were guilty (20:5), and all of Gibeah became an accessory to their crimes by failing to discipline them. The tribe of Benjamin, in turn, had a responsibility to bring Gibeah to justice; failing that, they all shared the guilt. Had Israel not done something about Benjamin’s sin, the guilt would then have extended to the entire nation.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 20:13</div>
<p>20:13-17 Benjamin’s response was consistent with its independent and warlike character (cp. Gen 49:27). The tribe had justifiable confidence in the warriors for which it was famous (Judg 3:12-30; 1 Chr 12:2). Though outnumbered almost three to one, they defeated the rest of Israel twice and inflicted heavy casualties (Judg 20:18-24).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 20:16</div>
<p>20:16 left-handed: See study note on 3:15. • The sling was of the same type as the one David used to kill Goliath (1 Sam 17:40-51). The slinger buried a stone in a small pouch attached to a leather thong, then swung it around his head to gain momentum before releasing one end of the thong to shoot the missile. A slinger had the advantage of distance over a swordsman.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 20:18</div>
<p>20:18 To go first means to lead, not necessarily to precede (Gen 49:10).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 20:19</div>
<p>20:19-21 The three battles took place around the guilty city. Despite superior numbers and having asked the Lord for guidance, the Israelite troops facing the defenders behind their walls were soundly beaten back the first day.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v22" data-v="22">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 20:22</div>
<p>20:22-23 Following a time of weeping and probably confession (cp. Josh 7) the Israelite troops received divine guidance and regained confidence.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 20:24</div>
<p>20:24-25 A second debacle shook the confidence of the Israelites (20:26-28).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v26" data-v="26">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 20:26</div>
<p>20:26-28 The pressure was building to admit defeat and go home. The activities in the presence of the Lord increased in intensity as the Israelites sought the Lord’s direction.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v29" data-v="29">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 20:29</div>
<p>20:29-44 The third day’s battle followed the same strategies of deception and ambush that were used to conquer Ai (Josh 8:1-29).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v33" data-v="33">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 20:33</div>
<p>20:33 Baal-tamar is an unidentified town.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v36" data-v="36">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 20:36</div>
<p>20:36-48 This section gives a second account of the battle.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v43" data-v="43">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 20:43</div>
<p>20:43 finally overtaking them east of Gibeah: The geography is complicated, with a further problem of possible confusion between Gibeah and nearby Geba (1 Sam 13:3–14:5; see also 1 Kgs 15:22).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v47" data-v="47">
<div className="cmv-head">Jude 20:47</div>
<p>20:47 the rock of Rimmon: “Rimmon” was a Benjamite personal name (2 Sam 4:2), and the place was about four miles east of Bethel. It was a stronghold, probably a cliff dwelling that protected fugitives by its inaccessibility.</p>
</div>
