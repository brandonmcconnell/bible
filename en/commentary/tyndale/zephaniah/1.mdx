<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zephaniah 1:1](/en/kjv/zephaniah/1#v1)</div>
<p>1:1–2:3 Zephaniah’s message from the Lord warns about worldwide judgment (1:1-6) and exhorts his readers to repent (1:7-13) before the devastation overtakes them (1:14-18) and to seek the Lord and live righteously and humbly before him in the hope that they may be spared his judgment (2:1-3).1:1 The Lord gave this message: Zephaniah was a prophet, God’s spokesman. • Hezekiah probably indicates the king of that name (see 2 Kgs 18:1–20:20).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zephaniah 1:2](/en/kjv/zephaniah/1#v2)</div>
<p>1:2-3 The list of the things that God will sweep away in his judgment is in reverse order to their creation (Gen 1:20-26): people and animals . . . birds . . . fish, because judgment is a reversal of creation.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zephaniah 1:4](/en/kjv/zephaniah/1#v4)</div>
<p>1:4-6 Having pronounced judgment on the whole earth (1:2-3), Zephaniah now focuses attention on his own people, Judah and Jerusalem.1:4 When Zephaniah made this prophecy, before Josiah’s reforms in 622 BC, idolatrous priests rampantly promoted Baal worship and other forms of paganism (2 Kgs 23:5).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zephaniah 1:5](/en/kjv/zephaniah/1#v5)</div>
<p>1:5 Scripture denounced the practice of worshiping heavenly bodies such as the sun, moon, and stars (see Deut 4:15-19; 2 Kgs 21:3; 23:12). • Molech might refer to the Canaanite god Baal, the Ammonite god Milcom, or the pagan deity Molech. Although the law explicitly prohibited Molech worship (Lev 20:1-5), it remained a constant temptation to Israel (2 Kgs 16:3; 21:6).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zephaniah 1:7](/en/kjv/zephaniah/1#v7)</div>
<p>1:7-18 The entire passage must be seen as one vast event. Some aspects would be fulfilled at Jerusalem’s fall in 586 BC; others would be repeated in various historical epochs (such as the destruction of Jerusalem in AD 70) until the whole prophecy is fulfilled at the end of time when God acts fully and finally to judge the world and renew creation (Rev 19:11–22:5).1:7 has prepared his people for a great slaughter and has chosen their executioners: Foreigners will slaughter the wicked among the Lord’s people.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zephaniah 1:8](/en/kjv/zephaniah/1#v8)</div>
<p>1:8 The leaders and princes of Judah were the tribal chieftains of Israel (Num 1:4), court officials (1 Chr 22:17), district supervisors (1 Kgs 20:14-15), city officials (Judg 8:6), military leaders (1 Kgs 2:5; 2 Kgs 1:9-14; 25:23, 26), or even religious leaders (Ezra 8:24). Their influential role put them in a position of heightened responsibility before God (cp. Jas 3:1).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zephaniah 1:9](/en/kjv/zephaniah/1#v9)</div>
<p>1:9 those who participate in pagan worship ceremonies (literally those who leap over the threshold): The people of Judah perpetuated the pagan custom of leaping over the threshold of a temple to avoid contact with it, in deference to that pagan god (see 1 Sam 5:1-5).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zephaniah 1:10](/en/kjv/zephaniah/1#v10)</div>
<p>1:10 The Fish Gate was in the northern section of Jerusalem (Neh 12:39).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zephaniah 1:13](/en/kjv/zephaniah/1#v13)</div>
<p>1:13 God would send an invading force to plunder Jerusalem. The destruction would come so quickly that those with ill-gotten gain would not survive to enjoy their wealth.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zephaniah 1:14](/en/kjv/zephaniah/1#v14)</div>
<p>1:14-18 The meaning of the day of the Lord broadens to include God’s dealings with the whole earth.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zephaniah 1:15](/en/kjv/zephaniah/1#v15)</div>
<p>1:15 darkness and gloom: See also Isa 13:9-10; Joel 2:1-2; Amos 5:18-20.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zephaniah 1:17](/en/kjv/zephaniah/1#v17)</div>
<p>1:17 grope around like the blind: An example of God’s justice (see also Rom 1:21-32). Because God’s people were blind ethically and spiritually (see Exod 23:8; Rom 2:19; 1 Jn 2:11) and had sinned against the Lord and his commandments, they would incur the just penalties specified in God’s covenant with them (Deut 28:28-29).</p>
</div>

</div>
