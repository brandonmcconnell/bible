<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:1](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v1)</div>
<p>14:1-45 The testimony of the faithful spies Joshua and Caleb was rejected, and rebellion spread through the entire community. Only Moses’ intervention saved Israel from complete destruction. Those who failed to obey God died during a forty-year wilderness sojourn.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:3](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v3)</div>
<p>14:3-4 The threats about which they complained were real, but the Israelites should have trusted the Lord. In the ancient Near East, women and children were often taken as plunder in the aftermath of battle. • return to Egypt: How quickly the Israelites forgot their bondage (Exod 2:23-24) and the hardship of the wilderness (Num 11:1)!</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:5](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v5)</div>
<p>14:5 Moses and Aaron prostrated themselves out of humility, fear, grief, and anguish.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:6](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v6)</div>
<p>14:6 Joshua and Caleb joined in the effort to reverse the rebellion and avert God’s wrath; they tore their clothing in a gesture of grief.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:7](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v7)</div>
<p>14:7-9 Joshua and Caleb understood that Israel’s lack of courage and their criticism of Moses and Aaron were rebellion against the Lord, and they knew what results this would produce. • the Lord is with us: This was the source of their confidence (cp. Rom 8:31).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:10](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v10)</div>
<p>14:10 Joshua and Caleb, with their encouragement and expression of trust in God, brought even more opposition; the protests against the leaders were converted into threats of violence against the faithful spies. All of the Israelites’ objections came to a sudden stop when the Lord’s glorious presence (Hebrew kabod; see Exod 24:15-18; Ezek 1) appeared at the Tabernacle, the site of divine communication.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:11](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v11)</div>
<p>14:11 God marveled that Israel had not yet learned to trust him, even after he had miraculously rescued them from slavery and provided for their needs as they traveled in the wilderness (cp. Deut 4:32-40; Matt 11:21; Luke 10:13).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:12](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v12)</div>
<p>14:12 God is patient, but he will not tolerate outright rebellion from his people (cp. Exod 32:7-10). The writer of Hebrews refers to this episode in his effort to strengthen the faith of Christians whose trust in the Good News was wavering (Heb 3:7-19; 12:3, 25; cp. Ps 95:8-11).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:13](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v13)</div>
<p>14:13-25 Moses interceded on behalf of his people, reminding God that his reputation was bound up with that of Israel and appealing to his faithfulness.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:17](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v17)</div>
<p>14:17-18 Moses had used this same argument earlier when he sought pardon for Israel’s rebellion (Exod 32:10-14). Moses knew that God would not let the Israelites off without punishment (Deut 7:9-10).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:19](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v19)</div>
<p>14:19 Israel’s history since leaving Egypt had been a series of transgressions forgiven by the Lord (chs 11–12; Exod 15:22-27; 17:1-7; 32:1-14; Lev 10).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:20](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v20)</div>
<p>14:20-25 As a result of Moses’ argument, the Lord pardoned Israel’s rebellion and did not obliterate them, but the obstinate adult generation would never see the Promised Land.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v22" data-v="22">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:22](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v22)</div>
<p>14:22 The people’s lack of obedience and faith was a refusal to listen to God’s voice, since they were eyewitnesses of the Lord’s glorious presence (see study note on 14:10) and miraculous signs (Exod 4:29-31; 7:1–11:10; 14:15-31; 16:1–17:13).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v23" data-v="23">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:23](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v23)</div>
<p>14:23-25 Having turned their backs on the Promised Land, the Israelites were now ordered to march the other way. • The Red Sea could refer to bodies of water on either side of the Sinai Peninsula (the Gulf of Aqaba or the Gulf of Suez).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v28" data-v="28">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:28](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v28)</div>
<p>14:28 Israel’s disobedience brought on the consequences they feared would come from obeying.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v34" data-v="34">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:34](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v34)</div>
<p>14:34 each day: Cp. 13:25. • to have me for an enemy: Cp. Ezek 5:8; 21:3.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v39" data-v="39">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:39](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v39)</div>
<p>14:39-45 The threat of divine punishment for their sins brought grief to the people of Israel, but their hearts were still rebellious and they again disobeyed the Lord’s command (14:25). The promise of God’s presence had been the basis for Caleb and Joshua’s initial confidence (14:9), but God would not fight alongside the Israelites until all the rebels had died.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v43" data-v="43">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:43](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v43)</div>
<p>14:43 The Lord will abandon you because you have abandoned the Lord: Moses’ prediction summarizes the whole chapter.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v44" data-v="44">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:44](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v44)</div>
<p>14:44 The Ark symbolized God’s presence and blessings (cp. 1 Sam 4:1-11).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v45" data-v="45">
<div className="cmv-head">[Numbers 14:45](/en/kjv/numbers/14#v45)</div>
<p>14:45 Hormah means “destruction” in Hebrew (see 21:1-3).</p>
</div>
