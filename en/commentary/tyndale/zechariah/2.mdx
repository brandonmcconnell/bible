<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zechariah 2:1](/en/kjv/zechariah/2#v1)</div>
<p>2:1-5 The scope of the visions narrows from the cosmos (vision 1), to the nations (vision 2), to the city of Jerusalem. The vision itself (2:1-3) is followed by an explanation of its meaning (2:4-5).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zechariah 2:2](/en/kjv/zechariah/2#v2)</div>
<p>2:2 how wide and how long it is (Or how long and wide it is to be): By taking this measurement, God showed that he was planning for Jerusalem’s future.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zechariah 2:5](/en/kjv/zechariah/2#v5)</div>
<p>2:5 The wall of fire represents divine protection (cp. 2 Kgs 6:17). • I, myself, will be: The emphatic construction of the verb “to be” might be a direct reference to God’s name (Exod 3:14-15). • Fire and glory allude to the Exodus (see Exod 13:22; 14:20; 40:34).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zechariah 2:6](/en/kjv/zechariah/2#v6)</div>
<p>2:6-13 God’s call for the exiles’ return from Babylon anticipated his people’s restoration to the land of Israel (2:6-9) and the restoration of his presence to his people in Jerusalem (2:10-13).2:6 Come away! The emphatic command carries the force of a promise that the Lord would lead his people out of exile and into a restored Jerusalem (cp. Isa 55:1). • the land of the north: It was necessary to travel north in order to move to the east, so Babylonia was thought of as lying in the north. • four winds: The Hebrew exiles would return to Judah and Jerusalem from all directions (see Ezek 12:14; 37:9).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zechariah 2:8](/en/kjv/zechariah/2#v8)</div>
<p>2:8 Anyone who harms you harms my most precious possession (literally Anyone who touches you touches the pupil of his eye): This might refer to Israel as God’s elect; it might also mean that those who harmed Israel poked themselves in the eye by bringing God’s judgment upon themselves.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zechariah 2:9](/en/kjv/zechariah/2#v9)</div>
<p>2:9 their own slaves will plunder them: At the Exodus, the Egyptians paid the Israelites to leave their land (see Exod 11:2-3; 12:35-36).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zechariah 2:12](/en/kjv/zechariah/2#v12)</div>
<p>2:12 Israel is the Lord’s inheritance or special possession—the people of Israel belonged to the Lord (Deut 32:9-10). • The land was holy because of God’s glorious presence in the Jerusalem Temple (see Pss 11:4; 15:1).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Zechariah 2:13](/en/kjv/zechariah/2#v13)</div>
<p>2:13 Be silent: The force of the Hebrew word (has) is similar to our English word “hush” (see Hab 2:20; Zeph 1:7).</p>
</div>

</div>
