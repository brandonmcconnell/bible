<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:1](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v1)</div>
<p>10:1 Samuel later repeated this process by anointing David with olive oil (16:13). • The people of Israel did not belong to the king but to the Lord. The king was to act as manager rather than owner (cp. 8:10-18).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:2](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v2)</div>
<p>10:2-6 These three “signs” were to convince a reluctant Saul (9:21) that he had in fact been chosen to be king.10:2 Rachel’s tomb at Zelzah: See Gen 35:16-20; the precise location is unknown.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:3](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v3)</div>
<p>10:3 oak of Tabor: This location, somewhere in Benjamin, is not to be confused with the village of the same name in Zebulun (1 Chr 6:77). • Bethel: See study note on 1 Sam 7:16.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:5](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v5)</div>
<p>10:5 Gibeah of God, Saul’s hometown (10:26), had been a lawless city (Judg 19–21). Nowhere else is it described as being “of God.” This description might point to God’s ownership of the town despite the occupation of the Philistines. • band: Prophets often lived in groups (e.g., 2 Kgs 2:3). • harp . . . lyre: Ancient prophets sometimes used musical instruments to stimulate and accompany prophesying (e.g., 2 Kgs 3:15).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:6](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v6)</div>
<p>10:6 prophesy: Cp. 2 Sam 23:2; Acts 2:29-31. • different person: Saul would become a spiritual and charismatic national leader (cp. 1 Sam 16:14).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:7](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v7)</div>
<p>10:7 for God is with you: God’s presence empowered Saul to carry out his responsibilities.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:8](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v8)</div>
<p>10:8 Gilgal: See study note on 7:16. • wait for seven days: Saul apparently fulfilled this instruction in 11:14-15 (cp. 13:8).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:9](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v9)</div>
<p>10:9 a new heart: God gave Saul courage and strength, helping him overcome his hesitancy and enabling him to be king (10:6).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:10](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v10)</div>
<p>10:10 the Spirit of God came powerfully upon Saul: See “The Spirit’s Presence” Theme Note; see also 11:6; Judg 14:6, 19; 15:14; cp. Acts 2:17-18, 39). • When Saul . . . began to prophesy, it was a clear sign that he had been chosen to lead Israel (see also 1 Sam 19:18-23).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:11](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v11)</div>
<p>10:11 Those who knew Saul might have known him to be nonreligious and thus an unlikely candidate for prophet. Their surprise might indicate disappointment at receiving a prophet instead of a king (cp. John 6:14-15).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:12](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v12)</div>
<p>10:12 “Can anyone become a prophet, no matter who his father is?” Unlike priests and kings, prophets never inherited their office. Possibly the people were asking why Saul, whose well-to-do father was well known, would associate himself with a group of people who had no pedigree.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:16](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v16)</div>
<p>10:16 Perhaps Saul didn’t tell his uncle because he had lingering uncertainty about what had transpired.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:17](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v17)</div>
<p>10:17 An earlier generation had gathered at Mizpah to decimate the tribe of Benjamin (Judg 20:1-4), making the city an ironic place to inaugurate a man of Benjamin as king.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:18](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v18)</div>
<p>10:18 rescued you . . . from all of the nations: Israel did not need a human king to rescue them; they needed God, but they had rejected him (10:19).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:19](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v19)</div>
<p>10:19 rejected your God: See also 8:7. Israel was rejecting God’s ability to protect and save them. • Clans are subdivisions of tribes.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:20](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v20)</div>
<p>10:20 chosen by lot: Cp. Lev 16:8; Josh 18:6; 1 Chr 26:12-16; Acts 1:26.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v25" data-v="25">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:25](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v25)</div>
<p>10:25 rights and duties of a king: See Deut 17:14-20. • He wrote them: Cp. Exod 24:4; Josh 24:26. • Samuel placed the document before the Lord—presumably in the Tabernacle. This position showed that God would honor those who lived by the covenant and punish those who violated it.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v26" data-v="26">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:26](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v26)</div>
<p>10:26–11:13 Saul proved his capacity for leadership by tolerating opposition and rescuing the town of Jabesh-gilead.10:26 whose hearts God had touched: God gave people confidence in Saul’s ability to lead (cp. 10:9).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v27" data-v="27">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 10:27](/en/kjv/1-samuel/10#v27)</div>
<p>10:27 These men were scoundrels (cp. 2:12) because they scorned God’s chosen king. • Gifts were given to show homage (Gen 32:13, 18, 20), political friendship (2 Kgs 20:12), or submission (Judg 3:15, 17). • ignored them: Saul’s self-restraint while being insulted was a sign of maturity. In contrast, Proverbs 16:14 speaks of a king’s potentially explosive rage when somebody crosses him. See also 1 Sam 11:12-13. • &#91;Nahash . . . Jabesh-gilead&#93;: See NLT note. Whether this paragraph was part of the original text is uncertain, but it provides historical context for 11:1-15 (e.g., 11:2).</p>
</div>
