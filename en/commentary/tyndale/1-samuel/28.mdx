<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 28:1](/en/kjv/1-samuel/28#v1)</div>
<p>28:1 another war: Hostilities between Israel and the Philistines were now routine. In exchange for providing refuge, Achish expected David’s military involvement and cooperation.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 28:3](/en/kjv/1-samuel/28#v3)</div>
<p>28:3-14 Saul consulted the dead, a practice forbidden by God’s law (see Lev 20:27; Deut 18:9-11).28:3 banned from the land: Saul had either expelled or killed most mediums, or he had merely outlawed the practice of necromancy (see study note on 28:9).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 28:4](/en/kjv/1-samuel/28#v4)</div>
<p>28:4 Shunem, a border town in the territory of Issachar (Josh 19:18), overlooked the valley of Jezreel. • Mount Gilboa is at the east end of the valley of Jezreel. The mountain took its name from a nearby village.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 28:6](/en/kjv/1-samuel/28#v6)</div>
<p>28:6 refused to answer: Earlier, Saul had consulted the Lord by sacred lots with mixed results (14:36-42). God’s refusal to speak was a sign of his judgment (14:37; Lam 2:9; Ezek 7:26; Amos 8:11-12; Mic 3:6-7).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 28:7](/en/kjv/1-samuel/28#v7)</div>
<p>28:7 Excluded from God’s counsel, Saul resorted to illegitimate methods for finding God’s will. Saul had earlier prohibited the occult practice that he now engaged in himself (28:3). • Endor was on the boundary between Israel and Philistia; the area near Israel’s border was probably a safer location for a banned practice.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 28:9](/en/kjv/1-samuel/28#v9)</div>
<p>28:9 outlawed: Literally cut off, suggesting that Saul might have had most of the mediums executed.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 28:12](/en/kjv/1-samuel/28#v12)</div>
<p>28:12 It is not clear why seeing Samuel caused the woman to recognize Saul.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 28:13](/en/kjv/1-samuel/28#v13)</div>
<p>28:13 Saul asked the medium to describe what she saw that caused her to scream. She described a divine being, a god (Hebrew ’elohim) coming up. Perhaps she observed a human figure with a godlike or angelic appearance, or her use of the term god might reflect a pagan belief that a person becomes a god—a spirit possessing supernatural abilities—after death.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 28:15](/en/kjv/1-samuel/28#v15)</div>
<p>28:15 God has left me: The same Hebrew term is used in 16:14 and Judg 16:20. • won’t reply by prophets or dreams: Cp. 1 Sam 28:6.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 28:16](/en/kjv/1-samuel/28#v16)</div>
<p>28:16 Saul’s enemy was not David but the Lord himself.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 28:17](/en/kjv/1-samuel/28#v17)</div>
<p>28:17 torn the kingdom from you: See 15:28.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 28:18](/en/kjv/1-samuel/28#v18)</div>
<p>28:18 you refused: Samuel’s message to Saul had not changed (cp. 15:19).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 28:19](/en/kjv/1-samuel/28#v19)</div>
<p>28:19 here with me: In Sheol, the abode of the dead (cp. 2:6, “the grave”; see also Num 16:30-33; Job 17:1-16; Ps 49:11; Rev 1:18).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 28:24](/en/kjv/1-samuel/28#v24)</div>
<p>28:24 fattening a calf . . . dough . . . bread: This meal was a major undertaking, a model of hospitality (cp. Gen 18:5-7). This meal the medium shared with Saul recalls the fine meal Samuel had shared with Saul (1 Sam 9:22-24) before he anointed Saul as king. That meal with God’s prophet marked the beginning of Saul’s kingship. This meal with a condemned spiritist came shortly before his death.</p>
</div>
