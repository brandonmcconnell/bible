<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 8:1](/en/kjv/1-samuel/8#v1)</div>
<p>8:1–12:25 During Israel’s transition to a monarchy, neither God (8:7-9) nor Samuel (12:1-25) was pleased by the people’s demand for a king. Saul, the first king (chs 9–11), failed in his role (chs 13–31) and fulfilled Samuel’s warnings (see 8:10-18; cp. 16:1-13).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 8:2](/en/kjv/1-samuel/8#v2)</div>
<p>8:2 Samuel’s two oldest sons functioned as judges in Beersheba, fifty miles south of their father’s home.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 8:3](/en/kjv/1-samuel/8#v3)</div>
<p>8:3 they were not like their father: They were more like Eli’s two sons (2:12-17). Their corruption was a primary reason the era of the judges ended.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 8:5](/en/kjv/1-samuel/8#v5)</div>
<p>8:5 you are now old, and your sons are not like you: Judges tended to be local leaders; kings, as national leaders, were more capable of uniting a whole nation in times of crisis (8:20). However, a spiritual problem underlay the request for a king (8:7-8). • Other nations, such as Egypt and Sumer, had monarchies for almost 2,000 years before Samuel’s time.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 8:6](/en/kjv/1-samuel/8#v6)</div>
<p>8:6 Samuel was displeased: He probably felt personally rejected (8:7).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 8:7](/en/kjv/1-samuel/8#v7)</div>
<p>8:7 Do everything they say: God rarely instructed a prophet to heed the voice of the errant populace. The will of the people and the will of God would converge in King David (see 2 Sam 7:8-17). • they are rejecting me: Their rejection of judgeship was a deeper rejection of God’s rule and sovereignty (see also 1 Sam 12:1-17).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 8:8](/en/kjv/1-samuel/8#v8)</div>
<p>8:8 followed other gods: The people’s request for a king was tantamount to idolatry. Israel’s monarchy was a divine concession rather than a divine gift. Kingship or any other human institution becomes idolatrous when it replaces trust in God.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 8:10](/en/kjv/1-samuel/8#v10)</div>
<p>8:10-18 These verses list the disadvantages of kingship. Each sentence begins with something the king would take. A king would be a confiscator, not just a protector.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 8:11](/en/kjv/1-samuel/8#v11)</div>
<p>8:11 While the people wanted a king to judge them, Samuel warned that the king would reign over them. They wanted a leader but received a ruler.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 8:12](/en/kjv/1-samuel/8#v12)</div>
<p>8:12-17 The king would take people as well as possessions.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 8:15](/en/kjv/1-samuel/8#v15)</div>
<p>8:15 A tenth of the harvest was already required as a sacred donation to support God’s Temple and servants (Deut 12:6, 17-18; 14:22-29; 26:12-15). The king would demand an additional tenth, a burdensome amount given the uncertainties of agriculture.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 8:17](/en/kjv/1-samuel/8#v17)</div>
<p>8:17 you will be his slaves: By demanding a king, the people whose ancestors had once been slaves in Egypt were risking a new enslavement (cp. 1 Kgs 12:1-20).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 8:18](/en/kjv/1-samuel/8#v18)</div>
<p>8:18 The people would beg for relief as their ancestors had done in Egypt under another punishing and exacting ruler (see Exod 3:7).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 8:20](/en/kjv/1-samuel/8#v20)</div>
<p>8:20 God had set Israel apart from the nations (Lev 20:26; Num 23:9). By insisting on a king and desiring to be like the nations, they were rejecting God’s plan (see 1 Sam 8:5-7; cp. Deut 17:14). • judge us and lead us into battle: Kings performed three basic functions: (1) waging offensive and defensive war, (2) administering law, and (3) ensuring economic well-being.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v21" data-v="21">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 8:21](/en/kjv/1-samuel/8#v21)</div>
<p>8:21 repeated to the Lord: The role of a prophet included bringing the people’s case before God (cp. Num 27:5).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v22" data-v="22">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Samuel 8:22](/en/kjv/1-samuel/8#v22)</div>
<p>8:22 give them a king: See study note on 8:7; cp. Rom 1:24-26.</p>
</div>
