<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Nehemiah 8:1](/en/kjv/nehemiah/8#v1)</div>
<p>8:1 The Water Gate was located toward the middle of the eastern wall of Jerusalem, south of the Temple and east of the palace ruins (3:26; 12:37). There was plenty of open space so no one would be excluded.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Nehemiah 8:2](/en/kjv/nehemiah/8#v2)</div>
<p>8:2 on October 8: The people came together on this day to celebrate the Festival of Trumpets (Lev 23:24-25), which marked the beginning of a new year in the postexilic calendar.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Nehemiah 8:3](/en/kjv/nehemiah/8#v3)</div>
<p>8:3 The timeframe from early morning until noon provided about six hours for reading and interpreting (8:7-8). • understand (see also 8:2, 8, 12): This was not just a ritual reading of the law. It included explanations, insight, and application.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Nehemiah 8:4](/en/kjv/nehemiah/8#v4)</div>
<p>8:4 These thirteen people were apparently community leaders. Some might have helped Ezra roll the scroll as he read.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Nehemiah 8:5](/en/kjv/nehemiah/8#v5)</div>
<p>8:5 The people all rose to their feet out of respect for God’s word (Job 29:8). Apparently, they stood the whole time Ezra was reading.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Nehemiah 8:6](/en/kjv/nehemiah/8#v6)</div>
<p>8:6 Ezra praised the Lord: See also 1:5; 9:32; Deut 10:17; Jer 32:18; Dan 9:4. • Amen! A statement of agreement (see Neh 5:13). Lifting hands (Ps 28:2) and bowing in humble worship were signs of reverence for God and submission to his word.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Nehemiah 8:7](/en/kjv/nehemiah/8#v7)</div>
<p>8:7-9 The Levites always had the role of teaching people God’s word (Deut 33:10; Mal 2:5-6). In this case, they probably explained the meaning of the Hebrew Scriptures to smaller groups of people in Aramaic. Most of the people spoke Aramaic, the language of Babylon, rather than Hebrew as their first language. Jewish leaders eventually translated large portions of the Hebrew Bible into Aramaic, allowing people to hear and study the Bible in a language they understood.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Nehemiah 8:9](/en/kjv/nehemiah/8#v9)</div>
<p>8:9-10 Ezra the priest and scribe: See study note on Ezra 7:6. • Don’t mourn or weep . . . today is a sacred day: God touched the people’s hearts, prompting them to regret their failures to keep God’s laws. While weeping is an appropriate response to sin (see Ezek 9:4; Luke 6:21), Ezra and Nehemiah encouraged the people to rejoice at what God had done. Because this New Year’s festival was to be a time of joy (Num 29:1-6), weeping would have destroyed the spirit of the day.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Nehemiah 8:10](/en/kjv/nehemiah/8#v10)</div>
<p>8:10 Rich foods such as meat were eaten only on a special occasion. • Sweet drinks referred to unfermented juice, in contrast to the vinegar of the common laborer (see Ruth 2:14). • It is right to share with those who have need (see Deut 15:11; Job 29:16; Prov 31:20; Matt 6:2-3; Acts 4:34-35).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Nehemiah 8:15](/en/kjv/nehemiah/8#v15)</div>
<p>8:15 make shelters in which they would live during the festival: The Festival of Shelters commemorated that the people of Israel had lived in tents during their wilderness journey (Lev 23:33-43; see also Num 29:12-39; Deut 16:13-15).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Nehemiah 8:16](/en/kjv/nehemiah/8#v16)</div>
<p>8:16 The Ephraim Gate might have been located on the north side of Jerusalem, facing toward the territory of the tribe of Ephraim (see 12:39).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Nehemiah 8:17](/en/kjv/nehemiah/8#v17)</div>
<p>8:17 since the days of Joshua: Although Israel had observed this festival on numerous occasions (1 Kgs 8:2; 2 Kgs 23:22; 2 Chr 7:8-10; 30:26; 35:18; Ezra 3:4), this was an exceptional celebration.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[Nehemiah 8:18](/en/kjv/nehemiah/8#v18)</div>
<p>8:18 Ezra read from the Book of the Law of God: The law was to be read every seven years at the Festival of Shelters (Deut 31:10-12). It reminded the people of the covenant stipulations and of God’s past acts of grace.</p>
</div>
