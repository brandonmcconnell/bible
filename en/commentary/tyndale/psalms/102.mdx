<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:1](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v1)</div>
<p>Ps 102 A frail and afflicted human being prays for the Lord’s compassion on Jerusalem and for his presence with its inhabitants.102:1-2 The psalmist appeals to the Lord not to turn away (or hide your face). Israel experienced his turning his face away during their exile.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:3](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v3)</div>
<p>102:3 Smoke is an image of transitory life (37:20; 68:2). • The psalmist’s bones represent his whole being (see 6:2; 34:19-22).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:4](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v4)</div>
<p>102:4 Like dried grass, the psalmist senses his life ebbing away.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:5](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v5)</div>
<p>102:5 skin and bones: The psalmist has wasted away because of his anguish (see 102:9; 109:24).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:6](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v6)</div>
<p>102:6 The owl was associated with ruin and desolation (Isa 34:11, 15).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:7](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v7)</div>
<p>102:7-8 The psalmist cannot sleep (see 63:6-8). Like a bird on the roof, he feels isolated from both friends (see 88:8) and enemies.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:10](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v10)</div>
<p>102:10 The psalmist blames the Lord for his troubles.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:11](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v11)</div>
<p>102:11 The psalmist’s existence seems empty and transitory (102:4; 109:23; 144:4).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:12](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v12)</div>
<p>102:12-17 The topic changes from the psalmist’s miseries to the Lord’s glory. A vision of the Lord’s compassionate kingship gives him confidence in Zion’s future.102:12 forever . . . to every generation: The frailty of humans (102:3-11) contrasts with the permanence of the Lord.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:13](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v13)</div>
<p>102:13 Now is the time for the Lord to restore Jerusalem from exile (see 75:2; Jer 29:10; Dan 9:1-19).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:15](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v15)</div>
<p>102:15-16 When the Lord arises (102:13), the nations will tremble with fear because they will see his glory (see 8:1; 19:1-4a; 24:7). The rebuilding of Zion and the Temple portray God’s glory and fulfill this promise. An even greater fulfillment will come with the New Jerusalem (Rev 21).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:17](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v17)</div>
<p>102:17 The people of Israel were destitute (or naked) during the Exile.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:18](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v18)</div>
<p>102:18-22 The Lord will have mercy; Jews and Gentiles alike will worship him.102:18 Let this be recorded: The written record stands as a memorial to the Lord’s goodness.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:19](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v19)</div>
<p>102:19 The heavenly sanctuary refers to God’s holy place on high (see 92:8-10).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v22" data-v="22">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:22](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v22)</div>
<p>102:22 The multitudes—both Jews and Gentiles—will join in the worship of the Lord (see 96:7, 9; 106:48; Isa 2:2-4; 43:10-13; 57:15; 60:3-7).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v23" data-v="23">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:23](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v23)</div>
<p>102:23-28 Although the psalmist suffers in the present, he bases his hope for the future on the Lord’s past faithfulness.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:24](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v24)</div>
<p>102:24 The psalmist draws a contrast between his days (102:23) and God’s years (cp. 90:4).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v25" data-v="25">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:25](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v25)</div>
<p>102:25-27 In contrast with the permanence of the Creator, the created order will wear out like a piece of clothing (see Isa 40:28). • The writer to the Hebrews describes this passage as having been addressed to Jesus, the Son (Heb 1:10-12).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v28" data-v="28">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 102:28](/en/kjv/psalms/102#v28)</div>
<p>102:28 The Lord’s presence provides the basis for the security and happiness of his people (23:6).</p>
</div>

</div>
