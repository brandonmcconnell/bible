<p>Ps 56 This individual lament moves from petition to confidence two times (56:1-4, 5-13).56:title David was seized by Philistines concerned about his loyalties and brought before King Achish (1 Sam 21:10-15).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 56:4](/en/kjv/psalms/56#v4)</div>
<p>56:4 mere mortals: Or mere flesh, in contrast to the immortal and infinitely more powerful God (see Isa 2:22).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 56:5](/en/kjv/psalms/56#v5)</div>
<p>56:5-7 The psalmist specifies his charge against his attackers as he prays for their evil to cease.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 56:8](/en/kjv/psalms/56#v8)</div>
<p>56:8 The psalmist makes a play on the Hebrew words translated sorrows (Hebrew nod) and bottle (Hebrew no’d).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 56:11](/en/kjv/psalms/56#v11)</div>
<p>56:11 mere mortals (Hebrew ’adam): The psalm also uses two other words for man: ’enosh (“people,” 56:1) and basar (“mere mortals,” 56:4). All express the ideas of mortality and finitude.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 56:13](/en/kjv/psalms/56#v13)</div>
<p>56:13 The light of God’s goodness and salvation will replace the darkness of death (see 18:25-29; 27:1).</p>
</div>
