<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 74:1](/en/kjv/psalms/74#v1)</div>
<p>Ps 74 The psalmist laments the destruction of the Temple in Jerusalem (586 BC) with vivid imagery, questions, fresh memories, and a direct appeal for the Lord to act (74:19-23).74:1-2 In the aftermath of Jerusalem’s fall, it seemed that God had forgotten his special covenant relationship with his people.74:1 God rejected his people because their sin aroused his anger (106:34-46). • Like a tender shepherd, God cares for his people, the sheep of his pasture (Pss 23; 100:3).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 74:2](/en/kjv/psalms/74#v2)</div>
<p>74:2 The Lord chose and redeemed Israel from Egypt in the Exodus (cp. Exod 15:13). • your own special possession: This is another expression for “your people” (Pss 28:9; 78:62; 94:14; 106:40).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 74:3](/en/kjv/psalms/74#v3)</div>
<p>74:3-8 Like a guide, the psalmist points out to the Lord what took place during the destruction of Jerusalem in 586 BC.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 74:12](/en/kjv/psalms/74#v12)</div>
<p>74:12 my king from ages past: The psalmist acknowledges God’s power and his historical bond with Israel (5:2; 44:4; 84:3).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 74:14](/en/kjv/psalms/74#v14)</div>
<p>74:14 Leviathan: See 104:26; Job 3:8; 41:1, 12, 22, 31; Isa 27:1. Here, the creature’s multiple heads suggest a mythical monster.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 74:15](/en/kjv/psalms/74#v15)</div>
<p>74:15 springs and streams to gush forth: God provided water in the desert for his people (see 68:8-9; Exod 17:6); he dried up the Red Sea (Exod 14:21) and the Jordan River (Josh 3:17).</p>
</div>
