<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 69:1](/en/kjv/psalms/69#v1)</div>
<p>Pss 69–72 These last four psalms of Book Two contrast with the psalms describing God’s mighty acts in creation and in redemption, capped by God’s march to Zion (Pss 65–68). Psalms 69–71 portray the king’s anguish, and Ps 72 responds with a prayer for God to bless the king.Ps 69 In this individual lament, the poet expresses his vulnerability, humiliation, and overwhelming pain, asking God to vindicate him for the sake of the righteous.69:title to the tune “Lilies”: Psalm 45 also notes this tune, but Ps 45 celebrates the glory of kingship, whereas Ps 69 is the cry of a tired king who feels abandoned by the Lord.69:1-2 The psalmist sees himself as almost drowning but still surviving chaotic forces and alienation from God (see 42:7; 69:14, 15; Isa 8:8).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 69:4](/en/kjv/psalms/69#v4)</div>

</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 69:5](/en/kjv/psalms/69#v5)</div>
<p>69:5-6 The psalmist confesses his sins and prays that God’s response to him will encourage others in similar conditions to place their hope in the Lord.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 69:7](/en/kjv/psalms/69#v7)</div>
<p>69:7-12 Mistreated and abandoned by family, friends, and community, the psalmist turns to the Lord for comfort, relief (69:13-18), and redress (69:22-28).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 69:9](/en/kjv/psalms/69#v9)</div>
<p>69:9 Though the psalmist concerns himself with God and all that is holy, insults have been his earthly reward. This verse was later applied to Jesus’ anger at the Temple’s money changers (John 2:17) and to his undeserved suffering (Rom 15:3).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 69:10](/en/kjv/psalms/69#v10)</div>
<p>69:10-12 When the psalmist denies himself normal comforts to seek God in prayer, the wicked ridicule him.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 69:13](/en/kjv/psalms/69#v13)</div>
<p>69:13-18 The psalmist’s only recourse is to pray for rescue and relief from his enemies.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 69:19](/en/kjv/psalms/69#v19)</div>
<p>69:19-21 Alone and misunderstood, the psalmist confesses that he cannot bear his doubts. His heart has been broken by his problems.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v21" data-v="21">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 69:21](/en/kjv/psalms/69#v21)</div>
<p>69:21 sour wine for my thirst: This description applies to Jesus’ suffering (Matt 27:34, 48; Luke 23:36; John 19:28-29).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v22" data-v="22">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 69:22](/en/kjv/psalms/69#v22)</div>
<p>69:22-23 The psalmist wants God to transfer his suffering (69:3) to his enemies. Paul applied these words to the unbelieving Jews of his day (Rom 11:9-10).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v25" data-v="25">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 69:25](/en/kjv/psalms/69#v25)</div>
<p>69:25 Peter quoted this verse after the death of Judas Iscariot (Acts 1:20).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v28" data-v="28">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 69:28](/en/kjv/psalms/69#v28)</div>
<p>69:28 Erase their names from the Book of Life: Cp. 1:5-6; 9:5; Rev 3:5; 20:15; 21:22-27.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v29" data-v="29">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 69:29](/en/kjv/psalms/69#v29)</div>
<p>69:29-33 The psalmist prays that his pain will turn to praise.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v31" data-v="31">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 69:31](/en/kjv/psalms/69#v31)</div>

</div>

</div>
