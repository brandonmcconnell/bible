<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:1](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v1)</div>
<p>Ps 22 All previous laments pale in comparison with this outcry against the enemy and God’s abandonment. The psalm contains two main sections: the lament (22:1-21) and praise for redemption (22:22-31), which leads naturally to Ps 23. The apostles saw in this psalm an expression of the sufferings of Jesus Christ, who ultimately fulfilled the purpose of David’s humiliation, rejection by people, and divine abandonment (Matt 27:35, 39, 43, 46; Mark 15:34; John 19:23-24, 28; Heb 2:12).22:1-21 The psalmist alternates between reflections on his present dire circumstances and memories of the Lord’s past faithfulness; he ends with a prayer for rescue.22:1-2 A deep sense of alienation from God overwhelms the psalmist (see 10:1).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:3](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v3)</div>
<p>22:3 God, the holy King, is set apart from all his creation, but he chooses to reveal himself to Israel. • God greatly desires that his people offer praises to him.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:6](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v6)</div>
<p>22:6-8 Both the Lord and people reject the psalmist. He vividly describes his anguish (22:1-2); he lives like a lowly animal.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:7](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v7)</div>
<p>22:7 Everyone mocks the psalmist’s confidence in the Lord (see 3:2; Matt 27:43), which shames him (Pss 44:13-14; 74:10; 89:50; 109:25).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:9](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v9)</div>
<p>22:9-11 The psalmist reflects on his past relationship with God (see 22:3-5). The Lord has been his very life.22:9 The Lord took care of the psalmist before he could do anything for himself (see 71:6; 139:13).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:12](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v12)</div>
<p>22:12-18 The psalmist returns to expressing his anguish and suffering (see 22:1-2, 6-8). His trouble comes from human beings who act like beasts.22:12 Bashan, a region known for its herds and vegetation, produced strong bulls (68:30).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:14](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v14)</div>
<p>22:14 The psalmist’s sense of being poured out like water expresses a meaningless, transitory existence that leaves him depleted.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:15](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v15)</div>
<p>22:15 Facing adversity is as horrible as dying of thirst (see 137:6). • Jesus may have had this verse in mind when he said, “I am thirsty” (John 19:28).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:16](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v16)</div>
<p>22:16 The wild pack of dogs represents fierce and unscrupulous people.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:17](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v17)</div>
<p>22:17-18 I can count all my bones: The psalmist’s enemies have utterly shamed him by taking his garments. Jesus also suffered this disgrace (see Matt 27:35). • stare at me and gloat: Enemies make the psalmist the object of their scorn (see Pss 35:24, 26; 38:16; 55:3; cp. 59:10). • throw dice: Literally cast lots. The casting of lots was sometimes a way for godly people to find God’s direction (Lev 16:8; Num 27:21; Acts 1:24-26). Here, the lots are thrown simply as a game of chance. The soldiers threw dice for Jesus’ clothing after the crucifixion (Matt 27:35; Mark 15:24; Luke 23:34; John 19:24).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:19](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v19)</div>
<p>22:19-21 The psalmist faces his troubles by praying for God’s rescue. Only the Lord has the strength to ward off dogs, lions, and oxen.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:20](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v20)</div>
<p>22:20 The sword makes evident the lethal intent of the enemy.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v22" data-v="22">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:22](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v22)</div>
<p>22:22-24 The psalmist praises the Lord and calls for the godly to join him. He tells of God’s goodness to the needy, including himself.22:22 I will proclaim your name: The writer to the Hebrews ascribes these words to Jesus (Heb 2:12). • my brothers and sisters: Literally my brothers. In the New Testament quotation, the Greek translation picks up the broader meaning as “my brothers and sisters.”</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v23" data-v="23">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:23](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v23)</div>
<p>22:23 The godly respond with awe (see 22:25).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:24](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v24)</div>
<p>22:24 Unlike his response to the wicked (see 21:8-12), God cares about the needy and hears their prayers (51:17; 69:33; 102:17).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v25" data-v="25">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:25](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v25)</div>
<p>22:25-31 The psalmist vows to praise the Lord, the great King of the earth (22:27-28) who cares for the needy. God alone deserves praise.22:25 The great assembly might include all nations (22:27; 26:12; 35:18).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v26" data-v="26">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:26](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v26)</div>
<p>22:26 God knows his people’s needs and addresses them (63:5; 81:16; 103:5; 107:9; 132:15; 145:16; 147:14).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v27" data-v="27">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:27](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v27)</div>
<p>22:27 families of the nations: All clans and tribes of humanity (see Gen 12:3; Rev 5:9-10) will live in submission to the Lord (Ps 96:1-3; see Pss 2, 72; Isa 2:2-4; 1 Cor 15:25-27).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v28" data-v="28">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:28](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v28)</div>
<p>22:28 The universal kingdom belongs to God alone (Obad 1:21).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v29" data-v="29">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:29](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v29)</div>
<p>22:29 The Lord invites all people, rich and poor (22:26), to the banquet as long as they worship him.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v30" data-v="30">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:30](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v30)</div>
<p>22:30 Future generations will join those from the past who praised the Lord (22:3-5). The descendants of the godly will receive blessings (22:30-31), unlike the offspring of the wicked (21:10).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v31" data-v="31">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 22:31](/en/kjv/psalms/22#v31)</div>
<p>22:31 The Lord’s acts of redemption (40:9; 50:6; 71:15, 16, 24; 97:6) demonstrate that he is righteous (see 4:1). His righteousness provides the character required for rule, judicial decisions, and relationships. This virtue leads to harmony and order (see 50:6).</p>
</div>
