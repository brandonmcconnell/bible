<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 122:1](/en/kjv/psalms/122#v1)</div>
<p>Ps 122 Jerusalem is here idealized as the city of God, the city of David, and the city of faith. A group of people on a pilgrimage joyfully arrive in the city (122:1-2). The psalmist sings an ode to Jerusalem (122:3-5) and prays for the city’s peace (122:6-9).122:1 The central sanctuary was the house of the Lord. This phrase originally referred to the Tabernacle (Exod 23:19; 34:26).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 122:2](/en/kjv/psalms/122#v2)</div>
<p>122:2 Several psalms celebrate or look forward to being inside the gates of the city (9:14; 87:2; 100:4; 118:19).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 122:3](/en/kjv/psalms/122#v3)</div>
<p>122:3-5 Jerusalem is a metaphor for God’s heavenly dwelling. David was the archetype for God’s chosen rulers.122:3 In the time of David and Solomon, Jerusalem was renowned as a place of security (1 Kgs 10:6-7).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 122:4](/en/kjv/psalms/122#v4)</div>
<p>122:4 Each of the people of Israel belonged to one of the tribes, and all of the tribes belonged to the Lord. • To give thanks is to present an offering acceptable to the Lord (50:14, 23; 100:4; 118:19). • as the law requires of Israel: See Exod 23:17; Deut 16:16.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 122:5](/en/kjv/psalms/122#v5)</div>
<p>122:5 the thrones where judgment is given: In the eras of David and Solomon (1 Kgs 3:28), Jerusalem was the home of wisdom and justice; it would be so again (Isa 9:7).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 122:6](/en/kjv/psalms/122#v6)</div>
<p>122:6-9 Prayers for the peace of Jerusalem are motivated by love of family and God’s sanctuary.</p>
</div>
