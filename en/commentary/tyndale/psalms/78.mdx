<p>Ps 78 This wisdom psalm exhorts the people to learn wisdom and faithfully pass it on.78:1-8 The prologue is an invitation to learn wisdom.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:2](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v2)</div>
<p>78:2 The psalmist recites Israel’s story (78:5-72) in a parable in order to teach wisdom and insight. • Jesus quoted this verse to explain why he taught in parables (Matt 13:35).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:3](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v3)</div>
<p>78:3-4 This story of what God has done should be told from generation to generation (see Deut 6:20-25; Prov 4:1-4).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:5](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v5)</div>
<p>78:5-6 The telling of the story should motivate God’s people to obey what he commanded (Deut 6:4-9).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:7](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v7)</div>
<p>78:7-8 God holds each generation responsible for its own response, which should be to maintain faith and hope (see 9:18; 27:14) and avoid being stubborn, rebellious, and unfaithful (see Deut 9:6-7; 10:16; 30:6; 31:27).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:9](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v9)</div>
<p>78:9-11 Ephraim, the second son of Joseph, received a special blessing from Jacob (Gen 48:15-20; 49:22-26; Deut 33:13-17). The land that his descendants received included fertile valleys and strategic roads (Judg 8:2). However, the people of Ephraim did not live in obedience to God’s gracious covenant.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:12](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v12)</div>
<p>78:12-16 The focus shifts from Ephraim (78:9-11) to Israel as a whole as the psalmist reflects on God’s power in Egypt (see 81:4-7) and in the wilderness.78:12 The miracles are the ten plagues against Egypt that God performed during Israel’s captivity (Exod 7–12).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:13](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v13)</div>
<p>78:13 God divided the sea when Israel crossed through the Red Sea (see Exod 14–15).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:14](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v14)</div>
<p>78:14 God used a cloud and a pillar of fire to guide and protect Israel in the wilderness (Exod 13:21-22).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:17](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v17)</div>
<p>78:17-31 Israel rebelled in the wilderness despite God’s abundant provision.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v23" data-v="23">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:23](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v23)</div>
<p>78:23-25 Abundance comes through the doors of heaven (Gen 8:2; 2 Kgs 7:2; Mal 3:10). • Israel ate manna for forty years in the wilderness (Exod 16:31-36; John 6:31).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v26" data-v="26">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:26](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v26)</div>
<p>78:26-31 God powerfully satisfied the people’s desires, but they did not respond with gratitude or faith. Therefore, God exercised his justice.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v32" data-v="32">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:32](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v32)</div>
<p>78:32-39 Israel’s rebellion resulted in God’s judgment, but even their short-lived and insincere repentance motivated God to exercise forbearance.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v33" data-v="33">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:33](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v33)</div>
<p>78:33 An entire generation died in the wilderness (Num 14:22-23, 28-35).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v34" data-v="34">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:34](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v34)</div>
<p>78:34-39 Israel’s continued existence, in spite of the people’s rebellion and unbelief (78:7-8), was due to God’s compassion.78:34 They repented: This demonstration of zeal was not sincere.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v35" data-v="35">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:35](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v35)</div>
<p>78:35-39 The Israelites remembered the right doctrine (9:9; 91:1), but they did not allow God to change their hearts (Lev 26:41).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v40" data-v="40">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:40](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v40)</div>
<p>78:40-55 The Israelites rebelled even though the Lord had rescued them from powerful plagues.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v56" data-v="56">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:56](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v56)</div>
<p>78:56-64 Israel’s rebellion desecrated the Promised Land. The people built shrines to false gods and worshiped idols (78:58) during the period of the judges (e.g., Judg 2:11; 3:7, 17-18).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v60" data-v="60">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:60](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v60)</div>
<p>78:60 God abandoned his dwelling at Shiloh because of the people’s idol worship (1 Sam 4:11; Jer 7:12).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v67" data-v="67">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:67](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v67)</div>
<p>78:67 The tribe of Ephraim was not completely rejected, but God favored Judah (78:68).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v68" data-v="68">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:68](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v68)</div>
<p>78:68-69 In the choice of Mount Zion instead of Shiloh as God’s sanctuary on earth, God moved from the north to the south (see Ps 69).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v70" data-v="70">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 78:70](/en/kjv/psalms/78#v70)</div>
<p>78:70-72 The God who had turned against his own people (78:62) chose David, a shepherd (Gen 46:34; 1 Sam 16:10-11), to become their shepherd (Ps 78:52; cp. 23:1).</p>
</div>
