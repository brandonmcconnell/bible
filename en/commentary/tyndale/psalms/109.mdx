<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 109:1](/en/kjv/psalms/109#v1)</div>
<p>Ps 109 The psalmist begins this lament with a general charge against his accusers: They do evil in return for good (109:1-5). At the end of the psalm, he turns to the Lord in prayer, asking for his protection (109:21-25) and vengeance (109:26-31).109:1 The psalmist sees no evidence that God cares (see 28:1).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 109:4](/en/kjv/psalms/109#v4)</div>
<p>109:4-5 The psalmist’s acts of kindness and godliness (see 35:12-14) have only met with stubborn opposition and expressions of hatred.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 109:6](/en/kjv/psalms/109#v6)</div>
<p>109:6-19 The NLT translates these verses as the words of the psalmist’s accusers; Hebrew lacks They say, which makes it unclear who is speaking.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 109:8](/en/kjv/psalms/109#v8)</div>
<p>109:8-11 Family members of those guilty of capital crimes often died with the perpetrator (Num 16:1-33; Josh 7:1-25) or had their property confiscated (1 Kgs 21:13-16).109:8 The wicked person apparently held a position of leadership. Peter referred to this psalm in regard to Judas Iscariot (Acts 1:20-26).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 109:13](/en/kjv/psalms/109#v13)</div>
<p>109:13 If one’s family name was blotted out, it meant there was no offspring to carry on the family name or remember its history (see Prov 10:7).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 109:14](/en/kjv/psalms/109#v14)</div>
<p>109:14 Children inherit the sinful nature of their parents (51:5; Exod 20:5; Eph 2:3). The speaker wants the Lord to punish the perpetrator for the guilt of family members who went before him (cp. Matt 23:35-36).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 109:16](/en/kjv/psalms/109#v16)</div>
<p>109:16 The defendant committed capital crimes and did not exercise even basic decency.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 109:18](/en/kjv/psalms/109#v18)</div>
<p>109:18 Cursing is the accused person’s whole way of life.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 109:20](/en/kjv/psalms/109#v20)</div>
<p>109:20 The psalmist appeals to the principle of retribution (see 94:23).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v21" data-v="21">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 109:21](/en/kjv/psalms/109#v21)</div>
<p>109:21-25 Vulnerable and miserable, the psalmist appeals to his Lord for help. The accusations have killed his inner spirit and even his physical stamina.109:21 The Lord’s reputation is at stake. If the wicked triumph, others could think that the Lord is also wicked and be drawn to that wickedness (1:1).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v23" data-v="23">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 109:23](/en/kjv/psalms/109#v23)</div>
<p>109:23 A shadow at dusk suggests a transitory, empty existence (102:11; 144:4); a single locust could easily be brushed off one’s clothing.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v26" data-v="26">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 109:26](/en/kjv/psalms/109#v26)</div>
<p>109:26-31 These verses form a prayer for God’s presence and for vindication. The psalmist wants everyone to see that he is vindicated by the Lord, not by any human agent.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v28" data-v="28">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 109:28](/en/kjv/psalms/109#v28)</div>
<p>109:28 The Lord’s servant demonstrates loyalty and obedience.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v29" data-v="29">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 109:29](/en/kjv/psalms/109#v29)</div>
<p>109:29 The psalmist had been accused of being clothed with curses (109:18); now he asks that his accusers be clothed with disgrace and humiliation.</p>
</div>

</div>
