<div className="cm-chapter" lang="en" data-commentary="tyndale" data-book="Ezek" data-chapter="30">

<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">Ezek 30:1</div>
<p>30:1-19 This third message against Egypt, using the form of a lament, essentially repeats the content of the first message (29:1-16). Judgment was to be poured out on Egypt and her allies.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">Ezek 30:6</div>
<p>30:6-7 From Migdol to Aswan (Hebrew to Syene): This means “from north to south.” See study note on 29:8-13.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">Ezek 30:12</div>
<p>30:12 I will dry up the Nile River: Egypt was completely dependent on the Nile for its prosperity, so having the Nile dry up would threaten the Egyptians’ livelihoods.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">Ezek 30:13</div>
<p>30:13-14 From Memphis, the most important city in the north, to Thebes, the most important city in the south, all of the cities of Egypt would be destroyed.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">Ezek 30:14</div>
<p>30:14 The location of Zoan is modern Tanis in the eastern part of the Nile delta, near where the Israelites had once worked as Pharaoh’s slaves. • Thebes was the sacred city of the god Amon and the capital of Upper Egypt in the south (so called because it was up the Nile River).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">Ezek 30:15</div>
<p>30:15 Pelusium was a fortress town on the northeastern frontier of Egypt.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">Ezek 30:17</div>
<p>30:17 of Heliopolis and Bubastis: These cities were located in the Nile delta.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">Ezek 30:18</div>
<p>30:18 Tahpanhes was a fortress town on the northeastern frontier of Egypt. • dark day: Egypt would see its light turned to darkness when God came to judge it, as in the Exodus plague (Exod 10:21-23).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">Ezek 30:19</div>
<p>30:19 At the end of this terrible judgment, the Egyptians would once again recognize God’s existence and power, just as they had at the time of the Exodus. God’s strength and reality are ultimately undeniable, even by those who do not bow before him.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">Ezek 30:20</div>
<p>30:20-26 This fourth message against Egypt shows that God had already begun to act against his old enemy.30:20-26 Jerusalem was under siege by the Babylonians, but this message rules out even the faintest hope of assistance from the Egyptians.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v21" data-v="21">
<div className="cmv-head">Ezek 30:21</div>
<p>30:21-23 broken the arm of Pharaoh: The Lord had already shattered the Egyptians’ strength in the defeat of Pharaoh Hophra by Nebuchadnezzar (see Jer 37:5-11). Had Hophra succeeded in his mission, the pressure on Jerusalem would have been relieved, at least temporarily; now all hope of help from Egypt was gone. There was no prospect that the broken arm would heal or even be temporarily bound up so that Pharaoh could protect Jerusalem. Egypt would be totally helpless, unable even to hold a sword as it awaited the final death thrust.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">Ezek 30:24</div>
<p>30:24-26 While disabling Pharaoh (30:22), the Lord would strengthen the arms of Babylon’s king, increasing the already uneven nature of the contest. The fate of the forthcoming battle of the superpowers rested entirely in the Lord’s hands, and he had already determined its outcome. Nebuchadnezzar clashed with the Egyptians on a number of occasions, ending with victory in 567 BC.</p>
</div>

</div>
