<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">

<p>27:4 Anger can be dangerous but can be countered with a gentle answer (15:1). Jealousy is harder to resolve (6:34-35).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Proverbs 27:5](/en/kjv/proverbs/27#v5)</div>
<p>27:5 A rebuke improves life by correcting harmful behavior (13:1; 14:6). Hidden, unexpressed love has no value.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">

<p>27:6 Wounds from a . . . friend are from a loving rebuke (27:5). They are better than flattery (many kisses) from an enemy who intends to harm rather than help.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Proverbs 27:7](/en/kjv/proverbs/27#v7)</div>
<p>27:7 Even the best teaching (honey) will be refused if offered at the wrong time. The key to real learning is the receptivity of the student.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Proverbs 27:8](/en/kjv/proverbs/27#v8)</div>
<p>27:8 A person who forsakes his family and strays from home will more likely find trouble than whatever he was seeking (e.g., Luke 15:11-16).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
