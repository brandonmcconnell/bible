<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Ezra 5:1](/en/kjv/ezra/5#v1)</div>
<p>5:1 Haggai first prophesied on August 29, 520 BC (Hag 1:1). Zechariah began prophesying about two months later (Zech 1:1). The books of Haggai and Zechariah record their messages (see also Ezra 6:14).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Ezra 5:2](/en/kjv/ezra/5#v2)</div>
<p>5:2 The Jewish leaders had not led by faith (see Hag 1:1, 12). Now God’s Spirit stirred them up (Hag 1:14), and they obeyed by getting to work. • Zerubbabel and Jeshua figure prominently in the books of Haggai and Zechariah.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Ezra 5:3](/en/kjv/ezra/5#v3)</div>
<p>5:3 Who gave you permission? Unlike the hostile opposition recorded in ch 4, this seems to have been a routine inquiry to make sure that everything was done according to official requirements.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Ezra 5:5](/en/kjv/ezra/5#v5)</div>
<p>5:5 God was watching over them: All credit goes to God’s sovereign control of events, not to any human leader or prophet. God had promised that the ruins would be rebuilt (Isa 44:26).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Ezra 5:6](/en/kjv/ezra/5#v6)</div>
<p>5:6-17 Ezra includes a copy in Aramaic of Tattenai’s letter to King Darius. Unlike the letter of 4:11-16, this letter was a straightforward inquiry into the validity of the Jews’ activity.5:6 other officials: A Persian term for inspectors or investigators.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Ezra 5:8](/en/kjv/ezra/5#v8)</div>
<p>5:8 the great God: This title was a Persian way of referring to an important high deity; it does not indicate that the provincial authorities believed in Israel’s God. • Following the pattern of Solomon’s Temple (1 Kgs 6:36), after every three rows of prepared stones, a layer of timber was laid in its walls (see Ezra 6:4) to reduce potential damage from earthquakes.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Ezra 5:11](/en/kjv/ezra/5#v11)</div>
<p>5:11 the God of heaven: This title would be understood by the Persians: He was the universal high God, not an insignificant local deity. • a great king of Israel: Solomon (see 1 Kgs 5–8).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Ezra 5:12](/en/kjv/ezra/5#v12)</div>
<p>5:12 destroyed this Temple and exiled the people: See 2 Kgs 25:9-17.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Ezra 5:13](/en/kjv/ezra/5#v13)</div>
<p>5:13 a decree: See 1:1-4.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[Ezra 5:14](/en/kjv/ezra/5#v14)</div>
<p>5:14-15 The Jews provided detailed information that the Persians could check for accuracy (see 1:7-11).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Ezra 5:16](/en/kjv/ezra/5#v16)</div>
<p>5:16 Most likely, Sheshbazzar began the work and Zerubbabel finished it (see study note on 1:8).</p>
</div>
