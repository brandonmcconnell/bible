<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joel 3:1](/en/kjv/joel/3#v1)</div>
<p>3:1-16 Joel here foretold divine judgment on the nations, particularly those that had oppressed God’s people.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joel 3:2](/en/kjv/joel/3#v2)</div>
<p>3:2-3 The Lord would assemble the armies of the world (literally all the nations) in the valley of Jehoshaphat. This valley was not connected with King Jehoshaphat of Judah (1 Kgs 22:41); rather, this description, which means “the valley where the Lord judges,” identifies the site as the place where God would render his decision (see Joel 3:14) regarding the fate of the nations. • The sins of the nations include exiling God’s covenant people and victimizing helpless boys and girls in an inhuman slave trade (cp. Ezek 27:13; Rev 18:13).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joel 3:4](/en/kjv/joel/3#v4)</div>
<p>3:4-6 The Phoenician cities of Tyre and Sidon and the cities of Philistia were guilty of looting the precious treasures of Jerusalem and selling its people as slaves to the Greeks (see Amos 1:6, 9).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joel 3:7](/en/kjv/joel/3#v7)</div>
<p>3:7-8 The people of Tyre, Sidon, and Philistia had enslaved Israelites, so the Lord would punish them by selling their sons and daughters into slavery.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joel 3:9](/en/kjv/joel/3#v9)</div>
<p>3:9 The nations are mockingly called to a futile war against the Lord that would end in their destruction and judgment.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joel 3:10](/en/kjv/joel/3#v10)</div>
<p>3:10 Hammer your plowshares into swords: Deliberately reversing an image from Isa 2:4 and Mic 4:3, Joel ordered the nations to mobilize every resource for the forthcoming battle. Even those unfit for war, the weaklings, must become soldiers.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joel 3:12](/en/kjv/joel/3#v12)</div>
<p>3:12 The battle would take place in the valley of Jehoshaphat (see study note on 3:2-3), where the Lord would pronounce judgment on them all.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joel 3:13](/en/kjv/joel/3#v13)</div>
<p>3:13 God would defeat the nations like a sickle that mows down stalks of grain and like workers who tread grapes in the winepress (see Isa 63:2-3; Rev 14:14-20).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joel 3:14](/en/kjv/joel/3#v14)</div>
<p>3:14 Thousands upon thousands (literally roaring, roaring; or crowds, crowds): The repetition of the Hebrew word captures the chaotic noise made by an immense crowd. • valley of decision: The name of the valley is changed (cp. 3:2-3, 12) because now the Lord’s judgment had been decided.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joel 3:16](/en/kjv/joel/3#v16)</div>
<p>3:16 The Lord’s voice, like that of a lion, would roar from Zion, shaking heaven and earth and terrifying sinners (see Amos 1:2). Yet this same ferocious lion is also a refuge for his people.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joel 3:17](/en/kjv/joel/3#v17)</div>
<p>3:17 Then you will know: God’s ultimate purpose is that his people should know him and share his holy character. They must learn that he alone is God and that he reigns over all creation from Zion, his holy mountain (see also 2:27). The only true security in the present, and the only hope for the future, comes from God’s presence.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joel 3:18](/en/kjv/joel/3#v18)</div>
<p>3:18 Blessings would flow from the Lord’s sovereign presence in Zion (see also Ezek 47:1-12; Amos 9:13). There would be a superabundance of sweet wine and milk. The Temple, God’s own dwelling on earth, would be the source of a spring that would forever water the arid landscape (see Zech 14:8; Rev 22:1-2).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joel 3:19](/en/kjv/joel/3#v19)</div>
<p>3:19 Even as Judah is restored, her ancient enemies Egypt and Edom would experience God’s judgment for their crimes against his people.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joel 3:20](/en/kjv/joel/3#v20)</div>
<p>3:20-21 The conclusion of Joel’s prophecy is the antithesis of its beginning. In 1:1-20, Judah’s future seemed in grave doubt. Plague and drought, the signs of divine judgment, threatened Judah’s existence. Now Joel ends with the divine assurance that Judah . . . and Jerusalem will endure through all generations. God’s people in every generation rest secure in the hope that his kingdom will reign over heaven and earth (see also Isa 9:6-7; Dan 2:44; 7:13-14).</p>
</div>

</div>
