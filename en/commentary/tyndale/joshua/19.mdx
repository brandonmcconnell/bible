<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joshua 19:1](/en/kjv/joshua/19#v1)</div>
<p>19:1 Simeon, Jacob and Leah’s second son, was older than Judah. However, he had forfeited a leading role with his violent actions against Shechem (Gen 34:25-26). The tribal inheritance of his descendants reflected this; their land was carved out of Judah’s territory on the southern periphery of the Negev. This arid land was far from any centers of influence and power.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joshua 19:2](/en/kjv/joshua/19#v2)</div>
<p>19:2-9 Some of the towns given to the tribe of Simeon were also part of Judah’s allocation because Judah’s territory was too large for them (cp. 17:14-18). Judah apparently absorbed most of Simeon’s territory into its own before the end of the Old Testament period.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joshua 19:10](/en/kjv/joshua/19#v10)</div>
<p>19:10-48 The five small remaining tribes received land on the edges of the Israelite territory and had little national influence.19:10-23 The lands given to the tribes of Zebulun and Issachar were strategically located; the major international trading route from Egypt to Mesopotamia ran through their territories. When Israel was strong, this position brought prosperity. However, when Israel was weak, these tribes were vulnerable both to the armies of Egypt and to the successive Mesopotamian powers that fought for control of the ancient Near East.19:10-16 The tribe of Zebulun received land partly in the valley of Jezreel and partly in the hills of Lower Galilee.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joshua 19:13](/en/kjv/joshua/19#v13)</div>
<p>19:13 Gath-hepher was the hometown of the prophet Jonah (2 Kgs 14:25).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joshua 19:15](/en/kjv/joshua/19#v15)</div>
<p>19:15 This Bethlehem was not the birthplace of David and Jesus in Judah. • More than twelve towns are named; some apparently did not belong to Zebulun but were on its borders.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joshua 19:16](/en/kjv/joshua/19#v16)</div>
<p>19:16 Both by number of towns and by size of its territory, Zebulun was smallest of all the tribes. However, the New Testament village of Nazareth, where Jesus grew up, was in the tribal territory of Zebulun (Matt 2:19-23; 4:13-16).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joshua 19:17](/en/kjv/joshua/19#v17)</div>
<p>19:17-23 The land given to the tribe of Issachar included much of the fertile Jezreel Valley. During Old Testament times, this valley was largely a swamp surrounded by prosperous and important cities. This area was a center of Canaanite strength, so this small tribe had trouble gaining a foothold at first.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joshua 19:24](/en/kjv/joshua/19#v24)</div>
<p>19:24-31 The land allocated to the tribe of Asher included the Plain of Acco on the Mediterranean coast and western Galilee. To the southwest, Asher touched Carmel, sharing at least a short common border with land allotted to the tribe of Manasseh. To the north, the Phoenician cities of Tyre and Sidon limited Asher’s expansion.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v30" data-v="30">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joshua 19:30](/en/kjv/joshua/19#v30)</div>
<p>19:30 The twenty-two towns did not include Tyre and Sidon, which the nation of Israel never controlled.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v32" data-v="32">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joshua 19:32](/en/kjv/joshua/19#v32)</div>
<p>19:32-39 The tribe of Naphtali occupied eastern Galilee and overlooked the Sea of Galilee. Because a branch of an international trade route from Egypt to Mesopotamia ran through the territory of Naphtali, this tribe enjoyed periods of prosperity when Israel’s kings were strong. The city of Hazor was within Naphtali’s territory, guarding a section of that route. Naphtali is mentioned in Matt 4:13-16 in connection with Jesus’ ministry in Galilee.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v40" data-v="40">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joshua 19:40](/en/kjv/joshua/19#v40)</div>
<p>19:40-48 The original allotment given to the tribe of Dan lay west of Judah and southwest of Ephraim’s main territory, between Judah and Philistia.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v43" data-v="43">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joshua 19:43](/en/kjv/joshua/19#v43)</div>
<p>19:43 About fifty years after Israel came into Canaan under Joshua, the Philistines moved into the southern coastal plain and occupied the cities of Timnah and Ekron (Judg 14:1-2; 1 Sam 5:1-10). The Philistines were among the Sea Peoples who had perhaps been driven out of the Greek mainland and the Greek islands by an invasion from the north. The Sea Peoples also invaded and destroyed the Hittite Empire to the north of Israel. (Some scholars think that Homer’s Iliad reflects this movement.)</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v47" data-v="47">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joshua 19:47](/en/kjv/joshua/19#v47)</div>
<p>19:47 The tribe of Dan had trouble taking possession of their land because of the Philistines, so a group of Danites later moved northward to Laish (see Judg 18), which they renamed Dan, on the northern border of Israel’s territory.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v49" data-v="49">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joshua 19:49](/en/kjv/joshua/19#v49)</div>
<p>19:49-50 The piece of land given to Joshua closes the section on the allocation of land to the tribes of Israel (see study note on 14:1–19:51).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v51" data-v="51">
<div className="cmv-head">[Joshua 19:51](/en/kjv/joshua/19#v51)</div>
<p>19:51 After Joshua received his inheritance, the division of the land was completed. Joshua served God and led Israel faithfully for many years, and God was gracious to Joshua.</p>
</div>
