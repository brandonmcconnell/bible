<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 3:4](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/3#v4)</div>
<p>3:4 Argob might be synonymous with Bashan or might refer to a heavily populated part of Bashan.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 3:8](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/3#v8)</div>
<p>3:8 Mount Hermon is the southernmost peak in the Anti-Lebanon Mountains northeast of the Sea of Galilee; at 9,300 feet above sea level, it is also the highest. On a clear day, this impressive landmark is visible from many miles away.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 3:9](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/3#v9)</div>
<p>3:9 The alternative names Sirion and Senir suggest that Hermon was perhaps a later name given by the Israelites. Hermon is apparently related to the verb kharam, which means “to destroy” (see study note on 2:34), and perhaps describes the destruction summarized in 3:3-7.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 3:11](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/3#v11)</div>
<p>3:11 His bed was probably wooden, inlaid with iron. Alternatively, the Hebrew word might suggest a sarcophagus or coffin. • Rabbah is the same city as Rabbath Ammon (see study note on Deut 2:19). Apparently there was some kind of museum at Rabbah when Deuteronomy was written, and this artifact could be seen there.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 3:12](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/3#v12)</div>
<p>3:12-20 See Num 32.3:12 The tribes of Reuben and Gad had asked Moses to let them settle east of the Jordan rather than in Canaan, and he allowed them to do so (Num 32:1-5). • Aroer: See study note on Deut 2:36.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 3:13](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/3#v13)</div>
<p>3:13 The half-tribe of Manasseh made the same request as Reuben and Gad (3:12), and Moses accepted their request. Gad and Reuben settled between the Arnon (see 2:24) and the middle of Gilead (see 2:36), and Manasseh took everything north of that, including Bashan (see 3:1). • The Rephaites, a giant people related to the Anakites (see 2:11), are noted here as being indigenous to Bashan.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 3:14](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/3#v14)</div>
<p>3:14 Jair was a descendant of Manasseh from Makir and Gilead (1 Chr 2:22). • Geshurites and Maacathites, kingdoms of Bashan, lay along the west side of the Golan Heights, east of the Sea of Galilee. • The Towns of Jair was the name given Argob (see Deut 3:4) after Jair brought it under Israelite control.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 3:15](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/3#v15)</div>
<p>3:15 Makir was a clan in the tribe of Manasseh (Num 26:29) to which Jair was related (1 Chr 2:21-23). The clan of Makir settled south of Bashan in the northern part of Gilead (see Deut 3:13).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 3:17](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/3#v17)</div>
<p>3:17 from the Sea of Galilee down to the Dead Sea: The Hebrew name for the beautiful Sea of Galilee is kinnereth, which might come from kinnor (“harp”) because of its shape. • The Dead Sea (Hebrew yam hammelakh, “Salt Sea”), here also called the Sea of the Arabah (see study note on 1:1), has a very high mineral content (about 30 percent). • Pisgah is a section of the Abarim mountain range; its most prominent peak is Mount Nebo, where Moses died (see 34:1).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v21" data-v="21">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 3:21](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/3#v21)</div>
<p>3:21-29 See Num 20:2-13; Ps 106:32-33.3:21 He will do the same: Israel’s military success under Moses’ leadership could be expected to continue under Joshua because the Lord promised to remain with them.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 3:24](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/3#v24)</div>
<p>3:24 Is there any god? Moses did not believe that other gods existed; he was simply affirming that only the Lord is God. Nothing and no one else, real or imaginary, can rival the one true God.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v29" data-v="29">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 3:29](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/3#v29)</div>
<p>3:29 Beth-peor, otherwise known as Baal-peor (see 4:3) or simply Peor (Num 23:28; Josh 22:17), became Moses’ burial place (Deut 34:6). At that place the false prophet Balaam had earlier attempted to curse Israel on behalf of Balak, king of Moab (Num 23:27–24:25).</p>
</div>
