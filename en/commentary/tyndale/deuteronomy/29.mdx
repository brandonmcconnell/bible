<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 29:1](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/29#v1)</div>
<p>29:1 The land of Moab was the region east of the Jordan where Deuteronomy was composed and delivered to Israel.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 29:2](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/29#v2)</div>
<p>29:2–30:20 Anticipating his death, Moses composed a farewell address, briefly reviewing Israel’s history since the Exodus. He charged the nation to pledge their fidelity to the Lord. This section of Deuteronomy makes it clear that grace is not just a New Testament doctrine: It undergirds every aspect of the Lord’s relationship with Israel.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 29:6](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/29#v6)</div>
<p>29:6 but he provided for you: This clause does not appear in the Hebrew but is implied by its context.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 29:7](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/29#v7)</div>
<p>29:7 King Sihon of Heshbon, ruler of the Amorites, had controlled an area from the Arnon River to the Ammonite border. See Num 21:31-35. • Heshbon: See Deut 1:4; 2:24. • King Og of Bashan had ruled an Amorite kingdom east of the Sea of Galilee (see 3:1).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 29:8](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/29#v8)</div>
<p>29:8 Reuben and Gad and . . . the half-tribe of Manasseh were granted their request to settle in the area east of the Jordan (see 3:12-13).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 29:11](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/29#v11)</div>
<p>29:11 foreigners (literally sojourners): See study note on 10:18. • The saying chop your wood and carry your water was a cliché for any servile work (see Josh 9:22-27).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 29:12](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/29#v12)</div>
<p>29:12 The technical phrase to enter into the covenant meant subscribing to the terms of the agreement.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 29:15](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/29#v15)</div>
<p>29:15 future generations: The covenant made with Moses’ generation was also for generations yet to be born. Abraham’s descendants of the future would have the privileges of the covenant, but they would also need to obey it personally to enjoy its blessings (see 4:9).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 29:17](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/29#v17)</div>
<p>29:17 The detestable practices were abominable manifestations of paganism, especially regarding worship (see 7:25-26; 12:31; 13:14). • idols: The Hebrew term, meaning round things, probably alludes to dung. These objects were so reprehensible that they were compared to excrement.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 29:18](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/29#v18)</div>
<p>29:18 This metaphor describes idolatry (the root), which, if cultivated, would result in idol worship (the fruit).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 29:19](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/29#v19)</div>
<p>29:19 An attitude of nonchalance or smugness regarding God’s judgment would lead to utter ruin.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 29:20](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/29#v20)</div>
<p>29:20 erase their names: To forget the name of a people resulted in eventually forgetting their very existence. The most unspeakable tragedy would be for God to treat the people of Israel as though they had never existed (see 7:24; Exod 32:32-33; 2 Kgs 14:27).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v21" data-v="21">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 29:21](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/29#v21)</div>
<p>29:21 Book of Instruction (literally book of the torah): Here, this phrase refers to all of Deuteronomy, of which the list of curses (ch 28) is a part (see 27:3).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v23" data-v="23">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 29:23](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/29#v23)</div>
<p>29:23 like . . . Sodom and Gomorrah, Admah and Zeboiim: In the days of Abraham and Lot, these cities were destroyed because of their gross immorality; their destruction was associated with sulfur and salt (Gen 19:23-28).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v25" data-v="25">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 29:25](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/29#v25)</div>
<p>29:25-28 This explanation anticipates a future when people would look back at Israel’s history of rebellion and easily understand why they were in exile.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v27" data-v="27">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 29:27](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/29#v27)</div>
<p>29:27 The phrase this book refers at least to Deuteronomy and perhaps to the first five books of the Old Testament (the Pentateuch).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v29" data-v="29">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 29:29](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/29#v29)</div>
<p>29:29 secrets known to no one: There is an apparent contradiction between God’s covenant promises to Israel on the one hand and Israel’s being sent into exile on the other. What purpose could there be in such a mysterious turn of events? Only God knows. • the terms of these instructions (literally the words of this torah): See 4:44; 27:3.</p>
</div>

</div>
