<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 22:1](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/22#v1)</div>
<p>22:1 don’t ignore your responsibility: An individual was not to avoid a troubled scene by pretending not to see what was going on. To do so would be a failure of the individual’s responsibility to the covenant community.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 22:5](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/22#v5)</div>
<p>22:5 Adopting the dress and behavior of the opposite gender unnaturally blurs the lines between things that should be kept separate and distinct (also 22:9-11).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 22:9](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/22#v9)</div>
<p>22:9 any other crop between the rows: This would bring about a mingling of things that should be kept separate.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 22:10](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/22#v10)</div>
<p>22:10 Plowing with an ox and a donkey would be inefficient; this example illustrates the principle of separation and order (see also 2 Cor 6:14-18).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 22:11](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/22#v11)</div>
<p>22:11 Keeping wool and linen separate illustrated Israel’s separation as God’s holy people (see also Lev 19:19).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 22:12](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/22#v12)</div>
<p>22:12 Placing four tassels on the hem of a garment probably reminded the wearer to be loyal to the covenant, similar to tying a string around one’s finger (see Num 15:37-41).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 22:14](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/22#v14)</div>
<p>22:14 A husband who discovered evidence that his wife had lost her virginity prior to marriage (see 22:15) would accuse her of shameful conduct.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 22:15](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/22#v15)</div>
<p>22:15 proof of her virginity: A blood-stained cloth would indicate that the woman’s hymen was ruptured by her first act of intercourse with her husband.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 22:17](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/22#v17)</div>
<p>22:17 The bed sheet (literally covering) might also have been a garment of some kind, such as a nightgown.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 22:19](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/22#v19)</div>
<p>22:19 100 pieces of silver: The husband, who had already paid the woman’s father the normal bride price, must now pay additional compensation for defaming her character.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v21" data-v="21">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 22:21](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/22#v21)</div>
<p>22:21 by being promiscuous (literally acting as a prostitute): A single act of sexual intercourse by an unmarried woman was enough to earn this disgraceful label and the death penalty.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 22:24](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/22#v24)</div>
<p>22:24 Because betrothal was tantamount to marriage in the Old Testament, another man’s wife in this passage technically refers to a fiancée.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v29" data-v="29">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 22:29](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/22#v29)</div>
<p>22:29 fifty pieces of silver: The father could still expect a dowry for his unmarried daughter (see Gen 34:12; Exod 22:16). • he must marry: The assumption is that the girl and her father were willing to undertake the arrangement (see Exod 22:17).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v30" data-v="30">
<div className="cmv-head">[Deuteronomy 22:30](/en/kjv/deuteronomy/22#v30)</div>
<p>22:30 The phrase his father’s former wife does not refer to a man’s own mother because incest was a more serious crime (see Lev 18:7-8, 29).</p>
</div>

</div>
