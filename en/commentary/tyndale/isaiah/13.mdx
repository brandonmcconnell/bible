<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 13:1](/en/kjv/isaiah/13#v1)</div>
<p>13:1–23:18 This section contains prophecies of judgment against the nations. By including a prophecy against Judah and Jerusalem (22:1-25) in the midst of prophecies against the pagan nations, Isaiah emphasized that Israel’s identity as God’s people would not protect them from God’s punishment when they sinned as the other nations do.13:1 At the time of this prophecy, Assyria was the major power. Isaiah anticipated the rise of Babylon as an even crueler kingdom that would destroy Judah and Jerusalem (39:6-7; chs 46–47). The universal language of 13:1–14:23 suggests that in this context, Babylon represents all wicked and arrogant nations in the same way that “Babylon the Great” does in the book of Revelation (Rev 18). Therefore, while this description of Babylon’s fall applies to the fall of historic Babylon in 539 BC, it also applies until the final judgment against the ultimate kingdom of evil (Rev 19).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 13:2](/en/kjv/isaiah/13#v2)</div>
<p>13:2 A signal flag or banner that could be seen clearly from a great distance was often placed on a bare hilltop as a call to battle. • God stands against Babylon as the symbolic representative of all evil kingdoms.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 13:5](/en/kjv/isaiah/13#v5)</div>
<p>13:5 Just as Assyria was the rod of his anger (9:4; 10:5, 15), other nations serve as the Lord’s weapons and carry out his will. The Medes and Persians conquered Babylon in 539 BC. • God’s wrath is an expression of his justice as well as his intent to restore order to the world by obliterating evildoers (10:25-26; see Lam 2:22; Ezek 7:19).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 13:6](/en/kjv/isaiah/13#v6)</div>
<p>13:6 When pronouncing judgment that is still in the future, the prophets sometimes spoke as though it had already arrived. This emphasized the urgency of turning back to God (Ezek 30:3; Joel 1:15; 3:14; Obad 1:15; Zeph 1:7).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 13:8](/en/kjv/isaiah/13#v8)</div>
<p>13:8 Pangs . . . like those of a woman in labor: The day of the Lord brings sudden and overpowering pain and fear for the wicked (see also Jer 30:6; Hos 13:13; Mic 4:9, 10; 5:3).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 13:10](/en/kjv/isaiah/13#v10)</div>
<p>13:10 Cosmic upheaval and darkness are common images for the day of the Lord, a time of God’s judgment (see also Ezek 32:7-8; Joel 2:10, 31; 3:15; Matt 24:29; Mark 13:24; Rev 6:12-13; 8:12; cp. Isa 30:26).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 13:11](/en/kjv/isaiah/13#v11)</div>
<p>13:11 punish the world: See Rev 20:11.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 13:12](/en/kjv/isaiah/13#v12)</div>
<p>13:12 Solomon’s imported gold came from Ophir (1 Kgs 9:28).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 13:13](/en/kjv/isaiah/13#v13)</div>
<p>13:13 God will shake the heavens. The earth will move: Cosmic upheaval will accompany God’s coming in judgment (see study note on 5:25; see also Hag 2:7, 21; Heb 12:26-27; Rev 16:17-18; 18:21-22).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 13:16](/en/kjv/isaiah/13#v16)</div>
<p>13:16 These terrible acts of war would be perpetrated against Babylon, itself a cruel conquering power (see also Ps 137:8-9; Jer 6:11-12; Nah 3:10).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 13:17](/en/kjv/isaiah/13#v17)</div>
<p>13:17 The Lord is sovereign and controls history. He can stir up kingdoms to execute his will. • The Medes from northwestern Iran joined Persian King Cyrus in fighting against Babylon in 539 BC.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 13:19](/en/kjv/isaiah/13#v19)</div>
<p>13:19-22 This picture of Babylon as a perpetually haunted ruin contrasts with its magnificence at its peak (see Dan 4:29-30).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 13:20](/en/kjv/isaiah/13#v20)</div>
<p>13:20 Babylon will never be inhabited again: Babylon, often a symbol of idolatry, immorality, imperialism, and oppression in Scripture, will one day come completely to an end (see Rev 18:2; 19:2).</p>
