<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 25:1](/en/kjv/isaiah/25#v1)</div>
<p>25:1-12 The second part of the Little Apocalypse (see study note on 24:1–27:13) describes praise for God’s judgment (25:1-5, 11-12) and salvation (25:6-9).25:1-5 Isaiah here praises God for protecting the needy (see 12:1-6).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 25:3](/en/kjv/isaiah/25#v3)</div>
<p>25:3 Assyria and Egypt were strong . . . ruthless nations, but in the future they will exalt God rather than their own power.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 25:6](/en/kjv/isaiah/25#v6)</div>
<p>25:6-8 The banquet is a symbol of God’s providing richly for all those who will receive his gracious invitation (Rev 3:20-21; 19:9).25:6 In Jerusalem: Literally On this mountain—that is, the mountain of the Lord (see 2:2; 24:23): This does not refer to a geographical city but to Zion, the eternal city of God (see 1:8, 27).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 25:8](/en/kjv/isaiah/25#v8)</div>
<p>25:8 He will swallow up death forever: Isaiah’s hope was God’s power over sin and sin’s devastating results. In the New Testament this promise is realized in Jesus Christ (1 Cor 15:54). • The Lord’s presence will be a source of eternal comfort; he will wipe away all tears (see Rev 21:4).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 25:9](/en/kjv/isaiah/25#v9)</div>
<p>25:9-12 The new community of God’s people will respond in joy and faith. When the enemy is defeated, God’s people will be free forever.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 25:10](/en/kjv/isaiah/25#v10)</div>
<p>25:10 on Jerusalem: Literally on this mountain. • Moab (see 15:1–16:14) here represents the nations under judgment, those who trust only themselves (see 34:5-17). • like straw trampled down: The people would be helpless and dying in the most degrading circumstances.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 25:11](/en/kjv/isaiah/25#v11)</div>
<p>25:11 Isaiah used the image of a swimmer forcibly kept under water to illustrate that Moab’s people are hopelessly doomed and their human pride will be conquered.</p>
</div>
