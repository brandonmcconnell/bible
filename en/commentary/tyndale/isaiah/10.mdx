<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:1](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v1)</div>
<p>10:1-4 This prophecy was directed against Judah’s leaders, who used their positions to enrich themselves at the people’s expense.10:1 What sorrow introduces a threat of divine judgment.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:3](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v3)</div>
<p>10:3 The implied answer to Isaiah’s rhetorical questions was that there would be no one to turn to when God punished Judah. • Disaster came first from the distant land of Assyria and later from Babylon.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:5](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v5)</div>
<p>10:5–11:16 The primacy of the Lord’s moral law was established in 9:8–10:4; this passage works out the implications of the law: (1) Assyria was only a tool in God’s hands and was therefore as liable to judgment by God as any other nation (10:5-19, 28-34); (2) those among God’s people who trusted in him and obeyed his covenant would be rescued (10:20-27); and (3) God would establish his kingdom on earth (ch 11).10:5-19 This judgment was pronounced on Assyria because of their ruthless destruction of nations (10:7), blasphemous boasting (10:12), oppression (10:13-14), and self-perceived autonomy (10:15).10:5 The Assyrians were God’s instrument (rod . . . club) in judging Syria, Israel, and Judah, but they would not go unpunished for their own wickedness.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:6](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v6)</div>
<p>10:6 Despite their privileged calling as God’s covenant people, Judah had become a godless nation, making them the object of God’s anger (10:4). • The word plunder translates both the Hebrew words shalal and baz, recalling the name Maher-shalal-hash-baz (see 5:19; 8:1, 3, 5-10).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:7](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v7)</div>
<p>10:7 will not understand: The Assyrians did not realize they were following God’s intended purpose to punish Israel and Judah. Because they went about savagely killing and looting other nations of their own accord, God would also hold them guilty.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:9](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v9)</div>
<p>10:9 We destroyed: By 717 BC, the regions to the north of Judah were firmly in Assyrian hands. Calno, in northern Syria (referred to as Calneh in Amos 6:2), fell to the Assyrian king Tiglath-pileser III in 738 BC. Carchemish, on the Euphrates River, was taken by King Sargon II in 717 BC. Hamath, on the Orontes River, was subjugated in 738 and 720 BC. Arpad was located south of Calno. Damascus and Samaria, the capitals of Syria and Israel, were taken in 732 and 722 BC, respectively (Isa 7:8; 8:4).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:10](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v10)</div>
<p>10:10 whose gods were greater than those in Jerusalem and Samaria: Assyria would make this argument while threatening Jerusalem (36:19-20; 37:12).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:11](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v11)</div>
<p>10:11 Assyria destroyed Samaria under Shalmaneser V and Sargon II in 722 BC (2 Kgs 17:3-6). If Samaria and Judah worshiped the same God, and Assyria had already defeated Samaria, the king of Assyria could expect to defeat Judah also.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:12](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v12)</div>
<p>10:12 Any royal power that exalts itself against the Lord, the Great King, is proud and arrogant (see 2:11-12) and will be crushed.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:13](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v13)</div>
<p>10:13 my own powerful arm . . . my own shrewd wisdom (cp. 10:5): God alone is all-powerful and wise. He plans the future, and he determines which nations will rise to power and which will be defeated.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:15](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v15)</div>
<p>10:15 ax . . . saw . . . rod . . . wooden cane: Such instruments are only as good as the person who uses them. Assyria considered itself independent of the hand of God, but their conquests were made possible only by his permission and to serve his purposes (10:5).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:16](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v16)</div>
<p>10:16 The fulfillment of this prophecy was in 701 BC, when 185,000 Assyrian troops were killed by plague and flaming fire (see 37:36).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:20](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v20)</div>
<p>10:20-27 In the midst of Assyrian oppression, there was hope for the remnant. This passage develops more fully the meaning of Shear-jashub, a remnant will return (10:21; see 7:3; 8:18).10:20 left . . . the survivors: A remnant from Jerusalem barely survived, but their safety was secure because God promised to preserve them (see 1:8-9; 4:2; 6:13; 37:31-32). • All these wars would teach the Israelite remnant to no longer depend on allies such as Assyria. Instead, they would faithfully trust in the Lord (see 8:13; 17:7).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v22" data-v="22">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:22](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v22)</div>
<p>10:22 God had similarly promised descendants as numerous as the sand of the seashore to Abraham (Gen 22:17). • rightly decided to destroy: God’s decision was a just response to the people’s persistent wickedness.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:24](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v24)</div>
<p>10:24-34 God assured his people of his presence and purpose.10:24 as the Egyptians did long ago: See Exod 1:8–2:25.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v26" data-v="26">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:26](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v26)</div>
<p>10:26 Gideon triumphed over the Midianites: See Judg 7:25.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v27" data-v="27">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:27](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v27)</div>
<p>10:27 Israel’s bondage ended in 539 BC with a new exodus from exile.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v28" data-v="28">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 10:28](/en/kjv/isaiah/10#v28)</div>
<p>10:28-34 Look, the Assyrians are now at Aiath: The march described in 10:29-32 is a poetic account of Assyria’s defeat. The places follow a route from the north down to Jerusalem. Upon arriving at Jerusalem, the Assyrians were forced to retreat. God was with Jerusalem, and eventually Assyria would be utterly destroyed. This passage might foretell Sennacherib’s invasion in 701 BC (see chs 36–37), but it could just as well describe an earlier or later invasion.</p>
</div>
