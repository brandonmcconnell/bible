<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 18:1](/en/kjv/isaiah/18#v1)</div>
<p>18:1 Ethiopia was at the southern extremity of the kingdom of Egypt. At the time, Egypt was ruled by the dynasty of the Ethiopian Piankhi.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 18:3](/en/kjv/isaiah/18#v3)</div>
<p>18:3 Both the battle flag and ram’s horn were used to marshal and send signals to armies in battle (see Judg 3:27; 6:34; 2 Sam 2:28).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 18:4](/en/kjv/isaiah/18#v4)</div>
<p>18:4 The Lord is untroubled by human activities; he watches quietly from his dwelling place and acts in his own time (see Ps 2:1-4).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 18:5](/en/kjv/isaiah/18#v5)</div>
<p>18:5 your plans are ripening: Ethiopia had made every effort to form alliances to protect itself against Assyria, but its alliance with Judah would not help.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 18:7](/en/kjv/isaiah/18#v7)</div>
<p>18:7 Conquered or weaker nations were often forced to pay tribute to a dominant king in the region (see 16:1; 2 Kgs 3:4-5). The Ethiopians would come bringing gifts to honor the Lord for his defeat of the Assyrians (see also 2 Chr 32:23).</p>
</div>
