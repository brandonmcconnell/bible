<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 33:1](/en/kjv/isaiah/33#v1)</div>
<p>33:1 This was the sixth threat of woe (see study note on 28:1–33:24). • Although the Hebrew text does not specifically name Assyria as the object of the prophecy in this chapter, Isaiah undoubtedly had Assyria in mind; they were the destroyer most immediately at hand when the prophecy was written. The prophecy applies, however, to any who seek to destroy God’s people; this included, but was not limited to, the Babylonians (see chs 13–14). • When you are done betraying: Wicked nations often break political agreements with other nations when these contracts are no longer to their own advantage.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 33:2](/en/kjv/isaiah/33#v2)</div>
<p>33:2 The godly community prayed for God to be merciful in response to the promise of 30:18.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 33:9](/en/kjv/isaiah/33#v9)</div>
<p>33:9 Sharon, Bashan, and Carmel were all fertile areas. Their desolation represents the destruction Assyria had inflicted on Israel.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 33:11](/en/kjv/isaiah/33#v11)</div>
<p>33:11 In most passages, the Assyrians were turning other nations into worthless dry grass and stubble (see 17:13; 29:5; 40:24; 41:2). Here, ironically, they get a taste of their own medicine.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 33:14](/en/kjv/isaiah/33#v14)</div>
<p>33:14 The godless were people who lived without regard for God and his law. • The image of a devouring fire came from God’s judgment of offenders during the wilderness journey (Exod 24:17; Deut 4:24). God still expects people to worship him with reverence and awe (Heb 12:29).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 33:15](/en/kjv/isaiah/33#v15)</div>
<p>33:15 A godly life provides evidence that an individual truly knows God (see Col 3:5-17; Jas 2:14-18; 1 Pet 1:14-16; 1 Jn 1:5-6).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 33:17](/en/kjv/isaiah/33#v17)</div>
<p>33:17-24 This vision is about God’s reign in Zion.33:17 The king is the human representative (32:1) of the Great King, God himself (33:22). • The land belonging to God’s people would appear to be without borders because the foreign enemies had been judged (54:3).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 33:18](/en/kjv/isaiah/33#v18)</div>
<p>33:18 Assyrian officers brought great terror to Judah when the Assyrians defeated various cities around Jerusalem (2 Kgs 18:13).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 33:19](/en/kjv/isaiah/33#v19)</div>
<p>33:19 The strange, unknown language was the tongue of Assyria and later of Babylon (see 28:11).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">[Isaiah 33:24](/en/kjv/isaiah/33#v24)</div>
<p>33:24 The people of Israel were the people of Zion (cp. 33:20). • sick . . . the Lord will forgive their sins: See 43:25.</p>
</div>
