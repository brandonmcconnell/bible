<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Leviticus 22:2](/en/kjv/leviticus/22#v2)</div>
<p>22:2-6 The priest’s portions of the sacred offerings were regarded as “most holy” (see 2:3) and were not to come into contact with anything unclean (11:1–15:33). Accordingly, any descendant of Aaron who was unclean was forbidden to eat this food.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Leviticus 22:5](/en/kjv/leviticus/22#v5)</div>
<p>22:5 In Leviticus, the Hebrew word sherets always denotes an unclean small animal that swarms on land or in water (5:2; 11:10, 20-21, 23, 29, 31, 41-44).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Leviticus 22:9](/en/kjv/leviticus/22#v9)</div>
<p>22:9 will die for violating my instructions: As in 10:1-3. The temptation to offer improper offerings persisted throughout Israel’s history until after the Exile (Mal 1:6-9).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Leviticus 22:11](/en/kjv/leviticus/22#v11)</div>
<p>22:11 The only male outside the priest’s family who could eat the priest’s share was the priest’s slave. He would have no other food if this provision were denied him.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[Leviticus 22:14](/en/kjv/leviticus/22#v14)</div>
<p>22:14-16 Similar to provisions for the guilt offering, a common person who accidentally ate the priestly portion had to repay 120 percent (see 5:14-16). However, the offering of a ram was not required in this case.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Leviticus 22:15](/en/kjv/leviticus/22#v15)</div>
<p>22:15 An Israelite who ate the “most holy” priestly portion as if it were common food would defile it.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[Leviticus 22:18](/en/kjv/leviticus/22#v18)</div>
<p>22:18 Apparently individuals could present a whole burnt offering instead of a peace offering if they desired (see 1:3).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v21" data-v="21">
<div className="cmv-head">[Leviticus 22:21](/en/kjv/leviticus/22#v21)</div>
<p>22:21 A peace offering could function in several ways: as a thanksgiving offering, an offering to fulfill a vow, or a voluntary offering (see 3:1-17).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v23" data-v="23">
<div className="cmv-head">[Leviticus 22:23](/en/kjv/leviticus/22#v23)</div>
<p>22:23 The requirements for the voluntary offering were more lenient, allowing the use of a deformed animal. Fulfilling a vow, however, still required an animal that was not maimed or diseased. In no case would God accept a sacrifice that could not be eaten by a person or that was not a whole animal.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v27" data-v="27">
<div className="cmv-head">[Leviticus 22:27](/en/kjv/leviticus/22#v27)</div>
<p>22:27 Leaving a newborn animal with its mother for seven days allowed time to determine the animal’s health (see Exod 22:30).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v28" data-v="28">
<div className="cmv-head">[Leviticus 22:28](/en/kjv/leviticus/22#v28)</div>
<p>22:28 on the same day: There are several possible explanations for this command, although none is certain: (1) Senseless slaughtering would leave nothing of the herd; (2) it would be inhumane to slaughter the young and the mother at the same time; or (3) it would be too similar to pagan worship rites, where the newborn animal was cooked in the milk of its slain mother (see Exod 23:19).</p>
</div>
