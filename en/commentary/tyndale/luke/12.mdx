<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:1](/en/kjv/luke/12#v1)</div>
<p>12:1 Yeast permeates dough until it is entirely leavened. Leaven became a symbol for the permeating power of sin (Exod 12:14-20; 1 Cor 5:6). Like yeast, the hypocrisy of the Pharisees would spread until it affected all the people.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:2](/en/kjv/luke/12#v2)</div>
<p>12:2-3 These verses are a warning against hypocrisy. All thoughts will be revealed by God, who knows all things and will judge every human being (Job 10:4-7; 11:11; Pss 11:4; 33:15; 139:12; Prov 15:3).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:5](/en/kjv/luke/12#v5)</div>
<p>12:5 hell (Greek Gehenna): The Valley of Hinnom was a ravine along the southwestern side of Jerusalem. It became a metaphor for hell because Israelites had offered their children as sacrifices there (Jer 32:35) and later used it as a place to burn garbage (Jer 19:2, 10-13). Jesus’ disciples should never be afraid of enemies who can only take away their life on earth. Instead, they should fear God, who can give eternal punishment.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:6](/en/kjv/luke/12#v6)</div>
<p>12:6 The two copper coins were each worth about half an hour’s work for a day laborer.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:8](/en/kjv/luke/12#v8)</div>
<p>12:8 will also acknowledge: At the final judgment (see Dan 7:7-14).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:10](/en/kjv/luke/12#v10)</div>
<p>12:10 The Holy Spirit draws people to God. One who blasphemes the Holy Spirit rejects his testimony about Jesus and so rejects God. This is the unpardonable sin (see study notes on Matt 12:31-32).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:11](/en/kjv/luke/12#v11)</div>
<p>12:11-12 brought to trial: Jesus predicted persecution from Jews in the synagogues and from Gentile rulers and authorities. Paul and other apostles faced both kinds of persecution throughout Acts.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:13](/en/kjv/luke/12#v13)</div>
<p>12:13-21 This parable shows the danger of trusting in riches instead of in God.12:13 tell my brother to divide our father’s estate: In Judaism, the oldest son received a double portion of the inheritance (Deut 21:17) and was responsible for dividing up the rest after his father’s death. This younger brother wanted his share of the estate (cp. Luke 15:11-32).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:15](/en/kjv/luke/12#v15)</div>
<p>12:15 Guard against every kind of greed: See Exod 20:17; Deut 5:21; Job 31:24-25; Ps 49; Eccl 2:1-11.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:16](/en/kjv/luke/12#v16)</div>
<p>12:16-20 On the surface, the rich man had a perfectly prudent plan, but the constant focus was on himself (cp. Eccl 5:10); he never considered giving crops to the needy. He assumed that his riches would last and only wanted a place to store them, without bringing God into the equation at all (Jas 4:13-17). His judgment was just.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:19](/en/kjv/luke/12#v19)</div>
<p>12:19-20 My friend: Literally Soul. The man was speaking to himself. • You fool! In Scripture, a fool is not someone with a low intelligence, but one who dishonors and disobeys God (Ps 14:1-5). • You will die (literally Your soul will be demanded from you): The man was satisfied with his soul, but God was not.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v22" data-v="22">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:22](/en/kjv/luke/12#v22)</div>
<p>12:22-34 Jesus’ warning against greed (12:13-21) is followed by teaching about living in dependence on God. Jesus’ disciples were to focus on God’s Kingdom and his purpose in this world rather than being obsessed with possessions.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:24](/en/kjv/luke/12#v24)</div>
<p>12:24 Look at the ravens: Ravens were unclean (Lev 11:15; Deut 14:14), yet God cared even for them (Ps 147:9).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v25" data-v="25">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:25](/en/kjv/luke/12#v25)</div>
<p>12:25 add a single moment to your life (literally add a single cubit to his length): Worry cannot add even one step on the road of life.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v27" data-v="27">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:27](/en/kjv/luke/12#v27)</div>
<p>12:27 Solomon was among the richest kings of the world in his day (see 2 Chr 9:13-22).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v30" data-v="30">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:30](/en/kjv/luke/12#v30)</div>
<p>12:30 unbelievers: Greek ethnē, sometimes translated “the nations” or “the Gentiles.” To a Jewish audience, it meant all who were not God’s people.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v31" data-v="31">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:31](/en/kjv/luke/12#v31)</div>
<p>12:31 Seek the Kingdom of God: Adopt God’s value system, pursue his purpose in the world, and submit to his authority.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v32" data-v="32">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:32](/en/kjv/luke/12#v32)</div>
<p>12:32 little flock: God is the shepherd of his people (Ps 23; Jer 13:17; Ezek 34; Zech 10:3). Israel’s leaders were also identified as shepherds and were criticized for harming or neglecting their sheep (Ezek 34; Zech 10:3).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v33" data-v="33">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:33](/en/kjv/luke/12#v33)</div>
<p>12:33 Sell your possessions and give to those in need: This is not a command to liquidate all personal possessions, since elsewhere in the Bible it is assumed that believers will own property. It means recognizing that everything we have is God’s and should be used to serve him and his people (see Acts 2:44-45; 4:32-34). Believers are responsible for meeting the needs of the poor, especially in the church (Gal 6:10). • treasure for you in heaven: See Luke 16:1-13; Matt 6:19-21. When God gives wealth, he also gives a ministry to help the unfortunate.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v35" data-v="35">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:35](/en/kjv/luke/12#v35)</div>
<p>12:35-48 Jesus compared his faithful followers to servants in a master’s household who were always prepared for his return and faithfully managed the resources he had left with them.12:35 keep your lamps burning: This is a picture of readiness and vigilance (Exod 27:20-21; Lev 24:2; Ps 18:28; Matt 25:1-13).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v36" data-v="36">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:36](/en/kjv/luke/12#v36)</div>
<p>12:36 The master could return from the wedding feast at any time of day or night. Jesus’ followers must always be faithful and prepared for his return.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v37" data-v="37">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:37](/en/kjv/luke/12#v37)</div>
<p>12:37 he himself will seat them: In Middle Eastern culture, the master would never serve his servants! Jesus redefined the role of the master by serving his disciples, giving himself for them, and meeting all their needs (see 22:24-27). • An apron was typical garb for a servant.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v40" data-v="40">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:40](/en/kjv/luke/12#v40)</div>
<p>12:40 the Son of Man will come: See “The Son of Man” Theme Note.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v42" data-v="42">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:42](/en/kjv/luke/12#v42)</div>
<p>12:42 The faithful, sensible servant was a household manager. Wealthy homeowners appointed gifted and educated slaves as chief executive officers over their affairs.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v44" data-v="44">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:44](/en/kjv/luke/12#v44)</div>
<p>12:44 in charge of all he owns: Although servants in the Roman world were slaves (the master’s property), they could rise to very high social status, owning property, managing the entire household, and acting with the master’s authority.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v46" data-v="46">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:46](/en/kjv/luke/12#v46)</div>
<p>12:46 he will cut the servant in pieces: Such brutal judgment was common in the ancient world. It is uncertain whether Jesus was talking about temporal punishment or eternal damnation. • and banish him with the unfaithful: This could either mean “treat him as an unbeliever” (a temporal judgment) or “send him to hell” (an eternal judgment), depending on whether Jesus was referring to discipline for a believer or condemnation of an unbeliever. The point is that an unfaithful servant faces stringent judgment.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v47" data-v="47">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:47](/en/kjv/luke/12#v47)</div>
<p>12:47-48 who knows . . . who does not know: Sins of ignorance receive less punishment than intentional sins (Num 15:22-26; Ps 19:13; cp. Luke 23:34). • When someone has been given much: Believers will be held responsible for the knowledge and resources they have been given (see Jas 3:1). A sin in ignorance is still a sin.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v49" data-v="49">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:49](/en/kjv/luke/12#v49)</div>
<p>12:49 I have come to set the world on fire: Jesus’ ministry radically changed the status quo, bringing judgment on the wicked and purifying the righteous (see 3:16-17; 9:54; 17:29; Deut 4:24; Amos 5:6; Heb 12:29).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v50" data-v="50">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:50](/en/kjv/luke/12#v50)</div>
<p>12:50 a terrible baptism of suffering: God’s judgment is often pictured as an overwhelming flood (Pss 18:4; 42:7; 69:1-2; Isa 8:7-8; 30:27-28; Jon 2:5).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v51" data-v="51">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:51](/en/kjv/luke/12#v51)</div>
<p>12:51-53 I have come to divide people: Jesus’ ministry demands a decision for or against God that divides even family members.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v53" data-v="53">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:53](/en/kjv/luke/12#v53)</div>
<p>12:53 Jesus quotes Mic 7:6, which was interpreted by the rabbis as the crisis and persecution that would lead to the Messiah’s coming.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v54" data-v="54">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:54](/en/kjv/luke/12#v54)</div>
<p>12:54 Clouds beginning to form in the west brought rain from the Mediterranean Sea.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v55" data-v="55">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:55](/en/kjv/luke/12#v55)</div>
<p>12:55 The hot south wind blows into Israel from the Arabian Desert to the south and east (Job 37:17; Jer 4:11).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v58" data-v="58">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 12:58](/en/kjv/luke/12#v58)</div>
<p>12:58 on the way to court: This lawsuit over an unpaid debt would result in debtor’s prison. The officer is the bailiff of the court or the warden of the prison. It is advisable to negotiate accounts with your accuser, if possible, before reaching the courtroom. It is also best to reckon with God before the final day of judgment.</p>
</div>

</div>
