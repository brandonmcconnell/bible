<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:2](/en/kjv/luke/8#v2)</div>
<p>8:2-3 along with some women: Rabbis of Jesus’ day did not have women disciples, so this was unusual. Some of these women, like Joanna, were from wealthy families and contributed financially to Jesus’ ministry.8:2 Magdalene means “from Magdala,” a village north of Tiberias on the western side of the Sea of Galilee. Mary figures prominently in the burial and resurrection accounts (24:10; Mark 15:40, 47; John 19:25; 20:1, 18). Some have said that Mary Magdalene was the woman of Luke 7:36-38, leading to the belief that she was a former prostitute. There is no basis for this speculation.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:4](/en/kjv/luke/8#v4)</div>
<p>8:4-15 The parable of the farmer scattering seed depicts people’s varied responses to Jesus’ ministry of proclaiming God’s Kingdom.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:5](/en/kjv/luke/8#v5)</div>
<p>8:5 The custom in planting was first to scatter seed and then to plow it into the soil. • The seed that fell on a footpath running through the fields would not be plowed in, so it was stepped on and eaten by birds.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:6](/en/kjv/luke/8#v6)</div>
<p>8:6 fell among rocks: Often there was a limestone layer nine to twelve inches under the soil, so that the rain was trapped and seed sprouted quickly in the moist soil. But when the rain stopped, the sun evaporated the trapped moisture and the plant died.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:7](/en/kjv/luke/8#v7)</div>
<p>8:7 fell among thorns: One type of weed looked very much like wheat as it grew, but it had larger roots and took away the moisture and nutrients that the wheat needed.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:8](/en/kjv/luke/8#v8)</div>
<p>8:8 Anyone with ears to hear meant everyone who was willing to listen.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:10](/en/kjv/luke/8#v10)</div>
<p>8:10 the secrets of the Kingdom of God: What was hidden in the past about God’s reign was now revealed through Jesus’ words and actions. • When they look . . . they won’t understand: This quote is from Isa 6:9 (Greek version), a passage repeatedly used in the New Testament to explain Israel’s rejection of the gospel (see also Matt 13:14-15; Mark 4:12; John 12:40; Acts 28:26-27). In its original context, the passage refers to God’s certain judgment of Israel at the hands of the Assyrians. Israel’s sin and rebelliousness had taken them beyond the point of no return, so God blinded them in preparation for judgment. Those who rejected Jesus’ message were blind to the truth.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:11](/en/kjv/luke/8#v11)</div>
<p>8:11 The seed is God’s word: This refers to Jesus’ preaching of the Good News of the Kingdom.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:12](/en/kjv/luke/8#v12)</div>
<p>8:12 to have the devil come and take it away: Satan seeks to prevent unbelievers from seeing the truth.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:13](/en/kjv/luke/8#v13)</div>
<p>8:13 Deep roots indicate spiritual depth and maturity. The Greek word translated temptation can also mean “testing” or “trials.”</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:16](/en/kjv/luke/8#v16)</div>
<p>8:16-17 A small clay lamp was filled with olive oil and set in a wall alcove. Jesus’ message provides light for those who listen, and it reveals their heart attitudes.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:18](/en/kjv/luke/8#v18)</div>
<p>8:18 those who listen: Cp. Prov 9:9.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:19](/en/kjv/luke/8#v19)</div>
<p>8:19-20 Jesus had four brothers—James, Joseph, Judas, and Simon—and an unknown number of sisters (Matt 13:55; Mark 6:3). These children were probably born to Joseph and Mary after Jesus’ virginal conception and birth. (On the other hand, some believe that Mary remained a lifelong virgin and that Joseph was therefore a widower with children from a previous marriage when he married Mary.)</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v21" data-v="21">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:21](/en/kjv/luke/8#v21)</div>
<p>8:21 those who hear God’s word and obey it: Jesus was not rejecting his family but announcing that spiritual relationships—being part of God’s family—supersede family ties.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v22" data-v="22">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:22](/en/kjv/luke/8#v22)</div>
<p>8:22-25 Jesus’ calming of the storm demonstrated his authority over nature and strongly indicated his deity, since God is master of the seas (see Pss 65:7; 89:9; 104:6-7; 107:23-32).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v23" data-v="23">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:23](/en/kjv/luke/8#v23)</div>
<p>8:23 a fierce storm came down on the lake: Severe storms are common on the Sea of Galilee, which lies in a valley surrounded by high hills.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:24](/en/kjv/luke/8#v24)</div>
<p>8:24 he rebuked the wind and the raging waves: As Jesus rebuked demons (4:41) and diseases (4:39), so now he revealed his authority by rebuking the sea (cp. 2 Sam 22:16; Pss 18:15; 104:7; 106:9; Isa 50:2; Nah 1:4).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v26" data-v="26">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:26](/en/kjv/luke/8#v26)</div>
<p>8:26-39 This exorcism story demonstrated Jesus’ authority over spiritual forces. It also contrasted the faith and devotion of the freed man with the fear and rejection of the townspeople.8:26 the region of the Gerasenes: Gerasa was a city of the Decapolis, thirty miles southeast of the Sea of Galilee. Jesus deliberately went to a predominantly Gentile region and ministered there.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v27" data-v="27">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:27](/en/kjv/luke/8#v27)</div>
<p>8:27 living in the tombs outside the town: Touching a tomb or a dead body rendered a Jew unclean, or ceremonially defiled. Jesus willingly entered this unclean place to rescue a person enslaved by Satan.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v28" data-v="28">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:28](/en/kjv/luke/8#v28)</div>
<p>8:28 Jesus, Son of the Most High God: See study notes on 4:34, 41.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v29" data-v="29">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:29](/en/kjv/luke/8#v29)</div>
<p>8:29 evil (literally unclean) spirit: Unclean things are unfit for God’s presence (see also study note on 4:33).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v30" data-v="30">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:30](/en/kjv/luke/8#v30)</div>
<p>8:30 A Roman legion had about 6,000 soldiers, so this man had a great many demons.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v31" data-v="31">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:31](/en/kjv/luke/8#v31)</div>
<p>8:31 the bottomless pit: The Greek word abussos means “bottomless” and often refers to the underworld prison of evil spirits or fallen angels (Rev 9:1-2, 11; 11:7; 17:8; 20:1, 3; cp. 2 Pet 2:4). The metaphor is taken from the apparently bottomless depth of the ocean (the ancients had no means for sounding the ocean depths, so to them it was bottomless).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v32" data-v="32">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:32](/en/kjv/luke/8#v32)</div>
<p>8:32 a large herd of pigs: Pigs were unclean animals for Jews, which shows that the people of this region were Gentiles. The unclean demons were fit only for pigs.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v35" data-v="35">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:35](/en/kjv/luke/8#v35)</div>
<p>8:35 sitting at Jesus’ feet: The man sat in the position of a disciple, indicating his willingness to follow Jesus (see 8:37).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v37" data-v="37">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:37](/en/kjv/luke/8#v37)</div>
<p>8:37 begged Jesus to go away: Jesus’ power over spiritual forces frightened the townspeople, and their loss of property (the pigs) angered them. Their rejection contrasts with the faith of the healed man.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v39" data-v="39">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:39](/en/kjv/luke/8#v39)</div>
<p>8:39 go back to your family: The role of disciples is to proclaim to the world what Jesus has done for them.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v40" data-v="40">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:40](/en/kjv/luke/8#v40)</div>
<p>8:40-56 These two accounts of healing demonstrate Jesus’ authority over disease and death, and illustrate the power of faith.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v41" data-v="41">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:41](/en/kjv/luke/8#v41)</div>
<p>8:41 The leader of the local synagogue was the officer who maintained the local synagogue and organized services (see also 8:49; 13:14; Acts 13:15; 18:8, 17).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v43" data-v="43">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:43](/en/kjv/luke/8#v43)</div>
<p>8:43 with constant bleeding: Perhaps it was a menstrual disorder. The tragedy was both physical and spiritual, for such bleeding rendered the woman ceremonially unclean and thereby precluded her participation in community worship (Lev 15:25-30).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v44" data-v="44">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:44](/en/kjv/luke/8#v44)</div>
<p>8:44 The fringe of his robe probably refers to the tassels that pious Jews wore on the edges of their robes (see Num 15:38-39; Deut 22:12).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v45" data-v="45">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:45](/en/kjv/luke/8#v45)</div>
<p>8:45 Who touched me? Jesus recognized the touch of faith by a needy person.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v48" data-v="48">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:48](/en/kjv/luke/8#v48)</div>
<p>8:48 Your faith has made you well (literally your faith has saved you). Go in peace: Jesus said the same thing to the woman who had anointed his feet (7:50). The Greek word for “save” can indicate either spiritual or physical healing. Peace (cp. Hebrew shalom) refers to wholeness, not just lack of conflict (see 1:79; 2:14, 29; 7:50; 19:38, 42; 24:36).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v51" data-v="51">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:51](/en/kjv/luke/8#v51)</div>
<p>8:51 Peter, John, and James became the inner circle of Jesus’ disciples (see 5:1-11; 9:28; Mark 14:32-33).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v52" data-v="52">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:52](/en/kjv/luke/8#v52)</div>
<p>8:52 The house was filled with people weeping and wailing: See study note on 7:12. • she’s only asleep: Jesus emphasized the temporary nature of the girl’s state. For believers, death is only temporary “sleep” (cp. John 11:11; the same term is translated as “dead/died” in Acts 7:60; 13:36; 1 Cor 11:30; 15:6, 18, 20, 51; 1 Thes 4:14-15; 5:10).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v55" data-v="55">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:55](/en/kjv/luke/8#v55)</div>
<p>8:55 her life returned: Like the raising of the widow’s son (7:15), the girl’s normal mortal life was restored. At the final resurrection, believers will receive immortal, glorified bodies (see study note on 24:39).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v56" data-v="56">
<div className="cmv-head">[Luke 8:56](/en/kjv/luke/8#v56)</div>
<p>8:56 not tell anyone: See “The Secret of the Messiah” Theme Note.</p>
</div>

</div>
