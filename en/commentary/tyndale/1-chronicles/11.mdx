<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Chronicles 11:1](/en/kjv/1-chronicles/11#v1)</div>
<p>11:1-3 David first became king of Judah at Hebron and ruled there 7½ years (3:4; 2 Sam 5:5). The Chronicler omits the account of that period (see 2 Sam 2–4) and focuses on David’s reign over the entire nation for 33½ years, beginning with his covenant . . . with all the elders of Israel at Hebron (see 2 Sam 5:1-5).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Chronicles 11:4](/en/kjv/1-chronicles/11#v4)</div>
<p>11:4-9 David’s conquest and rebuilding of Jerusalem established it as the capital city of his reign over all Israel.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Chronicles 11:10](/en/kjv/1-chronicles/11#v10)</div>
<p>11:10-47 This account of David’s mighty warriors demonstrates that David had the support of Israel’s best and bravest men, as well as the support of all Israel (cp. 2 Sam 23:8-39).11:10 just as the Lord had promised: David’s kingship was God’s choice, not David’s or the people’s (cp. 11:3; 1 Sam 16:11-14).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v41" data-v="41">
<div className="cmv-head">[1 Chronicles 11:41](/en/kjv/1-chronicles/11#v41)</div>
<p>11:41-47 Zabad . . . Jaasiel: These warriors, not mentioned in the parallel passage at 2 Sam 23, were from locations east of the Jordan River. David had broad support among all the tribes of Israel as well as among foreigners.</p>
</div>
