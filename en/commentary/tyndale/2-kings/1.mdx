<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Kings 1:1](/en/kjv/2-kings/1#v1)</div>
<p>1:1 After Ahab’s death, the Moabites felt secure enough to declare themselves free of their Israelite overlords (see 3:4-5). The historical notice anticipates the troubles that Israel would experience after the short-lived reign of Ahaziah (see 1 Kgs 22:51).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Kings 1:2](/en/kjv/2-kings/1#v2)</div>
<p>1:2 An upper room or balcony enclosed with latticework was common; it allowed for the free flow of air and a degree of privacy, but latticework was easily broken. • Baal-zebub (lord of flies) may be a parody of a Canaanite deity named Baal-zebul (Baal is prince). The name of this deity was later used for Satan (see study note on Matt 10:25). Ahaziah followed his father Ahab in worshiping Baal.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Kings 1:3](/en/kjv/2-kings/1#v3)</div>
<p>1:3-4 The angel of the Lord does not seem to be a christophany in the book of Kings, as in other Old Testament texts (e.g., Judg 6:11-18). • Elijah, who had previously confronted Ahaziah’s father, Ahab, had a twofold message for the king: a condemnation for failure to acknowledge that Yahweh, not Baal, is God (see 1 Kgs 18:16-18), and a pronouncement of doom (see 1 Kgs 21:17-24).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Kings 1:9](/en/kjv/2-kings/1#v9)</div>
<p>1:9-10 The arrogant words of the army captain contained a contradiction, which Elijah pointed out: If Elijah was a man of God (i.e., God’s prophet), then his authority was from God and to ignore him was to invite peril (Deut 18:15-19). • Fire often symbolizes the presence of God in the Scriptures (see Exod 3:1-6; 19:18; cp. 1 Kgs 18:38). • Fifty soldiers was a common grouping (1 Kgs 18:4); here it indicates a division within Israel’s military organization.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Kings 1:11](/en/kjv/2-kings/1#v11)</div>
<p>1:11-13 Report of what happened to the previous contingent had apparently reached the king.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Kings 1:13](/en/kjv/2-kings/1#v13)</div>
<p>1:13-14 In contrast to the first two officers, the third commander pleaded for mercy before Elijah and God.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Kings 1:15](/en/kjv/2-kings/1#v15)</div>
<p>1:15 When the angel of the Lord (1:3) instructed Elijah, he responded in faithful obedience.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Kings 1:16](/en/kjv/2-kings/1#v16)</div>
<p>1:16 Is there no God in Israel? God’s message to Ahaziah remained unchanged (cp. 1:3-4).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Kings 1:17](/en/kjv/2-kings/1#v17)</div>
<p>1:17 The second year of the reign of Jehoram was 852 BC, during Jehoram’s co-regency with his father, Jehoshaphat (853–848 BC).</p>
</div>
