<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[Exodus 8:1](/en/kjv/exodus/8#v1)</div>
<p>8:1-15 The second plague was the plague of frogs (8:2). The Egyptians gave special reverence to amphibians because of their ability to live in two different worlds; Egyptians were deeply concerned with survival in the next world, after death. God showed that frogs have no special hold on life. This plague is sometimes said to have been a natural result of whatever happened to make the Nile River uninhabitable. However, the extent of the plague was more than a natural result.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Exodus 8:7](/en/kjv/exodus/8#v7)</div>
<p>8:7-8 The magicians were able to duplicate the plague in some sense, but Pharaoh did not ask them to rid the land of the frogs. Instead, he begged Moses to ask God to take them away. He already knew where the real power was.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Exodus 8:9](/en/kjv/exodus/8#v9)</div>
<p>8:9 In this instance, the evidence of God’s power was not in the event per se but in the timing. God could keep the frogs alive or he could end their lives. Pharaoh needed only to say when.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Exodus 8:10](/en/kjv/exodus/8#v10)</div>
<p>8:10 you will know that there is no one like the Lord our God: Once more the issue is highlighted (see 7:17). These events were not primarily about rescue, but about the nature of reality. Who was rescuing these people—one of the gods, or the one true God, the Lord?</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Exodus 8:13](/en/kjv/exodus/8#v13)</div>
<p>8:13 had predicted: Moses predicted that the plague would stop the next day; God predicted that Pharaoh would refuse to listen (see 8:15). The element of prediction is central to God’s lordship. God sees and controls the future; he is the Lord.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Exodus 8:16](/en/kjv/exodus/8#v16)</div>
<p>8:16-19 The third plague was the plague of gnats. The word translated “gnats” is very general. Technical Old Testament dictionaries often translate it as “vermin.” The English term “bugs” would come close. The whole land was infested with insects of one sort or another.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Exodus 8:19](/en/kjv/exodus/8#v19)</div>
<p>8:19 This is the finger of God! See study note on 7:11.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[Exodus 8:20](/en/kjv/exodus/8#v20)</div>
<p>8:20-32 The fourth plague was the plague of flies. The Egyptians worshiped insects such as flies that seemed to be able to turn death into life, as their young seemed to emerge from decaying matter. If the Egyptians thought that insects had the secret of life, God would let them have all the insects they wanted. Some say that this infestation was the natural result of all the dead frogs, but there were no flies in Goshen (8:22), the northeastern delta area where the Hebrews lived. God’s miraculous power was clearly seen in his ability to infest one area while sparing another at will.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v25" data-v="25">
<div className="cmv-head">[Exodus 8:25](/en/kjv/exodus/8#v25)</div>
<p>8:25 Pharaoh attempted to bargain with God. He wanted to obey partially while still retaining control. James says of such people, “Their loyalty is divided between God and the world” (Jas 1:8), and such a person cannot receive anything from the Lord.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v26" data-v="26">
<div className="cmv-head">[Exodus 8:26](/en/kjv/exodus/8#v26)</div>
<p>8:26-27 Moses pointed out the impossibility of what Pharaoh was asking on the grounds of the Egyptians’ own prejudice: They considered all Semites to be uncultured and uncouth (see Gen 43:32). • The Hebrews were leaving Egypt in order to worship God (see, e.g., Exod 5:1; 7:16; 8:1, 20). God’s purpose for the Exodus was to lead his people into a proper relationship with him.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v29" data-v="29">
<div className="cmv-head">[Exodus 8:29](/en/kjv/exodus/8#v29)</div>
<p>8:29-30 I will pray: Moses was learning the role of intercessor that would be so much a part of his life in future years (see, e.g., 32:11-13, 30-32; Num 14:13-19). It must have been increasingly tempting to let the Egyptians continue to experience the results of their king’s stubbornness, but Moses was willing to plead with God not to leave them in a permanent state of grief and distress.</p>
</div>
