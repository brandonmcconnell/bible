<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:1](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v1)</div>
<p>3:1-39 The house of David increased, while Saul’s house dwindled. The crucial occasion came when Abner, the real political power in Saul’s camp, switched allegiance to David, taking a considerable number of his northern kinsmen with him.3:1 a long war: This civil war likely continued for most of the seven-plus years that David ruled from Hebron. The truce called by Joab and Abner (2:26-28) was short-lived.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:2](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v2)</div>
<p>3:2-5 One way David “became stronger and stronger” (3:1) was by taking more wives and having a son through each. Three of David’s Hebron-born sons died violent deaths, two during David’s reign (Amnon, Absalom), and one shortly after his death (Adonijah).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:3](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v3)</div>
<p>3:3 Geshur was a small Canaanite kingdom in upper Transjordan (the area east of the Jordan) near the Sea of Galilee. Marrying into neighboring royal families created political alliances and secured David’s position against the northern tribes. Solomon also followed this practice (1 Kgs 3:1; 11:1).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:7](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v7)</div>
<p>3:7 accused Abner of sleeping with one of &#91;Saul’s&#93; concubines: It is unclear whether Abner actually did so. Taking a king’s wife or concubine was often part of a usurper’s attempt to replace the king (see 12:8; 16:21; 1 Kgs 2:17-25). • Rizpah: See also 2 Sam 21:8-14.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:8](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v8)</div>
<p>3:8 some Judean dog: Cp. 1 Sam 17:43. • by not handing you over to David: Abner had control over the life and death of Saul’s son.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:9](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v9)</div>
<p>3:9 help David get what the Lord has promised him! Abner was aware that God had chosen David to be king over all Israel (see also 3:18).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:10](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v10)</div>
<p>3:10 and give it to David: Cp. 1 Sam 28:17. • I will establish the throne of David: Whether an ally or enemy, Abner was a powerful man. Here he speaks like God (see 2 Sam 7:13).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:11](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v11)</div>
<p>3:11 didn’t dare say another word: Ishbosheth’s silence speaks loudly of his weakness as a ruler. Abner, who had installed him as king (2:8-9), was the real power behind Ishbosheth’s reign.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:12](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v12)</div>
<p>3:12 In the proposed solemn pact (or covenant), Abner recognized that David would be king, while perhaps he hoped to be second-in-command.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:13](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v13)</div>
<p>3:13 Saul had given his daughter Michal to David as his wife (1 Sam 18:20-27). However, when Michal saved David’s life from her father (1 Sam 19:11-17), Saul terminated the marriage and gave Michal to another man, Palti (1 Sam 25:44). For David to get her back now would further cement his claim to Saul’s kingdom (cp. 1 Kgs 2:13-25).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:14](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v14)</div>
<p>3:14 the lives of 100 Philistines: Saul had hoped that David would be killed in his attempt to secure the bride-price (see 1 Sam 18:17-27).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:15](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v15)</div>
<p>3:15 Ishbosheth took: Although Abner made the deal with David (3:12-13), it was Ishbosheth who gave Michal to David. This transaction illustrates Ishbosheth’s weakness as a king and his fear of Abner (3:11).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:16](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v16)</div>
<p>3:16 In spite of Palti’s anguished weeping, he was powerless to stop what was happening (cp. Judg 18:1-26). • Bahurim was probably a center of support for Saul’s clan just east of Jerusalem (cp. 2 Sam 16:5).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:17](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v17)</div>
<p>3:17 Apparently, there had been growing momentum among the elders of Israel in the north to accept David as king, though the text has made no mention of it to this point.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:18](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v18)</div>
<p>3:18 See also 3:9. By bringing relief from the Philistines, David would accomplish what Saul had failed to do (1 Sam 9:16).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:19](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v19)</div>
<p>3:19 Because Saul was from their tribe, the men of Benjamin no doubt viewed David, from Judah, as a usurper of Saul’s throne. However, Abner was well-respected in Saul’s regime and so was able to elicit support for David even among Saul’s own tribe.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v21" data-v="21">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:21](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v21)</div>
<p>3:21 The narrator emphasized that David sent Abner safely on his way as a friend and an ally (restated in 3:22-23). David was at peace with Abner and was not involved in the renowned military leader’s murder (cp. 3:28-29). • Despite the murder of Abner (3:27), the northern tribes eventually did make a covenant with David to make him their king, just as Abner had promised (5:1-3).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v26" data-v="26">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:26](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v26)</div>
<p>3:26 The precise location of the well of Sirah is unknown; presumably it was not far from Hebron. • David knew nothing about it: See study note on 3:21.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v27" data-v="27">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:27](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v27)</div>
<p>3:27 as if to speak with him privately: Joab accused Abner of deception (3:25), but he used deception himself to lure Abner to his death. • in revenge: Joab was motivated by the practice of blood vengeance, avenging the death of a kinsman by killing the killer.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v28" data-v="28">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:28](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v28)</div>
<p>3:28 David made it clear that he had nothing to do with Abner’s death, labeling it as a crime. David knew that because his second-in-command had killed Abner, rumors would circulate among those loyal to Saul’s dynasty that David had ordered the killing. • I vow by the Lord: David called on the Lord to hold him accountable if he were lying.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v29" data-v="29">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:29](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v29)</div>
<p>3:29 leprosy: While leprosy is a possible translation, the Hebrew here probably refers to a broader range of skin inflammations, not only Hansen’s disease. • who walks on crutches: This curse effectively alienated Joab from David, and from this point on they had only a professional relationship. David’s deathbed order to Solomon to get rid of Joab (1 Kgs 2:5-6) and Solomon’s exoneration of David in Abner’s death (1 Kgs 2:31-33) indicate that David’s mourning over Abner was genuine.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v30" data-v="30">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:30](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v30)</div>
<p>3:30 See 2:18-28.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v31" data-v="31">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:31](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v31)</div>
<p>3:31 David himself walked: David’s public presence in the mourning procession, his open weeping at the gravesite (3:32), and his dramatic fast (3:35) made evident to the public that he had not ordered Abner’s killing (3:37).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v32" data-v="32">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:32](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v32)</div>
<p>3:32 Burying Abner in Hebron, David’s capital city at the time, rather than at some northern site reinforced Abner’s shift of support from Saul to David. Presumably it encouraged others from the northern tribes to follow Abner’s example.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v33" data-v="33">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:33](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v33)</div>
<p>3:33 as fools die: Abner was deceived by Joab, an enemy who posed as a friend (see 3:27).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v34" data-v="34">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:34](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v34)</div>
<p>3:34 a wicked plot: Normal warfare could not defeat Abner, a seasoned warrior. Only treachery could.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v35" data-v="35">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:35](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v35)</div>
<p>3:35 begged him to eat: See also 12:17.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v37" data-v="37">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:37](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v37)</div>
<p>3:37 See study note on 3:31.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v39" data-v="39">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 3:39](/en/kjv/2-samuel/3#v39)</div>
<p>3:39 too strong for me to control: Just as Ishbosheth could not control Abner, David could not control Joab. However, David had faith that the Lord would repay Joab for murdering Abner.</p>
</div>
