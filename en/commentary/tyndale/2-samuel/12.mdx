<div className="cmv" id="v1" data-v="1">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 12:1](/en/kjv/2-samuel/12#v1)</div>
<p>12:1-31 Chapter 12 expands on the last phrase of 11:27.12:1 The Lord sent Nathan to David at least nine months after his adulterous sin. • This story is a rare Old Testament instance of a parable (see also Judg 9:8-15). Such stories can be effective for communicating truth. • David was rich in the royal treasures he possessed, the number of wives he had, and all the promises of God for his future. Uriah by contrast was poor: he had one wife, one home, and no lineage.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 12:3](/en/kjv/2-samuel/12#v3)</div>
<p>12:3 like a baby daughter: Nathan’s comparison of this lamb to a daughter (Hebrew bath) strikes a parallel with Bathsheba’s name (see study note on 11:3).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 12:4](/en/kjv/2-samuel/12#v4)</div>
<p>12:4 he took the poor man’s lamb: Samuel had previously warned that a king would take what was not his (1 Sam 8:11-17).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 12:5](/en/kjv/2-samuel/12#v5)</div>
<p>12:5-6 The rich man of the parable did not deserve to die according to the law; instead, he must repay four lambs (cp. Exod 22:1). Intriguingly, David would later lose four of his sons (Bathsheba’s first child, 2 Sam 12:18; Amnon, 13:29; Absalom, 18:14-15; Adonijah, 1 Kgs 2:25).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 12:7](/en/kjv/2-samuel/12#v7)</div>
<p>12:7-8 You are that man! David did deserve to die for his crime (Lev 20:10). • I anointed . . . I gave . . . I would have given you: David’s sin not only violated God’s commandments against murder, adultery, and coveting (Exod 20:1-17) but also amounted to a brazen disregard for all that the Lord had graciously given him.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 12:8](/en/kjv/2-samuel/12#v8)</div>
<p>12:8 His wives were probably the concubines of Saul’s harem (cp. 3:7). The phrase could refer to Saul’s wife Ahinoam, although David probably married a different woman with the same name (cp. 1 Sam 14:50; 25:43).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 12:10](/en/kjv/2-samuel/12#v10)</div>
<p>12:10 from this time on (literally forever): Contrast God’s gracious “forever” promises of 7:13-29. • The reminder of what David had done with the sword (12:9) and what role the sword would play in his family recalls David’s cavalier response to Joab’s report of the deaths of Uriah and other innocent Israelites (11:25).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 12:11](/en/kjv/2-samuel/12#v11)</div>
<p>12:11 I will cause your own household to rebel against you: Absalom’s revolt against David (chs 14–19) fulfilled this promise.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 12:14](/en/kjv/2-samuel/12#v14)</div>
<p>12:14 Nevertheless . . . your child will die: At times, God transfers punishment of fathers to their descendants (Exod 20:5; 34:7; Num 14:18; Deut 5:9; Jer 32:18). Although David repented and was forgiven (2 Sam 12:13), it did not cancel all retribution; it delayed it until a later generation (cp. 1 Kgs 21:27-29).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 12:20](/en/kjv/2-samuel/12#v20)</div>
<p>12:20 The series of actions described here show David resuming normal life activities. That he did so this soon after his son’s death amazed his advisers (12:21).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v21" data-v="21">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 12:21](/en/kjv/2-samuel/12#v21)</div>
<p>12:21-23 David grieved before his son’s death, hoping to ward off punishment.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v23" data-v="23">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 12:23](/en/kjv/2-samuel/12#v23)</div>
<p>12:23 Can I bring him back again? I will go to him one day: The irreversibility of his son’s death forced David to face his own mortality. But he also showed his confidence in the afterlife.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 12:24](/en/kjv/2-samuel/12#v24)</div>
<p>12:24 Even after Uriah’s death, Bathsheba was still called Uriah’s wife (12:9; see also Matt 1:6). Only here is she called David’s wife. • Solomon: Pronounced Shelomoh in Hebrew, it probably means “his peace,” from the Hebrew shalom. It might mean “his replacement”; cp. Shelemiah (Jer 36:14, “Yahweh has provided compensation”) and Shelumiel (Num 1:6, “God &#91;is&#93; my compensation”); both contain the root shelem (“replacement, compensation”).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v25" data-v="25">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 12:25](/en/kjv/2-samuel/12#v25)</div>
<p>12:25 Jedidiah means “loved by Yahweh.” This God-given second name for Solomon, mentioned only here, guaranteed his future, as it expressed God’s special love for him.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v26" data-v="26">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 12:26](/en/kjv/2-samuel/12#v26)</div>
<p>12:26-31 David’s battle with the Ammonites, begun in ch 10, ended in success.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v30" data-v="30">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 12:30](/en/kjv/2-samuel/12#v30)</div>
<p>12:30 David removed the crown: David had, in effect, become the Ammonites’ king. • a vast amount of plunder: Instructions against taking such booty during a conquest (Deut 7:25-26) show how dangerous David’s actions were. Such wealth might seduce the king’s heart away from God.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v31" data-v="31">
<div className="cmv-head">[2 Samuel 12:31](/en/kjv/2-samuel/12#v31)</div>
<p>12:31 He also made slaves of the people of Rabbah and forced them to labor with: Enslaving defeated peoples was in accord with Deut 20:11. Solomon later did the same with the Canaanites (1 Kgs 9:20-22; see also Judg 1:30, 33). The alternate reading might indicate torture inflicted on the defeated Ammonites (cp. textual note on 1 Chr 20:3).</p>
</div>

</div>
