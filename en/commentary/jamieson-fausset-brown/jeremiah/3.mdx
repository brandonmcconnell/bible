## Introduction

<div className="cm-intro">
<p>GOD'S MERCY NOTWITHSTANDING JUDAH'S VILENESS. (Jer. 3:1-25)</p>
<p>They say--rather, as Hebrew, "saying," in agreement with "the LORD"; Jer 2:37 of last chapter &#91;MAURER&#93;. Or, it is equivalent to, "Suppose this case." Some copyist may have omitted, "The word of the Lord came to me," saying.</p>
<p>shall he return unto her--will he take her back? It was unlawful to do so (Deu 24:1-4).</p>
<p>shall not--Should not the land be polluted if this were done?</p>
<p>yet return-- (Jer 3:22; Jer 4:1; Zac 1:3; compare Eze 16:51, Eze 16:58, Eze 16:60). "Nevertheless," &amp;c. (see on Isa 50:1).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:2](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v2)</div>
<p>high places--the scene of idolatries which were spiritual adulteries.</p>
<p>In . . . ways . . . sat for them--watching for lovers like a prostitute (Gen 38:14, Gen 38:21; Pro 7:12; Pro 23:28; Eze 16:24-25), and like an Arab who lies in wait for travellers. The Arabs of the desert, east and south of Palestine, are still notorious as robbers.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:3](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v3)</div>
<p>no latter rain--essential to the crops in Palestine; withheld in judgment (Lev 26:19; compare Joe 2:23).</p>
<p>whore's forehead-- (Jer 8:12; Eze 3:8).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:4](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v4)</div>
<p>from this time--not referring, as MICHAELIS thinks, to the reformation begun the year before, that is, the twelfth of Josiah; it means--now at once, now at last.</p>
<p>me--contrasted with the "stock" whom they had heretofore called on as "father" (Jer 2:27; Luk 15:18).</p>
<p>thou art--rather, "thou wast."</p>
<p>guide of . . . youth--that is, husband (Jer 2:2; Pro 2:17; Hos 2:7, Hos 2:15). Husband and father are the two most endearing of ties.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:5](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v5)</div>
<p>he--"thou," the second person, had preceded. The change to the third person implies a putting away of God to a greater distance from them; instead of repenting and forsaking their idols, they merely deprecate the continuance of their punishment. Jer 3:12 and Psa 103:9, answer their question in the event of their penitence.</p>
<p>spoken and--rather (God's reply to them), "Thou hast spoken (thus), and yet (all the while) thou hast done evil," &amp;c.</p>
<p>as thou couldest--with all thy might; with incorrigible persistency &#91;CALVIN&#93;.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:6](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v6)</div>
<p>From here to Jer 6:30, is a new discourse, delivered in Josiah's reign. It consists of two parts, the former extending to Jer 4:3, in which he warns Judah from the example of Israel's doom, and yet promises Israel final restoration; the latter a threat of Babylonian invasion; as Nabopolassar founded the Babylonian empire, 625 B.C., the seventeenth of Josiah, this prophecy is perhaps not earlier than that date (Jer 4:5, &amp;c.; Jer 5:14, &amp;c.; Jer 6:1, &amp;c.; Jer. 22:1-30); and probably not later than the second thorough reformation in the eighteenth year of the same reign.</p>
<p>backsliding--literally, "apostasy"; not merely apostate, but apostasy itself, the essence of it (Jer 3:14, Jer 3:22).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:7](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v7)</div>
<p>I said-- (Kg2 17:13).</p>
<p>sister-- (Eze 16:46; Eze 23:2, Eze 23:4).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:8](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v8)</div>
<p>I saw that, though (whereas) it was for this very reason (namely), because backsliding (apostate) Israel had committed adultery I had put her away (Kg2 17:6, Kg2 17:18), and given her a bill of divorce, yet Judah, &amp;c. (Eze 23:11, &amp;c.).</p>
<p>bill of divorce--literally, "a writing of cuttings off." The plural implies the completeness of the severance. The use of this metaphor here, as in the former discourse (Jer 3:1), implies a close connection between the discourses. The epithets are characteristic; Israel "apostate" (as the Hebrew for "backsliding" is better rendered); Judah, not as yet utterly apostate, but treacherous or faithless.</p>
<p>also--herself also, like Israel.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:9](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v9)</div>
<p>it--Some take this verse of Judah, to whom the end of Jer 3:8 refers. But Jer 3:10 puts Judah in contrast to Israel in this verse. "Yet for all this," referring to the sad example of Israel; if Jer 3:9 referred to Judah, "she" would have been written in Jer 3:10, not "Judah." Translate, "It (the putting away of Israel) had come to pass through . . . whoredom; and (that is, for) she (Israel) had defiled the land" &amp;c. &#91;MAURER&#93;. English Version, however, may be explained to refer to Israel.</p>
<p>lightness--"infamy." &#91;EWALD&#93;. MAURER not so well takes it from the Hebrew root, "voice," "fame."</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:10](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v10)</div>
<p>yet--notwithstanding the lesson given in Israel's case of the fatal results of apostasy.</p>
<p>not . . . whole heart--The reformation in the eighteenth year of Josiah was not thorough on the part of the people, for at his death they relapsed into idolatry (Ch2 34:33; Hos 7:14).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:11](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v11)</div>
<p>justified herself--has been made to appear almost just (that is, comparatively innocent) by the surpassing guilt of Judah, who adds hypocrisy and treachery to her sin; and who had the example of Israel to warn her, but in vain (compare Eze 16:51; Eze 23:11).</p>
<p>more than--in comparison with.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:12](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v12)</div>
<p>Go--not actually; but turn and proclaim towards the north (Media and Assyria, where the ten tribes were located by Tiglath-pileser and Shalmaneser, Kg2 15:29; Kg2 17:6; Kg2 18:9, Kg2 18:11).</p>
<p>Return . . . backsliding--Hebrew, Shubah, Meshubah, a play on sounds. In order to excite Judah to godly jealousy (Rom 11:14), Jehovah addresses the exiled ten tribes of Israel with a loving invitation.</p>
<p>cause . . . anger to fall--literally, "I will not let fall My countenance" (compare Gen 4:5-6; Job 29:3), that is, I will not continue to frown on you.</p>
<p>keep--"anger" is to be supplied (see on Jer 3:5).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:13](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v13)</div>
<p>Only acknowledge-- (Deu 30:1, Deu 30:3; Pro 28:13).</p>
<p>scattered thy ways, &amp;c.-- (Jer 2:25). Not merely the calves at Beth-el, but the idols in every direction, were the objects of their worship (Eze 16:15, Eze 16:24-25).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:14](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v14)</div>
<p>I am married--literally, "I am Lord," that is, husband to you (so Jer 31:32; compare Hos 2:19-20; Isa 54:5). GESENIUS, following the Septuagint version of Jer 31:32, and Paul's quotation of it (Heb 8:9), translates, "I have rejected you"; so the corresponding Arabic, and the idea of lordship, may pass into that of looking down upon, and so rejecting. But the Septuagint in this passage translates, "I will be Lord over you." And the "for" has much more force in English Version than in that of GESENIUS. The Hebrew hardly admits the rendering though &#91;HENGSTENBERG&#93;.</p>
<p>take you one of a city--Though but one or two Israelites were in a (foreign) city, they shall not be forgotten; all shall be restored (Amo 9:9). So, in the spiritual Israel, God gathers one convert here, another there, into His Church; not the least one is lost (Mat 18:14; Rom 11:5; compare Jer 24:5-7).</p>
<p>family--a clan or tribe.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:15](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v15)</div>
<p>pastors--not religious, but civil rulers, as Zerubbabel, Nehemiah (Jer 23:4; Jer 2:8).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:16](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v16)</div>
<p>they shall say no more--The Jews shall no longer glory in the possession of the ark; it shall not be missed, so great shall be the blessings of the new dispensation. The throne of the Lord, present Himself, shall eclipse and put out of mind the ark of the covenant and the mercy seat between the cherubim, God's former throne. The ark, containing the two tables of the law, disappeared at the Babylonian captivity, and was not restored to the second temple, implying that the symbolical "glory" was to be superseded by a "greater glory" (Hag 2:9).</p>
<p>neither . . . visit it--rather, "neither shall it be missed" (so in Jer 23:4).</p>
<p>done--rather, "neither shall it (the ark) be made (that is, be restored) any more" &#91;MAURER&#93;.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:17](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v17)</div>
<p>Jerusalem--the whole city, not merely the temple. As it has been the center of the Hebrew theocracy, so it shall be the point of attraction to the whole earth (Isa 2:2-4; Zac 2:10-11; Zac 14:16-21).</p>
<p>throne of . . . Lord--The Shekinah, the symbol of God's peculiar nearness to Israel (Deu 4:7) shall be surpassed by the antitype, God's own throne in Jerusalem (Psa 2:6, Psa 2:8; Eze 34:23-24; Zac 2:5).</p>
<p>imagination--rather, as Margin, "the obstinacy" or stubbornness.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:18](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v18)</div>
<p>Judah . . . Israel . . . together--Two distinct apostasies, that of Israel and that of Judah, were foretold (Jer 3:8, Jer 3:10). The two have never been united since the Babylonish captivity; therefore their joint restoration must be still future (Isa 11:12-13; Eze 37:16-22; Hos 1:11).</p>
<p>north-- (Jer 3:12).</p>
<p>land . . . given . . . inheritance-- (Amo 9:15).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:19](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v19)</div>
<p>The good land covenanted to Abraham is to be restored to his seed. But the question arises, How shall this be done?</p>
<p>put . . . among . . . children--the Greek for adoption means, literally, "putting among the sons."</p>
<p>the children--that is, My children. "How shall I receive thee back into My family, after thou hast so long forsaken Me for idols?" The answer is, they would acknowledge Him as "Father," and no longer turn away from Him. God assumes the language of one wondering how so desperate apostates could be restored to His family and its privileges (compare Eze 37:3; CALVIN makes it, How the race of Abraham can be propagated again, being as it were dead); yet as His purpose has decreed it so, He shows how it shall be effected, namely, they shall receive from Him the spirit of adoption to cry, "My Father" (Joh 1:12; Gal 4:6). The elect are "children" already in God's purpose; this is the ground of the subsequent realization of this relationship (Eph 1:5; Heb 2:13).</p>
<p>pleasant land-- (Jer 11:5; Eze 20:6; Dan 11:16, Margin).</p>
<p>heritage of . . . hosts--a heritage the most goodly of all nations &#91;MAURER&#93;; or a "heritage possessed by powerful hosts" (Deu 4:38; Amo 2:9). The rendering "splendors," instead of "hosts," is opposed by the fact that the Hebrew for "splendor" is not found in the plural.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:20](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v20)</div>
<p>Surely--rather, "But."</p>
<p>husband--literally, "friend."</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v21" data-v="21">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:21](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v21)</div>
<p>In harmony with the preceding promises of God, the penitential confessions of Israel are heard.</p>
<p>high places--The scene of their idolatries is the scene of their confessions. Compare Jer 3:23, in which they cast aside their trust in these idolatrous high places. The publicity of their penitence is also implied (compare Jer 7:29; Jer 48:38).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v22" data-v="22">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:22](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v22)</div>
<p>Jehovah's renewed invitation (Jer 3:12, Jer 3:14) and their immediate response.</p>
<p>heal--forgive (Ch2 30:18, Ch2 30:20; Hos 14:4).</p>
<p>unto thee--rather, "in obedience to thee"; literally, "for thee" &#91;ROSENMULLER&#93;.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v23" data-v="23">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:23](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v23)</div>
<p>multitude of mountains--that is, the multitude of gods worshipped on them (compare Psa 121:1-2, Margin).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v24" data-v="24">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:24](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v24)</div>
<p>shame--that is, the idols, whose worship only covers us with shame (Jer 11:13; Hos 9:10). So far from bringing us "salvation," they have cost us our cattle and even our children, whom we have sacrificed to them.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v25" data-v="25">
<div className="cmv-head">[Jeremiah 3:25](/en/kjv/jeremiah/3#v25)</div>
<p>(Ezr 9:7).</p>
