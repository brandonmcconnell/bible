## Introduction

<div className="cm-intro">
<p>In a time of great national distress, probably in David's reign, the Psalmist recounts God's gracious dealings in former times, and the confidence they had learned to repose in Him. After a vivid picture of their calamities, he humbly expostulates against God's apparent forgetfulness, reminding Him of their faithfulness and mourning their heavy sorrows. (Psa. 44:1-26)</p>
<p>This period is that of the settlement of Canaan (Jos 24:12; Jdg 6:3).</p>
<p>have told--or, "related" (compare Exo 10:2).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 44:2](/en/kjv/psalms/44#v2)</div>
<p>plantedst them--that is, "our fathers," who are also, from the parallel construction of the last clause, to be regarded as the object of "cast them out," which means--literally, "send" them out, or, "extend them." Heathen and people denote the nations who were driven out to make room for the Israelites.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 44:4](/en/kjv/psalms/44#v4)</div>
<p>Thou art my King--literally, "he who is my King," sustaining the same covenant relation as to the "fathers."</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 44:5](/en/kjv/psalms/44#v5)</div>
<p>The figure drawn from the habits of the ox.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 44:6](/en/kjv/psalms/44#v6)</div>
<p>God is not only our sole help, but only worthy of praise.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 44:7](/en/kjv/psalms/44#v7)</div>
<p>put . . . to shame--(compare Psa 6:10), disgraced.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 44:8](/en/kjv/psalms/44#v8)</div>
<p>thy name--as in Psa 5:11.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 44:9](/en/kjv/psalms/44#v9)</div>
<p>But--contrasting, cast off as abhorrent (Psa 43:2).</p>
<p>goest not forth--literally, "will not go" (Sa2 5:23). In several consecutive verses the leading verb is future, and the following one past (in Hebrew), thus denoting the causes and effects. Thus (Psa 44:10-12), when defeated, spoiling follows; when delivered as sheep, dispersion follows, &amp;c.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 44:11](/en/kjv/psalms/44#v11)</div>
<p>The Babylonian captivity not necessarily meant. There were others (compare Kg1 8:46).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 44:13](/en/kjv/psalms/44#v13)</div>
<p>(Compare Deu 28:37; Psa 79:4).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 44:15](/en/kjv/psalms/44#v15)</div>
<p>shame of . . . face--blushes in disgrace.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 44:16](/en/kjv/psalms/44#v16)</div>
<p>Its cause, the taunts and presence of malignant enemies (Psa 8:2).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 44:17](/en/kjv/psalms/44#v17)</div>
<p>They had not apostatized totally--were still God's people.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 44:18](/en/kjv/psalms/44#v18)</div>
<p>declined--turned aside from God's law.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 44:19](/en/kjv/psalms/44#v19)</div>
<p>sore broken--crushed.</p>
<p>place of dragons--desolate, barren, rocky wilderness (Psa 63:10; Isa 13:22),</p>
<p>shadow of death--(Compare Psa 23:4).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 44:20](/en/kjv/psalms/44#v20)</div>
<p>A solemn appeal to God to witness their constancy.</p>
<p>stretched out . . . hands--gesture of worship (Exo 9:29; Psa 88:9).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v22" data-v="22">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 44:22](/en/kjv/psalms/44#v22)</div>
<p>Their protracted sufferings as God's people attests the constancy. Paul (Rom 8:36) uses this to describe Christian steadfastness in persecution.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v23" data-v="23">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 44:23](/en/kjv/psalms/44#v23)</div>
<p>This style of addressing God, as indifferent, is frequent (Psa 3:7; Psa 9:19; Psa 13:1, &amp;c.). However low their condition, God is appealed to, on the ground, and for the honor, of His mercy.</p>
