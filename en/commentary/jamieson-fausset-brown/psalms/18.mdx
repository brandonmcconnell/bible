## Introduction

<div className="cm-intro">
<p>"The servant of the LORD," which in the Hebrew precedes "David," is a significant part of the title (and not a mere epithet of David), denoting the inspired character of the song, as the production of one entrusted with the execution of God's will. He was not favored by God because he served Him, but served Him because selected and appointed by God in His sovereign mercy. After a general expression of praise and confidence in God for the future, David gives a sublimely poetical description of God's deliverance, which he characterizes as an illustration of God's justice to the innocent and His righteous government. His own prowess and success are celebrated as the results of divine aid, and, confident of its continuance, he closes in terms of triumphant praise. 2Sa. 22:1-51 is a copy of this Psalm, with a few unimportant variations recorded there as a part of the history, and repeated here as part of a collection designed for permanent use. (Psa. 18:1-50)</p>
<p>I will love thee--with most tender affection.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:2](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v2)</div>
<p>The various terms used describe God as an object of the most implicit and reliable trust.</p>
<p>rock--literally, "a cleft rock," for concealment.</p>
<p>strength--a firm, immovable rock.</p>
<p>horn of my salvation--The horn, as the means of attack or defense of some of the strongest animals, is a frequent emblem of power or strength efficiently exercised (compare Deu 33:17; Luk 1:69).</p>
<p>tower--literally, "high place," beyond reach of danger.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:3](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v3)</div>
<p>to be praised--for past favors, and worthy of confidence.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:4](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v4)</div>
<p>sorrows--literally, "bands as of a net" (Psa 116:3).</p>
<p>floods--denotes "multitude."</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:5](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v5)</div>
<p>death--and hell (compare Psa 16:10) are personified as man's great enemies (compare Rev 20:13-14).</p>
<p>prevented--encountered me, crossed my path, and endangered my safety. He does not mean he was in their power.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:6](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v6)</div>
<p>He relates his methods to procure relief when distressed, and his success.</p>
<p>temple--(Compare Psa 11:4).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:7](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v7)</div>
<p>God's coming described in figures drawn from His appearance on Sinai (compare Deu 32:22).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:8](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v8)</div>
<p>smoke out . . . his nostrils--bitter in His wrath (compare Psa 74:1).</p>
<p>by it--that is, the fire (Exo 19:18).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:9](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v9)</div>
<p>darkness--or, a dense cloud (Exo 19:16; Deu 5:22).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:10](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v10)</div>
<p>cherub--angelic agents (compare Gen 3:24), the figures of which were placed over the ark (Sa1 4:4), representing God's dwelling; used here to enhance the majesty of the divine advent. Angels and winds may represent all rational and irrational agencies of God's providence (compare Psa 104:3-4).</p>
<p>did fly--Rapidity of motion adds to the grandeur of the scene.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:11](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v11)</div>
<p>dark waters--or, clouds heavy with vapor.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:12](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v12)</div>
<p>Out of this obscurity, which impresses the beholder with awe and dread, He reveals Himself by sudden light and the means of His terrible wrath (Jos 10:11; Psa 78:47).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:13](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v13)</div>
<p>The storm breaks forth--thunder follows lightning, and hail with repeated lightning, as often seen, like balls or coals of fire, succeed (Exo 9:23).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:14](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v14)</div>
<p>The fiery brightness of lightning, in shape like burning arrows rapidly shot through the air, well represents the most terrible part of an awful storm. Before the terrors of such a scene the enemies are confounded and overthrown in dismay.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:15](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v15)</div>
<p>The tempest of the air is attended by appropriate results on earth. The language, though not expressive of any special physical changes, represents the utter subversion of the order of nature. Before such a God none can stand.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:16](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v16)</div>
<p>from above--As seated on a throne, directing these terrible scenes, God--</p>
<p>sent--His hand (Psa 144:7), reached down to His humble worshipper, and delivered him.</p>
<p>many waters--calamities (Job 30:14; Psa 124:4-5).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:18](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v18)</div>
<p>prevented-- (Psa 18:3).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:19](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v19)</div>
<p>a large place--denotes safety or relief, as contrasted with the straits of distress (Psa 4:1). All his deliverance is ascribed to God, and this sublime poetical representation is given to inspire the pious with confidence and the wicked with dread.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:20](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v20)</div>
<p>The statements of innocence, righteousness, &amp;c., refer, doubtless, to his personal and official conduct and his purposes, during all the trials to which he was subjected in Saul's persecutions and Absalom's rebellions, as well as the various wars in which he had been engaged as the head and defender of God's Church and people.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v23" data-v="23">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:23](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v23)</div>
<p>upright before him--In my relation to God I have been perfect as to all parts of His law. The perfection does not relate to degree.</p>
<p>mine iniquity--perhaps the thought of his heart to kill Saul (Sa1 24:6). That David does not allude to all his conduct, in all relations, is evident from Psa 51:1, &amp;c.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v25" data-v="25">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:25](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v25)</div>
<p>God renders to men according to their deeds in a penal, not vindictive, sense (Lev 26:23-24).</p>
<p>merciful--or, "kind" (Psa 4:3).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v26" data-v="26">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:26](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v26)</div>
<p>froward--contrary to.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v27" data-v="27">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:27](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v27)</div>
<p>the afflicted people--that is, the humbly pious.</p>
<p>high looks--pride (Psa 101:5; Psa 131:1).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v28" data-v="28">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:28](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v28)</div>
<p>To give one light is to make prosperous (Job 18:5-6; Job 21:17).</p>
<p>thou--is emphatic, as if to say, I can fully confide in Thee for help.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v29" data-v="29">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:29](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v29)</div>
<p>And this on past experience in his military life, set forth by these figures.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v30" data-v="30">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:30](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v30)</div>
<p>God's perfection is the source of his own, which has resulted from his trust on the one hand, and God's promised help on the other.</p>
<p>tried--"as metals are tried by fire and proved genuine" (Psa 12:6). Shield (Psa 3:3). Girding was essential to free motion on account of the looseness of Oriental dresses; hence it is an expressive figure for describing the gift of strength.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v33" data-v="33">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:33](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v33)</div>
<p>God's help farther described. He gives swiftness to pursue or elude his enemies (Hab 3:19), strength, protection, and a firm footing.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v35" data-v="35">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:35](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v35)</div>
<p>thy gentleness--as applied to God--condescension--or that which He gives, in the sense of humility (compare Pro 22:4).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v36" data-v="36">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:36](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v36)</div>
<p>enlarged my steps--made ample room (compare Pro 4:12).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v37" data-v="37">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:37](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v37)</div>
<p>In actual conflict, with God's aid, the defeat of his enemies is certain. A present and continued success is expressed.</p>

<div className="cmv" id="v39" data-v="39">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:39](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v39)</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v40" data-v="40">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:40](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v40)</div>
<p>given me the necks--literally, "backs of the necks"; made them retreat (Exo 23:27; Jos 7:8).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v42" data-v="42">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:42](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v42)</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v43" data-v="43">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:43](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v43)</div>
<p>Not only does He conquer civil foes, but foreigners, who are driven from their places of refuge.</p>

<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:44](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v44)</div>

<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:46](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v46)</div>

<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:47](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v47)</div>

<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:48](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v48)</div>
<p>liftest me up--to safety and honors.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v49" data-v="49">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 18:49](/en/kjv/psalms/18#v49)</div>
<p>Paul (Rom 15:9) quotes from this doxology to show that under the Old Testament economy, others than the Jews were regarded as subjects of that spiritual government of which David was head, and in which character his deliverances and victories were typical of the more illustrious triumphs of David's greater Son. The language of Psa 18:50 justifies this view in its distinct allusion to the great promise (compare Sa2 7:12). In all David's successes he saw the pledges of a fulfilment of that promise, and he mourned in all his adversities, not only in view of his personal suffering, but because he saw in them evidences of danger to the great interests which were committed to his keeping. It is in these aspects of his character that we are led properly to appreciate the importance attached to his sorrows and sufferings, his joys and successes.</p>
