## Introduction

<div className="cm-intro">
<p>For the historical occasion mentioned, compare 2Sa. 15:1-17:29. David, in the midst of great distress, with filial confidence, implores God's aid, and, anticipating relief, offers praise. (Psa 3:1-8)</p>
<p>Lord . . . increased--The extent of the rebellion (Sa2 15:13) surprises and grieves him.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 3:2](/en/kjv/psalms/3#v2)</div>
<p>say of my soul--that is, "of me" (compare Psa 25:3). This use of "soul" is common; perhaps it arose from regarding the soul as man's chief part.</p>
<p>no help . . . in God--rejected by Him. This is the bitterest reproach for a pious man, and denotes a spirit of malignant triumph.</p>
<p>Selah--This word is of very obscure meaning. It probably denotes rest or pause, both as to the music and singing, intimating something emphatic in the sentiment (compare Psa 9:16).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 3:3](/en/kjv/psalms/3#v3)</div>
<p>But--literally, "and" (Psa 2:6). He repels the reproach by avowing his continued trust.</p>
<p>shield--a favorite and often-used figure for protection.</p>
<p>my glory--its source.</p>
<p>lifter up of mine head--one who raises me from despondency.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 3:4](/en/kjv/psalms/3#v4)</div>
<p>cried . . . heard--Such has been my experience. The latter verb denotes a gracious hearing or answering.</p>
<p>out of--or, "from."</p>
<p>his holy hill--Zion (Psa 2:6). His visible earthly residence.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 3:5](/en/kjv/psalms/3#v5)</div>
<p>the Lord sustained me--literally, "will sustain me," as if his language or thought when he laid down, and the reason of his composure.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 3:6](/en/kjv/psalms/3#v6)</div>
<p>ten thousands of people--or, "myriads," any very great number (compare Sa2 16:18).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 3:7](/en/kjv/psalms/3#v7)</div>
<p>Arise, O Lord--God is figuratively represented as asleep to denote His apparent indifference (Psa 7:6). The use of "cheekbone" and "teeth" represents his enemies as fierce, like wild beasts ready to devour (Psa 27:2), and smiting their cheekbone (Kg1 22:24) denotes violence and insult.</p>
<p>thou hast broken--God took his part, utterly depriving the enemy of power to injure.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 3:8](/en/kjv/psalms/3#v8)</div>
<p>An ascription of praise to a delivering God, whose favor is an efficient benefit.</p>

</div>
