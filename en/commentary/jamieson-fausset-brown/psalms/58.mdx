## Introduction

<div className="cm-intro">
<p>David's critical condition in some period of the Sauline persecution probably occasioned this Psalm, in which the Psalmist teaches that the innate and actual sinfulness of men deserves, and shall receive, God's righteous vengeance, while the pious may be consoled by the evidence of His wise and holy government of men. (Psa 58:1-11)</p>
<p>O congregation--literally, "Oh, dumb"; the word used is never translated "congregation." "Are ye dumb? ye should speak righteousness," may be the translation. In any case, the writer remonstrates with them, perhaps a council, who were assembled to try his cause, and bound to give a right decision.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 58:2](/en/kjv/psalms/58#v2)</div>
<p>This they did not design; but</p>
<p>weigh . . . violence--or give decisions of violence. Weigh is a figure to express the acts of judges.</p>
<p>in the earth--publicly.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 58:3](/en/kjv/psalms/58#v3)</div>
<p>describe the wicked generally, who sin naturally, easily, malignantly, and stubbornly.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 58:4](/en/kjv/psalms/58#v4)</div>
<p>stoppeth her--literally, "his."</p>
<p>ear--that is, the wicked man (the singular used collectively), who thus becomes like the deaf adder which has no ear.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 58:6](/en/kjv/psalms/58#v6)</div>
<p>He prays for their destruction, under the figure of ravenous beasts (Psa 3:7; Psa 7:2).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 58:7](/en/kjv/psalms/58#v7)</div>
<p>which run continually--literally, "they shall go to themselves," utterly depart, as rapid mountain torrents.</p>
<p>he bendeth . . . his arrows--prepares it. The term for preparing a bow applied to arrows (Psa 64:3).</p>
<p>let them . . . pieces--literally, "as if they cut themselves off"--that is, become blunted and of no avail.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 58:8](/en/kjv/psalms/58#v8)</div>
<p>Other figures of this utter ruin; the last denoting rapidity. In a shorter time than pots feel the heat of thorns on fire--</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 58:9](/en/kjv/psalms/58#v9)</div>
<p>he shall take them away as with a whirlwind--literally, "blow him (them) away."</p>
<p>both living . . . wrath--literally, "as the living" or fresh as the heated or burning--that is, thorns--all easily blown away, so easily and quickly the wicked. The figure of the "snail" perhaps alludes to its loss of saliva when moving. Though obscure in its clauses, the general sense of the passage is clear.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 58:10](/en/kjv/psalms/58#v10)</div>
<p>wash . . . wicked--denoting great slaughter. The joy of triumph over the destruction of the wicked is because they are God's enemies, and their overthrow shows that He reigneth (compare Psa 52:5-7; Psa 54:7). In this assurance let heaven and earth rejoice (Psa 96:10; Psa 97:1, &amp;c.).</p>
