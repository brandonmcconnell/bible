## Introduction

<div className="cm-intro">
<p>Maschil--literally, "giving instruction." The Psalmist describes the blessings of His forgiveness, succeeding the pains of conviction, and deduces from his own experience instruction and exhortation to others. (Psa 32:1-11)</p>
<p>(Compare Rom 4:6).</p>
<p>forgiven--literally, "taken away," opposed to retain (Joh 20:23).</p>
<p>covered--so that God no longer regards the sin (Psa 85:3).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 32:2](/en/kjv/psalms/32#v2)</div>
<p>imputeth--charge to him, and treat him accordingly.</p>
<p>no guile--or, deceit, no false estimate of himself, nor insincerity before God (compare Rom 8:1).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 32:3](/en/kjv/psalms/32#v3)</div>
<p>A vivid description of felt, but unacknowledged, sin.</p>
<p>When--literally, "for," as in Psa 32:4.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 32:4](/en/kjv/psalms/32#v4)</div>
<p>thy hand--of God, or power in distressing him (Psa 38:2).</p>
<p>moisture--vital juices of the body, the parching heat of which expresses the anguish of the soul. On the other figures, compare Psa 6:2, Psa 6:7; Psa 31:9-11. If composed on the occasion of the fifty-first Psalm, this distress may have been protracted for several months.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 32:5](/en/kjv/psalms/32#v5)</div>
<p>A prompt fulfilment of the purposed confession is followed by a prompt forgiveness.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 32:6](/en/kjv/psalms/32#v6)</div>
<p>For this--that is, my happy experience.</p>
<p>godly--pious in the sense of Psa 4:3.</p>
<p>a time-- (Isa 55:6); when God's Spirit inclines us to seek pardon, He is ready to forgive.</p>
<p>floods, &amp;c.--denotes great danger (Psa 18:17; Psa 66:12).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 32:7](/en/kjv/psalms/32#v7)</div>
<p>His experience illustrates the statement of Psa 32:6.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 32:8](/en/kjv/psalms/32#v8)</div>
<p>Whether, as most likely, the language of David (compare Psa 51:13), or that of God, this is a promise of divine guidance.</p>
<p>I will . . . mine eye--or, My eye shall be on thee, watching and directing thy way.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 32:9](/en/kjv/psalms/32#v9)</div>
<p>The latter clause, more literally, "in that they come not near thee"; that is, because they will not come, &amp;c., unless forced by bit and bridle.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 32:10](/en/kjv/psalms/32#v10)</div>
<p>The sorrows of the impenitent contrasted with the peace and safety secured by God's mercy.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Psalms 32:11](/en/kjv/psalms/32#v11)</div>
<p>The righteous and upright, or those conforming to the divine teaching for securing the divine blessing, may well rejoice with shouting.</p>
<p>Next: Psalms Chapter 33</p>
</div>
