## Introduction

<div className="cm-intro">
<p>JOB'S ANSWER. (Job 23:1-17)</p>
<p>to-day--implying, perhaps, that the debate was carried on through more days than one (see Introduction).</p>
<p>bitter-- (Job 7:11; Job 10:1).</p>
<p>my stroke--the hand of God on me (Margin, Job 19:21; Psa 32:4).</p>
<p>heavier than--is so heavy that I cannot relieve myself adequately by groaning.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 23:3](/en/kjv/job/23#v3)</div>
<p>The same wish as in Job 13:3 (compare Heb 10:19-22).</p>
<p>Seat--The idea in the Hebrew is a well-prepared throne (Psa 9:7).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 23:4](/en/kjv/job/23#v4)</div>
<p>order--state methodically (Job 13:18; Isa 43:26).</p>
<p>fill, &amp;c.--I would have abundance of arguments to adduce.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 23:5](/en/kjv/job/23#v5)</div>
<p>he--emphatic: it little matters what man may say of me, if only I know what God judges of me.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 23:6](/en/kjv/job/23#v6)</div>
<p>An objection suggests itself, while he utters the wish (Job 23:5). Do I hereby wish that He should plead against me with His omnipotence? Far from it! (Job 9:19, Job 9:34; Job 13:21; Job 30:18).</p>
<p>strength--so as to prevail with Him: as in Jacob's case (Hos 12:3-4). UMBREIT and MAURER better translate as in Job 4:20 (I only wish that He) "would attend to me," that is, give me a patient hearing as an ordinary judge, not using His omnipotence, but only His divine knowledge of my innocence.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 23:7](/en/kjv/job/23#v7)</div>
<p>There--rather, "Then": if God would "attend" to me (Job 23:6).</p>
<p>righteous--that is, the result of my dispute would be, He would acknowledge me as righteous.</p>
<p>delivered--from suspicion of guilt on the part of my Judge.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 23:8](/en/kjv/job/23#v8)</div>
<p>But I wish in vain. For "behold," &amp;c.</p>
<p>forward . . . backward--rather, "to the east--to the west." The Hebrew geographers faced the east, that is, sunrise: not the north, as we do. So "before" means east: "behind," west (so the Hindus). Para, "before"--east: Apara, "behind"--west: Daschina, "the right hand"--south: Bama, "left"--north. A similar reference to sunrise appears in the name Asia, "sunrise," Europe, "sunset"; pure Babylonian names, as RAWLINSON shows.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 23:9](/en/kjv/job/23#v9)</div>
<p>Rather, "To the north."</p>
<p>work--God's glorious works are especially seen towards the north region of the sky by one in the northern hemisphere. The antithesis is between God working and yet not being beheld: as in Job 9:11, between "He goeth by," and "I see Him not." If the Hebrew bears it, the parallelism to the second clause is better suited by translating, as UMBREIT, "doth hide himself"; but then the antithesis to "behold" would be lost.</p>
<p>right hand--"in the south."</p>
<p>hideth--appropriately, of the unexplored south, then regarded as uninhabitable because of its heat (see Job 34:29).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 23:10](/en/kjv/job/23#v10)</div>
<p>But--correcting himself for the wish that his cause should be known before God. The omniscient One already knoweth the way in me (my inward principles: His outward way or course of acts is mentioned in Job 23:11. So in me, Job 4:21); though for some inscrutable cause He as yet hides Himself (Job 23:8-9).</p>
<p>when--let Him only but try my cause, I shall, &amp;c.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 23:11](/en/kjv/job/23#v11)</div>
<p>held--fast by His steps. The law is in Old Testament poetry regarded as a way, God going before us as our guide, in whose footsteps we must tread (Psa 17:5).</p>
<p>declined-- (Psa 125:5).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 23:12](/en/kjv/job/23#v12)</div>
<p>esteemed--rather, "laid up," namely, as a treasure found (Mat 13:44; Psa 119:11); alluding to the words of Eliphaz (Job 22:22). There was no need to tell me so; I have done so already (Jer 15:16).</p>
<p>necessary--"Appointed portion" (of food; as in Pro 30:8). UMBREIT and MAURER translate, "More than my law," my own will, in antithesis to "the words of His mouth" (Joh 6:38). Probably under the general term, "what is appointed to me" (the same Hebrew is in Job 23:14), all that ministers to the appetites of the body and carnal will is included.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 23:13](/en/kjv/job/23#v13)</div>
<p>in one mind--notwithstanding my innocence, He is unaltered in His purpose of proving me guilty (Job 9:12).</p>
<p>soul--His will (Psa 115:3). God's sovereignty. He has one great purpose; nothing is haphazard; everything has its proper place with a view to His purpose.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 23:14](/en/kjv/job/23#v14)</div>
<p>many such--He has yet many more such ills in store for me, though hidden in His breast (Job 10:13).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 23:15](/en/kjv/job/23#v15)</div>
<p>God's decrees, impossible to be resisted, and leaving us in the dark as to what may come next, are calculated to fill the mind with holy awe &#91;BARNES&#93;.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 23:16](/en/kjv/job/23#v16)</div>
<p>soft--faint; hath melted my courage. Here again Job's language is that of Jesus Christ (Psa 22:14).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 23:17](/en/kjv/job/23#v17)</div>
<p>Because I was not taken away by death from the evil to come (literally, "from before the face of the darkness," Isa 57:1). Alluding to the words of Eliphaz (Job 22:11), "darkness," that is, calamity.</p>
<p>cut off--rather, in the Arabic sense, brought to the land of silence; my sad complaint hushed in death &#91;UMBREIT&#93;. "Darkness" in the second clause, not the same Hebrew word as in the first, "cloud," "obscurity." Instead of "covering the cloud (of evil) from my face," He "covers" me with it (Job 22:11).</p>

</div>
