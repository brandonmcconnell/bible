## Introduction

<div className="cm-intro">
<p>JOB'S ANSWER CONTINUED. (Job 17:1-16)</p>
<p>breath . . . corrupt--result of elephantiasis. But UMBREIT, "my strength (spirit) is spent."</p>
<p>extinct--Life is compared to an expiring light. "The light of my day is extinguished."</p>
<p>graves--plural, to heighten the force.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v2" data-v="2">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 17:2](/en/kjv/job/17#v2)</div>
<p>UMBREIT, more emphatically, "had I only not to endure mockery, in the midst of their contentions I (mine eye) would remain quiet."</p>
<p>eye continue--Hebrew, "tarry all night"; a figure taken from sleep at night, to express undisturbed rest; opposed to (Job 16:20), when the eye of Job is represented as pouring out tears to God without rest.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 17:3](/en/kjv/job/17#v3)</div>
<p>Lay down now--namely, a pledge or security; that is, be my surety; do Thou attest my innocence, since my friends only mock me (Job 17:2). Both litigating parties had to lay down a sum as security before the trial.</p>
<p>put me in a surety--Provide a surety for me (in the trial) with Thee. A presage of the "surety" (Heb 7:22), or "one Mediator between God and man" (see on Job 16:21).</p>
<p>strike hands--"who else (save God Himself) could strike hands with me?" that is, be my security (Psa 119:122). The Hebrew strikes the hand of him for whom he goes security (Pro 6:1).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 17:4](/en/kjv/job/17#v4)</div>
<p>their heart--The intellect of his friends.</p>
<p>shalt . . . exalt--Rather imperative, "exalt them not"; allow them not to conquer &#91;UMBREIT&#93;, (Isa 6:9-10).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v5" data-v="5">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 17:5](/en/kjv/job/17#v5)</div>
<p>The Hebrew for "flattery" is "smoothness"; then it came to mean a prey divided by lot, because a smooth stone was used in casting the lots (Deu 18:8), "a portion" (Gen 14:24). Therefore translate, "He that delivers up his friend as a prey (which the conduct of my friends implies that they would do), even the eyes," &amp;c. &#91;NOYES&#93; (Job 11:20). Job says this as to the sinner's children, retorting upon their reproach as to the cutting off of his (Job 5:4; Job 15:30). This accords with the Old Testament dispensation of legal retribution (Exo 20:5).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 17:6](/en/kjv/job/17#v6)</div>
<p>He--God. The poet reverentially suppresses the name of God when speaking of calamities inflicted.</p>
<p>by-word-- (Deu 28:37; Psa 69:11). My awful punishment makes my name execrated everywhere, as if I must have been superlatively bad to have earned it.</p>
<p>aforetime . . . tabret--as David was honored (Sa1 18:6). Rather from a different Hebrew root, "I am treated to my face as an object of disgust," literally, "an object to be spit upon in the face" (Num 12:14). So Raca means (Mat 5:22) &#91;UMBREIT&#93;.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 17:7](/en/kjv/job/17#v7)</div>
<p>(Psa 6:7; Psa 31:9; Deu 34:7).</p>
<p>members--literally, "figures"; all the individual members being peculiar forms of the body; opposed to "shadow," which looks like a figure without solidity.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 17:8](/en/kjv/job/17#v8)</div>
<p>astonied--at my unmerited sufferings.</p>
<p>against the hypocrite--The upright shall feel their sense of justice wounded ("will be indignant") because of the prosperity of the wicked. By "hypocrite" or "ungodly," he perhaps glances at his false friends.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v9" data-v="9">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 17:9](/en/kjv/job/17#v9)</div>
<p>The strength of religious principle is heightened by misfortune. The pious shall take fresh courage to persevere from the example of suffering Job. The image is from a warrior acquiring new courage in action (Isa 40:30-31; Phi 1:14).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 17:10](/en/kjv/job/17#v10)</div>
<p>return--If you have anything to advance really wise, though I doubt it, recommence your speech. For as yet I cannot find one wise man among you all.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 17:11](/en/kjv/job/17#v11)</div>
<p>Only do not vainly speak of the restoration of health to me; for "my days are past."</p>
<p>broken off--as the threads of the web cut off from the loom (Isa 38:12).</p>
<p>thoughts--literally, "possessions," that is, all the feelings and fair hopes which my heart once nourished. These belong to the heart, as "purposes" to the understanding; the two together here describe the entire inner man.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 17:12](/en/kjv/job/17#v12)</div>
<p>They--namely, "my friends."</p>
<p>change the night into day--that is, would try to persuade me of the change of my misery into joy, which is impossible &#91;UMBREIT&#93; (Job 11:17); (but) the light of prosperity (could it be enjoyed) would be short because of the darkness of adversity. Or better for "short," the Hebrew "near"; "and the light of new prosperity should be near in the face of (before) the darkness of death"; that is, they would persuade me that light is near, even though darkness approaches.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 17:13](/en/kjv/job/17#v13)</div>
<p>Rather, "if I wait for this grave (Sheol, or the unseen world) as my house, and make my bed in the darkness (Job 17:14), and say to corruption," rather, "to the pit" or "grave," &amp;c. (Job 17:15). Where then is my hope? &#91;UMBREIT&#93;. The apodosis is at Job 17:15.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 17:14](/en/kjv/job/17#v14)</div>
<p>Thou art my father, &amp;c.--expressing most intimate connection (Pro 7:4). His diseased state made him closely akin to the grave and worm.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 17:15](/en/kjv/job/17#v15)</div>
<p>Who shall see it fulfilled? namely, the "hope" (Job 11:18) which they held out to him of restoration.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 17:16](/en/kjv/job/17#v16)</div>
<p>They--namely, my hopes shall be buried with me.</p>
<p>bars-- (Isa 38:10). Rather, the wastes or solitudes of the pit (sheol, the unseen world).</p>
<p>rest together--the rest of me and my hope is in, &amp;c. Both expire together. The word "rest" implies that man's ceaseless hopes only rob him of rest.</p>

</div>
