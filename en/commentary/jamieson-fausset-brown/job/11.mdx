## Introduction

<div className="cm-intro">
<p>FIRST SPEECH OF ZOPHAR. (Job 11:1-20)</p>
<p>Zophar assails Job for his empty words, and indirectly, the two friends, for their weak reply. Taciturnity is highly prized among Orientals (Pro 10:8, Pro 10:19).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v3" data-v="3">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 11:3](/en/kjv/job/11#v3)</div>
<p>lies--rather, "vain boasting" (Isa 16:6; Jer 48:30). The "men" is emphatic; men of sense; in antithesis to "vain boasting."</p>
<p>mockest--upbraidest God by complaints, "shall no man make thee ashamed?"</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v4" data-v="4">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 11:4](/en/kjv/job/11#v4)</div>
<p>doctrine--purposely used of Job's speeches, which sounded like lessons of doctrine (Deu 32:2; Pro 4:2).</p>
<p>thine--addressed to God. Job had maintained his sincerity against his friends suspicions, not faultlessness.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v6" data-v="6">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 11:6](/en/kjv/job/11#v6)</div>
<p>to that which is!--Rather, "they are double to &#91;man's&#93; wisdom" &#91;MICHAELIS&#93;. So the Hebrew is rendered (Pro 2:7). God's ways, which you arraign, if you were shown their secret wisdom, would be seen vastly to exceed that of men, including yours (Co1 1:25).</p>
<p>exacteth--Rather, "God consigns to oblivion in thy favor much of thy guilt."</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v7" data-v="7">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 11:7](/en/kjv/job/11#v7)</div>
<p>Rather, "Penetrate to the perfections of the Almighty" (Job 9:10; Psa 139:6).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v8" data-v="8">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 11:8](/en/kjv/job/11#v8)</div>
<p>It--the "wisdom" of God (Job 11:6). The abruptness of the Hebrew is forcible: "The heights of heaven! What canst thou do" (as to attaining to them with thy gaze, Psa 139:8)?</p>
<p>know--namely, of His perfections.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v10" data-v="10">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 11:10](/en/kjv/job/11#v10)</div>
<p>cut off--Rather, as in Job 9:11, "pass over," as a storm; namely, rush upon in anger.</p>
<p>shut up--in prison, with a view to trial.</p>
<p>gather together--the parties for judgment: hold a judicial assembly; to pass sentence on the prisoners.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v11" data-v="11">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 11:11](/en/kjv/job/11#v11)</div>
<p>(Psa 94:11).</p>
<p>consider--so as to punish it. Rather, from the connection, Job 11:6, "He seeth wickedness also, which man does not perceive"; literally, "But no (other, save He) perceiveth it" &#91;UMBREIT&#93;. God's "wisdom" (Job 11:6), detects sin where Job's human eye cannot reach (Job 11:8), so as to see any.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v12" data-v="12">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 11:12](/en/kjv/job/11#v12)</div>
<p>vain--hollow.</p>
<p>would be--"wants to consider himself wise"; opposed to God's "wisdom" (see on Job 11:11); refuses to see sin, where God sees it (Rom 1:22).</p>
<p>wild ass's colt--a proverb for untamed wildness (Job 39:5, Job 39:8; Jer 2:24; Gen 16:12; Hebrew, "a wild-ass man"). Man wishes to appear wisely obedient to his Lord, whereas he is, from his birth, unsubdued in spirit.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v13" data-v="13">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 11:13](/en/kjv/job/11#v13)</div>
<p>The apodosis to the "If" is at Job 11:15. The preparation of the heart is to be obtained (Pro 16:1) by stretching out the hands in prayer for it (Psa 10:17; Ch1 29:18).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v14" data-v="14">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 11:14](/en/kjv/job/11#v14)</div>
<p>Rather, "if thou wilt put far away the iniquity in thine hand" (as Zaccheus did, Luk 19:8). The apodosis or conclusion is at Job 11:15, "then shalt thou," &amp;c.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v15" data-v="15">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 11:15](/en/kjv/job/11#v15)</div>
<p>Zophar refers to Job's own words (Job 10:15), "yet will I not lift up my head," even though righteous. Zophar declares, if Job will follow his advice, he may "lift up his face."</p>
<p>spot-- (Deu 32:5).</p>
<p>steadfast--literally, "run fast together," like metals which become firm and hard by fusion. The sinner on the contrary is wavering.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v16" data-v="16">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 11:16](/en/kjv/job/11#v16)</div>
<p>Just as when the stream runs dry (Job 6:17), the danger threatened by its wild waves is forgotten (Isa 65:16) &#91;UMBREIT&#93;.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v17" data-v="17">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 11:17](/en/kjv/job/11#v17)</div>
<p>age--days of life.</p>
<p>the noon-day--namely, of thy former prosperity; which, in the poet's image, had gone on increasing, until it reached its height, as the sun rises higher and higher until it reaches the meridian (Pro 4:18).</p>
<p>shine forth--rather, "though now in darkness, thou shall be as the morning"; or, "thy darkness (if any dark shade should arise on thee, it) shall be as the morning" (only the dullness of morning twilight, not nocturnal darkness) &#91;UMBREIT&#93;.</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v18" data-v="18">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 11:18](/en/kjv/job/11#v18)</div>
<p>The experience of thy life will teach thee there is hope for man in every trial.</p>
<p>dig--namely, wells; the chief necessity in the East. Better, "though now ashamed (Rom 5:5, opposed to the previous 'hope'), thou shalt then rest safely" &#91;GESENIUS&#93;;</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v19" data-v="19">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 11:19](/en/kjv/job/11#v19)</div>
<p>(Psa 4:8; Pro 3:24; Isa 14:30); oriental images of prosperity.</p>
<p>make suit--literally, "stroke thy face," "caress thee" (Pro 19:6).</p>
</div>

<div className="cmv" id="v20" data-v="20">
<div className="cmv-head">[Job 11:20](/en/kjv/job/11#v20)</div>
<p>A warning to Job, if he would not turn to God.</p>
<p>The wicked--that is, obdurate sinners.</p>
<p>eyes . . . fail--that is, in vain look for relief (Deu 28:65). Zophar implies Job's only hope of relief is in a change of heart.</p>
<p>they shall not escape--literally, "every refuge shall vanish from them."</p>
<p>giving up of the ghost--Their hope shall leave them as the breath does the body (Pro 11:7).</p>

</div>
