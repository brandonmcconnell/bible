<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 23:34; Acts 25:5; Acts 8:40"> Festus therefore, being come into the eparchy, after three days went up to Jerusalem from Caesarea.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Acts 25:15; Proverbs 4:16; Acts 24:1"> And the chief priests and the chief of the Jews laid informations before him against Paul, and besought him,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Acts 9:24; Acts 23:12–15; 1 Samuel 23:19–21"> asking as a grace against him that he would send for him to Jerusalem, laying people in wait to kill him on the way.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Acts 24:23"> Festus therefore answered that Paul should be kept at Caesarea, and that he himself was about to set out shortly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Acts 25:25; John 18:29–30; Acts 25:18–19"> Let therefore the persons of authority among you, says he, going down too, if there be anything in this man, accuse him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 25:17; Acts 25:10; Matthew 27:19"> And having remained among them not more than eight or ten days, he went down to Caesarea; and on the next day, having sat down on the judgment-seat, commanded Paul to be brought.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Acts 24:13; Luke 23:10; Acts 25:24"> And when he was come, the Jews who were come down from Jerusalem stood round, bringing many and grievous charges which they were not able to prove:</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Acts 24:12; Acts 28:17; 2 Corinthians 1:12"> Paul answering for himself, Neither against the law of the Jews, nor against the temple, nor against Caesar, have I offended &#91;in&#93; anything.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 24:27; Acts 12:3; Acts 25:20"> But Festus, desirous of obliging the Jews, to acquire their favour, answering Paul, said, Art thou willing to go up to Jerusalem, there to be judged before me concerning these things?</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Matthew 27:18; 2 Corinthians 4:2; Acts 28:18"> But Paul said, I am standing before the judgment-seat of Caesar, where I ought to be judged. To the Jews have I done no wrong, as thou also very well knowest.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 28:19; Acts 26:32; Acts 25:25"> If then I have done any wrong and committed anything worthy of death, I do not deprecate dying; but if there is nothing of those things of which they accuse me, no man can give me up to them. I appeal to Caesar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 19:21; Philippians 1:12–14; Philippians 1:20"> Then Festus, having conferred with the council, answered, Thou hast appealed to Caesar. To Caesar shalt thou go.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Acts 8:40; Acts 26:1; Acts 26:27–28"> And when certain days had elapsed, Agrippa the king and Bernice arrived at Caesarea to salute Festus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Acts 24:27"> And when they had spent many days there, Festus laid before the king the matters relating to Paul, saying, There is a certain man left prisoner by Felix,</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Acts 25:1–3; Esther 3:9; Luke 18:3–5"> concerning whom, when I was at Jerusalem, the chief priests and the elders of the Jews laid informations, requiring judgment against him:</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="John 7:51; Deuteronomy 19:17–18; Acts 23:30"> to whom I answered, It is not &#91;the&#93; custom of the Romans to give up any man before that the accused have the accusers face to face, and he have got opportunity of defence touching the charge.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Acts 25:6; Acts 25:10"> When therefore they had come together here, without putting it off, I sat the next day on the judgment-seat and commanded the man to be brought:</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> concerning whom the accusers, standing up, brought no such accusation of guilt as I supposed;</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Acts 23:29; Acts 18:15; 1 Corinthians 15:3–4"> but had against him certain questions of their own system of worship, and concerning a certain Jesus who is dead, whom Paul affirmed to be living.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Acts 25:9"> And as I myself was at a loss as to an inquiry into these things, I said, Was he willing to go to Jerusalem and there to be judged concerning these things?</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Timothy 4:16; Luke 2:1; Acts 25:10–11"> But Paul having appealed to be kept for the cognisance of Augustus, I commanded him to be kept till I shall send him to Caesar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 9:15; Matthew 10:18; Isaiah 52:15"> And Agrippa &#91;said&#93; to Festus, I myself also would desire to hear the man. To-morrow, said he, thou shalt hear him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Acts 26:30; Acts 25:13; 1 John 2:16"> On the morrow therefore, Agrippa being come, and Bernice, with great pomp, and having entered into the hall of audience, with the chiliarchs and the men of distinction of the city, and Festus having given command, Paul was brought.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 22:22; Acts 25:7; Acts 25:2–3"> And Festus said, King Agrippa, and all men who are here present with us, ye see this person, concerning whom all the multitude of the Jews applied to me both in Jerusalem and here, crying out against &#91;him&#93; that he ought not to live any longer.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Acts 23:29; Acts 25:11–12; Luke 23:4"> But I, having found that he had done nothing worthy of death, and this &#91;man&#93; himself having appealed to Augustus, I have decided to send him;</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 26:2–3"> concerning whom I have nothing certain to write to my lord. Wherefore I have brought him before you, and specially before thee, king Agrippa, so that an examination having been gone into I may have something to write:</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="John 7:51; Proverbs 18:13"> for it seems to me senseless, sending a prisoner, not also to signify the charges against him.</span>
