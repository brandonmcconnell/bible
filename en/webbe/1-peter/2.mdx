<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Psalms 34:8; Hebrews 6:5–6; Psalms 63:5"> if indeed you have tasted that the Lord is gracious.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Psalms 118:22–23; Acts 4:11–12; 1 Peter 2:7"> Come to him, a living stone, rejected indeed by men, but chosen by God, precious.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Ephesians 2:20–22; 1 Peter 2:9; 1 Corinthians 3:9"> You also as living stones are built up as a spiritual house, to be a holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Isaiah 28:16; Romans 9:32–33; 1 Peter 2:4"> Because it is contained in Scripture,<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“Behold,</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“Behold”, from “הִנֵּה” or “ἰδοὺ”, means look at, take notice, observe, see, or gaze at. <span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Psalms 118:22–23; Mark 12:10–11; Acts 4:11–12"> For you who believe therefore is the honour, but for those who are disobedient,<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“The stone which the builders rejected</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">has become the chief cornerstone,”</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Isaiah 8:14; 1 Corinthians 1:23; Romans 9:32–33"> and,<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“a stumbling stone and a rock of offence.”</span>For they stumble at the word, being disobedient, to which also they were appointed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Exodus 19:5–6; Deuteronomy 7:6; Revelation 1:6"> But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for God’s own possession, that you may proclaim the excellence of him who called you out of darkness into his marvellous light.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Hosea 2:23; Hebrews 4:16; Hosea 1:9–10"> In the past, you were not a people, but now are God’s people, who had not obtained mercy, but now have obtained mercy.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 John 2:15–17; Galatians 5:24; 1 Peter 4:2"> Beloved, I beg you as foreigners and pilgrims to abstain from fleshly lusts which war against the soul,</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Peter 3:16; Matthew 5:16; Titus 2:7–8"> having good behaviour amongst the nations, so in that of which they speak against you as evildoers, they may see your good works and glorify God in the day of visitation.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Titus 3:1; Romans 13:1–7; 1 Timothy 2:1–2"> Therefore subject yourselves to every ordinance of man for the Lord’s sake: whether to the king, as supreme,</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Romans 13:3–4"> or to governors, as sent by him for vengeance on evildoers and for praise to those who do well.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Peter 3:17; Titus 2:8; Matthew 7:26"> For this is the will of God, that by well-doing you should put to silence the ignorance of foolish men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Galatians 5:13; Romans 6:22; Romans 6:18"> Live as free people, yet not using your freedom for a cloak of wickedness, but as bondservants of God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Proverbs 24:21; Romans 12:10; Romans 13:7"> Honour all men. Love the brotherhood. Fear God. Honour the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Titus 2:9–10; Ephesians 6:5–7; Colossians 3:22–25"> Servants, be in subjection to your masters with all respect, not only to the good and gentle, but also to the wicked.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Matthew 5:10–12; 1 Peter 3:14–17; 1 Peter 2:20"> For it is commendable if someone endures pain, suffering unjustly, because of conscience towards God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Peter 2:19; 1 Peter 3:17; Matthew 5:10–12"> For what glory is it if, when you sin, you patiently endure beating? But if when you do well, you patiently endure suffering, this is commendable with God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 John 2:6; Matthew 16:24; John 13:15"> For you were called to this, because Christ also suffered for us, leaving you<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="TR reads “us” instead of “you”">b</a></sup>an example, that you should follow his steps,</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Isaiah 53:9; 2 Corinthians 5:21; Hebrews 4:15"> who didn’t sin, “neither was deceit found in his mouth.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Isaiah 53:7; 1 Peter 4:19; Hebrews 12:3"> When he was cursed, he didn’t curse back. When he suffered, he didn’t threaten, but committed himself to him who judges righteously.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Isaiah 53:4–6; Matthew 8:17; Romans 6:11"> He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree, that we, having died to sins, might live to righteousness. You were healed by his wounds.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="or, stripes">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Isaiah 53:6; Ezekiel 34:6; Luke 15:4–6"> For you were going astray like sheep; but now you have returned to the Shepherd and Overseer<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Overseer” is from the Greek ἐπίσκοπον, which can mean overseer, curator, guardian, or superintendent.">d</a></sup>of your souls.</span>
