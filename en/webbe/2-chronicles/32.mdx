<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Kings 18:13–37; 2 Kings 17:6; 2 Chronicles 20:1–2"> After these things and this faithfulness, Sennacherib king of Assyria came, entered into Judah, encamped against the fortified cities, and intended to win them for himself.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Luke 9:53; Luke 9:51; 2 Kings 12:17"> When Hezekiah saw that Sennacherib had come, and that he was planning to fight against Jerusalem,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Proverbs 24:6; Proverbs 20:18; Proverbs 15:22"> he took counsel with his princes and his mighty men to stop the waters of the springs which were outside of the city, and they helped him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Chronicles 32:30; 2 Kings 18:9; 1 Kings 19:21"> Then many people gathered together and they stopped all the springs and the brook that flowed through the middle of the land, saying, “Why should the kings of Assyria come, and find abundant water?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Chronicles 25:23; 1 Kings 9:24; 2 Kings 25:4"> He took courage, built up all the wall that was broken down, and raised it up to the towers, with the other wall outside, and strengthened Millo in David’s city, and made weapons and shields in abundance.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Isaiah 40:2; 2 Chronicles 30:22; Nehemiah 8:16"> He set captains of war over the people, gathered them together to him in the wide place at the gate of the city, and spoke encouragingly to them, saying,</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Kings 6:16; Romans 8:31; Ephesians 6:10"> “Be strong and courageous. Don’t be afraid or dismayed because of the king of Assyria, nor for all the multitude who is with him; for there is a greater one with us than with him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Jeremiah 17:5; 2 Chronicles 20:17; Isaiah 41:10"> An arm of flesh is with him, but the LORD our God is with us to help us and to fight our battles.” The people rested themselves on the words of Hezekiah king of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Kings 18:17; Joshua 10:31; Isaiah 36:2"> After this, Sennacherib king of Assyria sent his servants to Jerusalem, (now he was attacking Lachish, and all his forces were with him), to Hezekiah king of Judah, and to all Judah who were at Jerusalem, saying,</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Kings 18:19; Isaiah 36:4"> Sennacherib king of Assyria says, “In whom do you trust, that you remain under siege in Jerusalem?</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Matthew 27:43; Psalms 11:1–3; Isaiah 36:18"> Doesn’t Hezekiah persuade you to give you over to die by famine and by thirst, saying, ‘The LORD our God will deliver us out of the hand of the king of Assyria’?</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Chronicles 31:1; Deuteronomy 12:13–14; Deuteronomy 12:26–27"> Hasn’t the same Hezekiah taken away his high places and his altars, and commanded Judah and Jerusalem, saying, ‘You shall worship before one altar, and you shall burn incense on it’?</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Kings 18:33–35; Isaiah 44:8–10; Daniel 4:30"> Don’t you know what I and my fathers have done to all the peoples of the lands? Were the gods of the nations of those lands in any way able to deliver their land out of my hand?</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Isaiah 10:11–12; Exodus 15:9–11; Isaiah 42:8"> Who was there amongst all the gods of those nations which my fathers utterly destroyed that could deliver his people out of my hand, that your God should be able to deliver you out of my hand?</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Exodus 5:2; Daniel 3:15; Isaiah 36:18"> Now therefore don’t let Hezekiah deceive you nor persuade you in this way. Don’t believe him, for no god of any nation or kingdom was able to deliver his people out of my hand, and out of the hand of my fathers. How much less will your God deliver you out of my hand?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Psalms 73:9; John 15:21; Job 15:25–26"> His servants spoke yet more against the LORD God and against his servant Hezekiah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Isaiah 37:14; 2 Kings 19:12; Isaiah 37:28–29"> He also wrote letters insulting the LORD, the God of Israel, and speaking against him, saying, “As the gods of the nations of the lands, which have not delivered their people out of my hand, so shall the God of Hezekiah not deliver his people out of my hand.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Samuel 17:10; 2 Kings 18:26–28; 1 Samuel 17:26"> They called out with a loud voice in the Jews’ language to the people of Jerusalem who were on the wall, to frighten them and to trouble them, that they might take the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Kings 19:18; Isaiah 2:8; Jeremiah 32:30"> They spoke of the God of Jerusalem as of the gods of the peoples of the earth, which are the work of men’s hands.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Psalms 91:14–15; Isaiah 37:14–20; 2 Chronicles 14:11"> Hezekiah the king and Isaiah the prophet, the son of Amoz, prayed because of this, and cried to heaven.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Isaiah 37:36–38; Psalms 76:5; Psalms 18:50"> The LORD sent an angel, who cut off all the mighty men of valour, the leaders, and captains in the camp of the king of Assyria. So he returned with shame of face to his own land. When he had come into the house of his god, those who came out of his own body<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="i.e., his own sons">a</a></sup>killed him there with the sword.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Isaiah 31:4–5; Psalms 18:48–50; 2 Thessalonians 3:5"> Thus the LORD saved Hezekiah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem from the hand of Sennacherib the king of Assyria and from the hand of all others, and guided them on every side.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 Chronicles 17:5; Matthew 2:11; 2 Chronicles 1:1"> Many brought gifts to the LORD to Jerusalem, and precious things to Hezekiah king of Judah, so that he was exalted in the sight of all nations from then on.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Isaiah 38:1–8; 2 Kings 20:1–11; Isaiah 38:21–22"> In those days Hezekiah was terminally ill, and he prayed to the LORD; and he spoke to him, and gave him a sign.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Chronicles 24:18; 2 Chronicles 26:16; 2 Chronicles 32:31"> But Hezekiah didn’t reciprocate appropriate to the benefit done for him, because his heart was lifted up. Therefore there was wrath on him, Judah, and Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Jeremiah 26:18–19; 2 Chronicles 34:27–28; 2 Chronicles 33:12"> However, Hezekiah humbled himself for the pride of his heart, both he and the inhabitants of Jerusalem, so that the LORD’s wrath didn’t come on them in the days of Hezekiah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="1 Chronicles 27:25–31; 2 Chronicles 1:12; Proverbs 10:22"> Hezekiah had exceedingly great riches and honour. He provided himself with treasuries for silver, for gold, for precious stones, for spices, for shields, and for all kinds of valuable vessels;</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 Samuel 7:8; 2 Chronicles 26:10; 1 Kings 4:26"> also storehouses for the increase of grain, new wine, and oil; and stalls for all kinds of animals, and flocks in folds.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="1 Chronicles 29:12; Proverbs 10:22; 1 Samuel 2:7"> Moreover he provided for himself cities, and possessions of flocks and herds in abundance; for God had given him abundant possessions.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="1 Kings 1:33; Isaiah 22:9–11; Psalms 1:1–3"> This same Hezekiah also stopped the upper spring of the waters of Gihon, and brought them straight down on the west side of David’s city. Hezekiah prospered in all his works.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Deuteronomy 8:2; Proverbs 17:3; Genesis 22:1"> However, concerning the ambassadors of the princes of Babylon, who sent to him to enquire of the wonder that was done in the land, God left him to test him, that he might know all that was in his heart.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Isaiah 36:1–22; 2 Kings 18:1–20; 2 Chronicles 31:20–21"> Now the rest of the acts of Hezekiah and his good deeds, behold, they are written in the vision of Isaiah the prophet, the son of Amoz, in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Proverbs 10:7; 1 Kings 1:21; 2 Chronicles 16:14"> Hezekiah slept with his fathers, and they buried him in the ascent to the tombs of the sons of David. All Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem honoured him at his death. Manasseh his son reigned in his place.</span>
