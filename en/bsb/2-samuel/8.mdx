## David’s Triumphs

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Chronicles 18:1–17; 2 Samuel 7:9; 2 Samuel 21:15–22"> Some time later, David defeated the Philistines, subdued them, and took Metheg-ammah<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Metheg-ammah means bridle of the mother city.">a</a></sup>from the hand of the Philistines.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Numbers 24:17; 1 Samuel 10:27; 2 Samuel 8:6"> David also defeated the Moabites, made them lie down on the ground, and measured them off with a cord. He measured off with two lengths those to be put to death, and with one length those to be spared. So the Moabites became subject to David and brought him tribute.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Samuel 14:47; 2 Samuel 10:16; 2 Samuel 10:19"> David also defeated Hadadezer son of Rehob, king of Zobah, who had marched out to restore his dominion along the Euphrates River.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="LXX, Syriac, and Vulgate (see also 1 Chronicles 18:3); Hebrew along the River, or an alternate reading along the Euphrates">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Joshua 11:6; Joshua 11:9; 1 Chronicles 18:4"> David captured from him a thousand chariots, seven thousand charioteers,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="LXX (see also DSS and 1 Chronicles 18:4); MT captured from him seventeen hundred charioteers">c</a></sup>and twenty thousand foot soldiers, and he hamstrung all the horses except a hundred he kept for the chariots.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Kings 11:23–25; Isaiah 7:8; Job 9:13"> When the Arameans of Damascus came to help King Hadadezer of Zobah, David struck down twenty-two thousand of their men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:14; 2 Samuel 7:9; Psalms 18:34–46"> Then he placed garrisons in Aram of Damascus, and the Arameans became subject to David and brought him tribute. So the LORD made David victorious wherever he went.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Chronicles 9:15–16; 1 Kings 14:26–27; 1 Kings 10:16–17"> And David took the gold shields that belonged to the officers of Hadadezer and brought them to Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Chronicles 18:8; 1 Chronicles 22:16; Ezekiel 47:16"> And from Betah<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Hebrew; some LXX manuscripts Tebah; see 1 Chronicles 18:8.">d</a></sup>and Berothai, cities of Hadadezer, King David took a large amount of bronze.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Kings 8:65; 2 Chronicles 8:4; 1 Chronicles 18:9"> When King Toi<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Toi is a variant of Tou; also in verse 10; see 1 Chronicles 18:9.">e</a></sup>of Hamath heard that David had defeated the entire army of Hadadezer,</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Samuel 13:10; 1 Chronicles 18:10; Psalms 129:8"> he sent his son Joram<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Joram is a variant of Hadoram; see 1 Chronicles 18:10.">f</a></sup>to greet King David and bless him for fighting and defeating Hadadezer, who had been at war with Toi. Joram brought with him articles of silver and gold and bronze,</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Kings 7:51; 1 Chronicles 29:2; 1 Chronicles 22:14–16"> and King David dedicated these to the LORD, along with the silver and gold he had dedicated from all the nations he had subdued—</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Samuel 10:14; 2 Samuel 8:2; 1 Chronicles 18:11"> from Edom<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Some Hebrew manuscripts, LXX, and Syriac (see also verse 14 and 1 Chronicles 18:11); most Hebrew manuscripts Aram">g</a></sup>and Moab, from the Ammonites and Philistines and Amalekites, and from the spoil of Hadadezer son of Rehob, king of Zobah.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Kings 14:7; 2 Samuel 7:9; 1 Chronicles 18:12"> And David made a name for himself when he returned from striking down eighteen thousand Edomites<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="A few Hebrew manuscripts, LXX, and Syriac (see also verse 14 and 1 Chronicles 18:12); most Hebrew manuscripts Arameans">h</a></sup>in the Valley of Salt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Genesis 27:29; Genesis 25:23; 2 Samuel 8:6"> He placed garrisons throughout Edom, and all the Edomites were subject to David. So the LORD made David victorious wherever he went.</span>
</div>
</div>

## David’s Officers

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Amos 5:15; Psalms 89:14; Psalms 72:2"> Thus David reigned over all Israel and administered justice and righteousness for all his people:</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Kings 4:3; 1 Chronicles 11:6; 2 Samuel 19:13"> Joab son of Zeruiah was over the army;<br />Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was the recorder;</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Chronicles 6:8; 1 Chronicles 18:16; 1 Chronicles 24:3–4"> Zadok son of Ahitub and Ahimelech son of Abiathar were priests;<br />Seraiah<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Seraiah is also called Sheva, Shisha, and Shavsha; see 2 Samuel 20:25, 1 Kings 4:3, and 1 Chronicles 18:16.">i</a></sup>was the scribe;</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Samuel 30:14; 2 Samuel 20:7; 2 Samuel 20:23"> Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the Cherethites and Pelethites;<br />and David’s sons were priestly leaders.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Literally priests, or possibly chief officials; see LXX, Targum Yonaton, and 1 Chronicles 18:17.">j</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>
