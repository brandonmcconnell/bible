## David’s Messengers Disgraced

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Samuel 11:1–3; Judges 11:12–28; Judges 10:7–9"> Some time later, the king of the Ammonites died and was succeeded by his son Hanun.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Samuel 11:1; Deuteronomy 23:3–6; Nehemiah 13:1–3"> And David said, “I will show kindness to Hanun son of Nahash, just as his father showed kindness to me.”<br />So David sent some of his servants to console Hanun concerning his father. But when they arrived in the land of the Ammonites,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Genesis 42:16; Genesis 42:9; 1 Corinthians 13:5"> the princes of the Ammonites said to Hanun their lord, “Just because David has sent you comforters, do you really believe he is showing respect for your father? Has not David instead sent his servants to explore the city, spy it out, and overthrow it?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Isaiah 20:4; Jeremiah 41:5; 1 Chronicles 19:3–4"> So Hanun took David’s servants, shaved off half of each man’s beard, cut off their garments at the hips, and sent them away.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Chronicles 19:5; 1 Kings 16:34; Joshua 6:24–26"> When this was reported to David, he sent messengers to meet the men, since they had been thoroughly humiliated. The king told them, “Stay in Jericho until your beards have grown back, and then return.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:3; 2 Samuel 8:5; Genesis 34:30"> When the Ammonites realized that they had become a stench to David, they hired twenty thousand Aramean foot soldiers from Beth-rehob and Zoba, as well as a thousand men from the king of Maacah and twelve thousand men from Tob.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Samuel 23:8–39; 1 Chronicles 19:8–19"> On hearing this, David sent Joab and the entire army of mighty men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Samuel 10:6; Joshua 19:28; Numbers 13:21"> The Ammonites marched out and arrayed themselves for battle at the entrance of the city gate, while the Arameans of Zobah and Rehob and the men of Tob and Maacah were by themselves in the open country.</span>
</div>
</div>

## David Defeats Ammon and Aram

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Judges 20:42–43; Joshua 8:21–22"> When Joab saw the battle lines before him and behind him, he selected some of the best men of Israel and arrayed them against the Arameans.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> And he placed the rest of the troops under the command of his brother Abishai, who arrayed them against the Ammonites.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Galatians 6:2; Nehemiah 4:20; Philippians 1:27–28"> “If the Arameans are too strong for me,” said Joab, “then you will come to my rescue. And if the Ammonites are too strong for you, then I will come to your rescue.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Corinthians 16:13; Deuteronomy 31:6; 1 Samuel 3:18"> Be strong and let us fight bravely for our people and for the cities of our God. May the LORD do what is good in His sight.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Kings 20:13–21; 1 Kings 20:28–30; 1 Chronicles 19:14–15"> So Joab and his troops advanced to fight the Arameans, who fled before him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> When the Ammonites saw that the Arameans had fled, they too fled before Abishai, and they entered the city. So Joab returned from fighting against the Ammonites and came to Jerusalem.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Zechariah 14:2–3; Micah 4:11–12; Isaiah 8:9–10"> When the Arameans saw that they had been defeated by Israel, they regrouped.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Chronicles 19:16; 2 Samuel 8:3–8; 1 Chronicles 18:3"> Hadadezer sent messengers to bring more Arameans from beyond the Euphrates,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Hebrew the River">a</a></sup>and they came to Helam with Shobach<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Shobach is a variant of Shophach; see 1 Chronicles 19:16.">b</a></sup>the commander of Hadadezer’s army leading them.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Chronicles 19:17"> When this was reported to David, he gathered all Israel, crossed the Jordan, and went to Helam. Then the Arameans arrayed themselves against David and fought against him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Chronicles 19:18; 2 Samuel 8:4; Psalms 46:11"> But the Arameans fled before Israel, and David killed seven hundred charioteers and forty thousand foot soldiers.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Some LXX manuscripts (see also 1 Chronicles 19:18); Hebrew horsemen">c</a></sup>He also struck down Shobach the commander of their army, who died there.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:6; Revelation 18:10; Psalms 48:4–5"> When all the kings who were subject to Hadadezer saw that they had been defeated by Israel, they made peace with Israel and became subject to them. So the Arameans were afraid to help the Ammonites anymore.</span>
</div>
</div>
