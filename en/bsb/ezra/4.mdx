## Adversaries Hinder the Work

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Chronicles 22:9–10; Ezra 1:11; Ezra 10:16"> When the enemies of Judah and Benjamin heard that the exiles were building a temple for the LORD, the God of Israel,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Kings 19:37; 2 Kings 17:24; Galatians 2:4"> they approached Zerubbabel and the heads of the families, saying, “Let us build with you because, like you, we seek your God and have been sacrificing to Him since the time of King Esar-haddon of Assyria, who brought us here.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Nehemiah 2:20; Matthew 10:16; 2 Chronicles 36:22–3"> But Zerubbabel, Jeshua, and the other heads of the families of Israel replied, “You have no part with us in building a house for our God, since we alone must build it for the LORD, the God of Israel, as Cyrus king of Persia has commanded us.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Ezra 3:3; Nehemiah 6:9; Jeremiah 38:4"> Then the people of the land set out to discourage the people of Judah and make them afraid to build.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Nahum 1:11; Ezra 5:5–18; Psalms 2:1–2"> They hired counselors against them to frustrate their plans throughout the reign of Cyrus king of Persia and down to the reign of Darius king of Persia.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Opposition under Xerxes and Artaxerxes

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Esther 1:1; Daniel 9:1; Acts 24:5–9"> At the beginning of the reign of Xerxes,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Hebrew Ahasuerus">a</a></sup>an accusation was lodged against the people of Judah and Jerusalem.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Kings 18:26; Daniel 2:4; Isaiah 36:11"> And in the days of Artaxerxes king of Persia, Bishlam, Mithredath, Tabeel, and the rest of his associates wrote a letter to Artaxerxes. It was written in Aramaic and then translated.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="The original text of Ezra 4:8 through Ezra 6:18 is in Aramaic.">b</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Ezra 4:9; 2 Samuel 8:17; 2 Samuel 20:25"> Rehum the commander and Shimshai the scribe wrote the letter against Jerusalem to King Artaxerxes as follows:</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Ezra 5:6; Ezra 6:6; 2 Kings 17:24"> From Rehum the commander, Shimshai the scribe, and the rest of their associates—the judges and officials over Tripolis, Persia, Erech and Babylon, the Elamites of Susa,</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Ezra 4:17; Ezra 4:11; Ezra 7:12"> and the rest of the peoples whom the great and honorable Ashurbanipal<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Aramaic Osnappar, another name for Ashurbanipal">c</a></sup>deported and settled in the cities of Samaria and elsewhere west of the Euphrates.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Aramaic beyond the River; also in verses 11, 16, 17, and 20">d</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> (This is the text of the letter they sent to him.)<br />To King Artaxerxes,<br />From your servants, the men west of the Euphrates:</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Ezra 5:9; 2 Chronicles 36:13; Ezra 5:3"> Let it be known to the king that the Jews who came from you to us have returned to Jerusalem and are rebuilding that rebellious and wicked city. They are restoring its walls and repairing its foundations.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Ezra 7:24; Romans 13:6–7; Nehemiah 5:4"> Let it now be known to the king that if that city is rebuilt and its walls are restored, they will not pay tribute, duty, or toll, and the royal treasury will suffer.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="John 19:12–15; Ezekiel 33:31; John 12:5–6"> Now because we are in the service of the palace<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Literally because the salt of the palace is our salt">e</a></sup>and it is not fitting for us to allow the king to be dishonored, we have sent to inform the king</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Ezra 4:12; Esther 3:5–8; 2 Kings 24:20–1"> that a search should be made of the record books of your fathers. In these books you will discover and verify that the city is a rebellious city, harmful to kings and provinces, inciting sedition from ancient times. That is why this city was destroyed.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Ezra 4:20; 1 Kings 4:24; 2 Samuel 8:3"> We advise the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls are restored, you will have no dominion west of the Euphrates.</span>
</div>
</div>

## The Decree of Artaxerxes

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Romans 1:7; Ezra 5:7; Ezra 4:9–11"> Then the king sent this reply:<br />To Rehum the commander, Shimshai the scribe, and the rest of your associates living in Samaria and elsewhere in the region west of the Euphrates:<br />Greetings.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Nehemiah 8:8"> The letter you sent us has been translated and read in my presence.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Kings 18:7; Proverbs 25:2; Deuteronomy 13:14"> I issued a decree, and a search was conducted. It was discovered that this city has revolted against kings from ancient times, engaging in rebellion and sedition.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Genesis 15:18; 1 Kings 4:21; 1 Chronicles 18:3"> And mighty kings have ruled over Jerusalem and exercised authority over the whole region west of the Euphrates; and tribute, duty, and toll were paid to them.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> Now, therefore, issue an order for these men to stop, so that this city will not be rebuilt until I so order.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Esther 7:3–4; Esther 3:8–9; Ezra 4:13"> See that you do not neglect this matter. Why allow this threat to increase and the royal interests to suffer?</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Romans 3:15; Micah 2:1; Ezra 4:17"> When the text of the letter from King Artaxerxes was read to Rehum, Shimshai the scribe, and their associates, they went immediately to the Jews in Jerusalem and forcibly stopped them.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Haggai 1:15; Nehemiah 6:9; Ezra 5:5"> Thus the construction of the house of God in Jerusalem ceased, and it remained at a standstill until the second year of the reign of Darius king of Persia.</span>
</div>
</div>

</div>
