## Solomon’s Palace Complex

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Kings 9:10; 2 Chronicles 8:1; Ecclesiastes 2:4–5"> Solomon, however, took thirteen years to complete the construction of his entire palace.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Kings 10:17; 2 Chronicles 9:16; 1 Kings 10:21"> He built the House of the Forest of Lebanon a hundred cubits long, fifty cubits wide, and thirty cubits high,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="The house was approximately 150 feet long, 75 feet wide, and 45 feet high (45.7 meters long, 22.9 meters wide, and 13.7 meters high).">a</a></sup>with four rows of cedar pillars supporting the cedar beams.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Kings 6:5"> The house was roofed with cedar above the beams that rested on the pillars—forty-five beams, fifteen per row.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Ezekiel 40:22; Ezekiel 40:16; 1 Kings 6:4"> There were three rows of high windows facing one another in three tiers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> All the doorways<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Literally doorways and doorposts">b</a></sup>had rectangular frames, with the openings facing one another in three tiers.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Kings 7:12; Ezekiel 41:25–26"> Solomon made his colonnade fifty cubits long and thirty cubits wide,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="The colonnade was approximately 75 feet long and 45 feet wide (22.9 meters long and 13.7 meters wide).">c</a></sup>with a portico in front of it and a canopy with pillars in front of the portico.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Proverbs 20:8; Psalms 122:5; Isaiah 9:7"> In addition, he built a hall for the throne, the Hall of Justice, where he was to judge. It was paneled with cedar from floor to ceiling.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Syriac and Vulgate; Hebrew from floor to floor">d</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Chronicles 8:11; 1 Kings 3:1; 1 Kings 9:24"> And the palace where Solomon would live, set further back, was of similar construction. He also made a palace like this hall for Pharaoh’s daughter, whom he had married.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Kings 7:10–11; 1 Kings 5:17"> All these buildings were constructed with costly stones, cut to size and trimmed with saws inside and out from the foundation to the eaves, and from the outside to the great courtyard.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Revelation 21:19–20; 1 Corinthians 3:10–11; Isaiah 54:11"> The foundations were laid with large, costly stones, some ten cubits long<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="10 cubits is approximately 15 feet or 4.6 meters.">e</a></sup>and some eight cubits long.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="8 cubits is approximately 12 feet or 3.7 meters.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ephesians 2:20–22; 1 Peter 2:5"> Above these were costly stones, cut to size, and cedar beams.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Kings 6:36; 1 Kings 7:6; John 10:23"> The great courtyard was surrounded by three rows of dressed stone and a row of trimmed cedar beams, as were the inner courtyard and portico of the house of the LORD.</span>
</div>
</div>

## The Pillars and Capitals

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:11; 2 Chronicles 2:13–14; 1 Kings 7:40"> Now King Solomon sent to bring Huram<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Hebrew Hiram, a variant of Huram; also in verses 40 and 45; see 2 Chronicles 4:11. Note that this is not Hiram king of Tyre mentioned in 1 Kings 5:1.">g</a></sup>from Tyre.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Chronicles 2:14; 2 Chronicles 4:16; Exodus 31:2–6"> He was the son of a widow from the tribe of Naphtali, and his father was a man of Tyre, a craftsman in bronze. Huram had great skill, understanding, and knowledge for every kind of bronze work. So he came to King Solomon and carried out all his work.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:21; 2 Chronicles 3:15–17; 1 Kings 7:21"> He cast two pillars of bronze, each eighteen cubits high and twelve cubits in circumference.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Each pillar was approximately 27 feet high and 18 feet in circumference (8.2 meters high and 5.5 meters in circumference).">h</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Exodus 38:19; 2 Chronicles 4:12–13; Exodus 38:17"> He also made two capitals of cast bronze to set on top of the pillars, each capital five cubits high.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="5 cubits is approximately 7.5 feet or 2.3 meters.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Exodus 28:14; Exodus 28:24–25; Exodus 28:22"> For the capitals on top of the pillars he made a network of lattice, with wreaths of chainwork, seven for each capital.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Hebrew; LXX one for each capital">j</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Exodus 28:14; Exodus 39:15–18; Exodus 28:22"> Likewise, he made the pillars with two rows of pomegranates around each grating to cover each capital atop the pillars.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 Kings 7:22; 1 Kings 6:32–35; 1 Kings 6:18"> And the capitals atop the pillars in the portico were shaped like lilies, four cubits high.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="4 cubits is approximately 6 feet or 1.8 meters; also in verse 38.">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Chronicles 3:16; 2 Chronicles 4:13; Jeremiah 52:22–23"> On the capitals of both pillars, just above the rounded projection next to the network, were the two hundred pomegranates in rows encircling each capital.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Chronicles 3:17; 1 Kings 6:3; 1 Kings 7:12"> Thus he set up the pillars at the portico of the temple. The pillar to the south he named Jachin,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Jachin probably means He establishes.">l</a></sup>and the pillar to the north he named Boaz.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Boaz probably means in Him is strength.">m</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> And the tops of the pillars were shaped like lilies. So the work of the pillars was completed.</span>
</div>
</div>

## The Molten Sea

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:2; 2 Kings 25:13; Jeremiah 52:17"> He also made the Sea of cast metal. It was circular in shape, measuring ten cubits from rim to rim, five cubits in height, and thirty cubits in circumference.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="The Sea was approximately 15 feet from rim to rim, 7.5 feet in height, and 45 feet in circumference (4.6 meters from rim to rim, 2.3 meters in height, and 13.7 meters in circumference).">n</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Kings 6:18; 2 Chronicles 4:3; Exodus 37:17–22"> Below the rim, ornamental buds encircled it, ten per cubit all the way around the Sea, cast in two rows as a part of the Sea.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:20; 2 Chronicles 4:4–5; Matthew 28:19"> The Sea stood on twelve oxen, three facing north, three facing west, three facing south, and three facing east. The Sea rested on them, with all their hindquarters toward the center.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:5; 1 Kings 7:38; 1 Kings 6:18"> It was a handbreadth thick,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="A handbreadth is approximately 2.9 inches or 7.4 centimeters.">o</a></sup>and its rim was fashioned like the brim of a cup, like a lily blossom. It could hold two thousand baths.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="2,000 baths is approximately 11,600 gallons or 44,000 liters; LXX does not include this sentence.">p</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## The Ten Bronze Stands

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:14; 2 Kings 25:13; Jeremiah 52:17"> In addition, he made ten movable stands of bronze, each four cubits long, four cubits wide, and three cubits high.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="The stands were approximately 6 feet in length and width, and 4.5 feet high (1.8 meters in length and width, and 1.4 meters high).">q</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> This was the design of the stands: They had side panels attached to uprights,</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Hosea 5:14; Ezekiel 10:14; Exodus 37:7"> and on the panels between the uprights were lions, oxen, and cherubim. On the uprights was a pedestal above, and below the lions and oxen were wreaths of beveled work.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Ezekiel 10:10–13; Ezekiel 3:13; Ezekiel 1:15–21"> Each stand had four bronze wheels with bronze axles and a basin resting on four supports, with wreaths at each side.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31"> The opening to each stand inside the crown at the top was one cubit deep,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="One cubit is approximately 18 inches or 45.7 centimeters.">r</a></sup>with a round opening like the design of a pedestal, a cubit and a half wide.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-s" id="fnref-s" data-fn="A cubit and a half is approximately 2.25 feet or 68.6 centimeters wide; similarly in verse 32.">s</a></sup>And around its opening were engravings, but the panels of the stands were square, not round.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32"> There were four wheels under the panels, and the axles of the wheels were attached to the stand; each wheel was a cubit and a half in diameter.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Ezekiel 1:18; Ezekiel 1:16"> The wheels were made like chariot wheels; their axles, rims, spokes, and hubs were all of cast metal.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34"> Each stand had four handles, one for each corner, projecting from the stand.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35"> At the top of each stand was a circular band half a cubit high.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-t" id="fnref-t" data-fn="Half a cubit is approximately 9 inches or 22.9 centimeters high.">t</a></sup>The supports and panels were cast as a unit with the top of the stand.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="1 Kings 7:29; 1 Kings 6:29; Ezekiel 41:18–20"> He engraved cherubim, lions, and palm trees on the surfaces of the supports and panels, wherever each had space, with wreaths all around.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37"> In this way he made the ten stands, each with the same casting, dimensions, and shape.</span>
</div>
</div>

## The Ten Bronze Basins

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="Exodus 40:11–12; Zechariah 13:1; 1 John 1:7"> He also made ten bronze basins, each holding forty baths<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-u" id="fnref-u" data-fn="40 baths is approximately 232 gallons or 880 liters.">u</a></sup>and measuring four cubits across, one basin for each of the ten stands.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:10; 2 Chronicles 4:6"> He set five stands on the south side of the temple and five on the north, and he put the Sea on the south side, at the southeast corner of the temple.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Completion of the Bronze Works

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:11–16; Exodus 24:6; Exodus 39:32–43"> Additionally, Huram made the pots,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-v" id="fnref-v" data-fn="Many Hebrew manuscripts, LXX, Syriac, and Vulgate (see also verse 45 and 2 Chronicles 4:11); many other Hebrew manuscripts basins">v</a></sup>shovels, and sprinkling bowls.<br />So Huram finished all the work that he had undertaken for King Solomon in the house of the LORD:</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="1 Kings 7:15–22; 2 Chronicles 4:12"> the two pillars;<br />the two bowl-shaped capitals atop the pillars;<br />the two sets of network covering both bowls of the capitals atop the pillars;</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="1 Kings 7:20"> the four hundred pomegranates for the two sets of network (two rows of pomegranates for each network covering both the bowl-shaped capitals atop the pillars);</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43"> the ten stands;<br />the ten basins on the stands;</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44" data-xref="1 Kings 7:23; 1 Kings 7:25"> the Sea;<br />the twelve oxen underneath the Sea;</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v45" data-v="45" data-xref="Exodus 27:3; Exodus 38:3; 2 Chronicles 4:16"> and the pots, shovels, and sprinkling bowls.<br />All the articles that Huram made for King Solomon in the house of the LORD were made of burnished bronze.</span>
<span className="v" id="v46" data-v="46" data-xref="Joshua 3:16; 2 Chronicles 4:17; Genesis 33:17"> The king had them cast in clay molds in the plain of the Jordan between Succoth and Zarethan.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-w" id="fnref-w" data-fn="Zarethan is a variant of Zeredah; see 2 Chronicles 4:17.">w</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v47" data-v="47" data-xref="1 Chronicles 22:14; 1 Chronicles 22:3; 2 Chronicles 4:18"> Solomon left all these articles unweighed, because there were so many. The weight of the bronze could not be determined.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Completion of the Gold Furnishings

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v48" data-v="48" data-xref="Exodus 37:10–16; 2 Chronicles 4:8; Exodus 39:36"> Solomon also made all the furnishings for the house of the LORD:<br />the golden altar;<br />the golden table on which was placed the Bread of the Presence;</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v49" data-v="49" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:7; 2 Chronicles 4:20; Zechariah 4:11–14"> the lampstands of pure gold in front of the inner sanctuary, five on the right side and five on the left;<br />the gold flowers, lamps, and tongs;</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v50" data-v="50" data-xref="Exodus 25:29; 2 Chronicles 4:21–22; Exodus 27:3"> the pure gold basins, wick trimmers, sprinkling bowls, ladles, and censers;<br />and the gold hinges for the doors of the inner temple (that is, the Most Holy Place<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-x" id="fnref-x" data-fn="Or the Holy of Holies">x</a></sup>) as well as for the doors of the main hall of the temple.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v51" data-v="51" data-xref="2 Chronicles 5:1; 2 Samuel 8:7–11; 1 Chronicles 28:11–18"> So all the work that King Solomon had performed for the house of the LORD was completed.<br />Then Solomon brought in the items his father David had dedicated—the silver, the gold, and the furnishings—and he placed them in the treasuries of the house of the LORD.</span>
</div>
