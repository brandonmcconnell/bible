## The Queen of Sheba

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Matthew 12:42; Luke 11:31; 2 Chronicles 9:1–12"> Now when the queen of Sheba heard about the fame of Solomon concerning the name of the LORD, she came to test him with difficult questions.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Acts 25:23; Psalms 4:4; Job 4:2"> She arrived in Jerusalem with a very large caravan—with camels bearing spices, gold in great abundance, and precious stones.<br />And she came to Solomon and spoke to him all that was on her mind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Proverbs 1:5–6; Matthew 13:11; 1 Kings 10:1"> And Solomon answered all her questions; nothing was too difficult for the king to explain.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Matthew 12:42; 1 Kings 3:28; 2 Chronicles 9:3–4"> When the queen of Sheba saw all the wisdom of Solomon, the palace he had built,</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Chronicles 26:16; 1 Kings 4:22–23; 2 Kings 16:18"> the food at his table, the seating of his servants, the service and attire of his attendants, his cupbearers, and the burnt offerings he presented at the house of the LORD, it took her breath away.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Chronicles 9:5–6"> She said to the king, “The report I heard in my own country about your words and wisdom is true.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Corinthians 2:9; John 20:25–29; 1 John 3:2"> But I did not believe these things until I came and saw with my own eyes. Indeed, not even half was told to me. Your wisdom and prosperity have far exceeded the report I heard.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Proverbs 8:34; Proverbs 10:21; Luke 11:31"> How blessed are your men!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="LXX, Syriac, and Vulgate your wives">a</a></sup>How blessed are these servants of yours who stand continually before you and hear your wisdom!</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Psalms 72:2; 1 Kings 5:7; 2 Samuel 8:15"> Blessed be the LORD your God, who has delighted in you to set you on the throne of Israel. Because of the LORD’s eternal love for Israel, He has made you king to carry out justice and righteousness.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Kings 10:2; Matthew 2:11; 1 Kings 9:14"> Then she gave the king 120 talents of gold,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="120 talents is approximately 4.52 tons or 4.1 metric tons of gold.">b</a></sup>a great quantity of spices, and precious stones. Never again were spices in such abundance brought in as those the queen of Sheba gave to King Solomon.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Kings 9:27–28; 2 Chronicles 8:18; Psalms 45:9"> (The fleet of Hiram that brought gold from Ophir also brought from Ophir a great cargo of almug<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Almug is probably a variant of algum; also in verse 12; see 2 Chronicles 2:8.">c</a></sup>wood and precious stones.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Chronicles 25:1–31; Psalms 150:3–5; Revelation 14:2–3"> The king made the almug wood into steps for the house of the LORD and for the king’s palace, and into lyres and harps for the singers. Never before had such almug wood been brought in, nor has such been seen again to this day.)</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Psalms 37:4; Matthew 15:28; Psalms 20:4"> King Solomon gave the queen of Sheba all she desired—whatever she asked—besides what he had given her out of his royal bounty. Then she left and returned to her own country, along with her servants.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Solomon’s Wealth and Splendor

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Chronicles 9:13–28; 1 Kings 9:28"> The weight of gold that came to Solomon each year was 666 talents,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="666 talents is approximately 25.1 tons or 22.8 metric tons of gold.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Psalms 72:10; 1 Chronicles 9:24; Isaiah 21:13"> not including the revenue from the merchants, traders, and all the Arabian kings and governors of the land.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Kings 14:26–28; 2 Chronicles 12:9–10; 2 Chronicles 9:15–16"> King Solomon made two hundred large shields of hammered gold; six hundred shekels of gold<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="600 shekels is approximately 15.1 pounds or 6.8 kilograms of gold.">e</a></sup>went into each shield.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Kings 7:2; 1 Kings 14:26"> He also made three hundred small shields of hammered gold; three minas of gold<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="3 minas is approximately 3.77 pounds or 1.71 kilograms of gold; possibly a reference to double minas, that is, approximately 7.54 pounds or 3.42 kilograms.">f</a></sup>went into each shield. And the king put them in the House of the Forest of Lebanon.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Psalms 45:8; 1 Kings 10:22; Revelation 20:11"> Additionally, the king made a great throne of ivory and overlaid it with pure gold.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> The throne had six steps, and its back had a rounded top. There were armrests on both sides of the seat, with a lion standing beside each armrest.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Numbers 24:9; Genesis 49:9; Numbers 23:24"> Twelve lions stood on the six steps, one at either end of each step. Nothing like this had ever been made for any kingdom.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Kings 10:17; 1 Kings 7:2; 2 Chronicles 9:20–22"> All King Solomon’s drinking cups were gold, and all the utensils of the House of the Forest of Lebanon were pure gold. There was no silver, because it was accounted as nothing in the days of Solomon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="1 Kings 22:48; Genesis 10:4; 2 Chronicles 20:36–37"> For the king had the ships of Tarshish<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Or a fleet of trading ships; twice in this verse">g</a></sup>at sea with Hiram’s fleet, and once every three years the ships of Tarshish would arrive bearing gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Or baboons">h</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Kings 3:12–13; Colossians 2:2–3; 1 Kings 4:29–34"> So King Solomon surpassed all the kings of the earth in riches and wisdom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Kings 3:12; James 1:5; 1 Kings 3:28"> The whole world sought an audience with Solomon to hear the wisdom that God had put in his heart.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Job 42:11; Psalms 72:10; Psalms 72:15"> Year after year, each visitor would bring his tribute: articles of silver and gold, clothing, weapons, spices, horses, and mules.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Chronicles 9:25; 1 Kings 4:26; Deuteronomy 17:16"> Solomon accumulated<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Literally Solomon accumulated chariots and horses; he had">i</a></sup>1,400 chariots and 12,000 horses,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Or horsemen or charioteers">j</a></sup>which he stationed in the chariot cities and also with him in Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 Chronicles 1:15–17; Deuteronomy 17:17; 2 Chronicles 9:27"> The king made silver as common in Jerusalem as stones, and cedar as abundant as sycamore in the foothills.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Hebrew Shephelah or lowlands; that is, the western foothills of Judea">k</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Deuteronomy 17:16; 2 Chronicles 9:28; Isaiah 31:1–3"> Solomon’s horses were imported from Egypt and Kue;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Probably an area in Cilicia, a province in the southeast of Asia Minor">l</a></sup>the royal merchants purchased them from Kue.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Joshua 1:4; 2 Kings 7:6–7; Malachi 1:1"> A chariot could be imported from Egypt for six hundred shekels of silver,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="600 shekels is approximately 15.1 pounds or 6.8 kilograms of silver.">m</a></sup>and a horse for a hundred and fifty.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="150 shekels is approximately 3.8 pounds or 1.7 kilograms of silver.">n</a></sup>Likewise, they exported them to all the kings of the Hittites and to the kings of Aram.</span>
</div>
</div>

</div>
