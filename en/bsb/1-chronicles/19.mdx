## David’s Messengers Disgraced

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Samuel 11:1–2; 2 Samuel 10:1–19; 1 Samuel 12:12"> Some time later, Nahash king of the Ammonites died and was succeeded by his son.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Deuteronomy 23:3–6; 1 Samuel 30:26; Genesis 19:37–38"> And David said, “I will show kindness to Hanun son of Nahash, because his father showed kindness to me.”<br />So David sent messengers to console Hanun concerning his father. But when David’s servants arrived in the land of the Ammonites to console him,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Judges 1:23–24; 1 Corinthians 13:5–7; 1 Kings 12:8–11"> the princes of the Ammonites said to Hanun, “Just because David has sent you comforters, do you really believe he is showing respect for your father? Have not his servants come to you to explore the land, spy it out, and overthrow it?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Samuel 10:4–5; Isaiah 15:2; Mark 12:4"> So Hanun took David’s servants, shaved their beards, cut off their garments at the hips, and sent them away.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Joshua 6:24–26; Matthew 18:31; 1 Kings 16:34"> When someone came and told David about his men, he sent messengers to meet them, since the men had been thoroughly humiliated. The king told them, “Stay in Jericho until your beards have grown back, and then return.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Chronicles 18:5; Genesis 34:30; 1 Chronicles 18:9"> When the Ammonites realized that they had become a stench to David, Hanun and the Ammonites sent a thousand talents of silver<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="1,000 talents is approximately 37.7 tons or 34.2 metric tons of silver.">a</a></sup>to hire for themselves chariots and horsemen from Aram-naharaim,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="That is, Mesopotamia; Aram-naharaim means Aram of the two rivers, likely the region between the Euphrates and Balih Rivers in northwestern Mesopotamia.">b</a></sup>Aram-maacah, and Zobah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Numbers 21:30; Joshua 13:9; Joshua 13:16"> So they hired for themselves thirty-two thousand chariots, as well as the king of Maacah with his troops, who came and camped near Medeba while the Ammonites were mustered from their cities and marched out for battle.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Chronicles 11:6; 1 Chronicles 11:10–47; 2 Samuel 23:8–39"> On hearing this, David sent Joab and the entire army of mighty men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Kings 20:1; 1 Kings 20:24; Isaiah 28:6"> The Ammonites marched out and arrayed themselves for battle at the entrance to the city, while the kings who had come stayed by themselves in the open country.</span>
</div>
</div>

## David Defeats Ammon and Aram

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Samuel 10:9–14; Judges 20:42–43; Joshua 8:22"> When Joab saw the battle lines before him and behind him, he selected some of the best men of Israel and arrayed them against the Arameans.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Chronicles 18:12; 1 Chronicles 11:20; 1 Chronicles 19:9"> And he placed the rest of the troops under the command of his brother Abishai, who arrayed them against the Ammonites.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Philippians 1:27–28; Nehemiah 4:20; Ecclesiastes 4:9–12"> “If the Arameans are too strong for me,” said Joab, “then you will come to my rescue. And if the Ammonites are too strong for you, then I will come to your rescue.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Nehemiah 4:14; Psalms 27:14; 2 Samuel 10:12"> Be strong and let us fight bravely for our people and for the cities of our God. May the LORD do what is good in His sight.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="1 Kings 20:19–21; 2 Chronicles 13:5–16; Jeremiah 46:15–16"> So Joab and his troops advanced to fight the Arameans, who fled before him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Romans 8:31; Leviticus 26:7"> When the Ammonites saw that the Arameans had fled, they too fled before Joab’s brother Abishai, and they entered the city. So Joab went back to Jerusalem.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Samuel 10:16; Zechariah 14:1–3; Micah 4:11–12"> When the Arameans saw that they had been defeated by Israel, they sent messengers to bring more Arameans from beyond the Euphrates,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Hebrew the River">c</a></sup>with Shophach<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Shophach is a variant of Shobach; also in verse 18; see 2 Samuel 10:16.">d</a></sup>the commander of Hadadezer’s army leading them.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Isaiah 22:6–7; 1 Chronicles 19:9"> When this was reported to David, he gathered all Israel, crossed the Jordan, advanced toward the Arameans, and arrayed for battle against them. When David lined up to engage them in battle, they fought against him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="2 Samuel 10:18; Psalms 33:16; Psalms 18:32"> But the Arameans fled before Israel, and David killed seven thousand of their charioteers and forty thousand foot soldiers. He also killed Shophach the commander of their army.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Samuel 10:19; Joshua 9:9–11; 1 Kings 20:1"> When Hadadezer’s subjects saw that they had been defeated by Israel, they made peace with David and became subject to him. So the Arameans were unwilling to help the Ammonites anymore.</span>
</div>
</div>
