## Melchizedek and Abraham

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Genesis 14:18–20; Psalms 57:2; Hebrews 6:20"> This Melchizedek was king of Salem and priest of God Most High.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Genesis 14:18">a</a></sup>He met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Numbers 18:21; Psalms 85:10–11; 1 Samuel 8:17"> and Abraham apportioned to him a tenth of everything. First, his name means “king of righteousness.” Then also, “king of Salem” means “king of peace.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Hebrews 7:17; Hebrews 7:23–28; Exodus 6:20–27"> Without father or mother or genealogy, without beginning of days or end of life, like the Son of God, he remains a priest for all time.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Genesis 14:20; Acts 2:29; Acts 7:8–9"> Consider how great Melchizedek was: Even the patriarch Abraham gave him a tenth of the plunder.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Leviticus 27:30–33; 2 Chronicles 31:4–6; Numbers 18:21–32"> Now the law commands the sons of Levi who become priests to collect a tenth from the people—that is, from their brothers—though they too are descended from Abraham.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Romans 4:13; Hebrews 7:3–4; Genesis 13:14–17"> But Melchizedek, who did not trace his descent from Levi, collected a tenth from Abraham and blessed him who had the promises.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Chronicles 30:27; Genesis 48:15–20; 1 Timothy 3:16"> And indisputably, the lesser is blessed by the greater.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Hebrews 6:20; Hebrews 5:6; Revelation 1:18"> In the case of the Levites, mortal men collect the tenth; but in the case of Melchizedek, it is affirmed that he lives on.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Romans 5:12; Genesis 14:20; Hebrews 7:4"> And so to speak, Levi, who collects the tenth, paid the tenth through Abraham.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Genesis 35:11; Hebrews 7:5; Genesis 46:26"> For when Melchizedek met Abraham, Levi was still in the loin of his ancestor.</span>
</div>
</div>

## A Superior Priesthood

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Hebrews 8:7; Galatians 2:21; Galatians 4:9"> Now if perfection could have been attained through the Levitical priesthood (for on this basis the people received the law), why was there still need for another priest to appear—one in the order of Melchizedek and not in the order of Aaron?</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Jeremiah 31:31–34; Isaiah 66:21; Ezekiel 16:61"> For when the priesthood is changed, the law must be changed as well.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Hebrews 7:11; Hebrews 7:14; Numbers 17:5"> He of whom these things are said belonged to a different tribe, from which no one has ever served at the altar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Micah 5:2; Isaiah 11:1; Luke 3:33"> For it is clear that our Lord descended from Judah, a tribe as to which Moses said nothing about priests.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Psalms 110:4; Hebrews 7:17–21; Hebrews 7:3"> And this point is even more clear if another priest like Melchizedek appears,</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Revelation 1:18; Colossians 2:20; Hebrews 7:28"> one who has become a priest not by a law of succession, but by the power of an indestructible life.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Psalms 110:4; Hebrews 5:6; Hebrews 7:21"> For it is testified:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“You are a priest forever</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">in the order of Melchizedek.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Psalms 110:4">b</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Romans 8:3; Acts 13:39; Galatians 4:9"> So the former commandment is set aside because it was weak and useless</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Acts 13:39; Hebrews 4:16; Hebrews 9:9"> (for the law made nothing perfect), and a better hope is introduced, by which we draw near to God.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Numbers 18:7; Hebrews 5:6; Psalms 110:4"> And none of this happened without an oath. For others became priests without an oath,</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Psalms 110:4; Hebrews 7:17; Hebrews 6:16–18"> but Jesus became a priest with an oath by the One who said to Him:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“The Lord has sworn and will not change His mind:</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">‘You are a priest forever.’”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Psalms 110:4">c</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="1 Corinthians 11:25; Genesis 44:32; Hebrews 8:6–12"> Because of this oath, Jesus has become the guarantee of a better covenant.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Nehemiah 12:10–11; 1 Chronicles 6:3–14; Hebrews 7:8"> Now there have been many other priests, since death prevented them from continuing in office.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="John 12:34; Hebrews 7:28; Isaiah 9:6–7"> But because Jesus lives forever, He has a permanent priesthood.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Romans 8:34; 1 John 2:1–2; 1 Timothy 2:5"> Therefore He is able to save completely<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Or forever">d</a></sup>those who draw near to God through Him, since He always lives to intercede for them.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Corinthians 5:21; Hebrews 4:14–15; 1 Peter 2:22"> Such a high priest truly befits us—One who is holy, innocent, undefiled, set apart from sinners, and exalted above the heavens.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Hebrews 9:28; Hebrews 9:12; Hebrews 9:14"> Unlike the other high priests, He does not need to offer daily sacrifices, first for His own sins and then for the sins of the people; He sacrificed for sin once for all when He offered up Himself.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Hebrews 2:10; Hebrews 7:21; Hebrews 1:2"> For the law appoints as high priests men who are weak; but the oath, which came after the law, appointed the Son, who has been made perfect forever.</span>
</div>
</div>
