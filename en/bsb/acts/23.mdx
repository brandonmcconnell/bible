## Paul before the Sanhedrin

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 24:16; 2 Timothy 1:3; 2 Corinthians 1:12"> Paul looked directly at the Sanhedrin<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Or the Council; also in verses 6, 15, 20, and 28">a</a></sup>and said, “Brothers, I have conducted myself before God in all good conscience to this day.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="John 18:22; Acts 24:1; Micah 5:1"> At this, the high priest Ananias ordered those standing near Paul to strike him on the mouth.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Deuteronomy 25:1–2; John 7:51; Matthew 23:27–28"> Then Paul said to him, “God will strike you, you whitewashed wall! You sit here to judge me according to the law, yet you yourself violate the law by commanding that I be struck.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> But those standing nearby said, “How dare you insult the high priest of God!”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Exodus 22:28; Ecclesiastes 10:20; Jude 1:8–9"> “Brothers,” Paul replied, “I was not aware that he was the high priest, for it is written: ‘Do not speak evil about the ruler of your people.’<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Exodus 22:28 (see also LXX)">b</a></sup>”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 24:15; Acts 24:21; Acts 26:5–8"> Then Paul, knowing that some of them were Sadducees and the others Pharisees, called out in the Sanhedrin, “Brothers, I am a Pharisee, the son of a Pharisee. It is because of my hope in the resurrection of the dead that I am on trial.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Psalms 55:9; John 7:40–43; Matthew 10:34"> As soon as he had said this, a dispute broke out between the Pharisees and Sadducees, and the assembly was divided.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Luke 20:27; Matthew 22:23; Mark 12:18"> For the Sadducees say that there is neither a resurrection nor angels nor spirits, but the Pharisees acknowledge them all.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 22:7; Acts 22:17–18; John 12:29"> A great clamor arose, and some scribes from the party of the Pharisees got up and contended sharply, “We find nothing wrong with this man. What if a spirit or an angel has spoken to him?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="James 1:19; James 3:14–2; Acts 22:24"> The dispute grew so violent that the commander was afraid they would tear Paul to pieces. He ordered the soldiers to go down and remove him by force and bring him into the barracks.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 18:9; Acts 19:21; 2 Timothy 4:17"> The following night the Lord stood near Paul and said, “Take courage! As you have testified about Me in Jerusalem, so also you must testify in Rome.”</span>
</div>
</div>

## The Plot to Kill Paul

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 23:30; Isaiah 8:9–10; Acts 23:21"> When daylight came, the Jews formed a conspiracy and bound themselves with an oath not to eat or drink until they had killed Paul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="John 16:2; 2 Samuel 15:31; 2 Samuel 15:12"> More than forty of them were involved in this plot.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Acts 23:12; Jeremiah 8:12; Isaiah 3:9"> They went to the chief priests and elders and said, “We have bound ourselves with a solemn oath not to eat anything until we have killed Paul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Isaiah 59:7; Psalms 37:32–33; Proverbs 1:16"> Now then, you and the Sanhedrin petition the commander to bring him down to you on the pretext of examining his case more carefully. We are ready to kill him on the way.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Acts 23:10; 1 Corinthians 3:19; Acts 23:32"> But when the son of Paul’s sister heard about the plot,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Or the ambush">c</a></sup>he went into the barracks and told Paul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Proverbs 22:3; Matthew 8:8–9; Matthew 10:16"> Then Paul called one of the centurions and said, “Take this young man to the commander; he has something to tell him.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Ephesians 3:1; Ephesians 4:1; Acts 28:17"> So the centurion took him to the commander and said, “Paul the prisoner sent and asked me to bring this young man to you. He has something to tell you.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Jeremiah 31:32; Esther 9:12; Esther 7:2"> The commander took the young man by the hand, drew him aside, and asked, “What do you need to tell me?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Acts 23:14–15; Acts 23:1; Psalms 12:2"> He answered, “The Jews have agreed to ask you to bring Paul to the Sanhedrin tomorrow on the pretext of acquiring more information about him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Acts 23:12–14; Acts 14:5–6; 2 Corinthians 11:26"> Do not let them persuade you, because more than forty men are waiting to ambush him. They have bound themselves with an oath not to eat or drink until they have killed him; they are ready now, awaiting your consent.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Mark 1:44; Joshua 2:14"> So the commander dismissed the young man and instructed him, “Do not tell anyone that you have reported this to me.”</span>
</div>
</div>

## Paul Sent to Felix

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Matthew 14:25; Acts 23:33; Luke 12:38"> Then he called two of his centurions and said, “Prepare two hundred soldiers, seventy horsemen, and two hundred spearmen to go to Caesarea in the third hour of the night.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="That is, at nine tonight">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 25:14; Acts 23:26; Acts 24:10"> Provide mounts for Paul to take him safely to Governor Felix.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> And he wrote the following letter:</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 15:23; Acts 26:25; Luke 1:3"> Claudius Lysias,<br />To His Excellency, Governor Felix:<br />Greetings.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Acts 22:25–29; Acts 21:31–33; Acts 24:6"> This man was seized by the Jews, and they were about to kill him when I came with my troops to rescue him. For I had learned that he is a Roman citizen,</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Acts 22:30"> and since I wanted to understand their charges against him, I brought him down to their Sanhedrin.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Acts 26:31; Acts 25:25; Acts 18:15"> I found that the accusation involved questions about their own law, but there was no charge worthy of death or imprisonment.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Acts 23:35; Acts 25:16; Acts 25:5–6"> When I was informed that there was a plot against the man, I sent him to you at once. I also instructed his accusers to present their case against him before you.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 Timothy 2:3–4; Luke 7:8; Acts 23:23–24"> So the soldiers followed their orders and brought Paul by night to Antipatris.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Acts 23:23; Acts 23:10"> The next day they returned to the barracks and let the horsemen go on with him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Acts 23:26; Acts 23:23–24; Acts 28:16"> When the horsemen arrived in Caesarea, they delivered the letter to the governor and presented Paul to him.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Acts 21:39; Acts 25:1; Daniel 2:49"> The governor read the letter and asked what province Paul was from. Learning that he was from Cilicia,</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Acts 23:30; Acts 25:16; Matthew 27:27"> he said, “I will hear your case when your accusers arrive.” Then he ordered that Paul be kept under guard in Herod’s Praetorium.</span>
</div>
</div>
