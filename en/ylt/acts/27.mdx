<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 25:25; Acts 25:12; Acts 10:1"> And when our sailing to Italy was determined, they were delivering up both Paul and certain others, prisoners, to a centurion, by name Julius, of the band of Sebastus,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Acts 19:29; Philemon 1:24; Colossians 4:10"> and having embarked in a ship of Adramyttium, we, being about to sail by the coasts of Asia, did set sail, there being with us Aristarchus, a Macedonian of Thessalonica,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Acts 24:23; Acts 28:16; Acts 27:43"> on the next &#91;day&#93; also we touched at Sidon, and Julius, courteously treating Paul, did permit &#91;him&#93;, having gone on unto friends, to receive &#91;their&#93; care.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Acts 4:36; Acts 15:39; Acts 13:4"> And thence, having set sail, we sailed under Cyprus, because of the winds being contrary,</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Acts 6:9; Acts 13:13; Acts 15:41"> and having sailed over the sea over-against Cilicia and Pamphylia, we came to Myria of Lycia,</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 28:11; Acts 27:1; Acts 6:9"> and there the centurion having found a ship of Alexandria, sailing to Italy, did put us into it,</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Acts 27:21; Titus 1:5; Titus 1:12"> and having sailed slowly many days, and with difficulty coming over-against Cnidus, the wind not suffering us, we sailed under Crete, over-against Salmone,</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> and hardly passing it, we came to a certain place called 'Fair Havens,' nigh to which was the city &#91;of&#93; Lasaea.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Numbers 29:7; Leviticus 23:27–29; Leviticus 16:29–31"> And much time being spent, and the sailing being now dangerous — because of the fast also being already past — Paul was admonishing,</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Daniel 2:30; Acts 27:20–26; Acts 27:41–44"> saying to them, 'Men, I perceive that with hurt, and much damage, not only of the lading and of the ship, but also of our lives — the voyage is about to be;'</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ezekiel 33:4; Hebrews 11:7; Ezekiel 3:17–18"> but the centurion to the pilot and to the shipowner gave credence more than to the things spoken by Paul;</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Psalms 107:30; Acts 27:8"> and the haven being incommodious to winter in, the more part gave counsel to sail thence also, if by any means they might be able, having attained to Phenice, &#91;there&#93; to winter, &#91;which is&#93; a haven of Crete, looking to the south-west and north-west,</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Luke 12:55; Song of Solomon 4:16; Job 37:17"> and a south wind blowing softly, having thought they had obtained &#91;their&#93; purpose, having lifted anchor, they sailed close by Crete,</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Mark 4:37; Psalms 107:25–27; Exodus 14:21–27"> and not long after there arose against it a tempestuous wind, that is called Euroclydon,</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="James 3:4; Acts 27:27"> and the ship being caught, and not being able to bear up against the wind, having given &#91;her&#93; up, we were borne on,</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> and having run under a certain little isle, called Clauda, we were hardly able to become masters of the boat,</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Acts 27:29; Acts 27:26; Acts 27:41"> which having taken up, they were using helps, undergirding the ship, and fearing lest they may fall on the quicksand, having let down the mast — so were borne on.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Jonah 1:5; Acts 27:38; Matthew 16:26"> And we, being exceedingly tempest-tossed, the succeeding &#91;day&#93; they were making a clearing,</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Job 2:4; Luke 9:24–25; Mark 8:35–37"> and on the third &#91;day&#93; with our own hands the tackling of the ship we cast out,</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Matthew 24:29; Matthew 8:24–25; Jeremiah 2:25"> and neither sun nor stars appearing for more days, and not a little tempest lying upon us, thenceforth all hope was taken away of our being saved.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Genesis 42:22; Psalms 107:5–6; Acts 27:7"> And there having been long fasting, then Paul having stood in the midst of them, said, 'It behoved &#91;you&#93;, indeed, O men — having hearkened to me — not to set sail from Crete, and to save this hurt and damage;</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 27:25; Acts 27:36; 2 Corinthians 4:8–9"> and now I exhort you to be of good cheer, for there shall be no loss of life among you — but of the ship;</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Acts 23:11; Romans 1:9; 2 Timothy 4:17"> for there stood by me this night a messenger of God — whose I am, and whom I serve —</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 23:11; 2 Timothy 4:16–17; Matthew 10:28"> saying, Be not afraid Paul; before Caesar it behoveth thee to stand; and, lo, God hath granted to thee all those sailing with thee;</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Romans 4:20–21; Luke 1:45; Numbers 23:19"> wherefore be of good cheer, men! for I believe God, that so it shall be, even as it hath been spoken to me,</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 28:1; Acts 27:17; Acts 27:29"> and on a certain island it behoveth us to be cast.'</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Jonah 1:6; Acts 27:30; Revelation 18:17"> And when the fourteenth night came — we being borne up and down in the Adria — toward the middle of the night the sailors were supposing that some country drew nigh to them;</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> and having sounded they found twenty fathoms, and having gone a little farther, and again having sounded, they found fifteen fathoms,</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Acts 27:17; Hebrews 6:19; Deuteronomy 28:67"> and fearing lest on rough places we may fall, out of the stern having cast four anchors, they were wishing day to come.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Acts 27:16; Acts 27:32"> And the sailors seeking to flee out of the ship, and having let down the boat to the sea, in pretence as &#91;if&#93; out of the foreship they are about to cast anchors,</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 Thessalonians 2:13–14; Psalms 91:11–12; Jeremiah 29:11–13"> Paul said to the centurion and to the soldiers, 'If these do not remain in the ship — ye are not able to be saved;'</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Philippians 3:7–9; Luke 16:8"> then the soldiers did cut off the ropes of the boat, and suffered it to fall off.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33"> And till the day was about to be, Paul was calling upon all to partake of nourishment, saying, 'Fourteen days to-day, waiting, ye continue fasting, having taken nothing,</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Matthew 10:30; 1 Kings 1:52; Luke 21:18"> wherefore I call upon you to take nourishment, for this is for your safety, for of not one of you shall a hair from the head fall;'</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Matthew 15:36; 1 Corinthians 10:30–31; 1 Timothy 4:3–4"> and having said these things, and having taken bread, he gave thanks to God before all, and having broken &#91;it&#93;, he began to eat;</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="2 Corinthians 1:4–6; Acts 27:22; Psalms 27:14"> and all having become of good cheer, themselves also took food,</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="Acts 2:41; Romans 13:1; 1 Peter 3:20"> (and we were — all the souls in the ship — two hundred, seventy and six),</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="Jonah 1:5; Matthew 6:25; Acts 27:18–19"> and having eaten sufficient nourishment, they were lightening the ship, casting forth the wheat into the sea.</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Acts 28:1"> And when the day came, they were not discerning the land, but a certain creek were perceiving having a beach, into which they took counsel, if possible, to thrust forward the ship,</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="Isaiah 33:23; Acts 27:29–30"> and the anchors having taken up, they were committing &#91;it&#93; to the sea, at the same time — having loosed the bands of the rudders, and having hoisted up the mainsail to the wind — they were making for the shore,</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="2 Corinthians 11:25–26; Ezekiel 27:26; Acts 27:26–29"> and having fallen into a place of two seas, they ran the ship aground, and the fore-part, indeed, having stuck fast, did remain immoveable, but the hinder-part was broken by the violence of the waves.</span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="Mark 15:15–20; Ecclesiastes 9:3; Psalms 74:20"> And the soldiers' counsel was that they should kill the prisoners, lest any one having swam out should escape,</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43" data-xref="Acts 27:3; Proverbs 16:7; Acts 27:11"> but the centurion, wishing to save Paul, hindered them from the counsel, and did command those able to swim, having cast themselves out first — to get unto the land,</span>
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44" data-xref="Acts 27:22; Psalms 107:28–30; John 6:39–40"> and the rest, some indeed upon boards, and some upon certain things of the ship; and thus it came to pass that all came safe unto the land.</span>
