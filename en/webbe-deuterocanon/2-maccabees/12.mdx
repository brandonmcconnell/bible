<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> So when this agreement had been made, Lysias departed to the king, and the Jews went about their farming.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2"> But some of the governors of districts, Timotheus and Apollonius the son of Gennaeus, and also Hieronymus and Demophon, and beside them Nicanor the governor of Cyprus, would not allow them to enjoy tranquillity and live in peace.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3"> Men of Joppa perpetrated this great impiety: they invited the Jews who lived amongst them to go with their wives and children into the boats which they had provided, as though they had no ill will towards them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> When<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Gr. they also.">a</a></sup>the Jews,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Gr. after.">b</a></sup>relying on the public vote of the city, accepted the invitation, as men desiring to live in peace and suspecting nothing, they took them out to sea and drowned not less than two hundred of them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> When Judas heard of the cruelty done to his fellow-countrymen, giving command to the men that were with him</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> and calling upon God the righteous Judge, he came against the murderers of his kindred, and set the harbour on fire at night, burnt the boats, and put to the sword those who had fled there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7"> But when the town gates were closed, he withdrew, intending to come again to root out the whole community of the men of Joppa.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> But learning that the men of Jamnia intended to do the same thing to the Jews who lived amongst them,</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> he attacked the Jamnites at night, and set fire to the harbour together with the fleet, so that the glare of the light was seen at Jerusalem, two hundred and forty furlongs<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="a furlong is about 201 metres or 220 yards, so 240 furlongs is about 48 km. or 30 miles">c</a></sup>distant.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> Now when they had drawn off nine furlongs<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="a furlong is about 201 metres or 220 yards, so 9 furlongs is about 1.8 km. or 1 1/8 miles">d</a></sup>from there, as they marched against Timotheus, an army of Arabians attacked him, no fewer than five thousand infantry and five hundred cavalry.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> And when a hard battle had been fought, and Judas and his company, by the help of God, had good success, the nomads being overcome implored Judas to grant them friendship, promising to give him livestock, and to help<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Gr. them.">e</a></sup>his people in all other ways.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> So Judas, thinking that they would indeed be profitable in many things, agreed to live in peace with them; and receiving pledges of friendship they departed to their tents.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> He also attacked a certain city, strong and fenced with earthworks and walls, and inhabited by a mixed multitude of various nations. It was named Caspin.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> Those who were within, trusting in the strength of the walls and their store of provisions, behaved themselves rudely towards Judas and those who were with him, railing, and furthermore blaspheming and speaking impious words.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> But Judas and his company, calling upon the great Sovereign of the world, who without rams and cunning engines of war hurled down Jericho in the times of Joshua, rushed wildly against the wall.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> Having taken the city by the will of God, they made unspeakable slaughter, so much that the adjoining lake, which was two furlongs<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="a furlong is about 201 metres or 220 yards, so 2 furlongs is about 402 metres or 1/4 mile">f</a></sup>broad, appeared to be filled with the deluge of blood.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> When they had gone seven hundred and fifty furlongs<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="a furlong is about 201 metres or 220 yards, so 750 furlongs is about 151 km. or 94 miles">g</a></sup>from there, they made their way to Charax, to the Jews that are called<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="That is, men of Tob: see Judges 11:3, 2 Samuel 10:6, and compare 1 Maccabees 5:13.">h</a></sup>Tubieni.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> They didn’t find Timotheus in that district, for he had by then departed from the district without accomplishing anything, but had left behind a very strong garrison in one place.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> But Dositheus and Sosipater, who were captains under Maccabaeus, went out and destroyed those who had been left by Timotheus in the stronghold, more than ten thousand men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> Maccabaeus, arranging his own army in divisions, set<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Gr. them.">i</a></sup>these two over the bands, and marched in haste against Timotheus, who had with him one hundred and twenty thousand infantry and two thousand and five hundred cavalry.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> When Timotheus heard of the approach of Judas, he at once sent away the women and the children with the baggage into the fortress called<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Compare Carnain,1 Maccabees 5:26, 43, 44.">j</a></sup>Carnion; for the place was hard to besiege and difficult of access by reason of the narrowness of the approaches on all sides.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> When the band of Judas, who led the first division, appeared in sight, and when terror and fear came upon the enemy, because the manifestation of him who sees all things came upon them, they fled in every direction, carried this way and that, so that they were often injured by their own men, and pierced with the points of their own swords.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> Judas continued the pursuit more vigorously, putting the wicked wretches to the sword, and he destroyed as many as thirty thousand men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24"> Timotheus himself, falling in with the company of Dositheus and Sosipater, implored them with much crafty guile to let him go with his life, because he had in his power the parents of many of them and the kindred of some.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Gr. and the result will be that these be disregarded. The Greek text here is perhaps corrupt.">k</a></sup>“Otherwise, he said, little regard will<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Or, have been shown">l</a></sup>be shown to these.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> So when he had with many words confirmed the agreement to restore them without harm, they let him go that they might save their kindred.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> Then Judas, marching against<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Compare Carnain, 1 Maccabees 5:26, 43, 44.">m</a></sup>Carnion and the temple of Atergatis, killed twenty-five thousand people.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27"> After he had put these to flight and destroyed them, he marched against Ephron also, a strong city,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="The Greek text here is perhaps corrupt.">n</a></sup>wherein were multitudes of people of all nations. Stalwart young men placed<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="Gr. in front of.">o</a></sup>on the walls made a vigorous defence. There were great stores of war engines and darts there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> But calling upon the Sovereign who with might shatters the<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="Some authorities read weight.">p</a></sup>strength of<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="Or, his enemies">q</a></sup>the enemy, they took the city into their hands, and killed as many as twenty-five thousand of those who were in it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29"> Setting out from there, they marched in haste against Scythopolis, which is six hundred furlongs<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="a furlong is about 201 metres or 220 yards, so 600 furlongs is about 121 km. or 75 miles">r</a></sup>away from Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30"> But when the Jews who were settled there testified of the good will that the Scythopolitans had shown towards them, and of their kind treatment of them in the times of their misfortune,</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31"> they gave thanks, and further exhorted them to remain well disposed towards the race for the future. Then they went up to Jerusalem, the feast of weeks being close at hand.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32"> But after the feast called Pentecost, they marched in haste against Gorgias the governor of Idumaea.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33"> He came out with three thousand infantry and four hundred cavalry.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34"> When they had set themselves in array, it came to pass that a few of the Jews fell.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35"> A certain Dositheus, one<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-s" id="fnref-s" data-fn="The Greek text is uncertain.">s</a></sup>of Bacenor’s company, who was on horseback and was a strong man, pressed hard on Gorgias, and taking hold of his cloke dragged him along by main force. While he planned to take the accursed man alive, one of the Thracian cavalry bore down on him and disabled his shoulder, and so Gorgias escaped to<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-t" id="fnref-t" data-fn="Compare 1 Maccabees 5:65.">t</a></sup>Marisa.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36"> When those who were with Esdris had been fighting long and were weary, Judas called upon the Lord to show himself, fighting on their side and leading in the battle.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37"> Then in the language of his ancestors he raised the battle cry joined with hymns. Then he rushed against Gorgias’ troops when they were not expecting it, and put them to flight.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38"> Judas gathered his army and came to the city of<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-u" id="fnref-u" data-fn="Gr. Odollam.">u</a></sup>Adullam. As the seventh day was coming on, they purified themselves according to the custom, and kept the Sabbath there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39"> On the following day,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-v" id="fnref-v" data-fn="The Greek text here is uncertain.">v</a></sup>when it had become necessary, Judas and his company came to take up the bodies of those who had fallen,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-w" id="fnref-w" data-fn="Or, and to bring them back to be with their kinsmen in the sepulchres">w</a></sup>and in company with their kinsmen to bring them back to the sepulchres of their ancestors.</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40"> But under the garments of each one of the dead they found<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-x" id="fnref-x" data-fn="Perhaps these were consecrated images of the idols.">x</a></sup>consecrated tokens of the idols of Jamnia, which the law forbids the Jews to have anything to do with. It became clear to all that it was for this cause that they had fallen.</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41"> All therefore, blessing the ways of the Lord, the righteous Judge, who makes manifest the things that are hidden,</span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42"> turned themselves to supplication, praying that the sin committed might be wholly blotted out. The noble Judas exhorted the multitude to keep themselves from sin, for they had seen with their own eyes what happened because of the sin of those who had fallen.</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43"> When he had made a collection man by man to the sum of two thousand drachmas of silver, he sent to Jerusalem to offer a sacrifice for sin, doing very well and honourably in this, in that he took thought for the resurrection.</span>
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44"> For if he wasn’t expecting that those who had fallen would rise again, it would be superfluous and idle to pray for the dead.</span>
<span className="v" id="v45" data-v="45"> But if he was looking forward to an honourable memorial of gratitude laid up for those who<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-y" id="fnref-y" data-fn="Gr. fall asleep.">y</a></sup>die<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-z" id="fnref-z" data-fn="Or, on the side of godliness">z</a></sup>in godliness, then the thought was holy and godly. Therefore he made the atoning sacrifice for those who had died, that they might be released from their sin.</span>
