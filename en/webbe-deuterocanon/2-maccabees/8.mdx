<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> But Judas, who is also called Maccabaeus, and those who were with him, making their way secretly into the villages, called together their kindred. Enlisting those who had continued in the Jews’ religion, they gathered together about six thousand.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2"> They called upon the Lord to look at the people who were oppressed by all, and to have compassion on the sanctuary that had been profaned by the ungodly men,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3"> and to have pity on the city that was suffering ruin and ready to be levelled to the ground, and to listen to the blood that cried out to him,</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> and to remember the lawless destruction of the innocent infants, and concerning the blasphemies that had been committed against his name, and to show his hatred of wickedness.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> When Maccabaeus had trained his men for service, the heathen at once found him irresistible, for the wrath of the Lord was turned into mercy.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> <sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="The Greek text of verses 6 and 7 is uncertain.">a</a></sup>Coming without warning, he set fire to cities and villages. And in winning back the most important positions, putting to flight no small number of the enemies,</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7"> he especially took advantage of the nights for such assaults. His courage was loudly talked of everywhere.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> But when Philip saw the man gaining ground little by little, and increasing more and more in his success, he wrote to Ptolemy, the governor of Coelesyria and Phoenicia, that he should support the king’s cause.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> Ptolemy quickly appointed Nicanor the son of Patroclus, one of the king’s<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="See 1 Maccabees 10:65. Compare 2 Maccabees 1:14; 7:24; 10:13; 14:11; 1 Maccabees 2:18.">b</a></sup>chief friends, and sent him, in command of no fewer than twenty thousand of all nations, to destroy the whole race of Judea. With him he joined Gorgias also, a captain and one who had experience in matters of war.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> Nicanor resolved by the sale of the captive Jews to make up for the king the tribute of two thousand talents which he was to pay to the Romans.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> Immediately he sent to the cities upon the sea coast, inviting them to buy Jewish<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Gr. bodies.">c</a></sup>slaves, promising to deliver seventy<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Gr. bodies.">d</a></sup>slaves for a talent, not expecting the judgement that was to overtake him from the Almighty.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> News came to Judas concerning Nicanor’s invasion. When he communicated to those who were with him the presence of the army,</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> those who were cowardly and distrustful of God’s judgement<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="The Greek text here is uncertain.">e</a></sup>ran away and left the country.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> Others sold all that they had left, and at the same time implored the Lord to deliver those who had been sold as slaves by the impious Nicanor before he ever met them,</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> if not for their own sakes, then for the covenants made with their ancestors, and because he had called them by his holy and glorious name.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> So Maccabaeus gathered his men together, six thousand in number, and exhorted them not to be frightened by the enemy, nor to fear the great multitude of the heathen who came wrongfully against them, but to fight nobly,</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> setting before their eyes the outrage that had been lawlessly perpetrated upon the holy place, and the torture of the city that had been turned to mockery, and further the overthrow of the way of life received from their ancestors.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> “For they,” he said, “trust their weapons and daring deeds, but we trust in the almighty God, since he is able at a nod to cast down those who are coming against us, and even the whole world.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> Moreover, he recounted to them the help given from time to time in the days of their ancestors, both in the days of Sennacherib, when one hundred and eighty-five thousand perished,</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> and in the land of Babylon, in the battle that was fought against the<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Gr. Galatians.">f</a></sup>Gauls, how they came to the battle with eight thousand in all, with four thousand Macedonians, and how, the Macedonians being hard pressed, the<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Some authorities read eight.">g</a></sup>six thousand destroyed the hundred and twenty thousand because of the help which they had from heaven, and took a great deal of plunder.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> And when he had with these words filled them with courage and made them ready to die for the laws and their country, he divided his army into four parts.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> He appointed his brothers, Simon, Joseph, and Jonathan, to be leaders of the divisions with him, giving each the command of one thousand and five hundred men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> Moreover Eleazer also, having read aloud the sacred book, and having given as watchword, “THE HELP OF GOD”, leading the first band himself, joined battle with Nicanor.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24"> Since the Almighty fought on their side, they killed more than nine thousand of the enemy, and wounded and<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Gr. disabled in their limbs.">h</a></sup>disabled most of Nicanor’s army, and compelled them all to flee.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> They took the money of those who had come there to buy them as slaves. After they had pursued them for some<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Or, while">i</a></sup>distance, they returned, being constrained by the time of the day;</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> for it was the day before the Sabbath, and for this reason they made no effort to chase them far.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27"> <sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="The exact meaning of this clause is uncertain.">j</a></sup>When they had gathered<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Gr. their weapons...the spoils of the enemy.">k</a></sup>the weapons of the enemy together, and had stripped off their spoils, they kept the Sabbath, greatly blessing and thanking the Lord who had saved them to this day, because he had begun to show mercy to them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> After the Sabbath, when they had given some of the spoils to the<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Or, wounded Gr. shamefully handled.">l</a></sup>maimed, and to the widows and orphans, they distributed the rest amongst themselves and their children.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29"> When they had accomplished these things and had made a common supplication, they implored the merciful Lord to be wholly reconciled with his servants.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30"> Having had an encounter with the forces of Timotheus and Bacchides, they killed more than twenty thousand of them, and made themselves masters of exceedingly high strongholds, and divided very much plunder, giving the<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Or, wounded Gr. shamefully handled.">m</a></sup>maimed, orphans, widows, and the aged an equal share with themselves.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31"> <sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="The exact meaning of this clause is uncertain.">n</a></sup>When they had gathered the weapons<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="Gr. of them.">o</a></sup>of the enemy together, they stored them all up carefully in the most strategic positions, and they carried the rest of the spoils to Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32"> They killed the<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="That is, probably, the captain of an irregular auxiliary force. Some write Phylarches, as a proper name.">p</a></sup>phylarch of Timotheus’s forces, a most unholy man, and one who had done the Jews much harm.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33"> <sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="The Greek text here is perhaps corrupt.">q</a></sup>As they celebrated the feast of victory in the<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="Or, country">r</a></sup>city of their fathers, they burnt those who had set the sacred<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-s" id="fnref-s" data-fn="Or, porches">s</a></sup>gates on fire, including Callisthenes, who had fled into<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-t" id="fnref-t" data-fn="Or, a solitary hut">t</a></sup>a little house. So they received the proper reward for their impiety.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34"> The thrice-accursed Nicanor, who had brought the thousand merchants to buy the Jews as slaves,</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35"> being through the help of the Lord humbled by them who in his eyes were held to be of least account, took off his glorious apparel, and passing through the country,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-u" id="fnref-u" data-fn="Gr. having made himself solitary.">u</a></sup>shunning all company like a fugitive slave, arrived at Antioch,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-v" id="fnref-v" data-fn="Or, having won the greatest possible favour by reason of the destruction of his army">v</a></sup>having, as he thought, had the greatest possible good fortune, though his army was destroyed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36"> He who had taken upon himself to make tribute sure for the Romans by the captivity of the men of Jerusalem published abroad that the Jews had One who fought for them, and that<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-w" id="fnref-w" data-fn="Or, because of this their way of life Gr. because of this manner.">w</a></sup>because this was so, the Jews were invulnerable, because they followed the laws ordained by him.</span>
</div>
</div>
