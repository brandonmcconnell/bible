<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> Now after a very little time, Lysias, the king’s guardian, kinsman, and chancellor, being very displeased about the things that had happened,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2"> collected about eighty thousand infantry and all his cavalry and came against the Jews, planing to make the city a home for Greeks,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3"> and to levy tribute on the temple, as<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Or, on all the sacred places of the heathen">a</a></sup>on the other sacred places of the nations, and to put up the high priesthood for sale every year.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> He took no account of God’s power, but was puffed up with his ten thousands of infantry, his thousands of cavalry, and his eighty elephants.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> Coming into Judea and approaching Bethsuron, which was a strong place and about five stadia<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="One stadia was roughly 189 metres or 618 feet, so 5 stadia was about a little less than 1 km or a little more than half a mile.">b</a></sup>away from Jerusalem, he pressed it hard.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> When Maccabaeus and his men learnt that he was besieging the strongholds, they and all the people with lamentations and tears made supplication to the Lord to send a good angel to save Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7"> Maccabaeus himself took up weapons first, and exhorted the others to put themselves in jeopardy together with him and help their kindred; and they went out with him very willingly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> As they were there, close to Jerusalem, a horseman appeared at their head in white apparel, brandishing<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Gr. a panoply.">c</a></sup>weapons of gold.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> They all together praised the merciful God, and were yet more strengthened in heart, being ready to<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Gr. wound.">d</a></sup>assail not only men but the wildest animals and walls of iron,</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> they advanced in array, having him who is in heaven to fight on their side, for the Lord had mercy on them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> Hurling themselves like lions against the enemy, they killed eleven thousand infantry and one thousand and six hundred cavalry, and forced all the rest to flee.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> Most of them escaped wounded and naked. Lysias himself also escaped by shameful flight.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> But as he was a man not void of understanding, pondering the defeat which had befallen him, and considering that the Hebrews could not be overcome because the Almighty God fought on their side, he sent again</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> and persuaded them to come to terms on condition that all their rights were acknowledged, and<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="The Greek text here is corrupt.">e</a></sup>promised that he would also persuade the king to become their friend.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> Maccabaeus gave consent upon all the conditions which Lysias proposed to him, being careful of the common good; for whatever requests Maccabaeus delivered in writing to Lysias concerning the Jews the king allowed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> The letter written to the Jews from Lysias was to this effect: “Lysias to the<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Gr. multitude.">f</a></sup>people of the Jews, greetings.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> John and Absalom, who were sent from you, having delivered the document written below, made request concerning the things written therein.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> Whatever things therefore needed to be brought before the king I declared to him, and what things were possible he allowed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> If then you will all preserve your good will towards the government, I will also endeavour in the future to contribute to your good.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> Concerning this, I have given order in detail, both to these men and to those who are sent from me, to confer with you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> Farewell. Written in the one hundred and forty-eighth year, on the twenty-fourth day of the month<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="This month name is not found elsewhere, and is perhaps corrupt.">g</a></sup>Dioscorinthius.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> And the king’s letter contained these words: “King Antiochus to his brother Lysias, greetings.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> Seeing that our father passed to the gods having the wish that the subjects of his kingdom<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Or, should not be disquieted but">h</a></sup>should be undisturbed and give themselves to the care of their own affairs,</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24"> we, having heard that the Jews do not consent to our father’s purpose to turn them to the customs of the Greeks, but choose rather their own way of living, and make request that the customs of their law be allowed to them—</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> choosing therefore that this nation also should be free from disturbance, we determine that their temple is to be restored to them, and that they live according to the customs that were in the days of their ancestors.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> You will therefore do well to send messengers to them and give them the right hand of friendship, that they, knowing our mind, may be of good heart, and gladly occupy themselves with the conduct of their own affairs.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27"> And to the nation, the king’s letter was as follows: “King Antiochus to the senate of the Jews and to the other Jews, greetings.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> If you are all well, it is as we desire. We ourselves also are in good health.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29"> Menelaus informed us that your desire was to return home and follow your own business.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30"> They therefore who depart home up to the thirtieth day of Xanthicus shall have our<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Gr. right hand.">i</a></sup>friendship, with full permission</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31"> that the Jews use their own foods and observe their own laws, even as formerly. None of them shall be in any way molested for the things that have been done in ignorance.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32"> Moreover I have sent Menelaus also, that he may encourage you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33"> Farewell. Written in the one hundred and forty-eighth year, on the fifteenth day of Xanthicus.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34"> The Romans also sent to them a letter in these words: “Quintus Memmius and Titus Manius, ambassadors of the Romans, to the people of the Jews, greetings.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35"> In regard to the things which Lysias the king’s kinsman granted you, we also give consent.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36"> But as for the things which he judged should be referred to the king, send someone promptly, after you have considered them, that we may publish such decrees as are appropriate for your case; for we are on our way to Antioch.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37"> Therefore send someone with speed, that we also may learn what is your mind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38"> <sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Gr. Be in good health.">j</a></sup>Farewell. Written in the one hundred and forty-eighth year, on the fifteenth day of Xanthicus.</span>
</div>
