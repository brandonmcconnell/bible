<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> The argument is exceedingly ridiculous, for reasoning doesn’t appear to rule over its own affections, but over those of the body,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2"> in such a way as that any one of you may not be able to root out desire, but reasoning will enable you to avoid being enslaved to it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3"> One may not be able to root out anger from the soul, but it is possible to withstand anger.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> Any one of you may not be able to eradicate malice, but reasoning has force to work with you to prevent you yielding to malice.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> For reasoning is not an eradicator, but an antagonist of the emotions.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> This may be more clearly comprehended from the thirst of King David.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7"> For after David had been attacking the Philistines the whole day, he with the soldiers of his nation killed many of them;</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> then when evening came, sweating and very weary, he came to the royal tent, around which the entire army of our ancestors was encamped.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> Now all the rest of them were at supper;</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> but the king, being very much thirsty, although he had numerous springs, could not by their means quench his thirst;</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> but a certain irrational longing for the water in the enemy’s camp grew stronger and fiercer upon him, undid and consumed him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> Therefore his bodyguards being troubled at this longing of the king, two valiant young soldiers, respecting the desire of the king, fully armed themselves, and taking a pitcher, got over the ramparts of the enemies.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> Unperceived by the guardians of the gate, they went throughout the whole camp of the enemy in quest.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> Having boldly discovered the fountain, they filled out of it the drink for the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> But he, though parched with thirst, reasoned that a drink regarded of equal value to blood would be terribly dangerous to his soul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> Therefore, setting up reasoning in opposition to his desire, he poured out the drink to God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> For the temperate mind has power to conquer the pressure of the emotions, to quench the fires of excitement,</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> and to wrestle down the pains of the body, however excessive, and through the excellency of reasoning, to spurn all the assaults of the emotions.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> But the occasion now invites us to give an illustration of temperate reasoning from history.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> For at a time when our fathers were in possession of undisturbed peace through obedience to the law and were prosperous, so that Seleucus Nicanor, the king of Asia, both assigned them money for divine service, and accepted their form of government,</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> then certain people, bringing in new things contrary to the public harmony, in various ways fell into calamities.</span>
</div>
