<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Isaiah 51:2; Romans 4:16; 2 Corinthians 11:22"> What then will we say that Abraham, our forefather, has found according to the flesh?</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:31; 2 Corinthians 11:30; Philippians 3:9"> For if Abraham was justified by works, he has something to boast about, but not towards God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Genesis 15:6; James 2:23; Galatians 3:6–8"> For what does the Scripture say? “Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Romans 11:6; Romans 9:32; Romans 11:35"> Now to him who works, the reward is not counted as grace, but as something owed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Philippians 3:9; Romans 3:22; Titus 3:3–7"> But to him who doesn’t work, but believes in him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is accounted for righteousness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Timothy 1:9; Philippians 3:9; 1 Corinthians 1:30"> Even as David also pronounces blessing on the man to whom God counts righteousness apart from works:</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Psalms 32:1–2; Psalms 130:3–4; Luke 7:47–50"> <span className="pl pl-1">“Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">whose sins are covered.</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Psalms 32:2; 1 Peter 2:24; 2 Corinthians 5:19–20"> <span className="pl pl-1">Blessed is the man whom the Lord will by no means charge with sin.”</span></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Galatians 3:14; Colossians 3:11; Galatians 3:26–28"> Is this blessing then pronounced only on the circumcised, or on the uncircumcised also? For we say that faith was accounted to Abraham for righteousness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Galatians 6:15; Galatians 5:6; Genesis 17:10"> How then was it counted? When he was in circumcision, or in uncircumcision? Not in circumcision, but in uncircumcision.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Romans 3:22; Luke 19:9; 2 Corinthians 1:22"> He received the sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of the faith which he had while he was in uncircumcision, that he might be the father of all those who believe, though they might be in uncircumcision, that righteousness might also be accounted to them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Song of Solomon 1:8; John 8:39–40; Proverbs 2:20"> He is the father of circumcision to those who not only are of the circumcision, but who also walk in the steps of that faith of our father Abraham, which he had in uncircumcision.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Galatians 3:29; Genesis 22:17–18; Genesis 28:14"> For the promise to Abraham and to his offspring that he would be heir of the world wasn’t through the law, but through the righteousness of faith.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Galatians 3:18–24; Philippians 3:9; Romans 4:16"> For if those who are of the law are heirs, faith is made void, and the promise is made of no effect.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Romans 5:13; Galatians 3:10; Romans 3:19–20"> For the law produces wrath; for where there is no law, neither is there disobedience.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Romans 3:24–26; Ephesians 2:8; Galatians 3:22"> For this cause it is of faith, that it may be according to grace, to the end that the promise may be sure to all the offspring, not to that only which is of the law, but to that also which is of the faith of Abraham, who is the father of us all.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:28; Genesis 17:4–5; John 5:21"> As it is written, “I have made you a father of many nations.” This is in the presence of him whom he believed: God, who gives life to the dead, and calls the things that are not, as though they were.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Romans 8:24; Genesis 15:5–6; Acts 27:25"> Against hope, Abraham in hope believed, to the end that he might become a father of many nations, according to that which had been spoken, “So will your offspring be.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Hebrews 11:11–19; Romans 4:20–21; Genesis 17:17"> Without being weakened in faith, he didn’t consider his own body, already having been worn out, (he being about a hundred years old), and the deadness of Sarah’s womb.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Luke 1:45; 1 Corinthians 16:13; Daniel 10:19"> Yet, looking to the promise of God, he didn’t waver through unbelief, but grew strong through faith, giving glory to God,</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Luke 1:37; Jeremiah 32:27; Genesis 18:14"> and being fully assured that what he had promised, he was also able to perform.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Romans 4:6; Romans 4:3"> Therefore it also was “credited to him for righteousness.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Corinthians 10:11; Romans 15:4; 1 Corinthians 10:6"> Now it was not written that it was accounted to him for his sake alone,</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 2:24; 1 Peter 1:21; Acts 13:30"> but for our sake also, to whom it will be accounted, who believe in him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead,</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="1 John 4:9–10; Isaiah 53:5–6; 2 Corinthians 5:21"> who was delivered up for our trespasses, and was raised for our justification.</span>
</div>
</div>
