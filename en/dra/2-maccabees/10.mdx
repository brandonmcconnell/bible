<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> But Machabeus, and they that were with him, by the protection of the Lord, recovered the temple and the city again.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2"> But he threw down the altars, which the heathens had set up in the streets, as also the temples of the idols.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3"> And having purified the temple, they made another altar: and taking fire out of the fiery stones, they offered sacrifices after two years, and set forth incense, and lamps, and the leaves of proposition.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> And when they had done these things, they besought the Lord, lying prostrate on the ground, that they might no more fall into such evils; but if they should at any time sin, that they might be chastised by him more gently, and not be delivered up to barbarians and blasphemous men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> Now upon the same day that the temple had been polluted by the strangers, on the very same day it was cleansed again, to wit, on the five and twentieth day of the month of Casleu.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> And they kept eight days with joy, after the manner of the feast of the tabernacles, remembering that not long before they had kept the feast of the tabernacles when they were in the mountains, and in dens like wild beasts.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7"> Therefore they now, carried boughs, and green branches, and palms for Him that had given them good success in cleansing his place.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> And they ordained by a common statute, and decree, that all the nation of the Jews should keep those days every year.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> And this was the end of Antiochus that was called the Illustrious.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> But now we will relate the acts of Eupator the son of that wicked Antiochus, abridging the account of the evils that happened in the wars.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> For when he was come to the crown. he appointed over the affairs of his realm one Lysias, general of the army of Phenicia and Syria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> For Ptolemee that was called Macer, was determined to be strictly just to the Jews, and especially by reason of the wrong that had been done them, and to deal peaceably with them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> But being accused for this to Eupator by his friends, and being oftentimes called traitor, because he had left Cyprus which Philometor had committed to him, and coming over to Antiochus the Illustrious, had revolted also from him, he put an end to his life by poison.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> But Gorgias, who was governor of the holds, taking with him the strangers, often fought against the Jews.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> And the Jews that occupied the most commodious hold, received those that were driven out of Jerusalem, and attempted to make war.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> Then they that were with Machabeus, beseeching the Lord by prayers to be their helper, made a strong attack upon the strong holds of the Idumeans:</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> And assaulting them with great force, won the holds, killed them that came in the way, and slew altogether no fewer than twenty thousand.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> And whereas some were fled into very strong towers, having all manner of provision to sustain a siege,</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> Machabeus left Simon and Joseph, and Zacheus, and them that were with them in sufficient number to besiege them, and departed to those expeditions which urged more.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> Now they that were with Simon, being led with covetousness, were persuaded For the sake of money by some that were in the towers: and taking seventy thousand didrachmas, let some of them escape.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> But when it was told Machabeus what was done, he assembled the rulers of the people, and accused those men that they had sold their brethren for money, having let their adversaries escape.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> So he put these traitors to death, and forthwith took the two towers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> And having good success in arms and in all things he took in hand, he slew more than twenty thousand in the two holds.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24"> But Timotheus who before had been overcome by the Jews, having called together a multitude of foreign troops, and assembled horsemen out of Asia, came as though he would take Judea by force of arms.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> But Machabeus and they that were with him, when he drew near, prayed to the Lord, sprinkling earth upon their heads and girding their loins with haircloth,</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> And lying prostrate at the foot of the altar, besought him to be merciful to them, and to be an enemy to their enemies, and an adversary to their adversaries, as the law saith.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27"> And so after prayer taking their arms, they went forth further from the city, and when they were come very near the enemies they rested.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> But as soon as the sun was risen both sides joined battle: the one part having with their valour the Lord for a surety o victory and success: but the other side making their rage their leader in battle.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29"> But when they were in the heat of the engagement there appeared to the enemies from heaven five men upon horses, comely with golden bridles, conducting the Jews:</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30"> Two of whom took Machabeus between them, and covered him on every side with their arms, and kept him safe: but cast darts and fireballs against the enemy, so that they fell down, being both confounded with blindness, and filled with trouble.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31"> And there were slain twenty thousand five hundred, and six hundred horsemen.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32"> But Timotheus fled into Gazara a strong hold, where Chereas was governor.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33"> Then Machabeus, and they that were with him, cheerfully laid siege to the fortress four days.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34"> But they that were within, trusting to the strength of the place, blasphemed exceedingly, and cast forth abominable words.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35"> But when the fifth day appeared, twenty young men of them that were with Machabeus, inflamed in their minds because of the blasphemy, approached manfully to the wall, and pushing forward with fierce courage got up upon it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36"> Moreover others also getting up after them, went to set Are to the towers and the gates, and to burn the blasphemers alive.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37"> And having for two days together pillaged and sacked the fortress, they killed Timotheus, who was found hid in a certain place: they slew also his brother Chereas, and Apollophanes.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38"> And when this was done, they blessed the Lord with hymns and thanksgiving, who had done great things in Israel, and given them the victory.</span>
