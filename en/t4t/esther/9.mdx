<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Esther 3:13; Esther 8:12; PSALMS 30:11"> The first law that the king had commanded was to be made effective on March 7th. On that day the enemies of the Jews hoped to get rid of them. But instead, on that same day the Jews defeated their enemies.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Esther 8:17; Esther 8:11; PSALMS 71:13"> Throughout the empire, the Jews gathered together in their cities to attack those who wanted to get rid of them. No one could fight against the Jews, because all the other people in the areas where the Jews lived were afraid of them, so they did not want to help anyone who attacked the Jews.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Ezra 8:36; Esther 8:5; DANIEL 6:1–2"> All the governors and other officials and important people in all the provinces helped the Jews, because they were afraid of Mordecai.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 3:1; 1 CHRONICLES 11:9; 1 SAMUEL 2:30"> They were afraid of him because in all the provinces they knew that Mordecai was now the king’s most important official, with the authority that Haman previously had. Mordecai was becoming more famous because the king was giving him more and more power.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="PSALMS 18:34–40; PSALMS 20:7–8; PSALMS 149:6–9"> On March 7th, the Jews attacked and killed with their swords all of their enemies. They did whatever they wanted to do, to the people who hated them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Esther 3:15"> Just in Susa alone, the capital city, they killed 500 people.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7"> Among those whom they killed were the ten sons of Haman. Their names were Parshandatha, Dalphon, Aspatha,</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> Poratha, Adalia, Aridatha,</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> Parmashta, Arisai, Aridai, and Vaizatha.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Esther 5:11; Esther 8:11; GENESIS 14:23"> Those were grandsons of Hammedatha and sons of Haman, the enemy of the Jews. The Jews killed them, but they did not take the things that belonged to the people whom they killed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> At the end of that day someone reported to the king the number of people whom the Jews killed in Susa.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Esther 7:2; Esther 5:6"> Then the king said to Queen Esther, “The Jews have killed 500 people here in Susa, including the ten sons of Haman! So I think that they must have killed many more people in the rest of my empire &#91;RHQ&#93;! But okay, now what else do you want me to do for you. You tell me, and I will do it.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Esther 8:11; DEUTERONOMY 21:23; Esther 9:15"> Esther replied, “If it pleases you, allow the Jews here in Susa to do again tomorrow what you commanded them to do today. And command that the bodies of Haman’s ten sons be hanged on the gallows/poles.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> So the king commanded that the Jews be permitted to kill more of their enemies the next day. After he issued another order in Susa, the bodies of Haman’s ten sons were hanged.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Esther 8:11; Esther 9:10; Esther 9:16"> On the next day, the Jews in Susa gathered together and killed 300 more people. But again, they did not take the things that belonged to the people whom they killed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Esther 8:11; Esther 9:2; LEVITICUS 26:7–8"> That happened on March 8th. On the following day, the Jews in Susa rested and celebrated. In all the other provinces, the Jewish people gathered together to defend themselves, and they killed 75,000 people who hated them, but again they did not take the things that belonged to the people whom they killed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Esther 9:21; Esther 9:1; Esther 8:9"> That occurred on March 7th, and on the following day they rested and celebrated.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Esther 9:21; Esther 9:1; Esther 9:15"> After the Jews in Susa gathered together and killed their enemies on March 7th and 8th, they rested and celebrated on March 9th.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Esther 8:17; REVELATION 11:10; Nehemiah 8:10–12"> That is why every year, on March 8th, the Jews who live in villages now celebrate defeating their enemies. They have feasts and give gifts of food to each other.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="DEUTERONOMY 31:19–22; 2 CORINTHIANS 1:10–11; PSALMS 145:4–12"> Mordecai wrote down all the things that had happened. Then he sent letters to the Jews who lived throughout the empire of King Xerxes.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> He told them that every year they should celebrate on the 8th and 9th of March,</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="PSALMS 30:11; Isaiah 14:3; Esther 9:19"> because those were the days when the Jews got rid of their enemies. He also told them that they should celebrate on those days by feasting and giving gifts of food to each other and to poor people. They would remember it as the month in which they changed from being very sorrowful to being very joyful, from crying to celebrating.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> So the Jews agreed to do what Mordecai wrote. They agreed to celebrate on those days every year.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Esther 3:5–13; Esther 9:10"> They would remember how Haman, son of Hammedatha, a descendant of King Agag, became an enemy of all the Jews. They would remember how he had made an evil plan to kill the Jews, and that he had ◄cast lots/thrown small marked stones► to choose the day to kill &#91;DOU&#93; them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="PSALMS 7:16; PSALMS 140:9; PSALMS 141:10"> They would remember that when Esther told the king about Haman’s plan, the king arranged that the evil plan that Haman had made to kill the Jews would fail, and that he would be killed instead of the Jews, and that Haman and that his sons were hanged.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Esther 9:20; NUMBERS 16:40; EZEKIEL 39:11"> Because the ◄lot/small marked stone► that Haman threw was called Pur, the Jews called these days Purim. And, because of everything that ◄Mordecai wrote/was written► in that letter, and because of all that happened to them,</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="ZECHARIAH 2:11; Isaiah 56:6; Esther 8:17"> the Jews throughout the empire agreed to celebrate in that manner on those two days every year. They said that they would tell their descendants and those people who became Jews to be certain to celebrate this festival every year. They should celebrate just as Mordecai told them to do in the letter that he wrote.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="EXODUS 13:8–9; JOSHUA 4:7; PSALMS 103:2"> They said that they would remember and celebrate on those two days every year, in each family, in every city, and in every province. They solemnly declared that they and their descendants would never stop remembering and celebrating those days called Purim.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Esther 9:20; Esther 2:15; Esther 8:10"> Then Mordecai and Queen Esther, who was the daughter of Abihail, wrote a second letter about the Purim feast. Esther used the authority that she had because of being the queen to confirm that what Mordecai had written in the first letter was true.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Esther 1:1; Esther 8:9; ZECHARIAH 8:19"> What they wrote in the second letter was, “We wish that all of you will be living peacefully and safely/righteously. We want you and your descendants to celebrate Purim each year on the days that we two established, and to do the things that we two told you to do.” In that letter, Queen Esther and Mordecai also gave them instructions about ◄fasting/abstaining from eating food► and being sorrowful. Then copies of that letter were sent to all the Jews who were living in the 127 provinces of the empire.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Esther 9:26"> The letter that Esther wrote about the manner in which they should celebrate the Purim feast was also written in an official record.</span>
