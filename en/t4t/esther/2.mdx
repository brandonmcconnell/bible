## Esther became the new queen

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="DANIEL 6:14–18; Esther 7:10; Esther 1:12–21"> After a while, King Xerxes quit being so angry. He thought about Vashti, and he thought about the law he had made because of what she had done, and he wanted another wife.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 KINGS 1:2; GENESIS 12:14; Esther 6:14"> So his personal servants said to him, “Your majesty, you should send some men to search throughout the empire for some beautiful young women/virgins for you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Esther 2:12–15; Esther 2:8–9; Esther 1:1–2"> After they find some, you can appoint some officials in each province to bring them to the place where you keep ◄your wives/the women you sleep with► here in Susa. Then Hegai, the man who is in charge of these women, can arrange for ointments to be put on their bodies to make them more beautiful.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="MATTHEW 14:6; MATTHEW 20:16; Esther 1:21"> Then the woman who pleases you most can become queen instead of Vashti.” The king liked what they suggested, so he did it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 16:5; 1 SAMUEL 9:1; Esther 10:3"> At that time there was a Jew living in Susa, the capital, whose name was Mordecai, the son of Jair. Jair was a descendant of Shimei. Shimei was a descendant of King Saul’s father Kish. They were all from the tribe descended from Benjamin.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 KINGS 24:14–15; 2 CHRONICLES 36:20; JEREMIAH 24:1"> Many years before that, King Nebuchadnezzar had taken ◄Mordecai/Mordecai’s family► and brought them from Jerusalem to Babylon, at the same time he brought King Jehoiachin of Judah and many other people to Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Esther 2:15; Esther 1:11; 2 CORINTHIANS 6:18"> Mordecai had a cousin whose Hebrew name was Hadassah. She had a beautiful face and beautiful body/figure. Her Persian name was Esther. After her father and mother died, Mordecai took care of Esther as though she were his own daughter.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Esther 2:3; Esther 2:15"> After the king commanded that they search for some beautiful women, they brought Esther and many other young women to the king’s palace in Susa, and ◄the king put Hegai/Hegai was put► in charge of them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="GENESIS 39:21; PROVERBS 16:7; Esther 2:12"> Hegai was very pleased with Esther, and he treated her well. He immediately arranged for her to be given ointments to make her even more beautiful, and he ordered that special food would be given to her. He arranged that seven maids from the king’s palace would take care of her, and arranged that she/they would stay in the best rooms.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Esther 2:20; MATTHEW 10:16; Esther 3:8"> Esther did not tell anyone that she was a Jew, because Mordecai had told her not to tell anyone.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="GENESIS 37:14; 1 SAMUEL 17:18; ACTS 15:36"> Every day Mordecai walked near the courtyard of the place where those women stayed. He asked people who entered the courtyard to find out and tell him what was happening to Esther.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 THESSALONIANS 4:4–5; PROVERBS 7:17; Isaiah 57:9"> Before these women were taken to the king, they put ointments on the bodies of these women for one year to make them more beautiful. For six months they rubbed olive oil mixed with myrrh on their bodies each day. For the next six months they rubbed ointments and perfumes on their bodies.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> Then, when one of these women was summoned to go to the king, she was allowed to wear whatever clothes and jewelry she chose.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Esther 4:11; GENESIS 34:19; Isaiah 62:4–5"> In the evening, they would take her to the king’s own room. The next morning, they would take her to another place where the women who had slept with the king stayed. There another official whose name was Shaashgaz was in charge of those women. Those women would live there for the rest of their lives, and one of those women would go back to the king again only if the king very much wanted her to come again, and only if he told Shaashgaz the name of the woman.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="SONG OF SONGS 6:9; Esther 2:3; Esther 9:29"> Everyone who saw Esther liked her. After King Xerxes had been ruling for seven years, it was Esther’s turn to go to him. When they took her to the king, it was during the middle part of the winter. She wore only the things that Hegai suggested.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="PSALMS 113:7–8; LUKE 1:48–52; EZEKIEL 17:24"> The king liked Esther more than he liked any of the other women that they brought to him. He liked her so much that he put on her head the queen’s crown, and he declared that Esther would be the queen instead of Vashti.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="SONG OF SONGS 3:11; LUKE 14:8; JUDGES 14:10–17"> To celebrate her becoming the queen, he had a big banquet/feast prepared for all his administrators and other officials. He generously gave expensive gifts to everyone, and he declared that in all the provinces there would be a holiday, a time when people did not have to pay taxes.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Esther 2:21; Esther 2:3–4; Esther 5:13"> Later all those women who had spent a night with the king were gathered together again. By that time Mordecai had become an official at the palace.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="EPHESIANS 6:1–3; Esther 2:10; Esther 2:7"> But Esther still did not tell anyone that she was a Jew. She continued to do what Mordecai had told her to do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Esther 6:2; 1 KINGS 16:9; 2 KINGS 21:23"> One day when Mordecai was doing his work in the palace, two of the king’s officials were there. Their names were Bigthana and Teresh. They were the guards who stood outside the king’s own rooms. They became angry with the king, and they were planning how they could assassinate/kill him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Esther 6:1–2; ROMANS 11:33; ECCLESIASTES 10:20"> But Mordecai heard about what they were planning, and he told that to Queen Esther. Then she told the king what Mordecai had found out.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="GENESIS 40:22; GENESIS 40:19; Esther 10:2"> The king investigated and found out that Mordecai’s report was true. So the king ordered that those two men be hanged. When that was done, ◄an official wrote a report/a report was written► about it in a book called ‘The book that records what happened while Xerxes was king’.</span>
</div>
