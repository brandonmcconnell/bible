## David’s men prepared for the battle

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="JOSHUA 8:10; 1 SAMUEL 8:12; EXODUS 17:9"> David arranged his soldiers for the battle. He divided them into groups, and appointed a commander for each 100 soldiers and a commander for each 1,000 soldiers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="JUDGES 7:16; 2 SAMUEL 15:19–22; 2 SAMUEL 10:7–10"> He sent them out in three groups. Joab commanded one group, Joab’s brother Abishai commanded a second group, and Ittai from Gath commanded the third group. David said to them, “I myself will go with you to the battle.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 21:17; EXODUS 17:10–12; 1 KINGS 22:31"> But his soldiers said, “No, we will not allow you to go with us. If they force us all to run away, they will not be concerned about that happening. Or if they kill half of us, that will also not be what they want. To them, killing you is more important than killing 10,000 of us. So it will be better that you stay here in the city and send help to us.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 18:24; 2 SAMUEL 18:1; 1 SAMUEL 29:2"> The king replied, “Okay, I will do whatever seems best to you.” So he stood at the gate and watched while his soldiers marched out, group by group.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="DEUTERONOMY 21:18–21; 2 SAMUEL 16:11; PSALMS 103:13"> While they were leaving, the king commanded Joab, Abishai, and Ittai, “For my sake, do not harm my son Absalom!” And all the troops heard it when David gave that order about Absalom to those three commanders.</span>

## Joab killed Absalom

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="JOSHUA 17:18; JOSHUA 17:15; JUDGES 12:4–6"> So the army went out to fight against the Israeli soldiers who were with Absalom. They fought the battle in the forest where people from the tribe of Ephraim lived.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="PROVERBS 24:21; 2 SAMUEL 2:31; 2 SAMUEL 2:26"> David’s soldiers defeated Absalom’s soldiers. They killed 20,000 of them!</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="EXODUS 15:10; JOSHUA 10:11; PSALMS 43:1"> The battle was fought &#123;They fought the battle&#125; all over that area, and the number of men who died because of dangerous things in the forest &#91;PRS&#93; was greater than the number of men who were killed ◄in the battle/by swords►.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 14:26; PROVERBS 30:17; JOB 31:3"> During the battle, Absalom suddenly came near some of David’s soldiers. Absalom was riding on his mule; and when the mule went under the thick branches of a large oak tree, Absalom’s head/hair was caught in the branches. The mule kept going, but Absalom was left dangling in the air.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> One of David’s soldiers saw what happened, and went and told Joab, “I saw Absalom hanging in an oak tree!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> Joab said to the man, “What? You say that you saw him hanging there, so why did you not kill him immediately &#91;RHQ&#93;? If you had killed him, I would have given you ten pieces of silver and a belt!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 18:5"> The man replied to Joab, “Even if you gave me 1,000 pieces of silver, I would not have done anything to harm &#91;IDM&#93; the king’s son. We all heard the king command you and Abishai and Ittai, ‘For my sake, do not harm my son Absalom!’</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 14:19–20; HEBREWS 4:13; 2 SAMUEL 4:10–12"> If I had disobeyed the king and killed Absalom, the king would have heard about it, because the king hears about everything, and even you would not have defended me!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="PSALMS 45:5; MATTHEW 12:40; 2 SAMUEL 14:30"> Joab said, “I am not going to waste time talking to you!” Then he took three spears and went to where Absalom was, and thrust them into Absalom’s chest/heart while he was still alive, dangling from the oak tree.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> Then ten young men who carried weapons for Joab surrounded Absalom and finished killing him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 2:28; 2 SAMUEL 20:22; 1 CORINTHIANS 14:8"> Then Joab blew his trumpet to signal that they should not fight any more, and his soldiers returned from pursuing those Israeli soldiers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="JOSHUA 7:26; JOSHUA 8:29; 2 SAMUEL 19:8"> They took Absalom’s body and threw it into a huge pit in the forest, and covered it with a huge pile of stones. Then all the remaining Israeli soldiers who had been with Absalom fled to their own towns.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="GENESIS 14:17; 2 SAMUEL 14:27; 1 SAMUEL 15:12"> Absalom had no sons to preserve his family name. So, while Absalom was alive, he built a monument to himself in the King’s Valley near Jerusalem, in order that people would remember him. He put his name on the monument, and people still call it Absalom’s monument.</span>
</div>

## The report of Absalom’s death reached David

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 15:36; 2 SAMUEL 18:31; PSALMS 9:4"> After Absalom had been killed, Zadok’s son Ahimaaz said to Joab, “Allow me to run to the king to tell him the good news that Yahweh has rescued him from the power of his enemies!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 18:5; 2 SAMUEL 18:33; 2 SAMUEL 17:16–21"> But Joab said to him, “No, I will not allow you to take news to the king today. Some other day I will allow you to take some news, but not today. If you took news today it would not be good news for the king, because his son is dead.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> Then Joab said to David’s servant who was from Ethiopia, “You go and tell the king what you have seen.” So the man from Ethiopia bowed in front of Joab, and started to run.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="ROMANS 1:28; EPHESIANS 5:4"> Then Ahimaaz said again to Joab, “Even though that man from Ethiopia is running, allow me to run behind him.” Joab replied, “My boy/son, why do you want to do that? You will not receive any reward for your news!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="JOHN 20:4"> But Ahimaaz replied, “That does not matter, I want to go.” So Joab said, “Okay, go.” So Ahimaaz ran along another road through the Jordan Valley and arrived where David was before the man from Ethiopia arrived.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 19:8; 2 SAMUEL 13:34; EZEKIEL 33:2–7"> David was sitting between the outer gate and the inner gate of the city. The watchman/lookout went up on top of the city wall and stood on the roof over the gates. He looked out and saw one man running alone.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> The watchman called down and reported it to the king. The king said, “If he is alone, that indicates/means that he is bringing good news.” The man who was running continued to come closer.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> Then the watchman saw another man running. So he called down to the gatekeeper, “Look! There is another man running!” And the king said, “He also is bringing some good news.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 KINGS 9:20; 1 KINGS 1:42; ROMANS 10:15"> The watchman said, “I think the first man must be Ahimaaz, because he is running like Ahimaaz runs.” The king said, “He is a good man, and I am sure he is coming with good news.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 14:4; GENESIS 14:20; GENESIS 24:27"> When Ahimaaz reached the king, he called out “I hope/desire that things will go well with you!” Then he prostrated himself on the ground in front of the king and said, “Your Majesty, praise Yahweh our God, who has rescued you from the men who were rebelling against you!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 18:22; 2 SAMUEL 20:9; 2 SAMUEL 18:19–20"> The king said, “Is the young man Absalom safe/all right?” Ahimaaz did not want to answer that question, so he replied, “When Joab sent me, I saw that there was a lot of confusion, but I do not know what it was about.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30"> Then the king said, “Stand aside.” So Ahimaaz stepped aside and stood there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 18:19; 2 SAMUEL 18:28; LUKE 18:7–8"> Suddenly the man from Ethiopia arrived, and said, “Your Majesty, I have good news for you! Yahweh has enabled your soldiers to defeat all those who rebelled against you!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="JUDGES 5:31; 1 SAMUEL 25:26; DANIEL 4:19"> The king said to him, “Is the young man Absalom safe/all right?” And the man from Ethiopia replied, “Sir, I wish/desire that what happened to him would happen to all of your enemies and to all those who rebel against you!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 19:4; 2 SAMUEL 12:10–23; PROVERBS 17:25"> The king realized that he meant that Absalom was dead, so he became ◄extremely distressed/overcome with grief►, and he went up to the room above the gateway and cried. While he was going up, he kept crying out, “O, my son Absalom! My son! O, my son Absalom, I desire/wish that I had died instead of you!”</span>
