## Joab cleverly arranged for Absalom to return

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 13:39; 2 SAMUEL 2:18; 2 SAMUEL 18:33"> Joab realized that the king yearned &#91;IDM&#93; to see Absalom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 11:6; AMOS 1:1; RUTH 3:3"> So Joab sent someone to Tekoa town to bring back a woman who was very clever. When she arrived, Joab said to her, “Pretend that you are grieving because someone has died. Put on clothes that show that you are mourning. Do not put any lotion/ointment on your body. Act as if you are a woman who has been mourning for a long time.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 14:19; EXODUS 4:15; Isaiah 51:16"> And go to the king, and tell him what I tell you to say.” Then Joab told her what to say &#91;MTY&#93; to the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 1:2; 2 KINGS 6:26–28; 1 SAMUEL 25:23"> So the woman from Tekoa went to the king. She prostrated herself in front of him and then bowed down to him, and said, “Your Majesty, help me!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="JUDGES 9:8–15; 2 SAMUEL 12:1–3"> The king replied, “What is your problem?” She replied, “Please, sir, I am a widow. My husband died some time ago.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="GENESIS 4:8; DEUTERONOMY 22:26–27; EXODUS 2:13"> I had two sons. But one day they quarreled with each other out in the fields. There was no one to separate them, and one of them struck the other one and killed him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="NUMBERS 35:19; MATTHEW 21:38; 2 SAMUEL 12:17"> Now, all my family oppose me. They are insisting that I allow them to kill my son who is still alive &#91;MTY&#93;, in order that they may get revenge for his killing his brother. But if they do that, I will not have any son to inherit my husband’s possessions. That would be like &#91;MET&#93; extinguishing the last coals of a fire, and my husband would not have a son to preserve our family’s name. &#91;DOU&#93;”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="PROVERBS 18:13; Isaiah 11:3–4; JOB 29:16"> Then the king said to the woman, “Go back home; I will take care of this matter for you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 SAMUEL 25:24; 1 KINGS 2:33; MATTHEW 27:25"> The woman from Tekoa replied to the king, “Your Majesty, if you are criticized for helping me, my family and I will accept the blame. You and the royal family will ◄be innocent/not have done what is wrong►.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> The king said to her, “If anyone says anything to threaten you/cause you trouble you, bring that person to me, and I will make sure that he will never cause you trouble again.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 SAMUEL 14:45; MATTHEW 10:30; NUMBERS 35:19"> Then the woman said, “Your Majesty, please pray that Yahweh your God will not allow my relative, who wants to get revenge on my son for killing &#91;MTY&#93; his brother, to be able to do that.” David replied, “As surely as Yahweh lives, your son will not be harmed at all. &#91;IDM&#93;”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="GENESIS 18:27; 1 SAMUEL 25:24; ACTS 26:1"> Then the woman said, “Your Majesty, please allow me to say one more thing to you.” He replied, “Speak!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 13:37–38; 2 SAMUEL 12:7; JUDGES 20:2"> The woman said, “Why have you done this bad thing to God’s people? You have not allowed your son Absalom to return home. By saying what you have just said, you have certainly declared &#91;RHQ&#93; that what you have done is wrong.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="JOB 30:23; NUMBERS 35:28; HEBREWS 9:27"> We all die; we are like &#91;SIM&#93; water that cannot be picked up after it is spilled on the ground. But God does not just cause us to die; he creates ways to bring us back when we become separated from him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> “Now, Your Majesty, I have come to you because others have threatened me. So I said to myself, ‘I will go and talk to the king, and perhaps he will do what I request him to do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 SAMUEL 26:19"> Perhaps he will listen to me, and save me from the man who is trying to kill my son. If my son is killed, it would result in us disappearing from the land that God gave to us.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 SAMUEL 29:9; 2 SAMUEL 19:27; 2 SAMUEL 14:20"> “And I thought, ‘What the king says will comfort/encourage me, because the king is like &#91;SIM&#93; an angel of God. He knows what is good and what is evil.’ I pray/desire that Yahweh our God will ◄be with/direct► you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="JEREMIAH 38:25; 1 SAMUEL 3:17–18; JEREMIAH 38:14"> Then the king said to the woman, “I will now ask you a question. Answer it, and tell me the truth. &#91;LIT&#93;” The woman replied, “Your majesty, ask your question.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 14:3; 1 SAMUEL 1:26; 1 SAMUEL 20:3"> The king said, “Was Joab the one who told you to do this?” She replied, “Yes, Your Majesty, as surely as you live, I cannot avoid telling you what is true. Yes, indeed, it was Joab who told me to come here, and who told me what to say.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 14:17; 2 SAMUEL 19:27; JOB 32:21–22"> He did it in order to cause you to think differently about this matter. Your Majesty, you are as wise as God’s angels, and it seems that you know everything that happens on the earth, so you know why Joab sent me here.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 14:11; 1 SAMUEL 14:39; MARK 6:26"> Then the king summoned Joab and said to him, “Listen! I have decided to do what you want. So go and get that young man Absalom and bring him back to Jerusalem.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="RUTH 2:2; JOB 31:20; JOB 29:11"> Joab prostrated himself on the ground, and then he bowed down before the king, and asked God to bless him. Then Joab said, “Your Majesty, today I know that you are pleased with me, because you have agreed to do what I requested.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 3:3; 2 SAMUEL 13:37–38"> Then Joab got up and went to Geshur, and got Absalom and brought him back to Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="REVELATION 22:4; GENESIS 43:3; 2 SAMUEL 3:13"> But the king said/commanded that Absalom would not be permitted to live in the palace. He said, “I do not want him to come to see me.” So Absalom lived in his own house, and did not go to talk to the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Isaiah 1:6; DEUTERONOMY 28:35; JOB 2:7"> Absalom was very handsome. He looked perfect, from the bottom of his feet to the top of his head. In all of Israel there was no one whom people admired more than Absalom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="EZEKIEL 44:20; 1 CORINTHIANS 11:14; 2 SAMUEL 18:9"> His hair was very thick, and he cut it only once each year, when it became too heavy for him. Using the standard weights, he would weigh the hair that he cut off, and it always weighed about five pounds.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 18:18; 2 SAMUEL 13:1; Isaiah 14:22"> Absalom had three sons and one daughter named Tamar. She was a very beautiful woman.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> After Absalom returned to Jerusalem, he lived there two years, and during that time he never was allowed to see the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 14:30–31; Esther 1:12; MATTHEW 22:3"> So he sent a messenger to Joab to ask him to come and talk to him, but Joab refused to come. So Absalom sent a message to him a second time, but he still would not come.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="JUDGES 15:4–5; 2 KINGS 10:6–7; 2 KINGS 9:33"> Then Absalom said to his servants, “You know that Joab’s field is next to mine, and that he has barley growing there. Go and light a fire there to burn his barley.” So Absalom’s servants went there and lit a fire, and all his barley burned.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31"> Joab knew who had done it, so he went to Absalom’s house and said to him, “Why have your servants burned the barley in my field?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="1 SAMUEL 20:8; EXODUS 14:12; PROVERBS 28:13"> Absalom replied, “Because you did not come to me when I sent messages to you requesting that you come. I wanted to request that you go to the king to say to him, ‘Absalom ◄wants to know what good it did/says that is was useless► &#91;RHQ&#93; for him &#91;RHQ&#93; to leave Geshur and come here. He thinks that it would have been better for him to have stayed there. He wants you to allow him to talk to you. And if you think that he has done something that is wrong, you can command that he be executed.’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="GENESIS 33:4; LUKE 15:20; GENESIS 27:26"> So Joab went to the king, and told him what Absalom had said. Then the king summoned Absalom, and he came to the king and knelt down in front of him with his face touching the ground. Then the king kissed Absalom to show that he was pleased to see him.</span>
