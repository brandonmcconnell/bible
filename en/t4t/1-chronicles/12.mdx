## Some warriors joined David

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 SAMUEL 27:2–6; 1 CHRONICLES 11:24–25; 1 CHRONICLES 8:33"> David went to Ziklag town to escape from King Saul. While he was there, many warriors came and joined him, and they helped him when he fought battles.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="JUDGES 3:15; JUDGES 20:16; 1 SAMUEL 17:49"> They carried bows and arrows. They were able to shoot arrows and to sling stones. They could use either their right arms or their left arms to do that. They were relatives of Saul from the tribe of Benjamin.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 CHRONICLES 11:28; 2 SAMUEL 21:6; 1 CHRONICLES 11:33"> Their leader was Ahiezer. Next in command was Joash. They were both sons of Shemaah from Gibeah city. These are the names of some of those warriors: Jeziel and Pelet, the sons of Azmaveth; Beracah; Jehu from Anathoth town;</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="JOSHUA 9:3; JOSHUA 15:36; 1 CHRONICLES 11:15"> Ishmaiah from the Gibeon city, who was a leader of the thirty mighty warriors; Jeremiah, Jahaziel, Johanan, and Jozabad from Gederah town;</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> Eluzai, Jerimoth, Bealiah, Shemariah, and Shephatiah from Haruph town;</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> Elkanah, Isshiah, Azarel, Joezer and Jashobeam, who were descendants of Korah;</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="JOSHUA 15:58; 1 CHRONICLES 4:18; 1 CHRONICLES 4:39"> Joelah and Zebadiah, who were sons of Jeroham, from Gedor town.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 2:18; 2 SAMUEL 17:10; 2 SAMUEL 23:20"> Some men from the tribe of Gad east of the Jordan River joined David when he was at his fortress in the caves in the desert. They were brave warriors who were trained for fighting battles and able to use shields and spears well. They &#91;SYN&#93; were as fierce as &#91;SIM, MTY&#93; lions, and they could run as fast as &#91;HYP, SIM&#93; deer/gazelles on the hills/mountains.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> Ezer was their leader. Next in command was Obadiah. Next was Eliab.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> Next was Mishmannah. Next was Jeremiah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> Next was Attai. Next was Eliel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> Next was Johanan. Next was Elzabad.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> Next was another man whose name was Jeremiah. The last was Macbannai.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="DEUTERONOMY 32:30; LEVITICUS 26:8"> Those men from the tribe of Gad were all army officers. Some of them commanded 1,000 soldiers, and some of them commanded 100 soldiers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="JOSHUA 3:15; JOSHUA 4:18; JEREMIAH 49:19"> They crossed to the west side of the Jordan River during March, at the time of the year when the river was flooded. They chased from there all the people who lived in the valleys on both sides of the river.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> Some other men from the tribe of Benjamin and from Judah also came to David in his fortress.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 PETER 2:23; JUDE 1:9; 2 CORINTHIANS 13:11"> David went out of the cave to meet them and said to them, “If you have come peacefully to help me, I am eager to have you join with me. But if you have come to enable my enemies to capture me, even though I &#91;SYN&#93; have not done anything to harm you, I hope/wish that the God whom our ancestors ◄worshiped/belonged to► will see it and condemn/punish you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="JUDGES 6:34; JUDGES 3:10; 2 SAMUEL 17:25"> Then God’s Spirit came upon Amasai, who was another leader of the thirty greatest warriors, and he said,<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“David, we want to be with you;</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">you who are the son of Jesse, we will join you.</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">We know that things will go very well &#91;DOU&#93; for you and for those who are with you,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">because your God is helping you.”</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 SAMUEL 29:2–9"> So David welcomed those men, and he appointed them to be leaders of his soldiers. Some men from the tribe of Manasseh also joined David when he went with the soldiers of Philistia to fight against Saul’s army. But David and his men did not really help the army of Philistia. After the leaders of Philistia talked about David and his soldiers, they sent David away. They said, “If David joins his master Saul again, we will all be killed &#123;his army will kill all of us&#125;!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="DEUTERONOMY 33:17; DEUTERONOMY 1:15; 1 SAMUEL 29:11"> When David went to Ziklag, these were the men from the tribe of Manasseh who went with him: Adnah, Jozabad, Jediael, Michael, another man whose name was Jozabad, Elihu, and Zillethai. Each of them had been a commander of 1,000 men in Saul’s army.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 SAMUEL 30:1–17; 1 CHRONICLES 12:20; 1 CHRONICLES 5:24"> They were all brave soldiers, and they helped David to fight against the groups of men who roamed throughout the country, robbing people. So those men became commanders in David’s army.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 3:1; JOB 17:9; GENESIS 32:2"> Every day more men joined David’s men, and his army became large, like &#91;SIM&#93; the army of God (OR, a very huge army).</span>

## Others joined David at Hebron

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 2:3–4; 1 CHRONICLES 11:10; 1 SAMUEL 16:1"> These are the numbers of soldiers who were ready for battle who joined David at Hebron city. They came to help him to become the king of Israel to replace Saul, as Yahweh had promised would happen.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24"> There were 6,800 men from Judah, who carried shields and spears.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> There were 7,100 men from the tribe of Simeon. They were all strong warriors trained to fight battles.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> There were 4,600 men from the tribe of Levi.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 KINGS 11:9; 2 KINGS 25:18; 1 CHRONICLES 6:49–57"> Jehoiada, who was a leader descended from Aaron, was in that group of descendants of Levi, and there were 3,700 men with him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 8:17; 1 CHRONICLES 6:8; 1 CHRONICLES 6:53"> Zadok, a strong young soldier, was also in that group, and there were 22 other leaders from his clan who came with him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 2:8–9; 1 CHRONICLES 12:2; GENESIS 31:23"> There were 3,000 men from the tribe of Benjamin who were Saul’s relatives. Most of them had previously wanted one of Saul’s descendants to be the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="GENESIS 6:4"> There were 20,800 men from the tribe of Ephraim who were all brave warriors and trained for fighting battles and famous in their own clans.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="JOSHUA 17:1–18"> There were 18,000 men from the half of the tribe of Manasseh that lived west of the Jordan River. They were all chosen to go and help David become the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="EPHESIANS 5:17; LUKE 12:56–57; Esther 1:13"> There were 200 men who were leaders from the tribe of Issachar, along with their relatives. Those leaders always knew what the Israelis should do, and they knew the right time to do it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="PSALMS 12:2; JOHN 1:47"> There were 55,000 men from the tribe of Zebulun. They were all experienced warriors and knew how to use all kinds of weapons well. They were completely loyal to David.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34"> There were 1,000 officers from the tribe of Naphtali. With them were 37,000 soldiers, each carrying shields and spears.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35"> There were 28,600 soldiers from the tribe of Dan, all trained to fight battles.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="1 CHRONICLES 12:33; JOEL 2:7"> There were 47,000 experienced soldiers from the tribe of Asher, all trained to fight battles.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="JOSHUA 14:3; DEUTERONOMY 3:12–16; JOSHUA 13:7–32"> There were also 120,000 soldiers from the area east of the Jordan River who joined David. They were from the tribes of Reuben, Gad, and the eastern half of the tribe of Manasseh. They had all kinds of weapons.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="GENESIS 49:8–10; 1 KINGS 8:61; PSALMS 101:2"> All those men were soldiers who volunteered to be in David’s army. They came to Hebron wanting very much to enable David to be the king of all of the Israeli people.</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="GENESIS 31:54; 2 SAMUEL 19:42; GENESIS 26:30"> The men spent three days there with David, eating and drinking, because their families had given them food to take with them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="1 SAMUEL 25:18; 2 SAMUEL 16:1; JEREMIAH 23:5–6"> Also, their fellow Israelis came from as far away as the area where the tribes of Issachar, Zebulun, and Naphtali lived, bringing food on donkeys, camels, mules, and oxen. They brought a lot of flour, fig cakes, raisins, wine, olive oil, cattle and sheep. And throughout Israel, the people were very joyful.</span>
</div>
