## Paul apologized for unknowingly denouncing the high priest.

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="ACTS 24:16; 2 TIMOTHY 1:3; 2 CORINTHIANS 1:12"> Paul looked straight at the Jewish council members and said: “My fellow Jews, all my life I have lived respecting our God, and I do not know of anything that I have done that I knew was wrong/evil.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="JOHN 18:22; ACTS 24:1; MICAH 5:1"> When Ananias the supreme priest heard what Paul said, he commanded the men who were standing near Paul to hit him on the mouth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="DEUTERONOMY 25:1–2; JOHN 7:51; MATTHEW 23:27–28"> Then Paul said to Ananias, “God will punish you (sg) for that, you hypocrite &#91;MET&#93;! You sit there and judge me, using the laws that God gave Moses. But you yourself disobey those laws, because you commanded me to be struck &#123;these men to strike me&#125; without having proved that I have done anything that is wrong!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> The men who were standing near Paul rebuked him. They said, “Are not you (sg) afraid to insult God’s servant, our supreme priest?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="EXODUS 22:28; ECCLESIASTES 10:20; JUDE 1:8–9"> Paul replied, “My fellow Jews, I am sorry that I said that. I did not know that the man who told one of you to hit me is the high priest. If I had known that, I would not have insulted our supreme priest, because I know that it is written &#123;that Moses wrote&#125; in our Jewish law, ‘Do not speak evil of any of your rulers!’”</span>
</div>
</div>

## The commander rescued Paul from the Council members.

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="ACTS 24:15; ACTS 24:21; ACTS 26:5–8"> Paul realized that some of the Council members were Sadducees and others were Pharisees. So, in order to cause the Pharisees and Sadducees to argue among themselves instead of accusing him, he called out loudly in the Council hall, “My fellow Jews, I am a Pharisee, like my father was. I have been put &#123;You have put me&#125; on trial here because I confidently expect that some day God will ◄cause people who have died to become alive again/raise people from the dead►.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="PSALMS 55:9; JOHN 7:40–43; MATTHEW 10:34"> When he said that, the Pharisees and Sadducees started to argue with one another about whether people who have died will become alive again or not.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="LUKE 20:27; MATTHEW 22:23; MARK 12:18"> The Sadducees believe that after people die, they will not become alive again. They also believe that there are no angels and no other kinds of spirits. But the Pharisees believe that all people who have died will one day become alive again. They also believe that there are angels and other kinds of spirits.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="ACTS 22:7; ACTS 22:17–18; JOHN 12:29"> So the Council members ◄were divided/did not agree with each other►, and they began shouting at one another as they argued. Some of the teachers of the laws that God gave Moses who were Pharisees stood up. One of them said, “We (exc) think that this man has done nothing wrong.” Another said, “Maybe an angel or some other spirit really spoke to him and what he says is true.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="JAMES 1:19; JAMES 3:14–2; ACTS 22:24"> Then the Pharisees and Sadducees argued even more loudly with one another. As a result, the commander ◄was afraid/thought► that they would tear Paul to pieces. So he commanded soldiers to go down from the barracks and forcefully take Paul away from the Council members and bring him up into the barracks. So the soldiers did that.</span>
</div>

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">

## Some Jews plotted to kill Paul.

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="ACTS 23:30; Isaiah 8:9–10; ACTS 23:21"> The next morning some of the Jews &#91;MTY&#93; who hated Paul met secretly and talked about how they could kill him. They promised themselves that they would not eat or drink anything until they had killed him. They asked God to curse them if they did not do what they promised.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="JOHN 16:2; 2 SAMUEL 15:31; 2 SAMUEL 15:12"> There were more than 40 men who planned to do that.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="ACTS 23:12; JEREMIAH 8:12; Isaiah 3:9"> They went to the chief priests and Jewish elders and told them, “God has heard us promise/vow that we (exc) will not eat or drink anything until we (exc) have killed Paul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Isaiah 59:7; PSALMS 37:32–33; PROVERBS 1:16"> So we request that you go to the commander and ask him, on behalf of the whole Jewish Council, to bring Paul down to us from the barracks. Tell the commander that you want to question Paul some more. But we (exc) will be waiting to kill Paul while he is on the way here.”</span>
</div>

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="ACTS 23:10; 1 CORINTHIANS 3:19; ACTS 23:32"> But the son of Paul’s sister heard what they were planning to do, so he went into the barracks and told Paul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="PROVERBS 22:3; MATTHEW 8:8–9; MATTHEW 10:16"> When Paul heard that, he called one of the officers and said to him, “Please take this young man to the commander, because he needs to tell him something important.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="EPHESIANS 3:1; EPHESIANS 4:1; ACTS 28:17"> So the officer took Paul’s nephew to the commander. <span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="JEREMIAH 31:32; Esther 9:12; Esther 7:2"> The commander took the young man by the hand, led him off by himself, and asked him, “What do you (sg) need to tell me?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="ACTS 23:14–15; ACTS 23:1; PSALMS 12:2"> The young man said, “There are some &#91;SYN&#93; Jews who have planned to ask you (sg) to bring Paul before their Council tomorrow. <span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="ACTS 23:12–14; ACTS 14:5–6; 2 CORINTHIANS 11:26"> Do not do what they ask you (sg) to do, because there are more than 40 Jewish men who will be hiding and waiting to attack Paul when he passes by on the way to the Council. ## Many soldiers escorted Paul safely to Governor Felix at Caesarea.

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="MATTHEW 14:25; ACTS 23:33; LUKE 12:38"> Then the commander called two of his officers and told them, “Get a group of 200 soldiers ready to travel. Take along 70 soldiers riding horses, and 200 other soldiers carrying spears. All of you must be ready to leave at nine o’clock tonight, to go down to Caesarea.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="ACTS 25:14; ACTS 23:26; ACTS 24:10"> And take along horses for Paul and those accompanying him to ride, and safely escort him to the palace of Governor Felix.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> Then the commander wrote a letter to send to the governor. This is what he wrote:</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="ACTS 15:23; ACTS 26:25; LUKE 1:3"> “I am Claudius Lysias writing to you. You, Felix, are our governor whom we (exc) respect, and I sincerely send you my greetings.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="ACTS 22:25–29; ACTS 21:31–33; ACTS 24:6"> I have sent you(sg) this man, Paul, because certain Jews seized him and were about to kill him. But I heard someone tell me that he is a Roman citizen, so I and my soldiers went and rescued him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="ACTS 22:30"> I wanted to know what those Jews were saying that he had done wrong, so I took him to their Jewish Council.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="ACTS 26:31; ACTS 25:25; ACTS 18:15"> I listened while they asked this man questions and he answered them. The things they accused him about were entirely concerned with their Jewish laws. But Paul has not disobeyed any of our Roman laws. So our officials should not execute him or even put him in prison &#91;MTY&#93;.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="ACTS 23:35; ACTS 25:16; ACTS 25:5–6"> Someone told me that some &#91;SYN&#93; Jews were secretly planning to kill this man, so I immediately am sending him to you, so that you(sg) may give him a fair trial there. I have also commanded the Jews who have accused him to go there to Caesarea and tell you (sg) what they are accusing him about.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 TIMOTHY 2:3–4; LUKE 7:8; ACTS 23:23–24"> So the soldiers did what the commander commanded them, taking this letter with them. They got Paul and took him with them during the night down to Antipatris city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="ACTS 23:23; ACTS 23:10"> The next day, the foot soldiers returned to the barracks in Jerusalem, and the soldiers who rode horses went on with Paul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="ACTS 23:26; ACTS 23:23–24; ACTS 28:16"> When the men escorting Paul arrived in Caesarea, they delivered the letter to the governor, and they delivered Paul to the governor. Then the horsemen returned to Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="ACTS 21:39; ACTS 25:1; DANIEL 2:49"> The governor read the letter, and then he said to Paul, “What province are you (sg) from?” Paul answered, “I am from Cilicia province.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="ACTS 23:30; ACTS 25:16; MATTHEW 27:27"> Then the governor said, “When the people who have accused you (sg) arrive, I will listen to what each of you says and then I will judge your case.” Then he commanded that Paul be guarded &#123;soldiers to guard Paul&#125; in the palace that King Herod the Great had built.</span>
