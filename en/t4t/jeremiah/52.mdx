## Zedekiah tried to flee from Jerusalem

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 KINGS 24:18; JOSHUA 10:29; 2 CHRONICLES 36:11"> Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he became the King of Judah. He ruled in Jerusalem for eleven years. His mother was Hamutal, the daughter of a man named Jeremiah from Libnah town.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 KINGS 14:22; 2 KINGS 24:19–20; EZEKIEL 17:16–20"> Zedekiah did many things that Yahweh says are evil, like his father Jehoiakim had done.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Isaiah 3:4–5; 2 CHRONICLES 36:13; Isaiah 3:1"> The events that are summarized here happened because Yahweh was angry with the people of Jerusalem and of other places in Judah, and finally he ◄exiled them/forced them to go to other countries► and said that he did not want to have anything to do with them any more.<br />Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="JEREMIAH 39:1; ZECHARIAH 8:19; EZEKIEL 24:1–2"> So, on January 15, when Zedekiah had been ruling for almost nine years, King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon led his entire army to attack Jerusalem. They surrounded the city and built dirt ramps up to the top of the city walls to enable them to attack the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> They continued to surround Jerusalem until Zedekiah had been ruling for almost eleven years.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="JEREMIAH 39:2; Isaiah 3:1; 2 KINGS 25:3"> When Zedekiah had been ruling for almost eleven years, by July 18 of that year, the ◄famine/lack of food► in the city had become very severe, and there was no more food for the people to eat.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="JEREMIAH 39:4–7; JEREMIAH 51:32; 2 KINGS 25:4"> Then the soldiers of Babylonia broke through a section of the city wall, and all the Israeli soldiers fled. But because the city was surrounded by soldiers from Babylonia, Zedekiah and the Israeli soldiers waited until it became dark. Then they left the city through the gate between the two walls behind the king’s garden. Then they ran towards the Jordan River Valley.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="JEREMIAH 34:21; JEREMIAH 32:4; JEREMIAH 38:23"> But the soldiers of Babylonia pursued King Zedekiah, and they caught up with him on the plains near Jericho. He was alone because all his men had deserted him and had scattered.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="JEREMIAH 39:5; NUMBERS 13:21; 2 KINGS 25:6"> The soldiers of Babylonia took him to the king of Babylon, who was at Riblah in the Hamath region. There the king of Babylon told his soldiers what they should do to punish Zedekiah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="JEREMIAH 22:30; 2 KINGS 25:7; GENESIS 21:16"> They forced Zedekiah to watch while they killed his sons and all the officials from Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="EZEKIEL 12:13; JEREMIAH 34:3–5"> Then they gouged out Zedekiah’s eyes. They fastened him with bronze chains and took him to Babylon. They put him in a prison, and he remained there until the day that he died.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 KINGS 25:8; JEREMIAH 39:9; JEREMIAH 52:29"> On August 17 of that year, which was when King Nebuchadnezzar had been ruling for almost 19 years, Nebuzaradan, who was the captain of the king’s bodyguards and one of the king’s officials, arrived in Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="PSALMS 79:1; MICAH 3:12; 2 CHRONICLES 36:19"> He commanded his soldiers to burn down the temple of Yahweh, the king’s palace, and all the houses in Jerusalem. They also destroyed all the important buildings in the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Nehemiah 1:3; 2 KINGS 25:10"> Then he supervised the soldiers from Babylonia while they tore down the walls on all sides of Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="JEREMIAH 15:1–2; ZECHARIAH 14:2"> Then Nebuzaradan ◄forced to go/exiled► to Babylon some of the poorest people, those Israelis who had said they would support the king of Babylon, the rest of the craftsmen, and other people who had remained in Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 KINGS 25:12; JEREMIAH 39:9–10; JEREMIAH 40:5–7"> But Nebuzaradan allowed some of the very poor people to remain in Judah to take care of the vineyards and fields.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="JEREMIAH 27:19–22; JEREMIAH 52:21–23; 2 CHRONICLES 4:12–15"> The soldiers from Babylonia broke into pieces the huge bronze pillars that were in front of the temple, and the large bronze water tank, and the ten bronze water carts, and they took all the bronze to Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="EXODUS 27:3; NUMBERS 4:14; 1 KINGS 7:45"> They also took away the basins for holding the ashes from the burned sacrifices, the shovels for cleaning out the ashes, the tools for snuffing out the wicks of the lamps, the basins for holding the blood of the sacrificed animals, the dishes for incense, and all the other bronze items that were used when sacrifices were made at the temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 KINGS 7:49–50; 2 CHRONICLES 4:6–22; LEVITICUS 26:12"> Nebuzaradan also told his soldiers to take away the small bowls, the dishes for burning incense, the basins, pots, lampstands, bowls for incense, and the bowls used for pouring out the wine offerings. They took all the other items that were made of pure gold or silver.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 KINGS 7:47; 2 KINGS 25:16; 2 CHRONICLES 4:18"> The bronze from the two pillars, the large water tank and the twelve statues of oxen that were beneath it, and the water carts, was more/heavier than they could weigh. Those things had been made for the temple during the time that Solomon was the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 KINGS 25:17; 1 KINGS 7:15–21; 2 CHRONICLES 3:15–17"> Each of the pillars was ◄27 feet/8 meters► tall and ◄18 feet/5.5 meters► around. They were hollow, and each had sides/walls that were ◄3 in./8 cm.► thick.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="EXODUS 28:25; 2 KINGS 25:17; 2 CHRONICLES 3:15"> The bronze head on the top of each pillar was ◄7-1/2 feet/over 2 meters► high and was decorated all around with a bronze network of figures that represented pomegranates.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 KINGS 7:20"> There was a total of 100 figures of pomegranates on the network at the top, 96 of which could be seen from the ground.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="2 KINGS 25:18; JEREMIAH 29:25; JEREMIAH 35:4"> When Nebuzaradan returned to Babylon, he took with him as prisoners Seraiah the Supreme Priest, Zephaniah who was Seraiah’s deputy, and the three men who guarded the entrances to the temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 KINGS 25:19; Esther 1:14; MATTHEW 18:10"> He found some other people who were hiding in the city. So from them he took a commander of the army of Judah, seven of the king’s advisors, the army commander’s chief secretary who was in charge of recruiting soldiers for the army, and 60 other soldiers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> Nebuzaradan took them all to the king of Babylon, who was still at Riblah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="EZEKIEL 33:28; Isaiah 32:13–14; Isaiah 6:11–12"> There at Riblah in the Hamath region, the king of Babylon commanded that they all be executed.<br />Many of the people of Judah were ◄forced to leave/exiled from► their own land.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 KINGS 24:12–16; 2 KINGS 24:2–3; DANIEL 1:1–3"> The number of people who were captured and sent to Babylon at that time, when Nebuchadnezzar had been ruling for almost seven years, was 3,023.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="JEREMIAH 52:12; GENESIS 12:5; 2 KINGS 25:11"> Then, when he had been ruling for almost 18 years, his soldiers took 832 more Israeli people to Babylonia.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="JEREMIAH 52:15; JEREMIAH 6:9"> When he had been ruling almost 23 years, he sent Nebuzaradan to Jerusalem again, and he brought back 745 more Israelis to Babylonia. That was a total of 4,600 Israelis who were taken to Babylonia.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Jehoiachin was released from prison

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 KINGS 25:27–30; PSALMS 3:3; GENESIS 40:13"> After King Jehoiachin of Judah had been in prison in Babylon for almost thirty-seven years, Evil-Merodach became the king of Babylon. He was kind to Jehoiachin and ordered that he be released from prison. That was on March 31 of the year that Evil-Merodach became king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="PROVERBS 12:25; DANIEL 5:18–19; DANIEL 2:37"> He always spoke kindly to Jehoiachin and gave him a position in which he was honored more than all the other kings who had been exiled/taken to Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 9:7; 1 KINGS 2:7; GENESIS 41:14"> He supplied new clothes for Jehoiachin, to replace the clothes that he had been wearing in prison. He also allowed Jehoiachin to eat with him every day, all during the rest of his life.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 9:10; LUKE 11:3; MATTHEW 6:11"> Every day, the King of Babylon gave him some money to buy the things that he needed. That continued until the day that Jehoiachin died.</span>
</div>
</div>
