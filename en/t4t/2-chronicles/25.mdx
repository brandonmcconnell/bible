## King Amaziah of Judah

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 KINGS 14:1–6"> Amaziah was 25 years old when he became the king of Judah, and he ruled from Jerusalem for 29 years. His mother was Jehoaddin; she was from Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 25:14; JAMES 4:8; ACTS 8:21"> Amaziah did many things that pleased Yahweh, but he did not do them enthusiastically.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 24:25–26; 2 KINGS 14:5–22; GENESIS 9:5–6"> As soon as he was in complete control of his kingdom, he caused to be executed the officials who had murdered his father.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="DEUTERONOMY 24:16; EZEKIEL 18:20; 2 KINGS 14:5–6"> But he did not command their sons to be executed; he obeyed what was in the laws that Moses had written. In those laws Yahweh had commanded, “People must not be executed because of what their children have done, and children must not be executed for what their parents have done. People must be executed only for the sins that they themselves have committed.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="NUMBERS 1:3; 2 CHRONICLES 17:14–18; 2 CHRONICLES 11:1"> Amaziah summoned the men of the tribes of Judah and Benjamin to come to Jerusalem, and there he put them in groups, each clan in a group by themselves. Then he appointed officers to command each group. Some officers commanded 100 men and some commanded 1,000 men. They counted the men who were at least 20 years old; altogether there were 300,000 men. They were all men who were prepared to be in the army, and able to fight well, using spears and shields.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> Amaziah also hired 100,000 capable soldiers from Israel and paid almost four tons of silver for them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 19:2; 1 KINGS 12:28; HOSEA 9:13"> But a prophet came to him and said, “Your majesty, you must not allow those soldiers from Israel to march with your soldiers, because Yahweh does not help the people of the tribe of Ephraim or from anywhere else in Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 14:11; 2 CHRONICLES 20:6; PHILIPPIANS 4:19"> Even if your soldiers go and fight courageously in battles, God will cause your enemies to defeat you; do not forget that God has the power to help armies or to cause them to be defeated.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="PROVERBS 10:22; DEUTERONOMY 8:18"> Amaziah asked that prophet, “If I do that, what about the huge amount of silver that I paid to hire those soldiers from Israel?” The prophet replied, “Yahweh is able to pay you back more money than you paid to hire those soldiers.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 KINGS 12:24; 2 SAMUEL 19:43; PROVERBS 29:22"> So Amaziah told those soldiers from Israel to return home. They left to go home, but they were very angry with the king of Judah for not allowing them to stay and fight.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 KINGS 14:7; PSALMS 60:1; 2 SAMUEL 8:13"> Then Amaziah became brave, and he led his army to the Salt Valley. There they killed 10,000 men from the Edom people-group.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 20:10; 1 CHRONICLES 20:3; 2 SAMUEL 12:31"> The army of Judah also captured 10,000 others, and took them to the top of a cliff and threw them all down over the cliff, with the result that their corpses were all smashed to pieces.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 8:5; 1 KINGS 16:29; 1 KINGS 9:17"> While that was happening, the soldiers from Israel whom Amaziah had sent home after not allowing them to fight along with his soldiers, raided cities and towns in Judea, from Samaria city to Beth-Horon town. They killed 3,000 people and took away a great amount of valuable things.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 28:23; DEUTERONOMY 7:25; DEUTERONOMY 7:5"> When Amaziah returned to Jerusalem after his army had slaughtered the soldiers from Edom, he brought the idols that were worshiped by the people of Edom. He set them up to be his own gods. Then he bowed down to worship them and offered sacrifices to them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="PSALMS 96:5; 2 CHRONICLES 25:11–12; 2 CHRONICLES 25:7"> Because of that, Yahweh was very angry with Amaziah. He sent a prophet to him, who said, “Why do you worship these foreign gods that were not even able to save their own people when your army attacked them?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 18:20–21; JEREMIAH 29:26; 2 CHRONICLES 18:25"> While he was still speaking, the king said to him, “We certainly did not appoint you to be one of my advisors. So stop talking! If you say anything more, I will tell my soldiers to kill you!” So the prophet said, “I know that God has determined to get rid of you, because you have begun to worship idols, and have not heeded my advice.” Then the prophet said nothing more.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 KINGS 14:8–14; 2 CHRONICLES 25:13; PROVERBS 20:3"> Some time later Amaziah, the king of Judah, consulted his advisors. Then he sent a message to Jehoash, the king of Israel. He wrote, “Come here and let’s talk together.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="JUDGES 9:8–15; 1 KINGS 4:33; PSALMS 80:13"> But Jehoash replied to King Amaziah, “One time a thistle growing in the mountains in Lebanon sent a message to a cedar tree saying, ‘Let your daughter marry my son.’ But a wild animal in Lebanon came along and trampled the thistle under its feet.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 26:16; 2 CHRONICLES 32:25; PROVERBS 18:6"> The meaning of what I am saying is that you are saying to yourself that your army has defeated the army of Edom, so you have become very proud. But you should stay at your home. It would not be good for you to cause trouble, which would result in you and your kingdom of Judah being destroyed.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 22:7; 1 KINGS 12:15; 1 PETER 2:8"> But Amaziah refused to heed Jehoash’s message. That happened because God wanted Jehoash’s army to defeat them, because they were worshiping the gods of Edom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 SAMUEL 6:19–20; 1 SAMUEL 6:9; JOSHUA 21:16"> So Jehoash’s army attacked. Their two armies faced each other at Beth-Shemesh city in Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="1 SAMUEL 4:10; 2 CHRONICLES 28:5–6; 1 KINGS 22:36"> The army of Judah was badly defeated by the army of Israel, and all the soldiers of Judah fled to their homes.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 21:17; Nehemiah 8:16; 2 CHRONICLES 26:9"> King Jehoash’s army also captured King Amaziah there. Then he brought Amaziah to Jerusalem, and his soldiers tore down the wall that was around the city, from the Ephraim Gate to the Corner Gate. That was a section that was about 600 feet long.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 CHRONICLES 26:15; 2 CHRONICLES 12:9; 2 KINGS 14:14"> His soldiers also carried away the gold and silver and other valuable furnishings from the temple which the descendants of Obed-Edom had previously been guarding. They also took away the valuable things in the palace, and they took to Samaria some prisoners whom they had captured.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 KINGS 14:17–22"> King Jehoash of Israel died, and King Amaziah of Judah lived for 15 years after that.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 20:34; 2 KINGS 14:15"> An account of all the other things that Amaziah did while he was the king of Judah is written in the scroll called ‘The History of the Kings of Judah and Israel’.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 24:25; JOSHUA 10:31; 2 KINGS 14:19"> From the time that Amaziah started to disobey Yahweh, some men in Jerusalem planned to kill him. He was able to escape to Lachish city, but those who wanted to kill him sent another group of people to Lachish and killed him there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 KINGS 14:20"> They put his corpse on a horse and brought it back to Jerusalem and buried it where his ancestors had been buried in the part of Jerusalem called ‘The City of David’.</span>
