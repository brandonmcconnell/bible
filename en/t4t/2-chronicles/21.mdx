## King Jehoram of Judah

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="JUDGES 9:5; 2 CHRONICLES 21:17; GENESIS 4:8"> After Jehoram was completely in control of his father’s kingdom, he had all of his younger brothers executed, along with some of the leaders of the nation.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 KINGS 8:17–22"> Jehoram was 32 years old when he became the king, and he ruled in Jerusalem for eight years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 18:1; 1 KINGS 12:28–30; 2 KINGS 8:18"> But he did many of the evil things that the kings of Israel had done. He did many things that Yahweh considers to be evil, things that the family of Ahab had done, because he married one of Ahab’s daughters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 KINGS 11:36; PSALMS 132:17–18; 2 SAMUEL 7:12–17"> However, because of the agreement that Yahweh had made with King David, Yahweh did not want to get rid of the descendants of David. He had promised that David’s descendants would always be the ones who ruled Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 21:10; 2 CHRONICLES 20:22–23; GENESIS 27:40"> While Jehoram was ruling, the people of the Edom region rebelled against the king of Judah and appointed their own king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> So Jehoram and his officers and his men in chariots went to Edom. There, the army of Edom surrounded them. Jehoram escaped during the night.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 15:2; JEREMIAH 2:13; 2 CHRONICLES 13:10"> But the king of Judah was never able to regain control of Edom, and Edom is still not controlled by Judah. The people in Libnah city between Judah and Philistia also rebelled against Judah. Those things happened because Jehoram turned away from obeying Yahweh, the God whom his ancestors belonged to.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="LEVITICUS 20:5; 1 KINGS 11:7; LEVITICUS 17:7"> On the hilltops in Judah he had also built shrines to worship idols, and had caused the people of Judah to stray away from Yahweh by worshiping foreign gods.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 14:2–5; 2 CHRONICLES 17:3–4; 2 KINGS 2:1"> One day, Jehoram received a letter from the prophet Elijah. Elijah had written this in the letter: “This is what Yahweh, the God whom your ancestor King David worshiped, says: 'You have not done things that please me like your father Jehoshaphat did or what King Asa did.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 21:4; 2 CHRONICLES 21:11; 1 KINGS 16:30–33"> Instead, you have continually done the evil things that the kings of Israel have done. You have encouraged the people in Jerusalem and other places in Judah to stop worshiping Yahweh, like the descendants of Ahab did. You have also murdered your own brothers, who were more righteous men than you are.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="LEVITICUS 26:21; EXODUS 20:5; HOSEA 5:11"> So now Yahweh is about to very severely punish the people in your kingdom and even your own children and your wives and everything that you own.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 21:18–19; ACTS 1:18; DEUTERONOMY 28:67"> And you yourself will have an intestinal disease that will continue to become worse, and you will suffer from it until you die.' ”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 17:11; 2 CHRONICLES 33:11; 1 KINGS 11:14"> Then Yahweh caused some men from the Philistia people-group and some Arabs who lived near the coast of the Mediterranean Sea, where people from Ethiopia had settled, to become angry with Jehoram.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 22:1; 2 CHRONICLES 22:6; 2 CHRONICLES 25:23"> Their army invaded Judah and took away from Jerusalem all the valuable things that they found in the king’s palace, and even his sons and wives. His youngest son, Ahaziah, was the only one of his sons whom they did not take away.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 21:15; ACTS 12:23; 2 KINGS 9:29"> After that happened, Yahweh caused Jehoram to be afflicted with an intestinal disease that no one could cure.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 16:14; JEREMIAH 34:5"> About two years later, while he was in great pain, he died because of that disease. The people of Judah had made bonfires to honor his ancestors when they died, but they did not make a bonfire for Jehoram.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="JEREMIAH 22:18; 2 CHRONICLES 28:27; JEREMIAH 22:28"> Jehoram was 32 years old when he became the king, and he ruled in Jerusalem for eight years. No one was sorry when he died. His corpse was buried in the part of Jerusalem called ‘The City of David’, but he was not buried where the other kings of Judah had been buried.</span>
</div>
