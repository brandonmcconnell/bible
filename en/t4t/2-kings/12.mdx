## King Joash of Judah

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 CHRONICLES 3:11; 2 KINGS 11:21; 2 KINGS 11:1–4"> When Jehu had been ruling Israel for almost seven years, Joash became the king of Judah. He ruled in Jerusalem for 40 years. His mother was Zibiah, from Beersheba city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 24:17–22; 2 CHRONICLES 26:4; 2 CHRONICLES 25:2"> All his life, he did what pleased Yahweh, because Jehoiada the priest instructed/taught him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 KINGS 14:4; 1 KINGS 22:43; 1 KINGS 15:14"> But the places where the people worshiped Yahweh on the tops of hills were not destroyed, and they continued to offer sacrifices and burn incense at those places, instead of at the place that God had chosen for them in Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 KINGS 22:4; EXODUS 35:5; 1 CHRONICLES 29:3–9"> Joash said to the priests, “You must take all the money which the people contribute, both the money they are required to give and the money that they themselves decide to give, as sacred offerings to buy things for the temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Isaiah 58:12; 1 KINGS 11:27; 2 KINGS 22:5–6"> Each priest must take the money from people who know him (OR, from one of the treasurers), and he must use that money to repair the temple whenever he sees that there is something that needs to be repaired.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 29:34; 1 PETER 5:2; Isaiah 56:10–12"> But after Joash had been ruling for almost twenty-three years, the priests still had not repaired anything in the temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 24:16; 2 CHRONICLES 24:5–14; 2 KINGS 11:4"> So Joash summoned Jehoiada and the other priests and said to them, “◄Why are you not repairing things in the temple?/You should have been repairing things in the temple!► &#91;RHQ&#93; From now on, you must not keep the money that you receive from people who know you (OR, the treasurers). You must give it to the people who will be repairing things in the temple!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> The priests agreed to do that, and they also agreed that they themselves would not do the repair work.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="MARK 12:41; PSALMS 84:10; JEREMIAH 35:4"> Then Jehoiada took a chest and bored a hole in the lid. He placed it alongside the altar for burning incense/sacrifices that was on the right as anyone enters the temple. The priests who guarded the entrance to the temple put in the box the money that was brought to the temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 SAMUEL 8:17; 2 KINGS 22:12; 2 KINGS 19:2"> Whenever they saw that there was a lot of money in the chest, the king’s secretary and the Supreme Priest would come and count the money. Then they would put it in bags and tie the bags shut.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 KINGS 22:5–6; 2 CHRONICLES 24:11–12; 2 CHRONICLES 34:9–11"> Then, after they weighed it, they would give the money to the men who supervised the work in the temple. Then the supervisors would use that money to pay the carpenters and builders who did the repair work in the temple,</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 KINGS 22:5–6; 1 KINGS 5:17–18; Ezra 3:7"> and the masons and the stone cutters. Also with some of that money they bought timber and stones that had been cut to be used in the repair work, and to pay all the other expenses for the repair work.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 24:14; 1 KINGS 7:48–50; Ezra 1:9–11"> But they did not use any of that money to pay men to make silver cups or wick trimmers or bowls or trumpets or any other items made of silver or gold to be used in the temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> All that money was given to the men who were doing the work of repairing the temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="2 KINGS 22:7; 3 JOHN 1:5; 1 CORINTHIANS 4:2–3"> The men who supervised the work always did things honestly, so the king’s secretary and the Supreme Priest never required that the supervisors report what they had spent the money for.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="LEVITICUS 7:7; LEVITICUS 5:15–18; LEVITICUS 4:24"> But the money that people gave to pay for the wrong things that they had done and the money they gave to purify themselves because of the sins that they had committed was not put in the chest. That money belonged to the priests.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 24:23–24; LUKE 9:53; JEREMIAH 42:15"> At that time, Hazael, the king of Syria, went with his army and attacked Gath city and conquered it. Then he decided that they would attack Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 KINGS 15:18; 2 KINGS 18:15–16; 2 KINGS 12:4"> So Joash, the king of Judah, took all the money that the previous kings, Jehoshaphat and Jehoram and Ahaziah, had dedicated to Yahweh. He added some of his own money, and all the gold that was in the rooms in the temple where valuable things were kept/stored, and the gold in his palace, and sent it all to King Hazael, to ◄appease him/persuade him to not attack Jerusalem►. So King Hazael took his army away from Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 KINGS 14:29; 1 KINGS 14:19; 1 KINGS 11:41"> If you want to read more of what Joash did, it is all written &#91;RHQ&#93; in the scroll called ‘The History of the Kings of Judah’.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 CHRONICLES 24:24–27; 2 KINGS 14:5; 2 SAMUEL 5:9"> Joash’s officials plotted against him, and two of them killed Joash on the road that goes down to the Silla district. The two men who did that were Jozabad, the son of Shimeath, and Jehozabad, the son of Shomer. Joash was buried in the place where his ancestors were buried, in the part of Jerusalem called ‘The City of David’. Then Joash’s son Amaziah became the king of Judah.</span>
