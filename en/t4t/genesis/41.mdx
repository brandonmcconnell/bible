## Joseph told the king that the meaning of his dreams was that there would be a big famine

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="GENESIS 20:3; GENESIS 29:14; EXODUS 4:9"> Two complete years later, the king of Egypt had a dream. In the dream, he was standing alongside the Nile River.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Isaiah 19:6"> Suddenly seven healthy fat cows come up out of the river. They started eating the grass that was on the riverbank.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="GENESIS 41:20–21; GENESIS 41:4"> Soon seven other cows, unhealthy-looking and thin, came up behind them from the Nile River. They stood alongside the fat cows that were on the riverbank.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 KINGS 3:15"> Then the unhealthy thin cows ate the seven healthy fat cows. And then the king woke up.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="DEUTERONOMY 32:14"> The king went to sleep again, and he had another dream. This time he saw seven heads of grain that were full of kernels of grain and ripe, and all growing on one stalk.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="EZEKIEL 17:10; HOSEA 13:15; EZEKIEL 19:12"> After that, the king saw that seven other heads of grain sprouted on that (OR, on another) stalk. They were thin and had been dried up by the hot east wind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="GENESIS 37:5; GENESIS 20:3"> Then the thin heads of grain swallowed up the seven ripe full heads. Then the king woke up. He realized that he had been dreaming.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="DANIEL 4:7; EXODUS 7:22; MATTHEW 2:1"> But the next morning he was worried about the meaning of the dream. So he summoned all the magicians and wise men who lived in Egypt. He told them what he had dreamed, but none of them could tell him the meaning of the two dreams.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="GENESIS 40:14; GENESIS 40:23; GENESIS 40:1–3"> Then the chief drink-server said to the king, “Now I remember something that I should have told you! I made a mistake by forgetting to tell it to you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="GENESIS 39:20; GENESIS 40:2–3; GENESIS 37:36"> One time you were angry with two of us. So you put me and the chief baker in the prison in the house of the captain of the palace guards.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="GENESIS 40:5–8"> While we were there, one night each of us had a dream, and the dreams had different meanings.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="GENESIS 40:12–19; GENESIS 37:36; GENESIS 39:1"> There was a young Hebrew man there with us. He was a servant of the captain of the palace guards. We told him what we had dreamed, and he told us what our dreams meant. He told each of us the meaning of our dreams.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="EZEKIEL 43:3; GENESIS 40:20–22; GENESIS 40:12"> And what happened was exactly the same as the meanings that he told us: You said I could have my previous job again, but the other man was killed by being hanged. The Hebrew man’s name was Joseph.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="DANIEL 2:25; PSALMS 113:7–8; PSALMS 105:19–22"> When the king heard that, he told some servants to bring Joseph to him, and they quickly brought Joseph out of the prison. Joseph shaved and put on better clothes, and then he went and stood in front of the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="DANIEL 5:16; DANIEL 5:12; PSALMS 25:14"> The king said to Joseph, “I had two dreams, and no one can tell me what they mean. But someone told me that when you hear someone tell about a dream he has had, you can tell that person what the dream means.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 CORINTHIANS 3:5; GENESIS 40:8; DANIEL 2:47"> But Joseph replied to the king, “No, I cannot do that. It is God who knows the meaning of dreams, but he will enable me to tell you their meaning, and they will mean something good.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> The king said to Joseph, “In my first dream I was standing on the bank of the Nile River.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="JEREMIAH 24:8; JEREMIAH 24:1–3; JEREMIAH 24:5"> Suddenly seven healthy fat cows came up out of the river, and they started eating the grass that was on the riverbank.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> Soon seven other cows, ugly and thin ones, came up behind them from the river. I never saw such ugly cows in all the land of Egypt!</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> The thin ugly cows ate the seven fat cows that came up first.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="EZEKIEL 3:3; PSALMS 37:19; REVELATION 10:9–10"> But afterwards, no one would have known that the thin cows ate them, because they were just as ugly as they were before. Then I woke up.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> Then I had another dream. I saw seven heads of grain. They were full of kernels of grain and ripe, and they were all growing on one stalk.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="PSALMS 129:6–7; HOSEA 13:15; 2 KINGS 19:26"> Then to my surprise I saw seven other heads of grain that sprouted. They were thin and had been dried up by the hot east wind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="DANIEL 4:7; GENESIS 41:8; EXODUS 8:19"> The thin heads of grain swallowed the seven good heads. I told these dreams to the magicians, but none of them could explain to me what they meant.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="DANIEL 2:45; REVELATION 4:1; EPHESIANS 1:17"> Then Joseph said to the king, “Both your dreams have the same meaning. God is revealing to you in your dreams what he is about to do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="GENESIS 41:47; GENESIS 41:2; GENESIS 41:53"> The seven healthy cows represent seven years. The seven good heads of grain also represent seven years. The two dreams both have the same meaning.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 KINGS 8:1; 2 SAMUEL 24:19"> The seven thin ugly cows that came up behind them and the seven worthless heads of grain that were dried up by the hot east wind each represent seven years ◄of famine/when food will be very scarce►.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="GENESIS 41:25; GENESIS 41:16"> It will happen just as I have told you, because God has revealed to you what he is about to do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="GENESIS 41:26; GENESIS 41:46–47; GENESIS 41:49"> There will be seven years in which there will be plenty of food throughout the land of Egypt,</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="GENESIS 47:13; GENESIS 41:54; JAMES 5:17"> but after that there will be seven years ◄of famine/when food will be very scarce►. Then people will forget all the years when there was plenty of food, because the famine that will come afterward will ruin the country.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Isaiah 24:20; 1 SAMUEL 5:6"> The people will forget how plentiful food was previously, because the famine will be very terrible.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="NUMBERS 23:19; Isaiah 46:10–11; Isaiah 14:24–27"> The reason God gave to you two dreams is that he wants you to know that this will happen, and he will cause it to happen very soon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="GENESIS 41:39; ACTS 6:3; DEUTERONOMY 1:13"> “Now I suggest that you should choose a man who is wise and can make good decisions. I suggest that you appoint him to direct the affairs of the whole country.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="PROVERBS 22:3; LUKE 16:5; PROVERBS 6:6–8"> You should also appoint supervisors over the country, in order that they can arrange to collect one-fifth of all the grain that is harvested during the seven years when food is plentiful.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="GENESIS 45:6–7; GENESIS 41:56; EXODUS 4:13"> They should collect this amount of grain during those seven years that are coming, when there will be plenty of food. You should supervise them as they store it in the cities.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="GENESIS 47:13–25"> This grain should be stored so that it can be eaten during the seven years when there will be a famine here in Egypt, so that the people in this country will not die from hunger.”</span>
</div>

## The king made Joseph the governor of all of Egypt, to handle the problem of the famine

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="ACTS 7:10; 1 KINGS 21:2; 2 SAMUEL 3:36"> The king and his officials thought that this would be a good plan.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="JOB 32:8; DANIEL 5:14; NUMBERS 27:18"> So the king said to them, “◄Can we find any other man like Joseph, a man to whom God has given his Spirit?/It is not likely that we will find another man like this man, one to whom God has given his Spirit!►” &#91;RHQ&#93;</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="GENESIS 41:33; GENESIS 41:28; GENESIS 41:25"> Then the king said to Joseph, “Because God has revealed all this to you, it seems to me that there is no one who is as wise as you and who can decide wisely about things.</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="PROVERBS 22:29; ACTS 7:10; PSALMS 105:21–22"> So I will put you in charge of everything in my palace. All the people here in Egypt must obey what you command. Only because I am king &#91;MTY&#93; will I have more authority than you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="MATTHEW 28:18; PHILIPPIANS 2:9–11; GENESIS 42:6"> Then the king said to Joseph, “I am now putting you in charge of the whole country of Egypt.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="DANIEL 5:29; DANIEL 5:7; Esther 3:10"> The king took from his finger the ring that had his seal on it, and he put it on Joseph’s finger. He put robes made of fine linen on him, and he put a gold chain around his neck.</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43" data-xref="ACTS 7:10; GENESIS 45:8; GENESIS 45:26"> Then he arranged for Joseph to ride around in the chariot that showed that he was the second-most important man in the country. When Joseph rode in the chariot, men shouted to the people who were on the road in front of him, “Get off the road!” So the king put Joseph in charge of everything in the country.</span>
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44" data-xref="PSALMS 105:22; EXODUS 11:7"> The king said to Joseph, “I am the king, but no one in the whole land of Egypt will do anything &#91;IDM&#93; if you do not permit them to do it.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v45" data-v="45" data-xref="GENESIS 46:20; EZEKIEL 30:17; EXODUS 2:16"> The king gave Joseph a new name, Zaphenath-Paneah. He also gave him Asenath to be his wife. She was the daughter of Potiphera, who was a priest in a temple in On city. Then Joseph became known (OR, traveled) through all the land of Egypt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v46" data-v="46" data-xref="DANIEL 1:19; 1 KINGS 12:6; 1 SAMUEL 16:21"> Joseph was 30 years old when he started to work for the king of Egypt. To do his work, he left the king’s palace and traveled throughout Egypt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v47" data-v="47" data-xref="GENESIS 26:12; PSALMS 72:16"> During the next seven years, the land produced abundant crops, so there was plenty of food.</span>
<span className="v" id="v48" data-v="48" data-xref="GENESIS 41:34–36; GENESIS 47:21"> As Joseph supervised them, his helpers collected one-fifth of all the grain that was produced during those years, and stored it in the cities. In each city, he had his helpers store up the grain that was grown in the fields that surrounded that city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v49" data-v="49" data-xref="PSALMS 78:27; JUDGES 6:5; GENESIS 22:17"> Joseph had them store up a huge amount of grain. It looked as plentiful as the sand on the seashore. There was so much grain that after a while they stopped keeping records of how much grain was stored, because there was more grain than they could measure.</span>
<span className="v" id="v50" data-v="50" data-xref="GENESIS 46:20; GENESIS 48:5; 2 SAMUEL 8:18"> Before the seven years of famine started, Joseph’s wife Asenath gave birth to two sons.</span>
<span className="v" id="v51" data-v="51" data-xref="PSALMS 30:5; PROVERBS 31:7; DEUTERONOMY 33:17"> Joseph named the first one Manasseh, which sounds like the Hebrew word that means ‘forget’, because, he said, “God has caused me to forget all my troubles and all my father’s family.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v52" data-v="52" data-xref="GENESIS 49:22; GENESIS 50:23; GENESIS 17:6"> He named his second son Ephraim, which means ‘to have children’, because, he said, “God has given me children here in this land where I have suffered.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v53" data-v="53" data-xref="GENESIS 41:29–31; PSALMS 73:20; LUKE 16:25"> Finally the seven years in which there was plenty of food ended.</span>
<span className="v" id="v54" data-v="54" data-xref="GENESIS 45:11; ACTS 7:11; PSALMS 105:16"> Then the seven years of famine started, just as Joseph had predicted. There was also a famine in all the other nearby lands, but although the crops did not grow, there was food everywhere in Egypt, because of the grain they had stored up in the cities.</span>
<span className="v" id="v55" data-v="55" data-xref="MATTHEW 17:5; MATTHEW 3:17; JEREMIAH 14:1–6"> When all the people of &#91;MTY&#93; Egypt had eaten all of their own food and were still hungry, they begged the king for food. So the king told all the people of Egypt, “Go to Joseph, and do what he tells you to do.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v56" data-v="56" data-xref="GENESIS 42:6; ZECHARIAH 5:3; LUKE 21:35"> When the famine was very bad throughout the whole country, Joseph ordered his helpers to open the storehouses. Then they sold the grain in the storehouses to the people of Egypt, because the famine was very severe all over Egypt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v57" data-v="57" data-xref="GENESIS 41:54; GENESIS 42:5; GENESIS 41:56"> And people from many &#91;HYP&#93; nearby countries came to Egypt to buy grain from Joseph, because the famine was very severe everywhere &#91;HYP&#93;.</span>
