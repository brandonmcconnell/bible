<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Chronicles 20:34; 2 Chronicles 19:2; 1 Kings 16:7"> Yahweh’s word came to Jehu the son of Hanani against Baasha, saying,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Kings 15:34; 1 Kings 14:7; 1 Samuel 2:8"> “Because I exalted you out of the dust and made you prince over my people Israel, and you have walked in the way of Jeroboam and have made my people Israel to sin, to provoke me to anger with their sins,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Kings 14:10; 1 Kings 15:29–30; Isaiah 66:24"> behold, I will utterly sweep away Baasha and his house; and I will make your house like the house of Jeroboam the son of Nebat.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Kings 14:11; 1 Kings 21:24"> The dogs will eat Baasha’s descendants who die in the city; and he who dies of his in the field, the birds of the sky will eat.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Kings 14:19; 1 Kings 15:31; 2 Chronicles 16:1–6"> Now the rest of the acts of Baasha, and what he did, and his might, aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Kings 14:17; 1 Kings 15:21; 1 Kings 16:13–14"> Baasha slept with his fathers, and was buried in Tirzah; and Elah his son reigned in his place.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Kings 14:14; Hosea 1:4; Psalms 115:4"> Moreover Yahweh’s word came by the prophet Jehu the son of Hanani against Baasha and against his house, both because of all the evil that he did in Yahweh’s sight, to provoke him to anger with the work of his hands, in being like the house of Jeroboam, and because he struck him.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> In the twenty-sixth year of Asa king of Judah, Elah the son of Baasha began to reign over Israel in Tirzah for two years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Kings 18:3; Genesis 39:4; Daniel 5:1–4"> His servant Zimri, captain of half his chariots, conspired against him. Now he was in Tirzah, drinking himself drunk in the house of Arza, who was over the household in Tirzah;</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Kings 9:31"> and Zimri went in and struck him and killed him in the twenty-seventh year of Asa king of Judah, and reigned in his place.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Samuel 25:22; 1 Kings 16:3; 1 Kings 15:29"> When he began to reign, as soon as he sat on his throne, he attacked all the house of Baasha. He didn’t leave him a single one who urinates on a wall<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="or, male">a</a></sup>among his relatives or his friends.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Kings 16:1; 1 Kings 16:7; 2 Chronicles 10:15"> Thus Zimri destroyed all the house of Baasha, according to Yahweh’s word which he spoke against Baasha by Jehu the prophet,</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Deuteronomy 32:21; 1 Kings 15:30; Isaiah 41:29"> for all the sins of Baasha, and the sins of Elah his son, which they sinned and with which they made Israel to sin, to provoke Yahweh, the God of Israel, to anger with their vanities.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> Now the rest of the acts of Elah, and all that he did, aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Kings 15:27; Joshua 19:44; Job 20:5"> In the twenty-seventh year of Asa king of Judah, Zimri reigned seven days in Tirzah. Now the people were encamped against Gibbethon, which belonged to the Philistines.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Chronicles 22:2; 2 Kings 8:26; Micah 6:16"> The people who were encamped heard that Zimri had conspired, and had also killed the king. Therefore all Israel made Omri, the captain of the army, king over Israel that day in the camp.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Judges 9:50; Luke 19:43–44; 2 Kings 18:9–12"> Omri went up from Gibbethon, and all Israel with him, and they besieged Tirzah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="2 Samuel 17:23; 1 Samuel 31:4–5; Job 2:9–10"> When Zimri saw that the city was taken, he went into the fortified part of the king’s house and burned the king’s house over him with fire, and died,</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 Kings 15:26; 1 Kings 14:16; 1 Kings 12:28"> for his sins which he sinned in doing that which was evil in Yahweh’s sight, in walking in the way of Jeroboam, and in his sin which he did to make Israel to sin.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Kings 16:14; 1 Kings 16:5; 1 Kings 16:27"> Now the rest of the acts of Zimri, and his treason that he committed, aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Matthew 12:25; Ephesians 4:3–5; Proverbs 28:2"> Then the people of Israel were divided into two parts: half of the people followed Tibni the son of Ginath, to make him king, and half followed Omri.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> But the people who followed Omri prevailed against the people who followed Tibni the son of Ginath; so Tibni died, and Omri reigned.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Kings 16:8; 1 Kings 16:29; 2 Chronicles 22:2"> In the thirty-first year of Asa king of Judah, Omri began to reign over Israel for twelve years. He reigned six years in Tirzah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Kings 13:32; 1 Kings 16:32; Acts 8:5–8"> He bought the hill Samaria of Shemer for two talents<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="A talent is about 30 kilograms or 66 pounds.">b</a></sup>of silver; and he built on the hill, and called the name of the city which he built, Samaria, after the name of Shemer, the owner of the hill.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Micah 6:16; 1 Kings 16:30–31; 1 Kings 14:9"> Omri did that which was evil in Yahweh’s sight, and dealt wickedly above all who were before him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="1 Kings 16:19; 1 Kings 16:13; 1 Kings 12:26–33"> For he walked in all the way of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, and in his sins with which he made Israel to sin, to provoke Yahweh, the God of Israel, to anger with their vanities.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="1 Kings 16:20; 1 Kings 16:14; 1 Kings 16:5"> Now the rest of the acts of Omri which he did, and his might that he showed, aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> So Omri slept with his fathers, and was buried in Samaria; and Ahab his son reigned in his place.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29"> In the thirty-eighth year of Asa king of Judah, Ahab the son of Omri began to reign over Israel. Ahab the son of Omri reigned over Israel in Samaria twenty-two years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="1 Kings 16:25; 1 Kings 21:25; 1 Kings 14:9"> Ahab the son of Omri did that which was evil in Yahweh’s sight above all that were before him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 Kings 17:16; 2 Kings 10:18; Judges 18:7"> As if it had been a light thing for him to walk in the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, he took as wife Jezebel the daughter of Ethbaal king of the Sidonians, and went and served Baal and worshiped him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="2 Kings 10:21; 2 Kings 10:26–27"> He raised up an altar for Baal in the house of Baal, which he had built in Samaria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="2 Kings 13:6; 1 Kings 21:25; 2 Kings 21:3"> Ahab made the Asherah; and Ahab did more yet to provoke Yahweh, the God of Israel, to anger than all the kings of Israel who were before him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Joshua 6:26; Joshua 23:14–15; Matthew 24:35"> In his days Hiel the Bethelite built Jericho. He laid its foundation with the loss of Abiram his firstborn, and set up its gates with the loss of his youngest son Segub, according to Yahweh’s word, which he spoke by Joshua the son of Nun.</span>
</div>
