<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> Is it any wonder? If the lusts of the soul, after participation with what is beautiful, are frustrated,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2"> on this ground, therefore, the temperate Joseph is praised in that by reasoning, he subdued, on reflection, the indulgence of the senses.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3"> For, although young, and ripe for sexual intercourse, he nullified by reasoning the stimulus of his emotions.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> It isn’t merely the stimulus of sensual indulgence, but that of every desire, that reasoning is able to master.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> For instance, the law says, “You shall not covet your neighbor’s wife, nor anything that belongs to your neighbor.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> Now, then, since it is the law which has forbidden us to desire, I shall much the more easily persuade you, that reasoning is able to govern our lusts, just as it does the affections which are impediments to justice.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7"> Since in what way is a solitary eater, a glutton, and a drunkard reclaimed, unless it is clear that reasoning is lord of the emotions?</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> Therefore, a man who regulates his course by the law, even if he is a lover of money, immediately puts pressure on his own disposition by lending to the needy without interest, and cancelling the debt on the seventh year.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> If a man is greedy, he is ruled by the law acting through reasoning, so that he doesn’t glean his harvest crops or vintage. In reference to other points we may perceive that it is reasoning that conquers his emotions.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> For the law conquers even affection toward parents, not surrendering virtue on their account.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> It prevails over love for one’s wife, rebuking her when she breaks the law.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> It lords it over the love of parents toward their children, for they punish them for vice. It domineers over the intimacy of friends, reproving them when wicked.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> Don’t think it is a strange assertion that reasoning can on behalf of the law conquer even enmity.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> It doesn’t allow cutting down the fruit trees of an enemy, but preserves them from the destroyers, and collects their fallen ruins.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> Reason appears to be master of the more violent emotions, like love of empire, empty boasting, and slander.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> For the temperate understanding repels all these malignant emotions, as it does wrath; for it masters even this.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> Thus Moses, when angered against Dathan and Abiram, did nothing to them in wrath, but regulated his anger by reasoning.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> For the temperate mind is able, as I said, to be superior to the emotions, and to correct some and destroy others.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> For why else did our most wise father Jacob blame Simeon and Levi for having irrationally slain the whole race of the Shechemites, saying, “Cursed be their anger!”?</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> For if reasoning didn’t possess the power of subduing angry affections, he would not have said this.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> For at the time when God created man, he implanted within him his emotions and moral nature.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> At that time he enthroned the mind above all as the holy leader, through the medium of the senses.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> He gave a law to this mind, by living according to which it will maintain a temperate, just, good, and courageous reign.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24"> How, then, a man may say, if reasoning is master of the emotions, has it no control over forgetfulness and ignorance?</span>
