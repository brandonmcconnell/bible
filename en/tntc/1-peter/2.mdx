## To grow you must eat

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Ephesians 4:31; James 1:21; James 4:11"> So then, laying aside all malignity<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="This refers to the deliberate effort to harm others. If they must ‘lay aside’ such attitudes and activities, the implication is that they have not yet, or at least not sufficiently—actually, if they are still involved in such activities they have quite a ways to go.">a</a></sup>—even all deceit and play-actings and envies, yes all malicious speaking—</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Hebrews 5:12–13; Ephesians 4:15; 2 Peter 3:18"> crave the pure spiritual ‘milk’,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Since Peter has just referred overtly to God's Word, it is generally assumed that the “milk” here is a metaphor for that Word. Peter is not saying that they are babies, just that they should imitate the craving.">b</a></sup>like newborn babies do, so that by it you may grow into salvation,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Until quite recently, based upon von Soden's apparent ascribing of some 80% attestation to the shorter reading (80% of the extant Greek manuscripts), I was rather critical of the longer form; I argued that Peter was writing to the “elect” [1:2], to the “redeemed” [1:18], to the “born again” [1:23], to a “holy priesthood” [2:5], to “believers” [2:7], to “slaves of God” [2:16]—they did indeed need to grow, but not “into salvation”. However, the evidence as presented by ECM [Editio Critica Maior] looks quite different—some 65% for the longer reading, including the best line of transmission, and joined by all three ancient versions, a not insignificant witness. Time and again Peter presents both divine sovereignty and human responsibility; God guarantees His part, we must do our part. According to 1:5 above the full manifestation [at least] of our salvation is still future. Here in 2:2 the immediately following “if indeed” would appear to allow for some uncertainty as to their spiritual condition; Peter is emphasizing their responsibility.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Psalms 34:8; Hebrews 6:5–6; Psalms 63:5"> if indeed you have tasted that the Lord is benevolent.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Psalms 118:22–23; Acts 4:11–12; 1 Peter 2:7"> Coming to Him, a living stone—rejected indeed by men but chosen by God, precious—</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Ephesians 2:20–22; 1 Peter 2:9; 1 Corinthians 3:9"> you also, as living stones, are being built<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Spiritual growth is a process, much like physical growth.">d</a></sup>into a spiritual house, a holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Much of the preaching nowadays focuses on what we are supposed to get, rather than what we are supposed to do. We need to talk more about what God is supposed to get—‘spiritual sacrifices’. Don't sacrifices cost?">e</a></sup></span>

## The Chief Cornerstone

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Isaiah 28:16; Romans 9:32–33; 1 Peter 2:4"> That is why the Scripture contains:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“Attention, I am laying in Zion a chief cornerstone,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">chosen, precious,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">and the one who rests his trust upon Him</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">will absolutely not be humiliated.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="The quote is from Isaiah 28:16. I suppose that the not being humiliated refers to the final Accounting.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Psalms 118:22–23; Mark 12:10–11; Acts 4:11–12"> So then, this recompense is for you who believe, but to those who disobey,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Instead of “disobey”, perhaps 8% of the Greek manuscripts have ‘disbelieve’ (as in NIV, NASB, TEV, etc.).">g</a></sup>“the stone that the builders rejected is just the one that became the chief cornerstone,”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="The quote is from Psalm 118:22.">h</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Isaiah 8:14; 1 Corinthians 1:23; Romans 9:32–33"> also “a stone of stumbling and a rock of offense”;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="The quote is from Isaiah 8:14. One is reminded of the Sovereign's words in Matthew 21:44. “Whoever falls on this stone will be broken to pieces; but upon whomever it falls, it will grind him to powder.”">i</a></sup>being disobedient to the Word, they stumble, to which, indeed, they were appointed.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Were they ‘appointed’ to stumble, a direct and necessary consequence of being disobedient to the Word, or were they ‘appointed’ to be disobedient? If the latter, this would become one of a very few passages that overtly point to ‘double predestination’ or preterition (that the lost are predestined to be lost). The answer hinges on the grammar, the antecedent of the pronoun ‘which’—is it the finite verb ‘stumble’ or the participle ‘disobey’? Being higher in rank, the finite form presumably takes precedence. Also, since there are hundreds of passages that clearly teach human responsibility, I take the former option to be the preferred interpretation.">j</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## A royal priesthood

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Exodus 19:5–6; Deuteronomy 7:6; Revelation 1:6"> But you are a chosen family, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a private-property people, so that you may proclaim the excellencies of Him who called you out of darkness into His marvelous light;</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Hosea 2:23; Hebrews 4:16; Hosea 1:9–10"> who formerly were not a people but now are God's people, who had not obtained mercy but now have obtained mercy.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Peter alludes to a number of OT passages, including Hosea 2:23. Presumably it is in this world, this life, that we are to proclaim God's excellencies—which raises the question: how much time do we spend doing that?">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 John 2:15–17; Galatians 5:24; 1 Peter 4:2"> Dear ones, I urge you to abstain from the lusts of the flesh, that war against the soul,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="When we indulge the flesh our soul is adversely affected; to ‘indulge’ is more than normal, necessary use.">l</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Peter 3:16; Matthew 5:16; Titus 2:7–8"> keeping your way of life among the nations praiseworthy (you being foreigners and sojourners), so that wherein they speak against you as evildoers, in spite of the praiseworthy deeds they have observed,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="The critics have already observed the good deeds—they know their criticism is false.">m</a></sup>they may glorify God in the day of visitation.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="What “day of visitation” might this be? Is it a personal visitation wherein God touches a critic, wakes him up and he glorifies God; or is it the Day of judgment where ‘every knee will bow and every tongue confess’, only it will be too late to do them any good? In either event, we are to live in such a way that we do not furnish any pretext to those who are looking for ways to ‘justify’ their unbelief.">n</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Respect civil authority

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Titus 3:1; Romans 13:1–7; 1 Timothy 2:1–2"> Therefore subordinate yourselves to every human institution because of the Lord, whether to a king, as being in authority,</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Romans 13:3–4"> or to governors, as being sent by him, both for punishment of evildoers and for praise of good-doers.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="But what happens if the ‘government’ does just the opposite, persecutes those who fear God and protects the corrupt? We have spiritual ‘weapons’ (authority) that we should learn how to use, but the time may come when we must say with Peter, “We ought to obey God rather than men” (Acts 5:29), and take the consequences.">o</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Peter 3:17; Titus 2:8; Matthew 7:26"> Because such is the will of God, to silence the ignorance of foolish people by doing good:</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Galatians 5:13; Romans 6:22; Romans 6:18"> as free (not using the freedom as a cover for evil), yet as slaves of God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Proverbs 24:21; Romans 12:10; Romans 13:7"> Respect everyone;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="Since every human being carries the ‘image of God’, that image should be respected (even when it is scarcely recognizable).">p</a></sup>love the brotherhood; fear God; honor the king.</span>
</div>

## A word for servants

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Titus 2:9–10; Ephesians 6:5–7; Colossians 3:22–25"> Servants, be submissive to your masters with all respect, not only to the good and gentle but also to the unscrupulous.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="Slavery was a normal aspect of the culture at that time. <span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Matthew 5:10–12; 1 Peter 3:14–17; 1 Peter 2:20"> For this is commendable, if because of conscience toward God someone endures grief, suffering unjustly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Peter 2:19; 1 Peter 3:17; Matthew 5:10–12"> For what credit is it if upon sinning and being beaten you endure patiently? But if upon doing good and suffering you endure patiently, this is commendable before God.</span>

<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Isaiah 53:6; Ezekiel 34:6; Luke 15:4–6"> Yes, you were like straying sheep, but now you have returned to the Shepherd and Overseer of our<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-w" id="fnref-w" data-fn="The evidence is split between “our” and ‘your’; both make good sense, but I take it that the best line of transmission reads “our”, inclusive.">w</a></sup>souls.</span>
