## Jesus denies divorce

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Mark 10:2; Hebrews 3:9; Matthew 16:1"> The Pharisees also came to Him to test Him, saying to Him, “Is it permissible for a man to divorce his wife for just any cause?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Genesis 2:23; Genesis 1:27; Genesis 5:2"> So in answer He said to them: “Have you never read that the Creator at the beginning made them a male and a female?</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Genesis 2:21–24; 1 Corinthians 7:4; 1 Corinthians 7:2"> And He said, ‘For this reason a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two shall be turned into one flesh.’<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“Two”—not three, four, five, etc. “The two” refers to one man and one woman—not two men, a man and an animal, a woman and a demon, or whatever. <span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Hebrews 13:4; Mark 10:9; 1 Corinthians 7:10–14"> So then, they are no longer two, but one flesh. Therefore, what God joined together let not man separate!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Deuteronomy 24:1–4; Matthew 5:31; Mark 10:4"> They say to Him, “Why then did Moses command to give a certificate of divorce and to dismiss her?”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="They distorted the Text: Moses did not command divorce. The Lord corrects their distortion.">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Malachi 2:13–14; Genesis 2:24; Mark 10:5"> He says to them: “Because of your hard-heartedness Moses permitted you to divorce your wives, but from the beginning it has not been so.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Matthew 5:32; Luke 16:18; 1 Corinthians 7:39"> And I say to you that whoever divorces his wife, except for fornication, and marries another commits adultery; and whoever marries a divorcee commits adultery.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“Fornication” is distinct from “adultery” and does not include it. <span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Corinthians 7:32–35; Proverbs 19:13–14; Proverbs 21:19"> His disciples say to Him, “If that's the situation of a man with his wife, it is better not to marry!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Why the strong reaction from the disciples? <span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Corinthians 7:17; 1 Corinthians 7:35; 1 Corinthians 7:7–9"> So He said to them: “Not all can assimilate this word, but those to whom it has been given.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="The demonstrative pronoun ‘this’ is probably cataphoric, referring forward to the content of verse 12.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Corinthians 7:32–38; 1 Corinthians 9:5; Isaiah 56:3–4"> For there are eunuchs who were born that way, from their mother's womb; and there are eunuchs who were castrated by men; and there are eunuchs who castrated themselves for the sake of the kingdom of the heavens. He who is able to assimilate it, let him assimilate.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="What in the world do ‘eunuchs’ have to do with divorce and remarriage? ## It is difficult for a rich person to enter the Kingdom

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Mark 10:17–30; John 3:15; Jude 1:21"> And then, a certain one came and said to Him, “Good teacher, what good thing must I do so that I may have eternal life?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Leviticus 18:5; Luke 10:26–28; Galatians 3:11–13"> But He said to him: “Why do you call me good? No one is good except One, God.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Jesus is not denying that He is good. He is challenging the man's opinion about Himself. The man was not recognizing Jesus to be God—if he had, Jesus would not have objected. Perhaps 1% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, omit “Good” before “teacher” in verse 16 and have Jesus saying, “Why do you ask me about what is good? There is One who is good” here in verse 17 (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.). The minority reading makes Matthew contradict Mark 10:18 and Luke 18:9; it probably originated in Egypt, that was dominated by Gnosticism.">g</a></sup>But if you want to enter into the life, keep the commandments.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Deuteronomy 5:16–21; Exodus 20:12–17; Mark 10:19"> He says to Him, “Which ones?”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Did he think that some were not necessary?">h</a></sup>So Jesus said: “ ‘You shall not murder,’ ‘You shall not commit adultery,’ ‘You shall not steal,’ ‘You shall not give false witness,’</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Leviticus 19:18; Exodus 20:12; Matthew 22:39"> ‘Honor your father and your mother,’ and ‘You shall love your neighbor as yourself.’ ”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Galatians 3:24; Mark 10:20–21; Philippians 3:6"> The young man says to Him: “I have kept all these things since my youth.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="“Since my youth” is omitted by 1% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">i</a></sup>What do I still lack?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Mark 10:21; Luke 18:22; Luke 12:33"> Jesus said to him, “If you want to be perfect, go sell your possessions and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come follow me.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Matthew 6:24; Ezekiel 33:31; Matthew 13:22"> So upon hearing that word the young man went away sorrowful, for he had great possessions.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Jesus emphasized the commandment about loving one's neighbor because that was precisely the one that the young man was not keeping. He went away sorrowful because Jesus made him see that he was not keeping it, and worse yet, was not prepared to keep it.">j</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Timothy 6:9–10; Matthew 13:22; Mark 10:23"> Then Jesus said to His disciples: “Assuredly I say to you that only with difficulty will a rich person enter into the kingdom of the heavens.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="John 5:44; Luke 18:25; Mark 10:24–25"> And again I say to you, it is easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle than for a rich person to enter into the kingdom of God.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="This seemingly difficult statement has given rise to several attempts to avoid the literal meaning. However, since in verse 26 Jesus says it is “impossible” we may stay with the literal meaning. Jesus had already said that one cannot serve God and money, so someone who “trusts in riches” (Mark 10:24, as in 99.5% of the Greek manuscripts) simply cannot be saved (unless he abandons that trust).">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Romans 10:13; Matthew 24:22; Romans 11:5–7"> Upon hearing it His disciples were really flabbergasted saying, “Who then can be saved?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Jeremiah 32:27; Luke 1:37; Job 42:2"> So looking at them Jesus said, “With men this is impossible, but with God all things are possible.”</span>
</div>

## It pays to serve Jesus

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Philippians 3:8; Luke 18:28; Luke 5:11"> Then Peter answered and said to Him: “See, we have left all and followed you. So what will we have?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Luke 22:28–30; 1 Corinthians 6:2–3; Matthew 25:31"> So Jesus said to them: “Assuredly I say to you that in the Regeneration,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="I take it that Jesus is referring to His millennial reign, the Messianic Kingdom, when the earth will be ‘regenerated’, being restored to conditions reminiscent of Eden. For instance, if He does not regenerate the magnetic field that surrounds it pretty soon, life on this planet will become less and less viable.">l</a></sup>when the Son of the Man sits on the throne of His glory, you who have followed me will also sit on twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="I wonder who will occupy the Iscariot's throne? I see no reason to doubt that the Lord is being literal here.">m</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Luke 18:29–30; Mark 10:29–30; Philippians 3:8"> And, everyone who left houses or brothers or sisters or father or mother or wife<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Perhaps 1.5% of the Greek manuscripts, of inferior quality, omit “or wife” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.)—the idea of having 100 wives was no doubt objectionable. <li id="fn-f"><a href="#fnref-f" className="fnb">f</a> <span className="fnr">19:12</span> What in the world do ‘eunuchs’ have to do with divorce and remarriage? Well, do eunuchs have sexual relations? A eunuch castrated by others was a victim of the actions of others. We also may be victimized by others, and be obliged to take the consequences.</li>

<li id="fn-i"><a href="#fnref-i" className="fnb">i</a> <span className="fnr">19:20</span> “Since my youth” is omitted by 1% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).</li>
