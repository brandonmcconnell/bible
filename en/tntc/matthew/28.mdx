## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">28:2</span> Perhaps 0.5% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, omit “from the door” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">28:2</span> The stone was not removed to let Jesus out, but to let the witnesses in.</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">28:5</span> Comparing this account with Mark 16:5-7 we conclude that the women did not see the angel outside on the stone—he only appeared to them inside the tomb. And if a shining angel had been on the stone Magdalene would not have assumed that the body had been stolen (John 20:2). If I had been one of those guards I think I would have converted on the spot—surely some of them believed and later they gave their eyewitness account to the Christian community.</li>
<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">28:6</span> Perhaps 0.5% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, omit “the Lord” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).</li>
<li id="fn-e"><a href="#fnref-e" className="fnb">e</a> <span className="fnr">28:7</span> It almost sounds like he was glad to get it off his chest. I wonder why.</li>
<li id="fn-f"><a href="#fnref-f" className="fnb">f</a> <span className="fnr">28:9</span> Some 12% of the Greek manuscripts omit this first clause (as in NIV, NASB, &#91;LB&#93;, TEV, etc.). Mark 16:9 makes clear that the first appearance was to Magdalene.</li>
<li id="fn-g"><a href="#fnref-g" className="fnb">g</a> <span className="fnr">28:10</span> The angel said “disciples”, but Jesus said “brothers”. It may be that He was referring precisely to His half-brothers James, Jude, etc. They would not have been in the upper room with the Eleven, and so did not see Him at that time.</li>
<li id="fn-h"><a href="#fnref-h" className="fnb">h</a> <span className="fnr">28:11</span> “Some”—so where were the rest? Those who were converted, as I suppose, would not want to be part of the cover-up.</li>
<li id="fn-i"><a href="#fnref-i" className="fnb">i</a> <span className="fnr">28:17</span> The Text actually says “they doubted”—it seems improbable that all of them would doubt (after all, they had seen Him at least twice already), so most translations render “some”. Although verse 16 refers specifically to the eleven, there may have been others (like His half-brothers, verse 10) along who had not seen Him yet.</li>
<li id="fn-j"><a href="#fnref-j" className="fnb">j</a> <span className="fnr">28:19</span> The familiar ‘therefore’ is found in perhaps 5% of the Greek manuscripts.</li>
<li id="fn-k"><a href="#fnref-k" className="fnb">k</a> <span className="fnr">28:19</span> Our Lord defines the Trinity here. According to Greek grammar the use of ‘and’ plus the definite article with items in a series makes clear that the items are distinct entities. So “the Father” is different from “the Son” is different from “the Holy Spirit”. So we have three persons. But He also said, “in the name”, singular, not ‘names’. So we have only one name. God is one ‘name’ or essence subsisting in three persons.</li>
<li id="fn-l"><a href="#fnref-l" className="fnb">l</a> <span className="fnr">28:20</span> The ‘you’ here refers to the eleven (see verse 16), so they were to pass down all the commands that Jesus had given them. To be a disciple of Jesus you have to do everything that Jesus had commanded the eleven to do—this includes healing and casting out demons, as well as preaching the Gospel.</li>
<li id="fn-m"><a href="#fnref-m" className="fnb">m</a> <span className="fnr">28:20</span> Since the age has not ended, Jesus is still with us. Praise God!</li>
</ol>
