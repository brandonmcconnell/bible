## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">18:6</span> ‘Into’ not ‘in’. The Lord Jesus always said “believe into &#91;Greek εις&#93; Me”, never “believe in &#91;Greek εν&#93; Me”. (In John 3:15 a very few Greek manuscripts of inferior quality have “in”, against 99.5% that have “into”.) People believe in Santa Claus, the Easter bunny, the goodness of man, or whatever, but it does not change their lives. Similarly there are many who say they believe in Jesus, but it makes no difference in their lives. You have to believe into Him—commitment and identification are involved—it is a move from being outside to being inside.</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">18:6</span> It is preferable to suffer an ignominious physical death than to destroy the faith of a child who believes into Jesus. What is worse than an ignominious physical death? Spiritual death—in verses 8-9 Jesus refers overtly to hell fire. If you send a child to hell, you go too! What about all the teachers who have dedicated themselves to destroying the faith of their students?</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">18:9</span> I believe this is literal, but largely hypothetical—by the time someone realizes what is happening, it is usually too late. Maybe that is why we are supposed to exhort one another daily (Hebrews 3:13).</li>
<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">18:10</span> “These little ones” refers to the literal children who were in the room. In verse 2 Jesus called one over, so it was obviously in the room, but the playmates would naturally stop to see what was going to happen, so there were several in the room. This verse suggests the existence of guardian angels.</li>
<li id="fn-e"><a href="#fnref-e" className="fnb">e</a> <span className="fnr">18:11</span> Just 1.5% of the Greek manuscripts, of inferior quality, omit verse 11 (as in NIV, &#91;NASB&#93;, LB, &#91;TEV&#93;, etc.).</li>
<li id="fn-f"><a href="#fnref-f" className="fnb">f</a> <span className="fnr">18:12</span> “In the mountains”—note that the 99 were not left in a safe fold somewhere; they were left unprotected. The shepherd takes a calculated risk.</li>
<li id="fn-g"><a href="#fnref-g" className="fnb">g</a> <span className="fnr">18:14</span> The Lord's statement here is round about, not direct—I tried to reflect that ‘round-aboutness’.</li>
<li id="fn-h"><a href="#fnref-h" className="fnb">h</a> <span className="fnr">18:17</span> In other words, such a person is to be excommunicated, ostracized.</li>
<li id="fn-i"><a href="#fnref-i" className="fnb">i</a> <span className="fnr">18:18</span> The tense of the Greek verb phrase here is a periphrastic future perfect, passive voice (so also in 16:18). <li id="fn-k"><a href="#fnref-k" className="fnb">k</a> <span className="fnr">18:20</span> The Greek text has “into my name”, not “in”—commitment is involved.</li>
