## John needs reassuring

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Mark 6:17; Matthew 14:3; Matthew 9:14"> But John, hearing in prison about the works of the Messiah, sent two of his disciples</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="John 11:27; Malachi 3:1; Deuteronomy 18:15–18"> to say to Him, “Are you the Coming One, or do we look for another?”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="John was in prison, which was contrary to his expectations; like all the others he expected Jesus to set up the Kingdom right then. In answer the Lord says, in effect, that the Kingdom is not based on our expectations.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> By way of answer Jesus said to them: “Go and report to John the things that you hear and see:</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Isaiah 35:4–6; Isaiah 61:1–3; Luke 4:18"> blind regain sight and lame walk, lepers are cleansed and deaf hear, dead are raised and the poor are evangelized.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Isaiah 8:14–15; 1 Corinthians 2:14; Matthew 26:31"> And, blessed be whoever is not offended at Me!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Verse 6 was directed specifically at John. When Jesus went to John to be baptized, John knew perfectly well that he was looking at the Messiah, but now, because of his circumstances, he is in doubt. In effect, Jesus is saying, “Whatever you do, do not rebel!” And He is still saying so to us: when your circumstances are painful and unexplained, do not rebel!">b</a></sup></span>

## Jesus commends John the Baptizer

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="James 1:6; John 5:35; Mark 1:3–5"> So as they departed Jesus began to say to the crowds concerning John: “What did you go out into the wilderness to see, a reed shaken by the wind?</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Matthew 3:4; 2 Kings 1:8; Zechariah 13:4"> But what did you go out to see, a man clothed in soft garments? Actually, those who wear soft material are in kings' houses.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Luke 1:76; Matthew 14:5; Matthew 21:24–26"> But really, what did you go out to see, a prophet? Yes, I tell you, and far more than a prophet.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Malachi 3:1; Isaiah 40:3; Malachi 4:5"> For this is he of whom it is written:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">‘Behold, I send my messenger before your face,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">who will prepare your way ahead of you.’</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="See Malachi 3:1.">c</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Luke 7:28; Ephesians 3:8; Matthew 3:11"> Assuredly I say to you, among those born of women there has not arisen a greater than John the Baptizer; but he who is least in the kingdom of the heavens is greater than he.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Evidently, as forerunner of the Kingdom John was not part of it—the Kingdom was rejected at that time; both forerunner and King were killed—those who participate in the actual future Kingdom will be more privileged. “Born of women” excludes Adam. Men like Noah, Abraham, Moses, Daniel would be of equal standing, just not “greater”. But those who live during the Messianic Kingdom (Millennium) will be more privileged than all except Adam, because the earth will return to conditions similar to Eden.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Luke 16:16; Luke 13:24; Ephesians 6:11–13"> But from the days of John the Baptizer until now the kingdom of the heavens is being aggressive and aggressive people are seizing upon it.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Note that Jesus is referring to a period of about two years. With the King physically present and giving a living demonstration of the Kingdom, the Kingdom was being more aggressive in this world than it had been since the time of Moses. In the present tense of a Greek verb a single form serves for both the middle and passive voices—the verb βιαζεται has usually been translated as passive in our versions, ‘suffers violence’, but I think it makes much better sense to translate it as middle, as I have done. Given the hostility of the religious leaders, it would take someone with plenty of backbone to openly side with Jesus.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Matthew 5:17–18; Romans 3:21; Acts 13:27"> For all the prophets and the Law prophesied until John,</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Malachi 4:5; Matthew 17:10–13; Mark 9:11–13"> and if you are willing to receive it, he is Elijah who is to come.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Jesus declares that the real Elijah is still going to come. John fulfilled the role for Messiah's first advent that Elijah will fill for the second.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Luke 8:8; Matthew 13:9; Mark 4:23"> He who has ears to hear, let him hear!</span>
</div>
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Luke 7:31–35; Matthew 12:34; Matthew 23:36"> “But to what shall I compare this generation? It is like children sitting in a marketplace and calling to their friends</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Isaiah 28:9–13; Isaiah 30:29; Jeremiah 9:17–20"> and saying,<br /><span className="pl pl-1">‘We played the flute for you and you did not dance;</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">we mourned for you and you did not lament.’</span></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Luke 1:15; Matthew 3:4; John 10:20"> For John came neither eating nor drinking and they say, ‘He has a demon’;</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Matthew 9:10–11; Luke 15:1–2; Luke 19:7"> the Son of the Man came eating and drinking and they say, ‘Just look, a glutton and a drunkard, a friend of tax collectors and sinners!’ Indeed, wisdom is vindicated by her children.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Instead of “her children”, just 0.5% of the Greek manuscripts, of inferior quality (objectively so), have “her works” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">g</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Jesus denounces three cities

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Luke 10:13–15; Jeremiah 8:6; 2 Timothy 2:25–26"> Then He began to reproach the cities in which most of His mighty works had been done, because they did not repent.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Ezekiel 3:6–7; Acts 12:20; Luke 9:10"> “Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! Because if the mighty works that were done in you had been done in Tyre and Sidon, they would have repented long ago in sackcloth and ashes.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Matthew 11:24; Matthew 10:15; Hebrews 10:26–31"> So I say to you, it will be more tolerable for Tyre and Sidon in the Day of judgment than for you!</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Isaiah 14:13–15; John 4:46–54; Genesis 13:13"> And you, Capernaum, ‘the one having been exalted to heaven’, you will be brought down to Hades! Because if the mighty works that were done in you had been done in Sodom, it would have remained until this day.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Matthew 10:15; Matthew 11:22; Mark 6:11"> So I say to you that it will be more tolerable for the land of Sodom in the Day of judgment than for you.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Evidently there are degrees of judgment, based on the amount of ‘light’ received. To be worse off than Sodom—help! (See Matthew 10:14-15.)">h</a></sup></span>
</div>

## “I praise You, Father”

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Matthew 21:16; Psalms 8:2; Matthew 16:17"> At that time Jesus reacted and said: “ ‘I praise You, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, that You have hidden these things from the wise and intelligent and have revealed them to ‘babes’.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Timothy 1:9; Ephesians 1:11; Job 33:13"> Yes, Father, because thus it was good to You.’<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="I take it that this was said out loud, so those around could hear.">i</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="John 14:6–9; Matthew 28:18; Luke 10:22"> “All things have been delivered to me by my Father, and no one really knows the Son except the Father; nor does anyone really know the Father except the Son, and the one to whom the Son may will to reveal Him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Matthew 11:29; John 7:37; John 6:37"> Come to me all you who are laboring and are loaded down, and I will give you rest.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Jeremiah 6:16; 1 John 2:6; Philippians 2:7–8"> Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, because I am gentle and lowly in heart, and you will find rest for your souls;</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="1 John 5:3; John 16:33; Philippians 4:13"> for my yoke is easy and my burden is light.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="To be without a yoke is not an option in this world.
