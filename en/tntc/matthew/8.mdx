## Peter's mother-in-law8:14 It has often been assumed that Matthew's account here is parallel to those in Mark 1:29-31 and Luke 4:38-39, but close attention to the contexts has convinced me that Matthew's account took place some time after that in Mark and Luke. In that event, Jesus healed the woman twice, which means that just because God heals you one time, it does not mean that you will never get sick again.

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Luke 4:38–41; Mark 1:29–34; 1 Corinthians 9:5"> Now when Jesus had come into Peter's house, He saw his mother-in-law laid up and burning with fever.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="2 Kings 13:21; Luke 4:38–39; Luke 8:54"> So He touched her hand and the fever left her; and she got up and began to serve Him.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Jesus healed the consequences as well—normally a high fever leaves you weak.">f</a></sup></span>

<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Isaiah 53:4; 1 Peter 2:24; Matthew 1:22"> so that what was spoken through Isaiah the prophet should be fulfilled, namely:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“He Himself took our infirmities and bore our diseases.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="See Isaiah 53:4. <span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Psalms 84:3; Luke 2:7; Isaiah 53:2–3"> So Jesus says to him, “The foxes have dens and the birds of the air have nests, but the Son of the Man does not have where He may lay His head.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="That is what the Text says, “the Son of the Man”, which appears to be a phrase coined by the Lord Jesus to refer to Himself; the phrase doesn't make very good sense in English, at first glance, but if “the man” refers to pristine Adam and “the son” to an only pristine descendant, it makes great sense. ## Jesus controls any number of demons

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Luke 8:26–39; Mark 5:1–20; Acts 10:38"> Upon His coming to the other side, to the region of the Gergesenes,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Some 98% of the Greek manuscripts read ‘Gergesenes’, rather than ‘Gadarenes’. It seems more likely that there was a village named ‘Gergesa’ nearby, whose people came and asked Jesus to leave. For a more detailed discussion, please see the Appendix: The ‘Legion’ and the pigs; where was it?">n</a></sup>two demonized men<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="As a tax collector, numerical precision was important to Matthew. Where other Gospels may select an individual as sufficient for the narrative, Matthew specifies that there were actually two (also blind men and donkeys).">o</a></sup>met Him, coming out from the tombs, very dangerous, so that no one could pass that way.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Mark 1:24; Luke 4:34; James 2:19"> And then—they cried out saying: “What do you have to do with us, Jesus, Son of God? Have you come here to torment us before the time?”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="The demons knew who Jesus really was, and also about their own destiny. <span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Deuteronomy 14:8; Leviticus 11:7; Mark 5:11"> Now a good way off from them there was a herd of many pigs feeding.</span>

<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Mark 5:13; Acts 2:23; Revelation 20:7"> So He said to them, “Go!” And coming out they went off into the herd of pigs. And then—the whole herd of pigs rushed down the steep bank into the sea and died in the water!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="I really doubt that the demons did it—why would they want to destroy their new ‘house’? <span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Luke 8:34–36; Mark 5:14–16; Acts 19:15–17"> So the swineherds ran away, and going into the town they told everything, including about the demonized men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Acts 16:39; Luke 8:37–39; Luke 5:8"> And then, the whole town went out to meet with Jesus; and upon seeing Him they begged Him to depart from their borders.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="Apparently Jesus never went back there; they did not want Him and that was that (I doubt that the inhabitants were Jews).
