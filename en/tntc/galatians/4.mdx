## Sons or slaves

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Galatians 4:23; Galatians 4:29; Genesis 24:2–3"> To proceed, as long as the heir is a child, he is no different from a slave, though he is owner of all,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2"> but is under guardians and stewards until the time appointed by the father.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Colossians 2:20; Colossians 2:8; Galatians 4:9"> Similarly, when we were children we were in slavery under the basic principles of the world.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="The only true freedom available in this life is to be a slave of Jesus Christ. In John 8:34 the Lord Jesus said, “whoever commits sin is a slave of sin”, and in verse 36, “if the Son makes you free, you will be free indeed”. Sin gives Satan an entrance, and he has various instruments for binding people (see verse 8 below).">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Isaiah 7:14; John 1:14; Romans 1:3"> But at just the right time God sent out His Son, born of a woman, born under law,</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Hebrews 9:15; Ephesians 1:5; 1 Peter 1:18–20"> so that He might redeem those under law, that we might receive the adoption as sons.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Romans 8:15–17; Romans 5:5; Romans 8:9"> And because you are sons,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="If you have the Holy Spirit in your heart, it is because you have been adopted into God's family; you are a ‘son’.">b</a></sup>God sent out the Spirit of His Son into your<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Perhaps 6% of the Greek manuscripts read ‘our’ instead of “your”, setting up a conflict with the verb “you are” at the beginning of the verse (as in NIV, NASB, TEV, etc.).">c</a></sup>hearts, calling, “Abba, Father”.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Father” is a translation of “Abba”.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Romans 8:16–17; Galatians 3:29–2; Galatians 3:26"> Therefore you are no longer a slave but a son,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Here Paul switches from plural to singular.">e</a></sup>and if a son, also an heir of God through Christ.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Evidently 1½% of the Greek manuscripts, of inferior quality, read “through God” instead of “of God through Christ” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.). ## Paul's concern

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Corinthians 8:4; Ephesians 2:11–12; 2 Thessalonians 1:8"> But at one time indeed, when you did not know God, you were slaves to those that by nature are not gods.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="The reference is presumably to demons. Since the ‘world’ (verse 3) is controlled by Satan, his agents impose the ‘principles’.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Corinthians 8:3; Galatians 3:3; Romans 8:3"> But now that you know God—better, are known by God—how can you turn back to those weak, yes decidedly inferior, basic principles, to which you wish to be enslaved all over again?</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Colossians 2:16–17; Romans 14:5; Leviticus 23:1–44"> You are observing days and months and seasons and years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 3:5; Philippians 2:16; Galatians 2:2"> I fear for you, lest somehow I have labored over you in vain.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Corinthians 2:5; 1 Kings 22:4; Genesis 34:15"> Brothers, I plead with you, become as I am, for I became like you. You did me no wrong.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Corinthians 2:3; 2 Corinthians 12:7–10; 2 Corinthians 11:30"> Rather, you know that I preached the Gospel to you the first time in spite of a physical infirmity.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="It will be observed that I have rendered “in spite of” a physical infirmity, whereas most versions have “because of”, in accordance with the normal meaning of the grammatical construction here (δια with the accusative is the usual way to express cause). What troubles me is that the normal meaning does not make much sense here; how could an infirmity be the cause of his preaching the Gospel to them? A counter-cause would make better sense, but I am not aware of any way to express it in Greek; the closest thing appears to be καιπερ ‘although’, that occurs all of five times in the NT In spoken English ‘raise’ and ‘raze’ are pronounced the same, but have opposite meanings, and this sort of thing is not rare in language. An ironic inflection reverses the meaning of what is said. I assume that something of the sort has occurred in this verse, and I have given the assumed intent.">h</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Matthew 10:40; 1 Thessalonians 2:13; Hebrews 13:2"> Also, you did not despise or recoil at my physical trial, but you received me as an angel of God, even as Christ Jesus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Romans 5:2; 1 John 3:16–18; Colossians 4:13"> So what about your blessedness? Because I can testify that if it were possible you would have plucked out your own eyes and given them to me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Amos 5:10; Proverbs 9:8; John 8:45"> Have I now become your enemy by telling you the truth?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="In this fallen world, telling the truth does have a habit of making enemies.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Romans 16:18; 2 Corinthians 11:13–15; 2 Peter 2:3"> They zealously court you<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Paul contrasts himself with the proselytizers: he tells them the truth, even if unpleasant; they start by ‘buttering up’ their intended victims, but then move to create a dependency so they can control them.">j</a></sup>—not for good but intending to isolate you, so that you may seek them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Philippians 2:12; Revelation 3:19; Philippians 1:27"> Now it is good to be zealous in a good thing at any time, and not only when I am present with you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Ephesians 4:13; Romans 8:29; Romans 13:14"> My little children, for whom I am again in travail until Christ is formed in you</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 2:17–18; 1 Corinthians 4:19–21; 1 Thessalonians 3:9"> —I wish I could be present with you now and change my tone; because I am perplexed about you!</span>
</div>
</div>

## ‘Hagar’ VS ‘Sarah’

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Galatians 3:10; Galatians 3:23–24; Romans 7:5–6"> Tell me, you who desire to be under law, do you not heed that law?</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Genesis 16:15; Genesis 21:10; Genesis 21:1–2"> For it is written that Abraham had two sons: one by the slave woman and one by the free woman.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Hebrews 11:11; Romans 9:7–8; Galatians 4:28–29"> However, the one by the slave woman was born according to the flesh, while the one by the free woman was through the promise.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="For Sarah to conceive required a miracle.">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Genesis 21:9–13; Hebrews 10:15–18; Genesis 16:8"> I will now allegorize them—these are two covenants: one is from Mount Sinai, bearing children into slavery, which is ‘Hagar’</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Luke 19:44; Acts 1:11; Galatians 1:17"> (for this ‘Hagar’ is Mount Sinai in Arabia<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="I do not know Paul's definition of ‘Arabia’, but what the maps call ‘Mt. Sinai’ probably is not the real one; consider: When Moses fled from Pharaoh he stopped in Midian (Exodus 2:15). Midian lies on the east side of the eastern ‘rabbit-ear’ of the Red Sea (the Gulf of Aqaba), in present day Saudi Arabia. It has never been part of the so-called ‘Sinai Peninsula’. It was at “Horeb, the mountain of God” that Moses saw the ‘burning bush’ (Exodus 3:1), and in verse 12 God tells Moses: “when you have brought the people out of Egypt, you shall serve God on this mountain”. Mt. Horeb has always been in Midian. For a complete discussion please see the Appendix: “Where is Mt. Sinai?”.">l</a></sup>and corresponds to the present day Jerusalem,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="The Jerusalem of Paul's day was in slavery to Rome.">m</a></sup>and is in slavery with her children);</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Hebrews 12:22; Philippians 3:20; Micah 4:1–2"> while the ‘Jerusalem’ that is above is free, which is the mother of us all.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Perhaps 3% of the Greek manuscripts omit “all” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">n</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Isaiah 54:1–5; Psalms 113:9; Ruth 4:14–16"> For it is written:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“Rejoice, barren one, who does not give birth;</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">break forth and shout, you who have no labor pains;</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">because the abandoned woman has many more children</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">than she who has the husband.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="The quote is from Isaiah 54:1—in that chapter Jehovah is telling Israel, His abandoned ‘wife’, that He is going to take her back, restore her, and her future felicity will make her forget the pain of rejection. However, that restoration will presumably be based on the new covenant (Jeremiah 31), as is Christ's Gospel.">o</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Galatians 4:23; Romans 9:8–9; Romans 4:13–18"> Now we,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="Perhaps 8% of the Greek manuscripts read ‘you’ for “we” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">p</a></sup>brothers, like Isaac, are children of promise.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Genesis 21:9; Romans 8:13; Galatians 5:11"> But, it is the same now as it was then: the one born according to flesh persecutes the one born according to Spirit.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Genesis 21:10–12; John 8:35; Romans 8:15–17"> However, what does the Scripture say? “Expel the slave woman and her son, because the son of the slave woman absolutely must not inherit with the son of the free woman!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="The quote is from Genesis 21:10, where Sarah is demanding this of Abraham, but God backs her up (verse 12).">q</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="John 8:36; John 1:12–13; Galatians 5:1"> So then, brothers, we are not children of a slave woman, but of the free woman.</span>
</div>
