## Paul commends Philemon

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Romans 1:8; Ephesians 1:16; 2 Thessalonians 1:3"> I always thank my God as I make mention of you (sg) in my prayers,</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Colossians 1:4; Ephesians 1:15; 1 Corinthians 16:1"> hearing of your love and faith that you have toward the Lord Jesus, and for all the saints,</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Philippians 1:9–11; 2 Peter 1:8; Colossians 1:9"> so that the sharing of your (sg) faith may become powerful through a full understanding of every good thing that is among you (pl) who are in Christ Jesus;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Verse 6 probably reads quite differently in your version of the Bible. Were you aware that the “your” refers to Philemon while the “you” refers to the congregation? [The MSS evidence is about evenly divided between “you” and ‘us’, but I follow the best line of transmission, as in AV and NKJV.] The verse usually ends with “you/us in Christ Jesus”, but the preposition is properly “into”, which does not make for smooth English. The reference is to those who have believed into Jesus (as He Himself invariably said)—they were outside but have moved inside. So Paul is referring to a group of regenerated people and to the aggregate of ‘good things’ to be found among them; which I assume would certainly include the gifts of the Spirit, as well as His fruit. As Philemon's understanding of these resources increases, the sharing of his faith will become more powerful, and for this Paul prays. At the end of the verse, perhaps 2% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, omit “Jesus” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Corinthians 7:13; 1 Thessalonians 3:9; 2 Corinthians 7:4"> yes, we have much thanksgiving<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Instead of “thanksgiving”, some 12% of the Greek manuscripts have ‘joy’, as in most versions.">e</a></sup>and encouragement because of your (sg) love, in that the hearts of the saints have been refreshed through you, brother.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Paul intercedes for Onesimus

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 2:6; 2 Corinthians 10:8; 2 Corinthians 3:12"> Now then, though I have all boldness in Christ to order you (sg) to do what is right,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Paul starts out by asserting his authority, but softens it with an emotional appeal, appealing to Philemon's emotions in various ways.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Corinthians 5:20; Ephesians 4:1; Romans 12:1"> I—being none other than Paul the old man and now also a prisoner for Jesus Christ—would rather appeal to you, for love's sake.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Colossians 4:9; 1 Corinthians 4:15; 1 Timothy 1:2"> I appeal to you on behalf of the son whom I begot while in my chains, Onesimus,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="When not a proper name, the word is an adjective meaning ‘useful’.">g</a></sup>whom I am sending back</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Timothy 4:11; Job 30:1–2; 1 Peter 2:10"> —formerly he was unprofitable to you, but now he is profitable, both to you and to me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Matthew 6:14–15; Jeremiah 31:20; Mark 11:25"> So receive<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Some 1.5% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, omit “receive”, as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc., which obliges them to adjust their rendering accordingly.">h</a></sup>him, who is my very heart</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Philippians 1:7; Ephesians 3:1; 1 Corinthians 16:17"> —I would have liked to keep him with me, so that he might minister to me in your place, while I am in chains for the Gospel,</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Corinthians 9:7; 1 Chronicles 29:17; 1 Peter 5:2–3"> but I did not want to do anything without your consent, so that your contribution might be voluntary, not as an imposition.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Genesis 50:20; Genesis 45:5–8; Isaiah 20:6"> Perhaps this is why he was removed<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="“Removed” is passive; Paul implies that God was behind the flight of Onesimus.">i</a></sup>for a while, so that you would have him forever</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Colossians 3:22; Matthew 23:8; 1 Corinthians 7:22"> —no longer as a slave, but more than a slave, a beloved brother, very much to me but even more to you, both in flesh and in the Lord.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="What does “both in flesh and in the Lord” mean? Onesimus is now a brother in the Lord, but was he also Philemon's physical brother? But if a brother, how did he become his slave? I don't know. If I had to guess I would imagine that Philemon's father begot Onesimus of a slave woman. Whatever the messy consequences of people's lives without Christ, this letter illustrates nicely how the Gospel can straighten things out.">j</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Paul asks for obedience

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Corinthians 8:23; Matthew 10:40; Philemon 1:12"> So if you consider me a partner, receive him as if it were me.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Verses 17-19 are often used as an illustration of substitutionary atonement, wherein Philemon would represent the Father, Paul the Son, and Onesimus the sinner—what Onesimus owes is charged to Paul, so Onesimus can go free. (Of course the second half of verse 19 doesn't fit; the Father doesn't owe His life to the Son.)">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Isaiah 53:4–7"> If he wronged you or owes anything, charge it to me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Galatians 6:11; Galatians 5:2; 1 Corinthians 4:15"> I, Paul, am writing with my own hand, “I will repay” (rather than say to you that you actually owe me your very self!).<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Dear me, Paul builds such a strong case that there is really no way that Philemon can refuse!">l</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="3 John 1:4; Philemon 1:7; Philemon 1:12"> Yes brother, let me have this benefit from you in the Lord; refresh my heart in the Lord.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Instead of “the Lord”, some 20% of the Greek manuscripts have ‘Christ’ (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">m</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Thessalonians 3:4; 2 Corinthians 2:3; 2 Corinthians 7:16"> Being confident of your obedience, I write to you, knowing that you (sg) will do even more than I say.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Corinthians 1:11; Philippians 2:24; 2 John 1:12"> But meanwhile, do prepare a guest room for me, because I hope that through your (pl) prayers I will be graciously bestowed on you (pl).<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Might verse 22 be a veiled threat, that Paul could show up to check up on Philemon?
