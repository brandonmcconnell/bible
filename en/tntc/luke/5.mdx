## A miraculous catch

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="John 21:6; Matthew 17:27"> When He had finished speaking, He said to Simon, “Put out into the deep and let down your nets for a catch.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="John 2:5; John 15:14; Luke 6:46–48"> And in answer Simon said to Him, “Master, we have worked hard the whole night and caught nothing, but based on your word I will let down the net.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Peter was the professional here, and figured he knew better than Jesus, but he does obey. However, Jesus had said to let down ‘nets’ (pl), but Peter let down only one. 2% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, read ‘nets’, to be followed by most modern versions. (Actually, Jesus put ‘let down’ in the plural, so there was at least one other in the boat, unless it was really His intention that both boats should go out.)">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Corinthians 15:58; John 21:6–11; Ecclesiastes 11:6"> When they had done this, they caught such a large number of fish that their net began to tear.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Galatians 6:2; Philippians 4:3; Acts 11:25"> So they signaled to their partners who were in the other boat to come help them; they came and filled both the boats, so that they began to sink.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Isaiah 6:5; Job 42:5–6; Revelation 1:17"> Upon experiencing this Simon Peter fell at Jesus' knees,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Jesus was still sitting in the boat, only now surrounded by fish, probably up to His knees.">c</a></sup>saying, “Depart from me, Lord, for I am a sinful man!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Do you find Peter's reaction to be curious? No doubt he felt pretty ‘small’ at that point.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Luke 4:32; Psalms 8:8; Mark 9:6"> Because astonishment gripped him and all who were with him at the haul of fish which they had caught;</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Matthew 4:19; Matthew 13:47; Ezekiel 47:9–10"> yes, this included James and John, sons of Zebedee, who were partners with Simon. Then Jesus said to Simon, “Don't be afraid; from now on you will be catching people.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Matthew 4:20; Luke 5:28; Matthew 19:27"> Pulling the boats up on the shore, they left it all<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="The hired men took care of the fish, etc. No doubt most of the crowd had stayed to watch, so getting rid of the fish probably was not a problem.">e</a></sup>and followed Him.</span>

## A leper—the proof

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Psalms 50:15; Luke 17:16; Matthew 8:2–4"> Now it happened, while He was in one of the towns—a man full of leprosy! Upon seeing Jesus he fell on his face and begged Him, saying, “Lord, if you want to, you can cleanse me!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Matthew 9:29–30; John 4:50–53; Hosea 14:4"> So He extended His hand and touched him, saying, “I want to; be cleansed!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="“I want to”—beautiful! To actually touch a leper was unheard of, but of course Jesus had no fear of contamination.">f</a></sup>Immediately the leprosy left him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Leviticus 14:2–32; Luke 17:14; Mark 1:44"> Then He ordered him to tell no one, “But go and show yourself to the priest and make an offering for your cleansing just as Moses prescribed, as a testimony to them.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="As a testimony about what? <span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Luke 6:12; Mark 1:35–36; Matthew 14:23"> So He Himself would withdraw into deserted places and pray.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="He made sure that He kept in touch with the Father.">h</a></sup></span>

## An examining council—the evaluation

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Luke 6:19; Luke 8:46; Matthew 11:5"> Now it happened on a certain day that He was teaching, and there were Pharisees and teachers of the law, who had come from every village of Galilee, and from Judea and Jerusalem, sitting there—and the power of the Lord was there to heal them.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="The eclectic text currently in vogue makes a mess of this statement by changing ‘them’ to ‘him’, following about 1% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality. ‘Him’ is in the accusative case, as is ‘them’, and is thus the direct object of the verb ‘to heal’. ## “Your sins are forgiven”

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Mark 2:3–12; Matthew 9:2–8; John 5:5–6"> And then, some men came carrying a paralyzed man on a cot, and tried to take him in and place him before Him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Mark 2:4; Matthew 10:27; Matthew 24:17"> When they could not find how to do it, because of the crowd, they went up on the roof and lowered him with the cot through the tiling into the center, in front of Jesus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Acts 14:9; Matthew 9:2; Mark 2:5"> Seeing their faith He said to him, “Man, your sins are forgiven you!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Those men were really determined, but they wanted healing, not forgiveness of sins; however, Jesus had His own agenda.">j</a></sup></span>

## A ‘discussion’ ensues

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Isaiah 43:25; Mark 2:6–7; Luke 7:49"> So the scribes and the Pharisees began to reason, saying: “Who is this who speaks blasphemies? Who can forgive sins but God alone?”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Their theology was not all that bad; they just did not recognize Jesus as God.">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Luke 24:38; Isaiah 66:18; Psalms 139:2"> But Jesus perceived their reasonings and reacted by saying to them: “Why are you reasoning in your hearts?</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Mark 2:9; Matthew 9:5"> Which is easier to say, ‘Your sins are forgiven you’, or to say, ‘Get up and walk!’?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="“Your sins are forgiven” is easier to say, because no one can see if it happened or not. If you say, “Get up and walk!” and he does not, it makes you look stupid.">l</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Revelation 1:13; Isaiah 53:11; Luke 8:54"> But that you may know that the Son of the Man<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="That is what the Text says, “the Son of the Man”, which appears to be a phrase coined by the Lord Jesus to refer to Himself; the phrase does not make very good sense in English, at first glance, but if “the man” refers to pristine Adam and “the son” to an only pristine descendant, it makes great sense. It seems to indicate a perfect human prototype, like Adam was before the fall—the human side of the God-man.">m</a></sup>has authority on earth to forgive sins”—He said to the paralyzed man: “I say to you, get up! Take your cot and go to your house.”</span>

## Enter Levi (Matthew)

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Mark 2:13–22; Matthew 9:9–17; John 1:43"> After these things He went out and saw a tax collector named Levi sitting at the tax office; and He said to him, “Follow me!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Luke 5:11; Matthew 19:22–27; 1 Kings 19:19–21"> So leaving everything he got up and followed Him.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="Wow! Evidently he really did, turning his responsibilities over to his colleagues. Because tax collectors were ostracized by the people, they probably wanted to join the crowds following Jesus, but felt excluded. So a direct invitation to Matthew was all he wanted.">o</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Mark 2:15; Luke 15:1; 1 Corinthians 10:27"> Then Levi made a great banquet for Him at his house; and there was a large crowd of tax collectors and others who were reclining with them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Matthew 21:28–32; Luke 5:17; Mark 7:3"> Well the scribes and Pharisees complained about them to His disciples, saying, “How can you eat and drink with the tax collectors and sinners?!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Mark 2:17; Matthew 9:12–13; Jeremiah 8:22"> So Jesus reacted and said to them: “Those who are healthy have no need of a physician, just those who are sick.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Luke 19:10; Luke 15:10; 1 Timothy 1:15–16"> I have not come to call righteous people to repentance, just sinners.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Matthew 9:14–17; Mark 2:18–22; John 3:25"> Then they said to Him, “Why do the disciples of John fast often and make prayers, and likewise those of the Pharisees, but yours keep eating and drinking?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="John 3:29; 2 Corinthians 11:2; Isaiah 54:5"> So He said to them: “Can you make the friends of the bridegroom fast while the bridegroom is with them?</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Luke 17:22; Acts 14:23; Acts 3:21"> But days are coming when the bridegroom will be taken away from them; then, in those days, they will fast.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="Matthew 9:16–17; Mark 2:21–22; 2 Corinthians 6:16"> Then He also told them a parable: “No one puts a patch from a new garment on an old one; otherwise, both the new makes a tear and that from the new does not match the old.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="Joshua 9:13; Psalms 119:83; Joshua 9:4"> And no one puts new wine into old wineskins; otherwise the new wine will burst those skins and will itself be spilled and the skins wasted.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="Hebrews 8:8–13; 2 Corinthians 5:17; Ezekiel 36:26"> Rather, new wine must be put into new wineskins, and both are preserved.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="I take it that the Lord is saying that a church that has become an ‘old wineskin’ cannot be renewed—any attempt to introduce ‘new wine’ will only result in division and bitterness. Bearers of ‘new wine’ should just start a new congregation. The truth stated in verse 39 is one of the factors. Less than 1.5% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, omit “and both are preserved” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">p</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Hebrews 11:39; Mark 7:7–13; Jeremiah 6:16"> Further, no one having drunk old wine immediately desires new, for he says, ‘The old is better.’ ”</span>
</div>
