## Parable of a stupid steward

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Peter 4:10; Matthew 25:14–30; James 4:3"> Then He said further to His disciples: “There was a certain rich man who had a manager, who was accused to him of wasting his goods.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 11:9–10; 1 Peter 4:5; Revelation 20:12"> So he called him in and said to him: ‘What is this I hear about you? Render an account of your stewardship, because you can no longer be manager.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Jeremiah 5:31; Isaiah 10:3; Proverbs 18:9"> Then the manager said within himself: ‘What shall I do? My master is taking the management away from me. I do not have strength to dig; I am ashamed to beg</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Proverbs 30:9; Jeremiah 4:22; James 3:15"> —I know what I will do, so that whenever I am removed from the management they may receive me into their houses.’<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Is this not stupid reasoning? Once he has lost his position, the others will have no reason to pay attention to him.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Luke 7:41–42; Matthew 18:24"> Summoning each one of his master's debtors, he said to the first, ‘How much do you owe my master?’</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Titus 2:10; Luke 16:12; Luke 16:9"> And he said, ‘A hundred baths of olive oil.’ So he said to him, ‘Take your bill and sit down quickly and write fifty.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Song of Solomon 8:11–12; Luke 20:9; Luke 20:12"> Then he said to another, ‘And how much do you owe?’ And he said, ‘A hundred measures of wheat.’ So he said to him, ‘Take your bill and write eighty.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 5:5; Ephesians 5:8; Proverbs 6:6–8"> The master even ‘commended’ the dishonest manager, because he had acted shrewdly. The sons of this age are shrewder in their own generation than the sons of the Light.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="According to the value system of the world it is ‘smart’ to take advantage of other people, but those who follow the Light must be different. Of course the master's ‘commendation’ was sarcastic, since the dishonest manager still lost his job.">b</a></sup></span>
</div>

<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Matthew 19:21; 1 Timothy 6:17–19; Daniel 4:27"> “I even say to you, make friends for yourselves by means of unrighteous mammon, so that whenever you fail, they may receive you into the eternal dwellings!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="The use of sarcasm is not rare in the Bible, and here the Lord is clearly being sarcastic: getting into the eternal dwellings does not depend on ‘buying’ friends down here; it depends on pleasing the Owner up there. <span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Luke 12:33; James 2:5; Proverbs 8:18–19"> If therefore you have not been faithful with the unrighteous mammon, who will commit to your trust the genuine?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Monetary value is the ‘very little’, and spiritual value is the ‘much’, the ‘genuine’.">d</a></sup></span>

<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Samuel 16:7; Proverbs 21:2; 1 Corinthians 4:5"> So He said to them: “You are those who justify yourselves before men, but God knows your hearts. ## A rich man and beggar Lazarus16:19 The Text does not state that this is a parable, so most probably it is not.

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Ezekiel 16:49; Amos 6:4–6; Luke 18:24–25"> “Now there was a certain rich man who was dressed in purple and fine linen, living in luxury every day.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Acts 3:2; 1 Samuel 2:8; James 2:5"> And there was a certain beggar named Lazarus, covered with sores, who had been placed at his gate,</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Matthew 15:27; Mark 7:28; John 6:12"> just wanting to be fed with the crumbs that fell from the rich man's table—why even the dogs would come and lick his sores!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="In fact the dogs were doing him a favor, since canine saliva is good for sores.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="1 Peter 2:24; Psalms 49:16–19; James 1:11"> In due time the beggar died and was carried away to Abraham's bosom by the angels. “The rich man also died and was buried.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Note the contrast. Of course the beggar's body had been buried, but the person was taken to Paradise. Here we have an explicit statement of angelic activity, which, however, is absent from the rich man.">j</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 Peter 2:4; Luke 16:28; Matthew 18:9"> And in Hades he looked up and saw Abraham at a distance, and Lazarus very close to him. And being in torment,</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="John 4:10; Revelation 22:1; Revelation 7:16–17"> he called out, saying, ‘Father Abraham, have mercy on me and send Lazarus, that he may dip the tip of his finger in water and cool my tongue; because I am tormented by this flame!’</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Luke 6:24; 1 John 2:15; Psalms 17:14"> But Abraham said: ‘Child, remember that in your lifetime you received your good things, while Lazarus had bad things; but now he<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="The best line of transmission (30% of the Greek manuscripts here) has the emphatic pronoun ‘he’, rather than ‘here’.">k</a></sup>is being comforted, and you tormented.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="John 3:36; Psalms 49:14; Psalms 50:22"> And besides all this, between us and you a great chasm has been fixed, so that those who want to pass from here to you cannot, nor can anyone from there cross over to us.’<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Several things in this account invite comment. Hades (Greek), or Sheol (Hebrew), is the ‘halfway house’ where departed spirits await the final judgment, but the results of that judgment are already known, since the saved are already separated from the lost. There is a chasm separating the two sides that cannot be crossed, but evidently one side can see and hear the other (the ‘dead’ are conscious and have feeling). People in prison who are waiting for their trial are already suffering. Strangely, the rich man still thinks he is more important than the beggar, since he wants the beggar to serve him—he still holds to the values that condemned him.">l</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27"> Then he said, ‘I beg you therefore, father, that you would send him to my father's house,</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Acts 2:40; Psalms 49:12–13; 1 Thessalonians 4:6"> because I have five brothers, so that he may testify to them, lest they also come to this place of torment.’<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="I find it interesting that he was concerned for his brothers; we can't say, “Better late than never”, since it made no difference.">m</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Acts 15:21; Luke 24:27; Luke 16:16"> Abraham said to him, ‘They have Moses and the prophets; let them hear them.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Revelation 16:9–11; Luke 16:24; Luke 13:5"> So he said to him, ‘Oh no, father Abraham—if someone from the dead should go to them, they will repent!’</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 Corinthians 4:3; Acts 19:8; John 11:43–53"> He said to him, ‘If they do not listen to Moses and the prophets, they will not be persuaded even if someone should rise from the dead.’ ”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Abraham states a disquieting reality: people who reject God's written revelation are self-condemned. Note also that Abraham did not say it would be impossible to send Lazarus, only that it would do no good. But it is clear that the lost cannot return, or the rich man could have gone himself.">n</a></sup></span>
</div>
