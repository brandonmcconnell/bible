<li id="fn-q"><a href="#fnref-q" className="fnb">q</a> <span className="fnr">11:44</span> Perhaps 1% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, omit “scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.)</li>
<li id="fn-r"><a href="#fnref-r" className="fnb">r</a> <span className="fnr">11:46</span> Notice that Jesus makes no effort to conciliate them.</li>
<li id="fn-s"><a href="#fnref-s" className="fnb">s</a> <span className="fnr">11:48</span> The context suggests ‘restore’ rather than ‘build’, since those prophets had been dead for centuries; if a grave had not been adequately marked, there would be no way of knowing where to build.</li>
<li id="fn-t"><a href="#fnref-t" className="fnb">t</a> <span className="fnr">11:49</span> In 1 Corinthians 1:24 Paul refers to Christ as ‘the wisdom of God’. In Matthew 23:34 Jesus said, “I send you prophets,” so here Jesus may be referring to Himself as ‘the wisdom of God’.</li>
<li id="fn-u"><a href="#fnref-u" className="fnb">u</a> <span className="fnr">11:51</span> If the reference is to Zechariah the prophet, his death is not recorded in Scripture, but the temple was rebuilt in his day, so he could have died there.</li>
<li id="fn-v"><a href="#fnref-v" className="fnb">v</a> <span className="fnr">11:51</span> Guilt accumulates. Often a subsequent generation pays.</li>
<li id="fn-w"><a href="#fnref-w" className="fnb">w</a> <span className="fnr">11:52</span> Hosea 4:6 reads, “My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge … Because you have forgotten the law of your God, I also will forget your children.” Notice the awful price paid by the children! Are today's ‘doctors of the law’ not guilty of the same thing?</li>
<li id="fn-x"><a href="#fnref-x" className="fnb">x</a> <span className="fnr">11:52</span> They were really perverse. If they did not want to go in themselves, that was their choice; but to try to stop others was really perverse!</li>
<li id="fn-y"><a href="#fnref-y" className="fnb">y</a> <span className="fnr">11:53</span> Instead of “Well when He had said these things to them”, perhaps 1% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, read “and when He left there” (as in NIV, NASB, TEV, etc.)</li>
<li id="fn-z"><a href="#fnref-z" className="fnb">z</a> <span className="fnr">11:54</span> Perhaps 1.5% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, omit “so that they might accuse Him” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.)</li>
</ol>
