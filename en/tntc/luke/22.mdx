<li id="fn-n"><a href="#fnref-n" className="fnb">n</a> <span className="fnr">22:34</span> This was the second warning; the first is recorded in John 13:37-38. For a complete discussion of Peter's denials, please see the Appendix: “How many times did Jesus say Peter would deny Him?”.</li>
<li id="fn-o"><a href="#fnref-o" className="fnb">o</a> <span className="fnr">22:36</span> If you are obviously armed, this tends to slow down those who would otherwise attack you or take advantage of you. It is better yet to have spiritual power.</li>
<li id="fn-p"><a href="#fnref-p" className="fnb">p</a> <span className="fnr">22:37</span> See Isaiah 53:12.</li>
<li id="fn-q"><a href="#fnref-q" className="fnb">q</a> <span className="fnr">22:44</span> I believe that Hebrews 5:7 refers to this moment. To transpire blood is normally fatal, so Jesus came close to dying there in the garden. But He had to get to the cross, so the Father sent an angel to strengthen Him. The sleeping disciples did not observe this, so Luke received this information ‘from Above’ (Luke 1:3).</li>
<li id="fn-r"><a href="#fnref-r" className="fnb">r</a> <span className="fnr">22:44</span> The eclectic Greek text currently in vogue places verses 43-44 within double brackets (based on 1.3% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality), thereby denying that Luke wrote them. These verses contain important information that should not be questioned on such a feeble basis.</li>
<li id="fn-s"><a href="#fnref-s" className="fnb">s</a> <span className="fnr">22:47</span> Some 45% of the Greek manuscripts, including the best line of transmission (that I follow), have, “he had given them this sign, ‘Whomever I kiss, it is he’ ”. Every other version I have seen follows the 55%.</li>
<li id="fn-t"><a href="#fnref-t" className="fnb">t</a> <span className="fnr">22:50</span> “The servant of the high priest”—presumably this man was leading the expedition. We know from John 18:10 that his name was Malchus.</li>
<li id="fn-u"><a href="#fnref-u" className="fnb">u</a> <span className="fnr">22:51</span> We know it was Peter (John 18:10), and if the Lord had not healed that ear, things would probably have been nastier for Peter in the ‘courtyard’.</li>
<li id="fn-v"><a href="#fnref-v" className="fnb">v</a> <span className="fnr">22:53</span> This was Satan's hour, being part of the Father's Plan; ‘the darkness’ refers to Satan's kingdom; ‘your hour’ means that they were part of that kingdom.</li>
<li id="fn-w"><a href="#fnref-w" className="fnb">w</a> <span className="fnr">22:56</span> I wonder how the girl knew that!</li>
<li id="fn-x"><a href="#fnref-x" className="fnb">x</a> <span className="fnr">22:60</span> Instead of ‘a’ rooster, perhaps 3% of the manuscripts have ‘the’ rooster (as in TR, AV, NKJV).</li>
<li id="fn-y"><a href="#fnref-y" className="fnb">y</a> <span className="fnr">22:61</span> I take it that here the Lord broke the Satanic spell, and Peter could think normally again.</li>
<li id="fn-z"><a href="#fnref-z" className="fnb">z</a> <span className="fnr">22:64</span> Perhaps 2% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, omit “they would hit His face and” (as in NIV, NASB, TEV, etc.).</li>
<li id="fn-aa"><a href="#fnref-aa" className="fnb">aa</a> <span className="fnr">22:64</span> Those men were cowardly and perverse, but probably acted under demonic influence.</li>
<li id="fn-ab"><a href="#fnref-ab" className="fnb">ab</a> <span className="fnr">22:68</span> Jesus tells them that He knows, and they know, that what they are doing is a farce.</li>
<li id="fn-ac"><a href="#fnref-ac" className="fnb">ac</a> <span className="fnr">22:71</span> They were condemning Him for blasphemy, but to do that they had to reject the obvious truth that Jesus was indeed the Son of God.</li>
</ol>
