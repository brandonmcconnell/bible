## Jesus' authority questioned

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Matthew 21:23–27; Luke 19:47–48; Acts 6:12"> Now it happened on one of those days, as He was teaching and evangelizing the people in the temple, that the priests<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Some 16% of the Greek manuscripts have ‘high priests’ (as in most versions).">a</a></sup>and scribes came to Him with the elders</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Exodus 2:14; Mark 11:28–33; John 2:18"> and addressed Him, saying, “Tell us by what authority you are doing these things; or who is he who gave you this authority?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Matthew 15:2–3; Luke 22:68; Colossians 4:6"> But in answer He said to them: “I also will ask you one thing; yes, tell me:</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Luke 15:18; Matthew 11:7–19; Matthew 17:11–12"> the baptism of John—was it from heaven or from men?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="John 1:34; John 1:15–18; John 1:30"> So they reasoned among themselves, saying: “If we say, ‘From heaven,’ he will say, ‘Why did you not believe him?’</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Matthew 21:26; Matthew 11:9; John 10:41"> But if we say, ‘From men,’ all the people will stone us, for they are persuaded that John was a prophet.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Isaiah 29:14; 2 Peter 3:3; Isaiah 41:28"> So they answered that they did not know where it was from.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Proverbs 26:4–5; Matthew 21:27; Mark 11:33"> And Jesus said to them, “Neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Well, of course, they knew that both John and Jesus were of God—they just did not want to repent and change their way of life.">b</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Parable of some perverse tenants

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Matthew 21:33–46; Mark 12:1–12; Isaiah 5:1–7"> Then He began to tell the people this parable: “A man planted a vineyard, leased it to farmers, and went away on a long journey.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Chronicles 36:15–16; Nehemiah 9:26; Matthew 21:34–36"> At the proper time he sent a slave to the farmers, that they might give him some of the fruit of the vineyard. But the farmers flogged him and sent him away empty-handed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Hosea 10:1; Hebrews 11:36–37; 1 Thessalonians 2:2"> Again he sent a different slave; so they flogged him also, treated him shamefully, and sent him away empty-handed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> And again he sent a third; but they wounded him also and threw him out.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Matthew 3:17; John 3:16–17; Jeremiah 36:7"> Then the owner of the vineyard said: ‘What shall I do? I will send my beloved son; hopefully they will respect him when they see him.’<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Presumably this would never occur in real life, but the Lord was aiming this directly at them, knowing what they would do to Him and what the Owner would do to them.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Romans 8:17; Hebrews 1:2; Acts 2:23"> But when the farmers saw him, they reasoned among themselves, saying: ‘This is the heir. Come, let's kill him, so that the inheritance may become ours.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Hebrews 13:12; Mark 12:6–9; Matthew 21:37–40"> So they threw him out of the vineyard and killed him. What therefore will the owner of the vineyard do to them?</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Luke 19:27; Acts 13:46; Matthew 21:41"> He will come and destroy those farmers and give the vineyard to others.” Having heard it they said, “May it never be!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Psalms 118:22; Isaiah 28:16; Acts 4:11"> Then He looked directly at them and said: “What then is this that is written:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">‘A stone that the builders rejected,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">the same became the cornerstone’?</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="See Psalm 118:22.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Isaiah 8:14–15; Zechariah 12:3; Daniel 2:44–45"> Everyone who falls on that stone will be broken to pieces, but on whomever it should fall—it will crush him!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Those are our two options: fall on Jesus and be broken, or be crushed in judgment.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Luke 19:47–48; Luke 20:14; Matthew 21:45–46"> The chief priests and the scribes wanted to lay hands on Him right then, but they were afraid<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="For ‘they were afraid’, some 35% of the Greek manuscripts have ‘they feared the people’ (as in most versions).">f</a></sup>—they knew He had spoken this parable against them.</span>
</div>
</div>

## To Caesar, Caesar's; to God, God's

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Luke 11:54; Matthew 22:15–22; Matthew 27:2"> Keeping a close watch on Him, they sent spies, who pretended to be innocent, so that they might latch on to something He said, so as to hand Him over to the power and the authority of the governor.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 2:4–5; Matthew 26:49–50; Matthew 22:16"> So they questioned Him, saying: “Teacher, we know that you speak and teach what is right, and you do not show favoritism but teach the way of God in truth.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="They were really almost too obvious.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Nehemiah 9:37; Mark 12:14–17; Acts 5:37"> Is it lawful for us to pay taxes to Caesar, or not?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Matthew 22:18; 1 Corinthians 3:19; Luke 5:22"> But He perceived their craftiness and said to them: “Why are you testing me?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Perhaps 1.5% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, omit “Why are you testing me?” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">h</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Matthew 18:28; Acts 11:28; Acts 26:32"> Show me a denarius—whose image and inscription does it have?” In answer they said, “Caesar's.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Romans 13:6–7; Mark 12:17; 1 Peter 2:13–17"> So He said to them, “Then render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Luke 20:20; Proverbs 26:4–5; Titus 1:10"> They were unable to latch on to His word in front of the people; and marveling at His answer they kept silent.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Sadducees deny resurrection

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Acts 4:1–2; 1 Corinthians 15:12; Mark 12:18–27"> Then some of the Sadducees, who deny that there is a resurrection, approached and questioned Him,</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Deuteronomy 25:5–10; Genesis 38:8; Genesis 38:11"> saying: “Teacher, Moses wrote to us that if a man's married brother dies childless, then his brother should take the widow and produce offspring for his brother.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Leviticus 20:20; Jeremiah 22:30"> Now there were seven brothers: the first took a wife and died childless;</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30"> then the second took the widow and he died childless;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Perhaps 1% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, omit “the widow and he died childless” (as in NIV, NASB, TEV, etc.).">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31"> then the third took her, in fact all seven in sequence—they all died childless.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 1:4; Ecclesiastes 9:5; Hebrews 9:27"> Finally, last of all, the woman died also.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Matthew 22:24–28; Mark 12:19–23"> Therefore, in the resurrection, whose wife will she be, since all seven had her?”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Whenever someone comes up with a hypothetical situation, be careful; there is probably a hidden agenda.">j</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Luke 16:8; Luke 17:27; Hebrews 13:4"> So in answer Jesus said to them: “The people of this age marry and are given in marriage;</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="2 Thessalonians 1:5; John 5:29; Daniel 12:2–3"> but those who are considered worthy<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="“Those who are considered worthy”—an interesting concept!">k</a></sup>to attain to that age, to the resurrection from among the dead, neither marry nor are given in marriage.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="Revelation 21:4; Philippians 3:21; Matthew 22:30"> Because they cannot die anymore,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="If no one dies, there is no need to produce new generations.">l</a></sup>being like angels—they are sons of God, being sons of the resurrection.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="Exodus 3:2–6; Mark 12:26–27; Acts 7:30–32"> But that the dead are raised, Moses indeed revealed about the Bush when he recorded: ‘The Lord, the God of Abraham and the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob.’<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="See Exodus 3:6.">m</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="John 11:25–26; Colossians 3:3–4; Romans 6:10–11"> So He is not the God of the dead, but of the living—to Him all are alive.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Acts 23:9; Mark 12:28–34; Matthew 22:34–40"> Then some of the scribes answered and said, “Teacher, you spoke well.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="Matthew 22:46; Mark 12:34; Proverbs 26:5"> And they no longer dared to question Him further.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Is the Christ David's Son?

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="Mark 12:35–37; Matthew 1:1; Matthew 22:41–45"> Then He said to them: “How is it that they say that the Messiah<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="The Greek Text has ‘Christ’, but since they were speaking Hebrew, the Lord certainly said ‘Messiah’.">n</a></sup>is David's Son?</span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="Psalms 110:1; Acts 2:34–35; Hebrews 1:13"> Why David himself affirms in the book of Psalms,<br /><span className="pl pl-1">‘The Lord said to my Lord: sit at my right hand</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43" data-xref="Luke 19:27; Psalms 110:1; Revelation 19:14–21"> <span className="pl pl-2">until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet.’</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="See Psalm 110:1.">o</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44" data-xref="Revelation 22:16; Luke 1:31–35; Matthew 1:23"> Since David calls Him ‘Lord’, how then is He his Son?”</span>
</div>
</div>

## Jesus denounces the scribes

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v45" data-v="45" data-xref="Matthew 15:10; Mark 12:38–39; Mark 8:34"> Then, in the hearing of the people, He said to His disciples:</span>
<span className="v" id="v46" data-v="46" data-xref="Luke 11:43; Mark 12:38–39; Luke 14:7"> “Beware of the scribes, who like to walk around in long robes, and love greetings in the marketplaces, and the best seats in the synagogues, and the best places at feasts;</span>
<span className="v" id="v47" data-v="47" data-xref="Mark 12:40; James 3:1; Titus 1:16"> who ‘devour’ widows' houses,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="Presumably, when the man died the widow no longer had the means to clear any debt; so they would just take over the house and put the widow out in the street.">p</a></sup>and for a show make long prayers. These will receive more severe judgment.”</span>
</div>
</div>
