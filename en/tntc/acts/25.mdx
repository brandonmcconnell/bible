<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 23:34; Acts 25:5; Acts 8:40"> Now upon assuming the province, after three days Festus went up from Caesarea to Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Acts 25:15; Proverbs 4:16; Acts 24:1"> Then the chief priests<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Some 60% of the Greek manuscripts have ‘high priest’, as in AV and NKJV.">a</a></sup>and the principal men of the Jews informed him against Paul and started importuning him,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Acts 9:24; Acts 23:12–15; 1 Samuel 23:19–21"> begging a favor from him, that he would summon him to Jerusalem—preparing an ambush to kill him along the way.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Those guys were really good haters!">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Acts 24:23"> However Festus answered that Paul should be kept at Caesarea, he himself being about to go there shortly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Acts 25:25; John 18:29–30; Acts 25:18–19"> He said, “So let those who are competent among you go down with me; if there is anything against this man, let them accuse him.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 25:17; Acts 25:10; Matthew 27:19"> When he had stayed among them more than ten days, he went down to Caesarea; the next day he sat on the judgment seat and commanded Paul to be brought.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Acts 24:13; Luke 23:10; Acts 25:24"> When he had come, the Jews who had come down from Jerusalem made a circle,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="This was presumably a tactic to intimidate, and to form a decent circle would require a number of people. Paul was not impressed, but Festus may have been.">c</a></sup>bringing against Paul many serious charges that they could not prove,</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Acts 24:12; Acts 28:17; 2 Corinthians 1:12"> while he defended himself, “Neither against the law of the Jews, nor against the temple, nor against Caesar did I commit any sin.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 24:27; Acts 12:3; Acts 25:20"> But Festus, wanting to do the Jews a favor,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="I wonder if those Jews had been involved in the decision to replace Felix (who didn't play ball with them) with Festus. If so, and if Festus was aware that he owed them a favor, that would account for his attitude here.">d</a></sup>answered Paul by saying, “Are you willing to go up to Jerusalem to be judged by me there concerning these things?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Matthew 27:18; 2 Corinthians 4:2; Acts 28:18"> So Paul said: “I am standing before Caesar's judgment seat, where I ought to be tried. To the Jews I have done no wrong, as even you know very well.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Paul could tell that Festus was not on the ‘up and up’.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 28:19; Acts 26:32; Acts 25:25"> Now if I really am in the wrong and have perpetrated anything worthy of death, I do not refuse to die; but if there is nothing to the things of which these are accusing me, no one has the right to give me to them.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Paul knew, and Festus knew, what the Jews had in mind.">f</a></sup>I appeal to Caesar!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 19:21; Philippians 1:12–14; Philippians 1:20"> Then Festus, when he had conferred with his counsel, answered: “You have appealed to Caesar. To Caesar you shall go!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="I suspect that this took Festus by surprise; and it put him in a bad light—on what basis is he going to send an innocent man to the Emperor? Presumably he could have just released Paul, but that would have turned the Jews against him. And then there was the Plan.">g</a></sup></span>

## Agrippa

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Acts 8:40; Acts 26:1; Acts 26:27–28"> Now when some days had passed, Agrippa the king and Bernice arrived in Caesarea to congratulate Festus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Acts 24:27"> Since they were spending many days there, Festus laid Paul's case before the king, saying: “There is a certain man left a prisoner by Felix;</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Acts 25:1–3; Esther 3:9; Luke 18:3–5"> about whom, when I was in Jerusalem, the chief priests and the elders of the Jews informed me, asking for punishment against him;</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="John 7:51; Deuteronomy 19:17–18; Acts 23:30"> to whom I answered that it is not a custom with Romans to deliver any man to destruction<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Some 7.5% of the Greek manuscripts omit ‘to destruction’ (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">h</a></sup>before the accused has his accusers face to face and has opportunity for defense against the accusation.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="I wonder if he really said that.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Acts 25:6; Acts 25:10"> So when they had assembled here, without any delay, the next day I sat on the judgment seat and commanded the man to be brought.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> When the accusers stood up, they brought no charge against him of such things as I supposed,</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Acts 23:29; Acts 18:15; 1 Corinthians 15:3–4"> but had certain issues against him about their religion, and about a certain dead Jesus whom Paul affirmed to be living.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Acts 25:9"> And since I was at a loss how to investigate such matters,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="This would likely be true, whether or not it was his motivation at the time.">j</a></sup>I asked whether he was willing to go to Jerusalem and be judged there concerning these things.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Timothy 4:16; Luke 2:1; Acts 25:10–11"> But when Paul appealed to be reserved for the decision of the Emperor, I commanded him to be kept until I can send him to Caesar.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 9:15; Matthew 10:18; Isaiah 52:15"> Then Agrippa said to Festus, “I myself would also like to hear the man.” So he said, “Tomorrow you shall hear him.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Acts 26:30; Acts 25:13; 1 John 2:16"> So the next day, when Agrippa and Bernice had come with great pomp and entered the auditorium, with the commanding officers and the more prominent men of the city, at Festus' command Paul was brought in.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 22:22; Acts 25:7; Acts 25:2–3"> And Festus said: “King Agrippa, and all you men who are here with us: consider this man,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Festus is not being complimentary.">k</a></sup>about whom the whole Jewish community petitioned me, both at Jerusalem and here, vociferating that it was not fitting for him to live any longer.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Acts 23:29; Acts 25:11–12; Luke 23:4"> But when I found that he had committed nothing worthy of death,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Festus declares Paul's innocence.">l</a></sup>besides he himself having appealed to the Emperor, I decided to send him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 26:2–3"> But I have nothing certain to write to my lord concerning him. Therefore I have brought him before all of you, and especially before you, King Agrippa, so that after the examination I may have something to write.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="John 7:51; Proverbs 18:13"> Because it seems to me unreasonable to send a prisoner and not to specify the charges against him.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Precisely. Festus is in a pickle (of his own making).">m</a></sup></span>
</div>
