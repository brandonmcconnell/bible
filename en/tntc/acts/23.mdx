<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 24:16; 2 Timothy 1:3; 2 Corinthians 1:12"> Then Paul, looking intently at the council, said, “Men, brothers, I have lived in all good conscience before God until this day.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="John 18:22; Acts 24:1; Micah 5:1"> So the high priest Ananias commanded those standing by him to strike his mouth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Deuteronomy 25:1–2; John 7:51; Matthew 23:27–28"> Then Paul said to him: “God will strike you, you whitewashed wall!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="One is reminded of the Lord's calling the Pharisees ‘whitewashed tombs’ (Matthew 23:27); what Paul said was probably not as bad, but definitely not a compliment!">a</a></sup>You sit there to judge me according to the law, yet you command me to be struck contrary to the law!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> So those standing by said, “Do you reproach God's high priest?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Exodus 22:28; Ecclesiastes 10:20; Jude 1:8–9"> Then Paul said, “I did not know, brothers, that he is high priest; for it is written: ‘Do not speak evil of a ruler of your people.’ ”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="See Exodus 22:28. Ananias was presumably the true high priest, but someone else was probably the political ‘high priest’ that year, and the political one would be wearing the priestly attire; presumably that is why Paul did not recognize the real high priest. Note that he sort of apologizes, but he does not revoke the curse!">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 24:15; Acts 24:21; Acts 26:5–8"> Now when Paul perceived that one part were Pharisees and the other Sadducees,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="I follow the best line of transmission in putting ‘Pharisees’ first; 80% of the Greek manuscripts reverse the order, as in most versions.">c</a></sup>he called out in the council, “Men, brothers, I am a Pharisee, a son of a Pharisee; I am being judged concerning the hope and resurrection of the dead!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Psalms 55:9; John 7:40–43; Matthew 10:34"> When he had said this, an argument started between the Pharisees and the Sadducees, and the assembly was divided.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="That was presumably Paul's intention, only the situation got out of hand!">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Luke 20:27; Matthew 22:23; Mark 12:18"> (Sadducees say that there is no resurrection, nor angel or spirit, but Pharisees confess both.)<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="The Greek term here means precisely ‘both’; angel and spirit are treated as a single category.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 22:7; Acts 22:17–18; John 12:29"> There was a great clamor, and the scribes of the Pharisee party stood up and started arguing vigorously, saying, “We find nothing wrong with this man; but if a spirit or angel has spoken to him, let us not fight against God!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Some 4.5% of the Greek manuscripts omit ‘let us not fight against God’ (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="James 1:19; James 3:14–2; Acts 22:24"> Well the dissension became such that the commander, fearing that Paul might be torn in pieces by them, commanded the soldiers to go down and snatch him out of their midst and bring him into the barracks.</span>

## A plot exposed

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 18:9; Acts 19:21; 2 Timothy 4:17"> Now the following night the Lord stood by him and said, “Take courage, Paul, because as you have testified about me in Jerusalem, so you must also testify in Rome.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Paul is in Jerusalem in disobedience against a clear divine prohibition, and things definitely are not going well. God appears to Paul and assures him that he will indeed get to Rome [although he is not going to enjoy the trip!].">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 23:30; Isaiah 8:9–10; Acts 23:21"> And when it was day, some of the Jews formed a conspiracy by binding themselves with a curse neither to eat nor to drink until they had destroyed Paul.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="When the plot failed, they presumably did eat and drink and resume normal life, but I wonder if they suffered any consequences of the curse.">h</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="John 16:2; 2 Samuel 15:31; 2 Samuel 15:12"> Now there were more than forty who formed this plot.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Acts 23:12; Jeremiah 8:12; Isaiah 3:9"> They came to the chief priests and the elders and said: “We have bound ourselves with a terrible curse not to taste anything until we have killed Paul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Isaiah 59:7; Psalms 37:32–33; Proverbs 1:16"> Now you, therefore, together with the council, explain to the commander that he should bring him down to you tomorrow, as though you are going to determine more accurately the facts in his case; but we are ready to destroy him before he comes near.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Acts 23:10; 1 Corinthians 3:19; Acts 23:32"> But when Paul's sister's son heard about the ambush, he went and entered the barracks and told Paul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Proverbs 22:3; Matthew 8:8–9; Matthew 10:16"> So Paul called one of the centurions and said, “Take this young man to the commander, because he has something to tell him.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Ephesians 3:1; Ephesians 4:1; Acts 28:17"> So he took him and brought him to the commander and said, “Paul the prisoner called me over and asked me to bring this young man to you because he has something to tell you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Jeremiah 31:32; Esther 9:12; Esther 7:2"> So taking him by the hand,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="This is cute! The commander has a sense of humor.">i</a></sup>the commander went aside and asked privately, “What is it that you have to tell me?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Acts 23:14–15; Acts 23:1; Psalms 12:2"> So he said: “The Jews have agreed to ask you to bring Paul down to the council tomorrow as though intending to inquire somewhat more accurately concerning him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Acts 23:12–14; Acts 14:5–6; 2 Corinthians 11:26"> But you should not believe them, because more than forty of their men are lying in wait for him, who have bound themselves with a curse neither to eat not to drink until they have destroyed him; even now they are ready, looking for your promise.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Mark 1:44; Joshua 2:14"> Then the commander dismissed the young man commanding, “Don't tell anyone that you have revealed these things to me!”</span>

## Paul sent to Felix

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Matthew 14:25; Acts 23:33; Luke 12:38"> Summoning two of the centurions he said: “Prepare two hundred soldiers, seventy horsemen and two hundred spearmen<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="A centurion commanded 100 men, so there were probably at least five centurions that set out with Paul.">j</a></sup>to go to Caesarea at the third hour of the night;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Earlier in the book, referring to the Jewish hour of prayer, Hebrew time is used, and on that basis this would be 9 p.m. But I find it scarcely credible that a Roman commander (not a Jew) addressing his troops (none of whom were Jews) would use Hebrew time; I would expect him to use Roman time, which would make it 3 a.m. (People would still be awake at 9 p.m., but not at 3 a.m.)">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 25:14; Acts 23:26; Acts 24:10"> and provide mounts to set Paul on so as to deliver him safely<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Why more than one horse for Paul? Was he allowing for the chance that they might have to run for it? Well, the nature of the curse implied some level of desperation, and 40+ desperate men could cause unforeseen complications.">l</a></sup>to Felix, the governor.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> He wrote a letter with this content:</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 15:23; Acts 26:25; Luke 1:3"> “Claudius Lysias, to the most excellent governor Felix: Greetings.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Acts 22:25–29; Acts 21:31–33; Acts 24:6"> This man was seized by the Jews and was about to be killed by them; but I came with the troops and rescued him, having learned that he is a Roman.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Nothing like stretching the truth to make yourself look good.">m</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Acts 22:30"> And wanting to know the reason they were accusing him, I took him down to their council.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Acts 26:31; Acts 25:25; Acts 18:15"> I found that he was being accused about questions of their law, but there was no accusation worthy of death or bonds.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Acts 23:35; Acts 25:16; Acts 25:5–6"> When I was told that the Jews<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Perhaps 11% of the Greek manuscripts omit ‘the Jews’ and ‘about to’ (as in NIV, NASB, LB, etc.).">n</a></sup>were about to execute a plot against the man, I sent him to you at once, also directing his accusers to state the charges against him before you. Farewell.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 Timothy 2:3–4; Luke 7:8; Acts 23:23–24"> So the soldiers, according to their orders, took Paul and brought him by night to Antipatris.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Acts 23:23; Acts 23:10"> The next day they left the horsemen to go on with him and returned to the barracks.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="Horsemen could move faster without the footmen, and presumably the greatest danger had been in and near Jerusalem.">o</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Acts 23:26; Acts 23:23–24; Acts 28:16"> When they entered Caesarea and delivered the letter to the governor, they also presented Paul to him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Acts 21:39; Acts 25:1; Daniel 2:49"> When the governor read it, he asked what province he was from; learning that it was Cilicia,</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Acts 23:30; Acts 25:16; Matthew 27:27"> he said, “I will give you a hearing when your accusers also arrive.” And he commanded him to be guarded in Herod's Praetorium.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="The quarters would be reasonably decent, not a dungeon.">p</a></sup></span>
</div>
