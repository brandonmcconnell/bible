## Malta

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 27:26; Acts 27:44; Acts 27:39"> Following the rescue, they learned that the island was called Malta.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Romans 1:14; Hebrews 13:2; Colossians 3:11"> Now the natives showed us unusual kindness, in that they kindled a fire and welcomed us all, because it had begun to rain and was cold.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Matthew 23:33; Matthew 12:34; Acts 28:4"> But when Paul had gathered a bundle of sticks and placed them on the fire, because of the heat a viper came out and fastened itself on his hand.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Luke 13:2; Luke 13:4; John 7:24"> So when the natives saw the beast hanging from his hand, they started saying to each other, “This man must be a murderer, whom Justice has not allowed to live, even though rescued from the sea.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Luke 10:19; Mark 16:18; Psalms 91:13"> But he just shook the beast off into the fire and suffered no harm.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 12:22; Acts 14:11–13; Matthew 21:9"> Well they were expecting him to swell up, or suddenly fall down dead; but after waiting for quite a while and seeing nothing unusual happening to him, they changed their mind and started saying he was a god.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Matthew 10:40–41; Luke 19:6–9; Acts 28:2"> Now in that region there were properties belonging to the chief man of the island, named Publius, who welcomed us and in a friendly manner took care of us for three days.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="To feed 276 people for three days would be a considerable expense—and they were hungry!">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Acts 9:40; Mark 6:5; James 5:14–16"> Well it happened that the father of Publius was sick in bed with fever and dysentery; Paul went in to him, prayed, and laying his hands on him, healed him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 5:12; Mark 6:54–56; Acts 5:15"> So, when this had happened, the rest of those on the island who had diseases started coming and being healed;</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 2:6; Philippians 4:11–12; Matthew 6:31–34"> who also honored us in many ways, and when we put to sea they provided the necessary things.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Paul proved to be a valuable person to have along, the more so since they were there for three months.">b</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Rome

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 27:6; 1 Corinthians 8:4; Isaiah 45:20"> Now after three months we put to sea in an Alexandrian ship that had wintered in the island, whose figurehead was ‘the Twin Brothers’.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> We put in at Syracuse and stayed there three days;</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Acts 27:13"> from there we tacked back and forth<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Since Luke makes a point of saying that at Rhegium they got a south wind, between Syracuse and Rhegium the wind was not favorable and they had to tack. Most versions miss the point here.">c</a></sup>and arrived at Rhegium. After one day a south wind sprang up, and on the second day we came to Puteoli,</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Acts 21:4; Acts 20:6; Acts 19:1"> where we found brothers who urged us to stay there seven days<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="The seven days gave them time to send news on ahead so there would be a welcoming committee at Three Inns. The centurion evidently cooperated.">d</a></sup>—that was how we went toward Rome.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Hebrews 13:3; Romans 15:24; Exodus 4:14"> And the brothers there, when they heard about our circumstances, came out to meet us as far as Appii Forum and Three Inns. When Paul saw them he thanked God and took courage.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Acts 27:3; Acts 24:23; Acts 28:30–31"> Now when we entered Rome, the centurion delivered the prisoners to the commander;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Perhaps 5% of the Greek manuscripts omit ‘the centurion delivered the prisoners to the commander’ (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">e</a></sup>but Paul was allowed to live by himself, with the soldier who guarded him.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="This was surely an unusual proceeding; I assume it was a privilege granted in recognition of services rendered.">f</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Paul and the Jews

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Acts 25:8; Acts 6:14; Acts 25:10"> It happened that after three days Paul called together the leaders of the Jews; and when they had assembled he said to them: “Men, brothers, though I had done nothing against ‘the people’ or the ancestral customs, still I was delivered as a prisoner from Jerusalem into the hands of the Romans,</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Acts 26:31–32; Acts 23:29; Acts 22:24–25"> who, when they had examined me, were intending to release me, because I was not guilty of any crime deserving death.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Acts 26:32; Acts 25:21; Acts 25:10–12"> But when the Jews spoke against it, I was compelled to appeal to Caesar; not that I had anything about which to accuse my nation.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Ephesians 6:20; 2 Timothy 2:9; Acts 26:6–7"> For this reason therefore I asked to see you and speak with you; it is because of the hope of Israel that I am bound with this chain.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Isaiah 54:17; Isaiah 41:11; Isaiah 50:8"> So they said to him: “We have neither received letters from Judea concerning you, nor have any of the brothers who came reported or spoken any evil about you.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="When Paul appealed to Caesar, his enemies evidently gave up, knowing they did not have a valid case.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="1 Peter 2:12; Acts 24:14; 1 Peter 3:16"> But we desire to hear from you what you think; because as for this sect, we know that it is spoken against everywhere.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Acts 17:2–3; Acts 19:8; Acts 18:28"> So arranging a day with him, even more people came to him at his lodging, to whom he kept expounding from morning until evening: solemnly testifying about the Kingdom of God and trying to convince them concerning Jesus, from both the Law of Moses and the Prophets.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 14:4; Acts 17:4–5; Acts 19:8–9"> Well some were persuaded by what had been said, and some kept refusing to believe.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Matthew 15:7; 2 Peter 1:21; Mark 7:6"> So being in disagreement among themselves they started to leave, after Paul had said this one word: “The Holy Spirit spoke correctly to our<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Instead of “our”, some 20% of the Greek manuscripts have ‘your’ (as in NIV, NASB, TEV, etc.).">h</a></sup>fathers through Isaiah the prophet,</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Isaiah 6:9–10; Ezekiel 12:2; Isaiah 42:19–20"> saying:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">‘Go to this people and say,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">“You will keep on hearing, but never understand;</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">you will keep on seeing, but never perceive”;</span><br /></span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Isaiah 6:10"> <span className="pl pl-1">because the heart of this people has become sluggish,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">and their ears are hard of hearing,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">and their eyes they have closed;</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">in order that they not see with their eyes,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">nor hear with their ears,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">nor understand with their heart,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">nor turn back, so I could heal them.’</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="See Isaiah 6:9-10. Here we have a chiastic structure: heart, ears, eyes; eyes, ears, heart.">i</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Luke 2:30–32; Acts 26:17–18; Romans 3:29–30"> Therefore let it be known to you that the salvation of God has been sent to the Gentiles; they will listen!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29"> And when he had said these things the Jews went their way, having a great dispute among themselves.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Perhaps 5% of the Greek manuscripts omit verse 29 entire (as in NIV, NASB and LB, but all three have a footnote saying that “some manuscripts” add the verse—their way of referring to 95%! Will not the uninformed reader be misled?).">j</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Two tranquil years

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30"> Paul stayed two whole years in his own rented house, and received all who came to see him,</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Acts 4:29; Ephesians 6:19–20; Matthew 4:23"> proclaiming the Kingdom of God and teaching the things concerning the Lord Jesus Christ, with all boldness, without hindrance.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="It has often been observed that the book seems to lack a proper conclusion. If we consider that the book is actually about God working through people, then of course that work has been in operation throughout the intervening centuries and continues in our day—even through us.">k</a></sup></span>
</div>
