<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Proverbs 29:5; Proverbs 26:28; Acts 24:26–27"> So when he had been called in, Tertullus began his accusation, saying: “Seeing that by you we enjoy much peace, and your foresight has brought prosperity to this nation,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Acts 23:26; Acts 26:25; Luke 1:3"> we recognize this, most noble Felix, with full gratitude, always and everywhere.</span>

<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Acts 28:22; Acts 24:14; Acts 26:5"> We have found this man to be a plague, a creator of discord among all the Jews throughout the world,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Wow! <span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 24:12; Acts 21:27–32; John 18:31"> and he even tried to profane the temple; so we arrested him.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="We have here a bothersome set of variants, and the only way to do justice to the situation is to give the evidence in Greek. The five principle variations hinge on the three underlined words; they are: 2) κριναι … επι σε (9.7%) [6 variants] 8) κριναι … επι σου (10.5%) [14 variants] 22) κριναι … προς σε (5.3%) [8 variants] 30) κρινειν … επι σου (4.4%) [4 variants] 34) κρινειν … επι σε (1.7%) OC,TR [3 variants] [OC is in small print] 37) replaces απηγαγεν with five words, plus two other changes: κριναι … επι σου (3.2%) [2 variants] 39) completely rewrites the material: κριναι … προς σε (3.4%) CP [6 variants] (eight further variants) (2.9%) [8 variants]. Variant 2) presumably has the best claim to be the standard form of the addition: κριναι clearly bests κρινειν, επι clearly bests προς, σε barely bests σου. [Although variant 8) appears to be slightly stronger than 2) numerically, the 14 internal variants, compared to 6, effectively diminish its credibility. The main variant in 2) is far stronger than that of 8).] It is also attested by syr and latpt. However, although some form of the addition commands 41.1% of the MSS, there are no less than 51 variants! What about the context? The addition makes good sense, and it fits nicely. But, it is not really necessary; that information Felix already knew. The text reads quite well without the addition also. I conclude that the short form was judged to be abrupt or incomplete, giving rise to the addition; presumably the Autograph did not contain it. Since Tertullus was an orator he may well have actually said what is in the addition, plus a good deal more besides, but did Luke write it? (The incidents recorded in Acts were well known by many contemporaries, and there were many written accounts in circulation [Luke 1:1], so it was entirely predictable that a variety of historically correct material would be added, here and there, to Luke's account.) The external evidence, though divided, is adequate to resolve this case: 58.9% against a severely fragmented 41.1%. The ancient versions, being divided, do not help us much this time. Although 59% is not a strong majority, by any means, still, the severe fragmentation of the 41% sort of leaves variant 1) without a worthy opponent. Variant 1) wins in “Antiquity”, “Number”, “Variety” and “Continuity”, so I have no doubt that it is original. [The reading of the TR, variant 34), really has little to commend it.]">c</a></sup></span>

<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 2:16; Micah 7:2–3; Psalms 4:2"> And the Jews also joined in the attack, affirming that these things were so.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Peter 3:15; 1 Samuel 2:25; Luke 18:2"> When the governor had nodded to him to speak, Paul answered: “Knowing, as I do, that you have been an equitable<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="I follow the best line of transmission in reading “equitable”, albeit with only 25% of the Greek manuscripts here. <span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 24:1; Acts 21:18; Acts 23:11"> because you can ascertain that it is not more than twelve days since I went up to Jerusalem to worship.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 25:8; Acts 28:17; Acts 24:18"> They did not find me disputing with anyone or stirring up a crowd—not in the temple, not in the synagogues, not around the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Acts 25:7; 1 Peter 3:16"> Nor can they prove the things of which they now accuse me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Acts 9:2; Romans 3:21; Acts 3:13"> But I do profess this to you, that according to the Way that they call a sect, that is how I worship the ancestral God, believing all things that stand written throughout the Law and the Prophets,</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Daniel 12:2; John 5:28–29; 1 Thessalonians 4:14–16"> having hope in God, which these themselves also look for, that there will be a resurrection of the dead,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Some 6.6% of the Greek manuscripts omit ‘of the dead’ (as in NIV, NASB, LB, etc.).">e</a></sup>both the just and unjust.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Everyone will be resurrected, but the two resurrections are very different—which is why Paul applied himself.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Acts 23:1; 1 Timothy 1:5; 2 Corinthians 4:2"> And this is why I apply myself to always have a clear conscience before both God and men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Galatians 2:10; Romans 15:25–28; 1 Corinthians 16:1–4"> Now after many years I came to bring alms and offerings to my nation,</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Acts 26:21; Acts 21:26–30; Acts 24:12"> in the midst of which certain Jews from Asia found me purified in the temple, with neither crowd nor confusion.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Acts 23:30; Acts 25:16"> They are the ones that had to be here before you and make accusation, if they had anything against me.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="According to Roman law, the accusers had to be there.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> Or let these themselves say what wrong they found in me, when I stood before the council,</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Acts 23:6; Acts 4:2; Acts 26:6–8"> unless it be for this one statement that I called out, standing among them, ‘Concerning the resurrection of the dead I am being judged by you today.’ ”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 24:6; Acts 24:24; Acts 25:26"> Upon hearing these things Felix, having an accurate knowledge of the things concerning the Way, adjourned the proceedings and said, “When Lysias the commander comes down I will decide your case.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Acts 28:16; Acts 27:3; Proverbs 16:7"> And he ordered the centurion that Paul should be kept in custody but have some freedom, and not to forbid any of his friends to provide for or to visit him.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Both Lysias and Felix knew that Paul was not really guilty; and the Jews had figured out that they were not going to get what they wanted from either of them. <span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 20:21; Galatians 2:16; Mark 6:20"> Now after some days, when Felix came with his wife Drusilla, who was a Jewess, he sent for Paul and heard him concerning the faith into Christ Jesus.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Some 45% of the Greek manuscripts omit ‘Jesus’, as in AV and NKJV.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="John 16:8; 1 Peter 3:15; Acts 10:42"> But as he expounded on righteousness, self-control, and the judgment to come, Felix became afraid and answered, “Go away for now; when I have occasion I will summon you.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="To believe into Jesus would require changes that Felix was not prepared to make.">j</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="1 Timothy 6:9–10; Proverbs 17:23; Isaiah 56:11"> At the same time he was also hoping that Paul would give him money, that he might release him; so he frequently summoned and conversed with him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Acts 25:9; Acts 25:14; Acts 12:3"> But after two years, Felix was succeeded by Porcius Festus; so Felix, wanting to do the Jews a favor, left Paul in prison.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="During those two years Paul had been eating at the empire's expense, but apparently this did not represent a problem to Felix.
