## Paul sent to Rome

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 25:25; Acts 25:12; Acts 10:1"> Now when it was decided that we<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="I take it that Luke has been around all the time.">a</a></sup>should sail for Italy, they delivered Paul and certain other prisoners to a centurion named Julius, of the imperial cohort.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Acts 19:29; Philemon 1:24; Colossians 4:10"> So we embarked in a ship of Atramytium, being about to sail to points along the Asian coast, and we put to sea; Aristarchus, a Macedonian of Thessalonica, was with us.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Acts 24:23; Acts 28:16; Acts 27:43"> The next day we landed at Sidon; Julius treated Paul kindly and permitted him to go to his friends and receive care.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Acts 4:36; Acts 15:39; Acts 13:4"> Putting to sea from there, we sailed under the lee of Cyprus, because the winds were contrary.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Acts 6:9; Acts 13:13; Acts 15:41"> And when we had sailed across the sea that is off Cilicia and Pamphylia, we landed at Myra in Lycia.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 28:11; Acts 27:1; Acts 6:9"> There the centurion found a ship of Alexandria sailing for Italy and put us on board.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Acts 27:21; Titus 1:5; Titus 1:12"> When we had sailed slowly many days and scarcely arrived opposite Cnidus, the wind did not allow us to go forward, so we sailed for the lee of Crete<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="They had been sailing west along the coast of Asia Minor and now turn south to Crete.">b</a></sup>by way of Salmone;</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> sailing along it with difficulty we came to a certain place called Fair Havens, near the town of Lasea.</span>
</div>
</div>

## A difference of opinion

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Numbers 29:7; Leviticus 23:27–29; Leviticus 16:29–31"> Much time had been lost and sailing was already dangerous, because even the Fast had already taken place; Paul repeatedly warned them</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Daniel 2:30; Acts 27:20–26; Acts 27:41–44"> saying, “Men, I perceive that the voyage is about to be with damage and great loss, not only of the cargo and the ship, but even of our lives.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ezekiel 33:4; Hebrews 11:7; Ezekiel 3:17–18"> But the centurion<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="It appears that the centurion had the last word.">c</a></sup>was persuaded by the pilot and ship owner rather than by what Paul said.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Psalms 107:30; Acts 27:8"> And since the harbor was not suitable to winter in, the majority advised sailing on, to see if they could get to Phoenix in order to winter, it being a harbor of Crete facing southwest and northwest.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="I suppose the point to be that it was completely open to the west; there is such a spot on the present coastline of Crete, though the name ‘phoenix’ has disappeared.">d</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## The storm

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Luke 12:55; Song of Solomon 4:16; Job 37:17"> So when the south wind blew gently, supposing that they had obtained their purpose, they weighed anchor and sailed along Crete, close to shore.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Mark 4:37; Psalms 107:25–27; Exodus 14:21–27"> But not long after, a cyclonic wind blasted down from the land (it is called Euroclydon).</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="James 3:4; Acts 27:27"> Well the ship was caught and could not head into the wind, so we gave up and were driven along.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="The blast of wind was sudden and they were too close to the shore to have much room to maneuver. To remain broadside to the wind was to be swamped, so they turned tail and the wind drove them out to the open Mediterranean. I imagine that a little supernatural activity was involved.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> Running under the lee of a small island called Clauda, we barely managed to secure the skiff;</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Acts 27:29; Acts 27:26; Acts 27:41"> when they had hoisted it aboard, they used cables to undergird the ship; and fearing that they might fall into the Syrtis,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="By the time they finished undergirding the ship they were probably about a fifth of the way across the Mediterranean (the wind was driving them south, in a hurry). The Syrtis evidently referred to two shallow bays on the African coast that had shifting sand bars and liked to snare boats. The sea anchor was presumably dropped off the stern to provide drag and slow their progress.">f</a></sup>they lowered the sea anchor and so were driven along.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Jonah 1:5; Acts 27:38; Matthew 16:26"> We were being so violently battered by the storm that the next day they began to jettison things,</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Job 2:4; Luke 9:24–25; Mark 8:35–37"> and on the third we threw off the ship's tackle with our own hands.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Matthew 24:29; Matthew 8:24–25; Jeremiah 2:25"> When neither sun nor stars appeared for many days and a major storm was still pounding us, all hope that we would be saved was now taken away.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Genesis 42:22; Psalms 107:5–6; Acts 27:7"> Now after long abstinence from food, Paul stood up in the midst of them and said: “Well men, it would have been better to take my advice and not sail from Crete, only to ‘gain’ this damage and loss.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Paul's ‘I told you so’ is really quite bland, and is followed by good news.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 27:25; Acts 27:36; 2 Corinthians 4:8–9"> But now I urge you to take heart, because there will be no loss of life among you, only of the ship.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Acts 23:11; Romans 1:9; 2 Timothy 4:17"> This night an angel of the God whose I am and whom I serve stood by me</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 23:11; 2 Timothy 4:16–17; Matthew 10:28"> and said: ‘Do not be afraid, Paul; you must stand before Caesar. Furthermore, God has granted you all those who sail with you.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Romans 4:20–21; Luke 1:45; Numbers 23:19"> So take courage, men, because I believe in God that it will be just as it was told me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 28:1; Acts 27:17; Acts 27:29"> Also, we must run aground on a certain island.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Jonah 1:6; Acts 27:30; Revelation 18:17"> Now when the fourteenth night had come, as we were being driven here and there in the Adriatic,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="This term presumably took in a larger area than it does today.">h</a></sup>about midnight the sailors sensed that they were nearing some land.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> They took a sounding and found twenty fathoms; going on a little they took a sounding again and found fifteen fathoms.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Acts 27:17; Hebrews 6:19; Deuteronomy 28:67"> So fearing that we might be driven into a rocky area, they dropped four anchors from the stern and started praying for day to come.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Acts 27:16; Acts 27:32"> Now under pretense of putting out anchors from the prow, the sailors lowered the skiff into the sea, intending to flee from the ship;</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 Thessalonians 2:13–14; Psalms 91:11–12; Jeremiah 29:11–13"> so Paul said to the centurion and to the soldiers, “Unless these stay in the ship, you cannot be saved.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Philippians 3:7–9; Luke 16:8"> Then the soldiers severed the ropes of the skiff and let it fall away.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="This seems a pity, since a skiff would have been useful in getting people to land later. But in any case, the centurion believed him!">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33"> While the day was coming on, Paul started urging them all to receive food, saying: “Today is the fourteenth day of waiting—you continue without eating, having taken nothing.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Matthew 10:30; 1 Kings 1:52; Luke 21:18"> Therefore I urge you to take nourishment, because this is for our survival, since not a hair will fall from the head of any of you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Matthew 15:36; 1 Corinthians 10:30–31; 1 Timothy 4:3–4"> Upon saying this he picked up some bread and gave thanks to God in front of them all; then he broke it and began to eat.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="2 Corinthians 1:4–6; Acts 27:22; Psalms 27:14"> So they all were encouraged and took food themselves.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="Acts 2:41; Romans 13:1; 1 Peter 3:20"> (In all, we were two hundred seventy-six souls on the ship.)</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="Jonah 1:5; Matthew 6:25; Acts 27:18–19"> So when they had eaten enough, they started lightening the ship by throwing out the wheat into the sea.</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Acts 28:1"> When it was day, they did not recognize the land, but they noticed a bay with a beach, onto which they planned to run the ship, if possible.</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="Isaiah 33:23; Acts 27:29–30"> Casting off the anchors they left them in the sea, at the same time untying the rudder ropes; and hoisting the foresail to the wind, they made for the beach.</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="2 Corinthians 11:25–26; Ezekiel 27:26; Acts 27:26–29"> But they fell into a place where two seas met and ran the vessel aground; the prow stuck fast and remained immovable, but the stern began to be broken up by the violence of the waves.</span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="Mark 15:15–20; Ecclesiastes 9:3; Psalms 74:20"> Now the plan of the soldiers was to kill the prisoners, lest any of them should swim away and escape.</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43" data-xref="Acts 27:3; Proverbs 16:7; Acts 27:11"> But the centurion, wanting to save Paul, stopped them from doing it and ordered those who could swim to jump in first and go toward the land,</span>
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44" data-xref="Acts 27:22; Psalms 107:28–30; John 6:39–40"> followed by the rest, some on planks and some on things from the ship. And in this way they all escaped safely to the land.</span>
</div>
</div>
