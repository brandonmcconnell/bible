## Enter Herod

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Matthew 24:9; John 15:20; Matthew 10:17–18"> Now about that time, Herod the king laid hands on some from the church to mistreat them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Hebrews 11:37; Matthew 20:23; Matthew 4:21–22"> James, the brother of John, he put to death by sword.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="So far as we know, James was the first of the Apostles to die (discounting the Iscariot), and he was martyred.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Exodus 23:15; John 12:43; Acts 24:27"> When he saw that it was pleasing to the Jews, he proceeded to arrest Peter as well (it was during the days of the unleavened loaves)</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Proverbs 19:21; Luke 22:33; Luke 21:12"> —upon seizing him he put him in prison, turning him over to sixteen soldiers<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Why so many? Had he heard about Peter's spiritual power?">b</a></sup>to guard him, intending to bring him out to the people after the Passover.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Enter angel

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Corinthians 12:26; 1 Thessalonians 5:17; James 5:16"> Well Peter was being held in the prison all right, but the congregation was making earnest prayer to God on his behalf.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 21:33; Hebrews 13:6; Philippians 4:6–7"> So when Herod was about to bring him out,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="‘Bring him out’ means to execute him.">c</a></sup>that night Peter was sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains, with guards protecting the prison in front of the doors.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Acts 5:19; Acts 16:26; Psalms 146:7"> Suddenly, an angel of the Lord was there, and a light shone in the cell; striking Peter on the side he roused him saying, “Quick, get up!” and the chains fell away from his wrists.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> Then the angel said to him, “Fasten your belt and put on your sandals.” So he did. Then he said to him, “Put on your cloak and follow me.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Psalms 126:1; Hebrews 11:8; Genesis 45:26"> So he went out and started following him, not realizing that what the angel was doing was real; he supposed he was seeing a vision.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Acts 5:19; Acts 16:26; Isaiah 45:1–2"> Passing through the first and second guard posts, they came to the iron gate that leads into the city, that opened for them by itself;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="The angel used supernatural power; and if we ever learn how to do it, we can too—Ephesians 3:20.">d</a></sup>upon exiting they advanced one block, and suddenly the angel left him.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Enter Rhoda

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Daniel 6:22; Daniel 3:28; 2 Peter 2:9"> When Peter had come to himself he said, “Now I know for certain that the Lord sent His angel and delivered me out of Herod's hand and from all the expectation of the Jewish people.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 John 5:14–15; Acts 12:5; Matthew 18:19–20"> Upon reflection he went to the house of Mary, the mother of John (the one called Mark), where a considerable number had gathered and were praying.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Acts 12:16; Luke 13:25; John 18:16–17"> When Peter knocked at the door of the gate, a servant girl named Rhoda came to answer.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Luke 24:41; Matthew 28:8"> Upon recognizing Peter's voice, she was so glad she did not open the gate, but ran in and announced that Peter was standing before the gate!</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Matthew 18:10; Luke 24:11; Mark 16:14"> But they said to her, “You're crazy!” but she kept insisting that it was so. So they said, “It is his angel.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> But Peter kept on knocking; so when they opened the door and saw him, they were astounded.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="They evidently did not expect their prayer to be answered. After all, they had probably prayed for James first, and he was killed anyway. When we pray about a difficult situation, we should start out by asking what the Father is doing—John 5:19.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Acts 21:40; Acts 13:16; Acts 19:33"> Motioning to them with his hand to be silent, he related to them how the Lord had brought him out of the prison. Then he said, “Tell these things to James<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="This James is the Lord's half-brother; he became the leader of the church in Jerusalem.">f</a></sup>and the brothers.” And going out he went off to a different place.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Presumably this means that he left Jerusalem; it was prudent to disappear for a while.">g</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Poor guards!

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Acts 5:22–25; Acts 16:27; Acts 19:23"> Now as soon as it was day, there was no small commotion among the soldiers about what had become of Peter!</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Acts 16:27; Psalms 37:32–33; John 12:10–11"> Well after searching for him and not finding him, Herod examined the guards and ordered that they be executed. Going down from Judea to Caesarea, he stayed there.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="He had doubtless told the Jews that he had Peter, and when he couldn't produce him he lost face. But the circumstances probably left him uncomfortable as well, so he decided to get away for awhile—none of which saved the poor guards. The guards were part of an evil system, and presumably would never have converted (although, if Peter had preached to them, some may have believed before the execution).">h</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Herod gets his ‘comeuppance’

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Ezra 3:7; Matthew 11:21–22; Ezekiel 27:17"> Now Herod was very angry with the people of Tyre and Sidon; so they came with one accord to him, and having won over Blastus, the king's chamberlain, they asked for peace, because their country was supplied with food from the king's.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="The famine mentioned in chapter eleven may have made them especially dependent.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> So on an appointed day Herod, arrayed in royal apparel, sat on the throne and started to deliver an address to them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 14:10–13; Revelation 13:4; Jude 1:16"> But the crowd started calling out, “The voice of a god and not of a man!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Psalms 115:1; Acts 10:25–26; 2 Thessalonians 2:4"> Immediately an angel of the Lord struck him down, because he did not give glory to God, and being eaten by worms, he died.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="The worms, of unusual size and impressive number, would come out of the corpse, indicating the cause of death—really gross!">j</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 6:7; Acts 19:20; 2 Thessalonians 3:1"> And the Word of God kept growing and multiplying.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Growing in influence and multiplying its effects.">k</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Acts 12:12; 1 Peter 5:13; Acts 15:37"> Now Barnabas and Saul returned to Antioch,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="We have here an important set of variant readings, wherein the evidence is rather badly divided. The discussion is quite complicated and extensive, so I refer the interested reader to the Appendix: “Where to place a Comma—Acts 12:25”.">l</a></sup>having fulfilled their mission, also taking with them John, the one called Mark.</span>
</div>
</div>
