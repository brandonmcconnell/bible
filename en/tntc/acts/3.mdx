## A man lame from birth

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Psalms 55:17; Acts 10:3; Acts 4:13"> Now Peter and John were going up together into the temple at the hour (the ninth)<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="If Luke is using Hebrew time, it was 3 p.m., if Roman, it was 9 a.m., both being Jewish times for prayer. ## Peter heals him

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Acts 14:9–10; John 11:40; Luke 4:20"> So Peter, with John, fastening his gaze on him said, “Look at us.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> So he gave them his attention, expecting to receive something from them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 3:16; Acts 10:38; Acts 4:10"> But Peter said: “I do not have silver and gold, but what I do have I give you. In the name of Jesus Christ the Natsorean, get up and walk!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Alack! <span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Mark 1:31; Mark 9:27; Acts 9:41"> And grasping him by his right hand he lifted him up;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="This was an act of faith on Peter's part; if nothing happened the man would fall back down.">d</a></sup>immediately his feet and ankles were strengthened.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Acts 14:10; Isaiah 35:6; Psalms 107:20–22"> So jumping up he stood, and began to walk!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="He had never learned to walk (lame from birth), so how could he just start walking, not to mention leaping? <span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 4:21; Acts 4:16; Luke 13:17"> Well all the people saw him walking and praising God;</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Acts 3:2; John 5:20; John 9:3"> and they recognized him—that he was the one who used to sit at the Beautiful Gate of the temple, with a view to alms—and they were filled with wonder and amazement because of what had happened to him.</span>

## Peter preaches

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 5:12; John 10:23; Luke 8:38"> Now as the lame man who had been healed held on<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="I assume that he was literally hanging on to them, perhaps their clothes—for whatever reason he did not want to be separated from them. Some 11% of the Greek manuscripts omit ‘the lame man who had been healed’ (as in NIV [the beggar], NASB, LB, TEV [the man], etc.).">g</a></sup>to Peter and John, all the people ran together to them in the portico, the one called ‘Solomon's’, really wondering.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Corinthians 3:5; John 3:27–28; Daniel 2:28–30"> So upon observing this Peter responded to the people: “Men of Israel, why are you marveling at this, or why are you staring at us as if we have made him walk by our own power or godliness?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Well, what had happened was extraordinary, to say the least, and probably deserved a little staring.">h</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Matthew 22:32; Acts 7:32; Matthew 27:17–25"> The God of Abraham and Isaac and Jacob, the God of our fathers, glorified His Servant Jesus, whom you delivered up; and you repudiated Him to Pilate's face, when he was intending to release Him.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Comparing Matthew 27:11-26, Mark 15:2-19, Luke 23:3-25 and John 18:33-19:15 it becomes clear that Pilate wanted no part of killing Jesus and tried hard to release Him. But they not only repudiated Jesus, they repudiated their claim to the Messiah, saying, “We have no king but Caesar”.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Mark 1:24; Acts 7:52; 1 Peter 3:18"> Yes you repudiated the holy and righteous One, and you asked that a murderer be granted to you,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Peter does not mince words; he is emphatic about their guilt.">j</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Acts 2:24; Revelation 21:6; Acts 2:32"> while you killed the Originator of the Life,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="A curious expression! How could anyone kill the Originator of the Life? Well, it was certainly their intention to kill Jesus, and Peter declares their guilt, but Jesus gave up His own life, as John 10:17-18 makes clear.">k</a></sup>whom God raised from among the dead, to which we are witnesses.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Matthew 21:21–22; Acts 4:10; Acts 14:9"> Well His name, based on faith in His name,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="A name represents the person, so it was faith in Jesus that produced the healing; but Peter put his faith into action.">l</a></sup>made this man strong, whom you see and know. Yes, the faith that is through Him<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="If the Lord Jesus is not the actual source of the faith, it is mediated through Him.">m</a></sup>has given him this wholeness in the presence of you all.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Luke 23:34; Acts 13:27; John 16:3"> “So now, brothers, I know that you did it in ignorance, as also your rulers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Isaiah 53:1–12; Acts 26:22–23; Acts 2:23"> But the things that God foretold through the mouth of all His prophets, that the Messiah would suffer, He has thus fulfilled.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Acts 2:38; Isaiah 44:22; Isaiah 43:25"> Repent therefore, and turn around, so that your sins may be erased, in order that times of refreshing may come from the Lord's face<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Although the Lord's return is still future, by His grace, and by walking in the Spirit, we have the privilege of experiencing our own little ‘times of refreshing’.">n</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Matthew 24:30–36; Revelation 19:11–16; 2 Thessalonians 2:2"> and that He may send Jesus, who had been ordained to be your Messiah,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="That is what the Text says. But now Jesus will only return when it is time to ‘restore all things’. But comparing the first clause of verse 19 with that of verse 20 it almost seems that Peter is saying that they could bring Jesus back right away.">o</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Romans 8:21; Isaiah 1:26; Acts 1:11"> whom Heaven must receive until the times of restoration of all things,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="I take this to be a reference to the Messianic Kingdom, the Millennium.">p</a></sup>of which times<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="The relative pronoun here is ambiguous as to the antecedent, it could refer to ‘things’ or ‘times’, but verse 24 below makes clear that it is ‘times’.">q</a></sup>God spoke long ago by the mouth of all His holy prophets.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Deuteronomy 18:15–19; Acts 7:37; Galatians 4:4"> “For example, Moses said to the fathers:<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="Some 4% of the Greek manuscripts omit “to the fathers” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">r</a></sup>‘The Lord our<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-s" id="fnref-s" data-fn="Instead of ‘our’, perhaps 40% of the Greek manuscripts read ‘your’.">s</a></sup>God will raise up for you a Prophet, like me, from among your brothers. You must listen to Him, in all that He may say to you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Deuteronomy 18:19; John 12:48; Hebrews 10:28–30"> It will be that every person who will not listen to that Prophet will be extirpated from among the people.’<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-t" id="fnref-t" data-fn="See Deuteronomy 18:15-19. </div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 3:21; Luke 24:27; 1 Samuel 3:20"> “Yes and all the prophets, from Samuel on down,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-u" id="fnref-u" data-fn="Samuel was a prophet.">u</a></sup>as many as have spoken, have also foretold<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-v" id="fnref-v" data-fn="I follow the best line of transmission in reading “foretold”, rather than ‘proclaimed’, albeit representing only 25% of the Greek manuscripts, at this point.">v</a></sup>these days.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Genesis 22:18; Galatians 3:8; Acts 2:39"> You are sons of the prophets, and of the covenant which God made with our fathers, saying to Abraham, ‘Yes, in your seed<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-w" id="fnref-w" data-fn="“Seed” is singular. Paul runs with this in Galatians 3:16.">w</a></sup>all the families<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-x" id="fnref-x" data-fn="The reference is to patriarchal families, units much smaller than whole tribes or ethnic nations; God emphasizes the importance of the family, while Satan works to destroy it.">x</a></sup>of the earth will be blessed.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Matthew 1:21; Acts 3:22; Jude 1:24"> God, having raised up His Servant Jesus,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-y" id="fnref-y" data-fn="Perhaps 4% of the Greek manuscripts, of inferior quality, omit “Jesus” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.). As is often the case, the eclectic text weakens the statement.">y</a></sup>sent Him to you first,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-z" id="fnref-z" data-fn="Wait a minute! When did this ‘sending’ take place? It was after the resurrection, but Jesus never showed Himself to the people at large during the forty days. </div>
