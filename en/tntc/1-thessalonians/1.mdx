## The Thessalonians are praised

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Romans 1:8–9; Ephesians 1:15–16; Philemon 1:4"> We always give thanks to God for all of you, making mention of you in our prayers,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Corinthians 13:13; Revelation 2:19; Galatians 5:6"> continually remembering before our God and Father your work for the Faith and your labor coming from the Love and your perseverance based on the Hope, our Lord Jesus Christ being their source,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="We are used to the rendering: ‘work of faith, labor of love, and patience of hope in our Lord Jesus Christ’. However, “faith”, “love”, “hope” and “Lord” all have the definite article and are in the genitive/ablative case. If we translate the definite articles it changes the ‘flavor’; we are looking at a particular faith, a particular love, a particular hope, and the three qualities either belong to the Lord or proceed from Him. The genitive/ablative case in Greek is quite ‘versatile’, and so just to render ‘of’ all the time is so ambiguous as to leave the precise intent undefined. (However, I can't guarantee that my attempt to ‘disambiguate’ is correct.)">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Thessalonians 2:13; Titus 3:4–5; 2 Peter 1:10"> knowing as we do, brothers loved by God, that you are chosen.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="They are loved and chosen by God, marvelous privilege—verse 3 describes their appropriate response, which I suppose is how Paul ‘knew’ that they were chosen.">e</a></sup></span>
</div>

## They became examples

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Thessalonians 3:7–9; Mark 16:20; 1 Corinthians 2:2–5"> You see, our Gospel did not come to you in word only, but also in power and in the Holy Spirit<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="In verses 1 and 2 it is clear that the Father and the Son are distinct persons; here Paul adds the Holy Spirit.">f</a></sup>and with complete certainty<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Powerful manifestations of the Holy Spirit do have a way of adding certainty to the spoken word; they also help to face affliction with joy (next verse).">g</a></sup>(of course you know what sort of men we proved to be among you for your sake).<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Why “for your sake”? They were giving an example to be followed.">h</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Corinthians 11:1; 1 Corinthians 4:16; Acts 13:52"> Yes you became imitators of us and of the Lord,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="To imitate the messengers was to imitate the Lord; their walk with God presumably had something to do with the powerful manifestations.">i</a></sup>having received the Word with the Holy Spirit's joy,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="“The Holy Spirit's joy” in the soul is an unanswerable proof of the reality of true conversion; it also enables you to face “severe affliction” with equanimity.">j</a></sup>in spite of severe affliction,</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Titus 2:7; 1 Peter 5:3; 1 Timothy 4:12"> so that you became examples<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="They bought into the messengers' life style of total commitment to Christ and His Kingdom.">k</a></sup>to all the believers in Macedonia and Achaia.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Romans 1:8; Romans 10:14–18; Isaiah 52:7"> That is because the Word of the Lord sounded out from you, not only in Macedonia and Achaia but also in every place<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="For something to resound far and wide requires a sufficient cause. Their response to the Gospel was sufficiently vigorous that it caused widespread comment, comment about their transformed lives and the Message that could produce such transformation.">l</a></sup>—your faith toward God has gone forth, so that we do not need to say anything.</span>
</div>

## Their hope

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Timothy 4:10; 1 Corinthians 12:2; Galatians 4:8–9"> They themselves report about you<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Instead of “you”, perhaps 60% of the Greek manuscripts have ‘us’, as in most versions. <span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 5:9; Isaiah 25:8–9; Romans 5:9–10"> and to wait for His Son coming out of the heavens<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="Verses 9-10 give a good picture of true conversion. ## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">1:1</span> Paul gives recognition to the junior members of the team.</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">1:1</span> The first image that the term ‘church’ evokes in the minds of many is that of a building; what is in view here is a group of people, and a very particular group of people.</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">1:1</span> To the ‘western’ mind the repetition of the full form, “God our Father and Sovereign Jesus Christ,” may seem unnecessary, if not stylistically poor, but this letter was written by a Hebrew. By switching from ‘the’ to ‘our’ Father he claims a personal relationship. Some 7.5% of the Greek manuscripts omit “from God our Father and Sovereign Jesus Christ” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).</li>
<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">1:3</span> We are used to the rendering: ‘work of faith, labor of love, and patience of hope in our Lord Jesus Christ’. However, “faith”, “love”, “hope” and “Lord” all have the definite article and are in the genitive/ablative case. If we translate the definite articles it changes the ‘flavor’; we are looking at a particular faith, a particular love, a particular hope, and the three qualities either belong to the Lord or proceed from Him. The genitive/ablative case in Greek is quite ‘versatile’, and so just to render ‘of’ all the time is so ambiguous as to leave the precise intent undefined. (However, I can't guarantee that my attempt to ‘disambiguate’ is correct.)</li>
<li id="fn-e"><a href="#fnref-e" className="fnb">e</a> <span className="fnr">1:4</span> They are loved and chosen by God, marvelous privilege—verse 3 describes their appropriate response, which I suppose is how Paul ‘knew’ that they were chosen.</li>
<li id="fn-f"><a href="#fnref-f" className="fnb">f</a> <span className="fnr">1:5</span> In verses 1 and 2 it is clear that the Father and the Son are distinct persons; here Paul adds the Holy Spirit.</li>
<li id="fn-g"><a href="#fnref-g" className="fnb">g</a> <span className="fnr">1:5</span> Powerful manifestations of the Holy Spirit do have a way of adding certainty to the spoken word; they also help to face affliction with joy (next verse).</li>
<li id="fn-h"><a href="#fnref-h" className="fnb">h</a> <span className="fnr">1:5</span> Why “for your sake”? They were giving an example to be followed.</li>
<li id="fn-i"><a href="#fnref-i" className="fnb">i</a> <span className="fnr">1:6</span> To imitate the messengers was to imitate the Lord; their walk with God presumably had something to do with the powerful manifestations.</li>
<li id="fn-j"><a href="#fnref-j" className="fnb">j</a> <span className="fnr">1:6</span> “The Holy Spirit's joy” in the soul is an unanswerable proof of the reality of true conversion; it also enables you to face “severe affliction” with equanimity.</li>
<li id="fn-k"><a href="#fnref-k" className="fnb">k</a> <span className="fnr">1:7</span> They bought into the messengers' life style of total commitment to Christ and His Kingdom.</li>
<li id="fn-l"><a href="#fnref-l" className="fnb">l</a> <span className="fnr">1:8</span> For something to resound far and wide requires a sufficient cause. Their response to the Gospel was sufficiently vigorous that it caused widespread comment, comment about their transformed lives and the Message that could produce such transformation.</li>
<li id="fn-m"><a href="#fnref-m" className="fnb">m</a> <span className="fnr">1:9</span> Instead of “you”, perhaps 60% of the Greek manuscripts have ‘us’, as in most versions. I follow the best line of transmission.</li>
<li id="fn-n"><a href="#fnref-n" className="fnb">n</a> <span className="fnr">1:9</span> Paul did not have to ‘toot his own horn’.</li>
<li id="fn-o"><a href="#fnref-o" className="fnb">o</a> <span className="fnr">1:10</span> Verses 9-10 give a good picture of true conversion. It involves turning your back on all the ‘idols’ that governed your life to trust in the true God; but the only acceptable way to truly relate to the Sovereign of the universe is as a slave = total commitment; this gives a valid hope for a bright future.</li>
<li id="fn-p"><a href="#fnref-p" className="fnb">p</a> <span className="fnr">1:10</span> Resurrection has to do with bodies (it is the body that is resurrected, to be reunited with the spirit), so the Son will return with a visible body. As the angels said, He will return ‘in like manner’ (Acts 1:11).</li>
<li id="fn-q"><a href="#fnref-q" className="fnb">q</a> <span className="fnr">1:10</span> Which expression of the Wrath is this? Jesus delivers us from the ultimate wrath, the Lake of fire, but based on 4:13-18 below I imagine that this refers to a pre-wrath Rapture.</li>
</ol>
