## Diotrephes is criticized

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Titus 1:7–16; Luke 22:24–27; Matthew 23:4–8"> I wrote to the congregation, but Diotrephes, who wants to dominate the others, does not acknowledge us.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="I believe this is the main reason for this letter—since Diotrephes censored the letter that John wrote to the congregation, he now writes to an individual, to get around the censorship. <span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Isaiah 66:5; 2 Corinthians 13:2; Luke 6:22"> So if I come, I will bring up what he is doing, slandering us with malignant words. And not satisfied with that, not only does he himself not receive the brothers, but he forbids those who want to do so, kicking them out of the congregation.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="One wonders where he got the power or authority to be able to do this. ## Demetrius is commended

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Isaiah 1:16–17; Psalms 37:27; Ephesians 5:1"> Dear one, do not imitate what is bad, but what is good. He who does good is of God, but he who does evil has not seen God.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="John is good at making flat statements, which most of us find to be uncomfortable, because we would like to think there are exceptions. I imagine that John would insist that anyone who has really seen God will not do evil.">h</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Timothy 3:7; John 21:24; Acts 22:12"> Demetrius is well spoken of by everyone—even by the truth itself. Yes, we also speak well of him, and we know<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="I follow the best line of transmission here, using the first person, albeit representing only 23% of the manuscripts. ## Footnotes

<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">1:3</span> There is no article with “truth” here (or in verse 4). To love someone in truth means to truly love, presumably, but to walk in truth does not mean to truly walk; a different meaning is required—the principal candidates would be God's Word and the Holy Spirit.</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">1:7</span> Users of the AV and NKJV are accustomed to ‘His name’, which is based on some 15% of the Greek manuscripts. I take it that the 85%, including the best line of transmission, are correct in reading “the Name”. Both readings refer to the Lord Jesus Christ, presumably, “the name which is above every name” (Philippians 2:9), and at which every knee shall bow.</li>
<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">1:7</span> The word rendered “pagans” is often translated ‘nations’ or ‘Gentiles’, but in this context the reference is not to nations, nor to non-Jews. The reference is probably to non-Christians. But what of the principle involved here? It seems clear that John is in agreement with the decision not to accept financial support from unbelievers. Would this be because of possible ‘strings’ attached, or is something going on in the spiritual realm? But really, who wants to tell a donor that he is a sinner?</li>
<li id="fn-e"><a href="#fnref-e" className="fnb">e</a> <span className="fnr">1:8</span> John is talking about giving hospitality, a place to stay and food to eat, but also about giving a good send off.</li>
<li id="fn-f"><a href="#fnref-f" className="fnb">f</a> <span className="fnr">1:9</span> I believe this is the main reason for this letter—since Diotrephes censored the letter that John wrote to the congregation, he now writes to an individual, to get around the censorship. Unfortunately, Diotrephes-types are still with us, and not even unusual.</li>
<li id="fn-g"><a href="#fnref-g" className="fnb">g</a> <span className="fnr">1:10</span> One wonders where he got the power or authority to be able to do this. Evidently people were letting him get away with it.</li>
<li id="fn-h"><a href="#fnref-h" className="fnb">h</a> <span className="fnr">1:11</span> John is good at making flat statements, which most of us find to be uncomfortable, because we would like to think there are exceptions. Don't unbelievers do good things? If a pastor does something bad, does that mean he doesn't know God? Just as all truth is from God and all lies are from Satan, all genuine good is from God and all evil is from Satan. I imagine that John would insist that anyone who has really seen God will not do evil.</li>
<li id="fn-i"><a href="#fnref-i" className="fnb">i</a> <span className="fnr">1:12</span> I follow the best line of transmission here, using the first person, albeit representing only 23% of the manuscripts.
