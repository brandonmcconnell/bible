<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">

## Once far, now near in Christ

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Colossians 2:11; Colossians 2:13; Colossians 3:11"> So then, remember that once you physical Gentiles—called ‘uncircumcision’ by the so-called ‘circumcision’ (that made in flesh with hands)—</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Galatians 4:8; Colossians 1:21; Jeremiah 31:31–34"> that at that time you were apart from Christ, excluded from citizenship in Israel and strangers to the covenants of the promise,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="“The promise”—that is what the Text says. ## Two into one

<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Galatians 3:28; Colossians 3:11; Colossians 3:15"> For He Himself is our peace, who has made both one and broken down the barrier of separation—the law with its commandments and ordinances—</span>

<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Colossians 1:20–22; Romans 5:10; 2 Corinthians 5:18–21"> and to reconcile them both in one body to God through the cross (by which He killed the enmity).<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="In this paragraph Paul is dealing with the enmity between Jew and Gentile; within the Jewish ‘religion’ Gentiles were definitely second class citizens—to be saved a Gentile had to become a spiritual Jew. <li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">2:2</span> Strictly speaking the Text has “according to the Aeon of this world, according to the ruler of the domain of the air”—the phrases are parallel, so ‘Aeon’ and ‘ruler’ have the same referent, a specific person or being. This spirit is presently at work (present tense) in ‘the sons of the disobedience’. ‘Sons’ of something are characterized by that something, and the something in this case is ‘the’ disobedience (the Text has the definite article)—a continuation of the original rebellion against the Sovereign of the universe. Anyone in rebellion against the Creator is under satanic influence, direct or indirect (in most cases a demon acts as Satan's agent, when something more than the influence of the surrounding culture is required). Anyone in rebellion against the Creator will also have strongholds of Satan in his mind.</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">2:3</span> This is the way people who do not belong to Christ may be expected to act. Far from being surprised, we should count on it, and our strategies (for living and evangelizing) should count on it.</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">2:3</span> To be in rebellion against the Creator is to be under His wrath.</li>
<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">2:6</span> This is tremendous! Here we have our authority. <li id="fn-e"><a href="#fnref-e" className="fnb">e</a> <span className="fnr">2:8</span> The Text has ‘the’ faith; the reference is to a specific faith, presumably the body of truth that revolves around the person of Jesus.</li>

<li id="fn-g"><a href="#fnref-g" className="fnb">g</a> <span className="fnr">2:10</span> We are not saved by good works, but for good works. We do good works because we are saved, and if you don't…</li>

<li id="fn-i"><a href="#fnref-i" className="fnb">i</a> <span className="fnr">2:12</span> “The promise”—that is what the Text says. The various covenants recorded in the Old Testament differ considerably from each other, but one might say that there is one promise common to all: God promises to bless obedience, those who obey Him. Within the Church of Jesus Christ that distinction is done away with; all are on the same footing, regardless of ethnic origin.</li>
