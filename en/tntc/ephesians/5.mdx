## Walk in love

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 John 4:11; 1 Peter 1:15–16; Ephesians 4:32"> Therefore be imitators of God as beloved children,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="John 13:34; Galatians 2:20; Colossians 3:14"> and walk in love, just as Christ loved us and gave Himself up for us, an offering and a sacrifice to God for a fragrant aroma.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Colossians 3:5; Ephesians 5:5; Galatians 5:19–21"> But, you should not even talk about fornication or any form of moral corruption or insatiableness<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Of course doing such things is out of the question, but why can't we talk about them? If you talk about it you will think about it, which will provide an opening for temptation.">a</a></sup>(as is fitting for saints);</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Colossians 3:8; Ephesians 4:29; 1 Thessalonians 5:18"> nor any obscenity, nor foolish talk or coarse joking (that do not belong), but rather thanksgiving.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Galatians 5:21; 1 Corinthians 6:9–10; Ephesians 5:3"> Because you can know this for sure: no fornicator, nor anyone who is morally corrupt or insatiable (actually an idolater),<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="To have an insatiable desire for something (say, money) is to be dominated by that something; it has become a god, an idolatry.">b</a></sup>has any inheritance in the Kingdom of the Christ and God.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="According to the grammatical construction here, “Christ and God” refer to the same person.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Romans 1:18; Colossians 2:8; Matthew 24:4"> Let no one deceive you with empty words,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Many ‘churches’ these days are making allowances for sexual immorality of various sorts (getting their values from Freud, not the Bible), but they will find themselves excluded from the Kingdom. Watch out for empty words!">d</a></sup>for because of these things the wrath of God comes upon the sons of the disobedience.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="See the note at 2:2 above.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Revelation 18:4; Ephesians 5:11; 1 Timothy 5:22"> Therefore do not be participants with them</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="John 8:12; 1 John 1:7; Acts 26:18"> —even though you were once darkness, you are now light in Sovereign. Walk in light Walk as children of light</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 John 3:9–10; Galatians 5:22–23; 3 John 1:11"> (for the fruit of the Spirit<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Instead of “Spirit”, some 5% of the Greek manuscripts have ‘light’ (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">f</a></sup>is in all goodness and righteousness and truth),</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Romans 12:1–2; Hebrews 12:28; Psalms 19:14"> approving what is pleasing to the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Romans 13:12; 1 Timothy 5:20; 2 Thessalonians 3:6"> Do not associate with the unfruitful works of the darkness, but rather expose/reprove them.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="I could not decide between ‘expose’ and ‘reprove’, since the verb here includes both ideas. So let us do both.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Ephesians 5:3; Jeremiah 23:24; Ecclesiastes 12:14"> (It is shameful even to speak of those things being done by them in secret.)</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="John 3:20–21; 1 Corinthians 4:5; Hosea 2:10"> Now all things, upon being exposed, are revealed by the light, for whatever reveals is light.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Isaiah 60:1; Romans 13:11–12; Isaiah 26:19"> Therefore He says:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“Wake up, O sleeper, and arise from the dead,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">and the Christ will shine on you.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="This is not a direct quote of any Old Testament passage. Not all God-given prophesies, though written down and read by subsequent generations, were included in the Canon.">h</a></sup></span>
</div>

## Walk in wisdom

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Colossians 4:5; James 3:13; Philippians 1:27"> See then that you walk carefully, not as unwise but as wise,</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Colossians 4:5; Amos 5:13; John 12:35"> redeeming the time, because the days are evil.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Romans 12:2; 1 Thessalonians 5:18; Colossians 1:9"> That is why you must not be foolish,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="The ultimate foolishness is to not understand the Lord's will.">i</a></sup>but understand what the Lord's will is.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Proverbs 20:1; Luke 1:15; Romans 13:13"> And do not get drunk on wine, which is harmful and wasteful;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Literally, ‘in which is dissipation’, so where did I get ‘harmful and wasteful’? Drunkenness always harms and wastes.">j</a></sup>rather be filled with the Spirit,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Observe that this is a command, so it depends on us. The Holy Spirit is waiting to fill us, if we will only yield to Him. For light to fill the room all you have to do is flip the switch.">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Colossians 3:16; 1 Corinthians 14:26; Psalms 95:2"> speaking to one another with psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing and making music in your hearts to the Lord;</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Colossians 3:17; 1 Thessalonians 5:18; Hebrews 13:15"> always giving thanks to God the Father concerning all things,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="We are to declare God's goodness, faithfulness and wisdom, even when we do not like what is happening.">l</a></sup>in the name of our Lord, Jesus Christ,</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Philippians 2:3; Ephesians 5:24; Ephesians 5:22"> submitting to one another in the fear of God.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Instead of “God”, perhaps 30% of the Greek manuscripts have ‘Christ’ (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">m</a></sup></span>
</div>

## About wives

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Colossians 3:18–1; 1 Peter 3:1–6; Titus 2:5"> Wives, subordinate yourselves<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Observe that this is something she should do of her own volition, not because she is beaten into submission.">n</a></sup>to your own husbands, as to the Lord,</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Corinthians 11:3–10; Colossians 1:18; Ephesians 1:22–23"> because a man is head of his wife as also Christ is Head of the Church—He also is Preserver of the body.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Ephesians 5:33; Colossians 3:20; Titus 2:7"> Yes, just as the Church is subject to Christ, so also the wives should be to their own husbands in everything.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="This “everything” obviously does not include things that God forbids.">o</a></sup></span>
</div>

## About husbands

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="1 Peter 3:7; Colossians 3:19; Ephesians 5:28"> Husbands, love your own wives just as Christ also loved the Church and gave Himself up on her behalf,</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Hebrews 10:22; John 15:3; Ezekiel 36:25"> in order that He might sanctify her, having cleansed her by the washing of the water: in a word,</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Colossians 1:22; Hebrews 9:14; 1 Peter 1:19"> that He might present her—the splendid Church—to Himself, not having a blemish or a wrinkle or any such thing, but that she be holy and blameless.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Ephesians 5:25; Matthew 19:5; Genesis 2:21–24"> Just so the husbands ought to love their own wives as their own bodies; he who loves his wife loves himself.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Ezekiel 34:14–15; Ephesians 5:31; Ecclesiastes 4:5"> After all, no one ever hated his own flesh; rather he nourishes and cherishes it, just as the Lord does the Church</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="1 Corinthians 6:15; 1 Corinthians 12:12–27; Romans 12:5"> —for we are members of His body, of His flesh and of His bones.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="Less than 2.5% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, omit “of His flesh and of His bones” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.—an easy case of ‘similar ending’). Flesh and bones are definitely physical, not spiritual; so what is Paul saying? Perhaps he is emphasizing that “His body” is just as real as a physical body.">p</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Genesis 2:24; Matthew 19:5; Mark 10:7–8"> “For this reason a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two will become one flesh.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="This is not just poetic language, it is a chemical reality. See Genesis 2:24.">q</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Revelation 21:2; 2 Corinthians 11:2; Isaiah 54:5"> This is a great mystery, but I speak concerning Christ and the Church.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="1 Peter 3:2–7; Esther 1:20; Ephesians 5:25"> In any case, each of you individually: let each one love his own wife as himself,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="Observe that ‘wife’ is singular. Although in the Old Testament men were not condemned for having more than one wife, in the context of the Church monogamy is the norm. The instruction in verse 33 is basic: if a wife does not feel loved and/or a husband does not feel respected, negative things start to happen.">r</a></sup>while the wife must respect her husband.</span>
</div>
