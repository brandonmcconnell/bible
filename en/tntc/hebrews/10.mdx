## Animal blood is insufficient

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Hebrews 8:5; Hebrews 9:11; Colossians 2:17"> You see, the Law is but a shadow of the good things to come, not their actual matter, so it can never perfect those who approach with the same sacrifices that they offer endlessly, year after year.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Isaiah 43:25; Micah 7:19; Psalms 103:12"> Otherwise would they not have ceased to be offered, since the worshipers would have had no more consciousness of sins, having been cleansed once for all?</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Hebrews 9:7; Exodus 30:10; Leviticus 23:27–28"> However, those sacrifices are an annual reminder of sins,</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Hebrews 10:11; Isaiah 1:11–15; Micah 6:6–8"> because it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="The author's reasoning here is both interesting and important; they could never stop, because they never felt clean.">a</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## “I have come to do Your will, O God”

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Psalms 40:6–8; Isaiah 1:11; Isaiah 7:14"> Therefore, upon coming into the world He&#91;S&#93; says:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“Sacrifice and offering You did not desire,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">but You prepared a body for me;</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="When Jehovah the Son accepted the ‘prepared body’ He accepted the whole Plan—the humiliation of being limited to a human body, being born as a helpless baby, living on this sin-cursed planet for 33 years, being rejected, the death on the cross—the works. Would not the humiliation of being confined to a human body be worse than the humiliation on the cross? When I participate in the ‘Lord's Supper’, since the bread represents His body, I thank Him for accepting that body. As a personal application, I am challenged to accept the ‘body’ prepared for me—God's plan for my life in its daily outworking, complete with the unpleasant circumstances. Every day I renew my own commitment: “I am here to do Your will, O God”.">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Malachi 1:10; Matthew 3:17; Psalms 147:11"> <span className="pl pl-1">with burnt offerings and sin offerings You were not pleased.</span><br /></span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Psalms 40:7–8; John 4:34; John 6:38"> <span className="pl pl-1">Then I said, ‘Yes indeed, I have come to do Your will, O God</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">—thus it is written about me in the scroll of the book.’ ”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="See Psalm 40:6-8. Instead of “body”, the Hebrew Text has ‘ears’. You cannot have ears without a body, and the purpose of the body was to work out the commands heard by the ears. The Holy Spirit was at perfect liberty to make the point clearer in this quote.">c</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Hebrews 10:5; Mark 12:33"> First He says, “Sacrifices and offerings, burnt offerings and sin offerings You did not desire, nor were You pleased with them” (which are offered according to the Law),</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Hebrews 7:18–19; Hebrews 12:27–28; Hebrews 10:7"> then He says, “Yes indeed, I have come to do Your will, O God.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Some 15% of the Greek manuscripts omit “O God” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, etc.).">d</a></sup>He removes the first in order to establish the second.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Hebrews 10:14; Hebrews 10:12; 1 Corinthians 6:11"> By that will<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="God's sovereignty, again.">e</a></sup>we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="You cannot have blood without a body.">f</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Christ perfects forever

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Hebrews 5:1; Numbers 28:3; Hebrews 10:4"> Now every priest has stood ministering daily and offering repeatedly the same sacrifices that can never take away sins;</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Hebrews 1:3; Colossians 3:1; Romans 8:34"> but He Himself, having offered for all time one sacrifice for sins, sat down at God's right.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Hebrews 1:13; Psalms 110:1; Luke 20:43"> Since that time He is waiting until His enemies are placed as a footstool for His feet,</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Hebrews 7:25; Hebrews 10:1; Hebrews 9:14"> because by one offering He has perfected forever those who are being sanctified.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Our sanctification is a process, but it is based on, and made possible by, Christ's ‘once for all’ sacrifice.">g</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Hebrews 3:7; Revelation 2:11; Revelation 3:6"> Now the Holy Spirit also testifies to us about this, after having foreseen it:</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Jeremiah 31:33–34; Hebrews 8:8–12; Romans 11:27"> <span className="pl pl-1">“ ‘This is the covenant that I will make with them</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">after those days,’ says the Lord,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">‘I will put my laws on their hearts</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">and I will write them on their minds,</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Jeremiah 31:34; Hebrews 8:12"> <span className="pl pl-1">and I will not at all remember</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">their sins and lawless deeds.’ ”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="See Jeremiah 31:33-34.">h</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Hebrews 10:2; Hebrews 10:14"> Now where there is remission of these, there is no longer an offering for sin.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Once all sins have been paid for, there is no further need for an offering.">i</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Hold fast the confession of the Hope

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Ephesians 2:18; Hebrews 9:12; Hebrews 4:16"> Therefore, brothers, having courage to enter the Most Holy Place by the blood of Jesus,</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="John 14:6; John 10:9; Hebrews 9:3"> by a new and living way that He inaugurated for us, through the curtain, that is to say, His flesh,</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Hebrews 2:17; Hebrews 4:14–16; Hebrews 8:1"> and having a Great Priest over the house of God,</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Ezekiel 36:25; John 3:5; Ephesians 5:26"> let us approach with a true heart in full assurance of faith, our hearts having been sprinkled<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Sprinkling indicates cleansing.">j</a></sup>from an evil conscience and our body having been washed with clean water.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="I suppose most people take a bath before going to church, but how about in our daily devotions?">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 5:24; 1 Corinthians 1:9; 2 Thessalonians 3:3"> Let us hold fast the confession of the Hope without wavering, for He who promised is faithful.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 5:11; 1 John 3:18; Hebrews 13:1"> And let us contemplate one another, for the stirring up of love and good works,</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Acts 2:42; 1 Thessalonians 5:11; Matthew 18:20"> not forsaking the assembling of ourselves (like some are doing), but exhorting one another, and all the more as you see the Day approaching.</span>
</div>
</div>

## A severe warning

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Peter 2:20–22; Hebrews 6:4–6; John 9:41"> Because, if we deliberately keep on sinning after having received the real knowledge of the Truth, there no longer remains a sacrifice for sins,</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Isaiah 26:11; Hebrews 2:3; Zephaniah 1:18"> just a certain fearful anticipation of judgment and fierce fire that is ready to consume the hostiles.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Hebrews 2:2; Deuteronomy 19:15; Deuteronomy 17:2–13"> Anyone who rejected Moses' law died without mercy on the testimony of two or three witnesses.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Hebrews 6:6; Ephesians 4:30; Isaiah 63:10"> Of how much worse punishment, do you suppose, will he be deemed worthy who has trampled the Son of God under foot, who has regarded as unholy the blood of the covenant by which he was sanctified,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="“By which he was sanctified”, that is what the Text says. “His people” presumably refers to people who belong to Him. Verse 31 is not talking about the ordinary consequences of ordinary sin—the clear implication is that God is especially unhappy! To “trample the Son of God under foot” refers to a public repudiation in insulting terms; the reference is to a former Christian who is in open rebellion against God.">l</a></sup>and who has insulted the Spirit of grace?</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Deuteronomy 32:35–36; Romans 12:19; Nahum 1:2"> For we know Him who said, “ ‘Vengeance is up to me,’ says the Lord,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Some 4% of the Greek manuscripts omit “says the Lord” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">m</a></sup>‘I will repay.’ ” And again, “The Lord will judge His people.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="See Deuteronomy 32:35-36.">n</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Matthew 10:28; Luke 12:5; Hebrews 12:29"> It is a dreadful thing to fall into the hands of the Living God!</span>
</div>
</div>

## A word of encouragement

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Hebrews 6:4; Hebrews 12:4; 2 Timothy 2:3–13"> However, remember those earlier days in which, having been enlightened, you endured a great struggle of sufferings:</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="1 Corinthians 4:9; Philippians 4:14; Hebrews 11:36"> partly being publicly exposed to both insults and oppression, and partly siding with those who were so treated.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="1 Peter 1:4; 2 Timothy 1:16; Matthew 5:11–12"> Indeed, you also shared in the suffering of my chains;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="I wonder if Paul is referring to the two years he was a prisoner in Caesarea, since he is evidently writing from Rome (13:24 below). The chief priests doubtless had spies there to make a note of anyone who helped him.">o</a></sup>you even accepted the plundering of your possessions with joy, knowing that you have for yourselves a better and enduring possession in the heavens.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="1 Corinthians 15:58; Galatians 6:8–10; Hebrews 3:6"> So do not throw away your confidence, which has a great recompense.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="Hebrews 12:1; Psalms 37:7; James 5:7–11"> You need perseverance so that, having done the will of God, you may receive the promise.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="James 5:7–9; Habakkuk 2:3–4; Luke 18:8"> For in a very little while:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“He who is coming will come and will not delay.</span><br /></span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="Galatians 3:11; Habakkuk 2:4; Romans 1:17"> <span className="pl pl-1">Now the righteous one will live by faith,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">yet if he backs away, my soul has no pleasure in him.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="See Habakkuk 2:3-4.">p</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="1 John 5:5; 1 Peter 1:5; Mark 16:16"> But we are not of those who back away into ruin,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="The emphasis here is on human responsibility. To “back away” from one's commitment to Christ and His Kingdom is to return into the ruin from which he had been delivered.">q</a></sup>but of those who believe to the saving of the soul.</span>
</div>
</div>
