## The Apostle and High Priest of our confession

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Timothy 1:9; Philippians 3:14; Hebrews 4:14–15"> So then, holy brothers, you who share in the celestial calling, do contemplate the Apostle and High Priest of our confession,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="To ‘confess’ is much more than to ‘profess’, it involves commitment.">a</a></sup>Jesus Christ!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Here we have the first indication as to the addressees; the missive is addressed to Christians. Perhaps 3% of the Greek manuscripts omit “Christ” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Numbers 12:7; 1 Timothy 3:15; Ephesians 2:22"> who was faithful to the One who appointed Him, as also was Moses in all His&#91;F&#93; house.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Corinthians 3:9; 1 Peter 2:5–7; Zechariah 6:12–13"> For this One has been counted worthy of more glory than was Moses, just as the builder of a house has more honor than the house itself</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Hebrews 3:3; Hebrews 1:2; Esther 2:10"> —every house is built by someone, but He who built all things is God—</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Numbers 12:7; Hebrews 3:2; Exodus 14:31"> also, Moses was indeed faithful as a servant in all His house, providing evidence of those things that would later be spoken,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="A curious statement—he provided evidence before it was called for, as it were. Of course Moses is referred to many times in the Scriptures written after his day.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Corinthians 6:16; 1 Corinthians 3:16; Hebrews 10:35"> but Christ is faithful as a Son over His house, whose house are we, if, that is, we hold fast the confidence, even the boast,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="I suppose that a sincere boasting is an exaggerated form of confidence.">d</a></sup>of the Hope<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="I take it that the Hope has to do with eternal life. To still be part of the “house” at the end, one must never give up “the confidence”.">e</a></sup>firm to the end.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Less than 1% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, omit “firm to the end” (as in NIV, LB, TEV, etc.).">f</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## The Holy Spirit's warning

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Psalms 95:7–11; Hebrews 3:15; Isaiah 55:3"> Therefore—just as the Holy Spirit says:<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="The author is affirming that Psalm 95 was inspired by the Holy Spirit.">g</a></sup><br /><span className="pl pl-1">“Today, if you would hear His voice,</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Proverbs 28:14; Exodus 17:7; Zechariah 7:11–12"> <span className="pl pl-2">do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">during the time of the testing</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="In the context the people were testing God, not vice versa.">h</a></sup><br /><span className="pl pl-2">in the desert,</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 7:36; Deuteronomy 4:9; Exodus 19:4"> <span className="pl pl-1">where your fathers tested me,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">proved me and saw my works for forty years.</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Psalms 95:10; 2 Thessalonians 2:10–12; Psalms 67:2"> <span className="pl pl-1">In consequence I became very angry with that generation</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">and said, ‘Their heart always leads them astray;</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">they have not known my ways.’</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Deuteronomy 1:34–35; Hebrews 4:3; Numbers 32:10–13"> <span className="pl pl-1">So I took an oath in my wrath,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">‘As if they will ever enter my rest!’ ”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="See Psalm 95:7-11.">i</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Jeremiah 17:5; Jeremiah 7:24; Jeremiah 17:9"> —take care, brothers, that there not be a malignant heart of unbelief in any of you, so as to go away from<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Notice the direction. The term ‘malignant’ implies Satanic influence.">j</a></sup>the living God;</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 5:11; Hebrews 10:24–25; 2 Timothy 4:2"> rather, exhort yourselves<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="The pronoun here is reflexive, not reciprocal, but being plural probably includes both ideas—each one should exhort himself, but we should also exhort each other.">k</a></sup>every day, while it is called ‘today’, so that none of you be hardened through sin's deceitfulness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Hebrews 3:6; Hebrews 6:11; Hebrews 12:10"> For we have become associates of the Christ, if, that is, we hold fast the beginning of the Endeavor<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="I take it that the Christian life or walk with God is called “the Endeavor” (much like ‘the Way’); people usually start with confidence and enthusiasm, but frequently slow down as the going gets rough.">l</a></sup>firm to the end,</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Hebrews 3:7–8; Psalms 95:7; Hebrews 10:38"> while it is still being said, “Today,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="There comes a time when the window of opportunity is closed, and there are no more ‘todays’.">m</a></sup>if you would hear His voice, do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Numbers 14:2; Numbers 14:30; Numbers 26:65"> So who were they who, upon hearing, rebelled? Really now, was it not all those who came out of Egypt by Moses?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Strictly speaking, since the Original Text did not use question marks, this verse could be translated as a statement: ‘For some, having heard, did rebel, but not all who came out of Egypt by Moses.’ However, since those who did not rebel were very few, a minuscule fraction of the people, most versions treat the verse as a question, as I have done.">n</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Numbers 14:29; Numbers 26:64–65; Numbers 14:32–33"> And with whom was He&#91;F&#93; angry for forty years? Was it not with those who sinned, whose corpses fell in the desert?</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Numbers 14:30; Hebrews 4:6; Deuteronomy 1:34–35"> Or to whom did He swear that they would not enter His rest, if not to those who disobeyed?</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Jude 1:5; John 3:36; Mark 16:16"> So we see that they were not able to enter because of unbelief.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="The implication is clear; if we disobey it is because we do not really believe—see 4:6 below.">o</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>
