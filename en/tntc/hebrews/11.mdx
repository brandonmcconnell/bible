## Abel, Enoch, Noah

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Hebrews 12:24; Genesis 4:3–5; 1 John 3:11–12"> By faith Abel offered to God a better sacrifice than did Cain, through which he obtained witness that he was righteous,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Although Genesis does not mention it, I assume that God had given instruction to Adam about the necessity of blood sacrifice.">c</a></sup>God testifying concerning his gifts; and by means of it he still speaks, even though being dead.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Genesis 5:21–24; Jude 1:14; Hebrews 11:6"> By faith Enoch was transferred so as not to see death, and could not be found because God had translated him; before his translation he had obtained witness that he was pleasing to God.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="His contemporaries probably looked down on Enoch as being a little ‘odd’, altogether too ‘religious’ or ‘spiritual’.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Proverbs 8:17; Jeremiah 29:13–14; Matthew 6:33"> Now without faith it is impossible to please Him, because the one approaching God must believe that He exists and that He becomes a rewarder of those who earnestly seek Him.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="When we get serious with God, He answers.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Genesis 6:13–1; 1 Peter 3:20; Ezekiel 14:14"> By faith Noah, being divinely warned of things not yet seen,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="It had never rained during the 1600 years since the creation of the world. (But he got the last laugh.)">f</a></sup>moved with godly fear, prepared an ark for the salvation of his household, by which he condemned the world and became an heir of the righteousness that is according to faith.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Note that this was centuries before Abraham.">g</a></sup></span>
</div>

## Abraham and Sarah

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Genesis 12:1–4; Acts 7:2–4; Matthew 7:24–25"> By faith Abraham, upon being called to go forth to the place that he would receive as an inheritance, obeyed and went, though not being acquainted with where he was going.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="But his obedience was not complete; he took his father, and that cost him 15 years in Haran; and from there he took Lot, the bad consequences of which lasted for centuries (indeed, for as long as there were Moabites and Ammonites).">h</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Genesis 12:8; Genesis 13:18; Genesis 13:3"> By faith he migrated into the land of promise as into a foreign country, dwelling in tents, along with Isaac and Jacob, the fellow heirs of the same promise;</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Hebrews 13:14; Philippians 3:20; Hebrews 12:22"> for he was waiting expectantly for the city with the real foundations, whose designer and builder is God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Romans 4:20–21; Hebrews 10:23; Genesis 18:11–14"> By faith Sarah herself also received power to conceive seed, and she bore a child<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Perhaps 3% of the Greek manuscripts omit “she bore a child” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">i</a></sup>when she was past the normal age, since she judged Him faithful who had promised.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Genesis 22:17; Genesis 32:12; Genesis 15:5"> And so from one man, actually an impotent,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="This statement helps us understand why Sarah urged Abraham to sleep with Hagar; she knew his virility was declining and was desperate for him to produce an heir. </div>

## Seeking a heavenly homeland

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Genesis 23:4; Psalms 39:12; Hebrews 11:27"> These all died believing—not having received the promises, but having seen and welcomed them from a distance, thus confessing that they were aliens and sojourners on the earth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Corinthians 4:18–7; Hebrews 11:16; Hebrews 13:14"> Now those who say such things make it clear that they are seeking a homeland.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Genesis 24:6–8; Genesis 12:10; Genesis 32:9–11"> If they were actually remembering that land from which they had departed, they would have had opportunity to return.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Hebrews 13:14; Hebrews 11:10; Philippians 3:20"> Instead they are aspiring to a better home—a heavenly one.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="I think we are obliged to understand that Abraham received a great deal more information from God than is recorded in Genesis—information that was duly passed on to Isaac and Jacob.">k</a></sup>Therefore God is not ashamed to be called their God; in fact He has prepared a city for them.</span>
</div>

## Patriarchs

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Genesis 22:16; Genesis 22:1–12; James 2:21–24"> By faith Abraham, upon being tested, offered up Isaac; yes, he who had received the promises was about to sacrifice his only begotten,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="That is what the Text says, “only begotten”. In terms of the promise, that included Sarah, Isaac was his only begotten, although he also had children with Hagar and Keturah.">l</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Genesis 21:12; Romans 9:7; Genesis 17:19"> of whom it had been said, “Through Isaac will your seed be reckoned,”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="See Genesis 21:12.">m</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Romans 4:17–21; Matthew 9:28; Ephesians 3:20"> calculating that God was indeed able to raise him from the dead; from whence in fact he did receive him, figuratively speaking.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Genesis 27:27–40; Genesis 28:2–3"> By faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau concerning things to come.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Genesis 47:31–1; Genesis 48:5–22"> By faith Jacob, when he was dying, blessed each of Joseph's sons and worshiped, leaning on the top of his staff.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="It has been alleged that this statement disagrees with Genesis 47:31, that has Jacob leaning on the head of the bed (following the Massoretic Text), rather than the top of his staff. ## Moses

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Exodus 1:22; Exodus 1:16; Hebrews 13:6"> By faith Moses was hidden for three months by his parents, after he was born, because they saw he was a fine child, and they were not afraid of the king's edict.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Exodus 2:10–11; Acts 7:21–24"> By faith Moses, when he had grown up, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="This may explain Exodus 2:15. <span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Psalms 84:10; 2 Timothy 2:3–10; Job 36:21"> choosing rather to be maltreated along with God's people than to have the temporary pleasure of sin,</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Hebrews 10:35; Hebrews 11:6; 1 Peter 4:14"> considering the reproach of Christ<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="This statement intrigues me. <span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 Corinthians 4:18; Hebrews 11:13; Hebrews 11:1"> By faith he left Egypt behind, not fearing the king's rage,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="This refers to the Exodus, not his flight 40 years earlier.">r</a></sup>because he persevered as though seeing Him who is invisible.</span>

<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Exodus 14:13–21; Psalms 106:9–11; Psalms 78:13"> By faith they passed through the Red Sea as on dry ground, whereas the Egyptians, attempting to do so, were swallowed up.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-s" id="fnref-s" data-fn="For a discussion of where this happened, please see the Appendix: “Where is Mt. <span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Joshua 6:3–20; 2 Corinthians 10:4–5"> By faith the walls of Jericho fell down, having been encircled for seven days.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-t" id="fnref-t" data-fn="Those walls were so thick that they contained houses. <span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="James 2:25; Joshua 6:22–25; Matthew 1:5"> By faith the prostitute Rahab, having received the spies in peace, did not perish with the disobedient.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-u" id="fnref-u" data-fn="And she entered the line that produced the Messiah—Matthew 1:5. Amazing!">u</a></sup></span>
</div>

## Exploits achieved through faith

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="1 Samuel 16:1; 1 Samuel 1:20; 1 Samuel 16:13"> And what more shall I say? For the time would fail me to tell about Gideon, about Barak and Samson and Jephtha, about David and Samuel and the prophets,</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Psalms 144:1–2; Hebrews 6:12–15; Hebrews 10:36"> who through faith subdued kingdoms, administered justice, obtained promises, closed lions' mouths,</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="2 Kings 6:16–18; 2 Corinthians 12:9–10; 1 Samuel 17:51–52"> quenched the power of fire, escaped the edge of the sword, out of weakness were made strong, became mighty in battle, put to flight foreign armies.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="2 Kings 4:27–37; 1 Kings 17:22–24; Philippians 3:11"> Women received their dead back by resurrection; while others were tortured, not accepting their deliverance, so that they might obtain a better resurrection.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-v" id="fnref-v" data-fn="I assume that by “better resurrection” is meant more rewards and honor once resurrected.">v</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="Jeremiah 20:2; Genesis 39:20; Jeremiah 37:15–21"> Still others were tried by mockings and scourgings, and even by chains and imprisonment.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="2 Chronicles 24:21; Jeremiah 26:23; 1 Kings 19:10"> They were stoned, they were sawed in two, they were tempted, they were murdered by sword. They went about in sheepskins and goatskins; being destitute, afflicted, mistreated</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="1 Kings 18:4; 1 Samuel 22:1; 1 Kings 19:9"> —of whom the world was not worthy—wandering in deserts and mountains and caves and holes in the ground.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-w" id="fnref-w" data-fn="We have had it pretty easy, wouldn't you say?">w</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Luke 10:23–24; Hebrews 11:13; Hebrews 11:2"> Now all these did not receive the promise, though having been approved through faith,</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="Revelation 6:11; Hebrews 8:6; Hebrews 10:11–14"> God having planned something better for us,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-x" id="fnref-x" data-fn="What do we have that is “better”? Well, we have the indwelling Holy Spirit, and we have the complete Bible, for starters.">x</a></sup>so that they should not be perfected without us.</span>
