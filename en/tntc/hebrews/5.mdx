## Qualifications for high priesthood

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Hebrews 8:3; Hebrews 10:11; Hebrews 7:27"> Now every high priest taken from among men is appointed to act on behalf of men in things pertaining to God, that he may offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Hebrews 4:15; Hebrews 2:18; 2 Corinthians 12:9–10"> being able to deal gently<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="A gentle priest is a comfort.">a</a></sup>with those who sin in ignorance and go astray, since he himself is also subject to weakness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Leviticus 9:7; Hebrews 9:7; Leviticus 16:6"> Because of this he must offer sacrifices for his own sins as well, just as he does for the people's.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Exodus 28:1; Numbers 16:40; Numbers 16:5"> Also, no one receives this honor on his own, but upon being called by God, just like Aaron.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Aaron's descendants receive the honor by virtue of birth, not choice.">b</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Christ meets the qualifications

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Psalms 2:7; John 8:54; Acts 13:33"> So also Christ did not exalt Himself to become High Priest, but it was He&#91;F&#93; who said to Him&#91;S&#93;,<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“You are my Son;</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">today I have begotten You.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="See Psalm 2:7.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Psalms 110:4; Hebrews 7:17; Hebrews 7:21"> Just as He&#91;F&#93; also says in another place,<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“You are a priest forever</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">according to the order of Melchizedek.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="See Psalm 110:4.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Mark 14:32–39; Luke 22:41–44; John 17:1"> He&#91;S&#93;, in the days of His flesh, having offered up both prayers and supplications, with a loud cry and tears, to the One who was able to save Him from death, and having been answered because of His godly fear,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="I believe the reference is to Gethsemane. Note that there was a single loud (desperate?) cry (that should have roused the drowsy disciples). His anguish was so severe that Jesus literally passed blood through the pores in His skin—normally fatal (Luke 22:44). I suspect that the fear mentioned here was the fear that He would die prematurely, there in the garden. He came to die, certainly, but it had to be on the cross. So the Father sent angels to strengthen Him, to keep Him from dying prematurely.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Philippians 2:8; Hebrews 10:5–9; John 4:34"> although being SON, He learned obedience by the things which He suffered.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Hebrews 2:10; Hebrews 9:12; Hebrews 9:15"> And having been completed<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="His qualifications to become High Priest were completed. As God, what could He know about suffering and obedience, except in theory? While in human form Jesus literally learned obedience—He had to feel it like we feel it.">f</a></sup>He became the Source of eternal salvation for all those obeying Him,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="That is what the Text says; we must obey Him.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Hebrews 5:5–6; Hebrews 6:20"> having been designated by God as High Priest ‘according to the order of Melchizedek’;</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Matthew 13:15; Acts 28:27; 2 Peter 3:16"> concerning whom we have much to say, but it is hard to explain, since you have become sluggish in hearing.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="The ‘difficulty’ is not so much in the subject matter, but in the spiritual condition of the hearers. This would seem to indicate that the author has a specific audience in view.">h</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## “Let's move on toward perfection”

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Hebrews 6:1; 1 Corinthians 3:1–3; 1 Peter 2:2"> Really, because although by this time you ought to be teachers, you need someone to teach you the elementary principles of God's oracles all over again; you have come to the point<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="The implication is that they are going backwards.">i</a></sup>of needing milk, not solid food!</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:20; 1 Peter 2:2; 2 Timothy 3:16"> Surely; whoever lives on milk is an infant, and therefore unskilled in the Word of righteousness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="1 Corinthians 2:14–15; 1 Thessalonians 5:21; Ephesians 1:18"> On the other hand, solid food is for the mature, those who by habitual use have trained their senses to distinguish good from bad.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Expressions like “habitual use” and “trained their senses” indicate directed, conscious effort on our part.">j</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>
