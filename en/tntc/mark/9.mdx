That is why Hebrews 3:12-14 is precisely to the point: “Beware, brethren, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief in departing from the living God; but exhort one another daily, while it is called ‘Today’, lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin. For we have become partakers of Christ if we hold the beginning of our confidence steadfast to the end.”</li>
<li id="fn-r"><a href="#fnref-r" className="fnb">r</a> <span className="fnr">9:42</span> The implication is clear: anyone who deliberately destroys a child's faith in Jesus has bought himself a one-way ticket to the Lake of Fire. I suppose that ‘child’ could include high school and college students.</li>
<li id="fn-s"><a href="#fnref-s" className="fnb">s</a> <span className="fnr">9:43</span> Strictly speaking, ‘Gehenna’ was the local dump outside Jerusalem—something was always being burned, and there would be plenty of worms. But Jesus here uses it as a figure for the Lake of Fire, the ‘second death’.</li>
<li id="fn-t"><a href="#fnref-t" className="fnb">t</a> <span className="fnr">9:44</span> See Isaiah 66:24. Perhaps 4% of the Greek manuscripts omit ‘into the unquenchable fire’ at the end of verses 43 and 45, and also omit verses 44 and 46 entire, to be followed by NIV, NASB, LB, &#91;TEV&#93;, etc., except that most keep ‘into the unquenchable fire’ in verse 43 (but not in verse 45). I find the figure of an immortal worm to be rather daunting—always chewing on you, but never finishing you off! (Evidently there were those who thought that saying it once was quite enough.)</li>
<li id="fn-u"><a href="#fnref-u" className="fnb">u</a> <span className="fnr">9:48</span> I here follow the best line of transmission and 30% of the Greek manuscripts.</li>
<li id="fn-v"><a href="#fnref-v" className="fnb">v</a> <span className="fnr">9:49</span> Now what might this mean? Well, those who believe into Him Jesus baptizes with the Holy Spirit and fire; those who don't, get a different fire.</li>
<li id="fn-w"><a href="#fnref-w" className="fnb">w</a> <span className="fnr">9:49</span> Some 7% of the Greek manuscripts omit the second clause of this verse, to be followed by NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc. But just what might this mean? Well, Romans 12:1 speaks of a ‘living sacrifice’ and in Matthew 5:13 Jesus calls His followers ‘the salt of the earth’. If you present yourself as a living sacrifice to Jesus, He will make you salty.</li>
<li id="fn-x"><a href="#fnref-x" className="fnb">x</a> <span className="fnr">9:50</span> For ‘salty’ people living and working together, ‘peace’ may not be easy.</li>
</ol>
