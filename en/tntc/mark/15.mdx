## The crucifixion

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Matthew 27:32; Luke 23:26; Romans 16:13"> A certain passer-by, Simon a Cyrenian (the father of Alexander and Rufus),<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="After carrying the cross, Simon doubtless stayed around to see what happened. In consequence he was no doubt converted, as were his two sons.">f</a></sup>coming in from the countryside, was compelled to carry His cross.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="A condemned person usually had to carry his cross, but this one was doubtless heavy, and evidently Jesus was having difficulty carrying it.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Matthew 27:33–44; John 19:17–27; Luke 23:27–38"> They brought Him to a place Golgotha, which is translated, ‘Place of a Skull’.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Matthew 27:34; John 19:28–30; Luke 23:36"> They offered Him wine mixed with myrrh to drink,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="This was a humanitarian gesture—myrrh is a crude analgesic and would deaden the pain. But Jesus refused it; He wanted nothing to cloud His senses or diminish the suffering.">h</a></sup>but He did not take it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Luke 23:34; John 19:23–24; Matthew 27:35–36"> After crucifying Him, they divided His clothes by casting lots for them, to see who would take what.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="John 19:14; Mark 15:33; Matthew 27:45"> It was the third hour<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Using Jewish time, that was 9 a.m.">i</a></sup>when they crucified Him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Matthew 27:37; Matthew 2:2; John 19:18–22"> The statement of His ‘crime’ that had been written was:<br />THE KING OF THE JEWS.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Luke 23:32–33; Matthew 27:38; John 19:18"> With Him they also crucified two bandits, one on His right and one on His left.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> So the Scripture was fulfilled which says, “And He was numbered with transgressors.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="See Isaiah 53:12. Around 11% of the Greek manuscripts omit verse 28 entire, to be followed by NIV, NASB, LB, [TEV], etc.">j</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Mark 14:58; Psalms 109:25; Lamentations 1:12"> Those who passed by kept ridiculing Him, wagging their heads and saying, “Hey! You who can destroy the temple and build it in three days,</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30"> save yourself and come down from the cross!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="John 12:23–24; Matthew 27:41–43; Luke 23:35–37"> Similarly, the chief priests, with the scribes, kept mocking among themselves saying: “He saved others; he can't save himself!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="This was precisely true, but not in the sense they intended. To save us, He could not save Himself.">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Matthew 27:44; Luke 23:39–43; Zechariah 9:9"> Let the Christ, the king of Israel, descend now from the cross, that we may see and believe him.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="They were lying; they would not have submitted to Him even then.">l</a></sup>Those who were crucified with Him insulted Him as well.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="But one of them changed his mind later.">m</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Matthew 27:45; Luke 23:44–45; Amos 8:9–10"> Now when the sixth hour had come &#91;noon&#93;, darkness came over the whole land until the ninth hour.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="The darkness could not have been a solar eclipse, as some have ignorantly argued. The Passover always occurs at full moon, and a solar eclipse only occurs at new moon. Further, even a total eclipse only lasts for a few minutes, not three hours.">n</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Psalms 22:1; Matthew 27:46; Hebrews 5:7"> At the ninth hour Jesus called out strongly, saying, “Eloi, Eloi, lima sabachthani?” which is translated, “O God, my God, why have You forsaken me?”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="See Psalm 22:1.">o</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Matthew 27:47–49; Mark 9:11–13; Matthew 17:11–13"> When some of the bystanders heard it they said, “Listen, he's calling Elijah.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="Psalms 69:21; Mark 15:23; John 19:28–30"> Then someone ran, filled a sponge with wine vinegar, put it on a reed and started offering it to Him to drink, saying, “You let him be! ‘Let's see if Elijah is coming to take him down’!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="To understand Mark's turn of phrase, we need Matthew's account (27:47-49). “Some of those who stood there, when they heard that, said, ‘This man is calling for Elijah!’ Immediately one of them ran and took a sponge, filled it with sour wine and put it on a reed, and offered it to Him to drink. The rest said, ‘Let him alone; let us see if Elijah will come to save him’.” The ‘let him’ is 2nd person singular. But in Mark's account, the ‘let him’ is 2nd person plural! How do we reconcile the two accounts? I suggest the following: First, the man was quite possibly Mark himself; next, anyone who really understood the language would know that Jesus had not called Elijah; so, he responds sarcastically to their erroneous interpretation and therefore unreasonable demand that he stop; he retorts, “You stop!” and repeats their interpretation in disgust.">p</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="John 19:30; Luke 23:46; Matthew 27:50"> Then Jesus gave a loud shout and breathed out His spirit;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="Both Matthew 27:50 and John 19:30 plainly state that Jesus dismissed His spirit. It was not the cross that killed Him [see the third note down]. As He Himself said in John 10:18, “No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of myself. I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it again.” He chose to die so that we might live. Thank you, Lord!">q</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="Hebrews 10:19–23; Luke 23:45; Hebrews 9:3–12"> and the veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="That veil represented the barrier between sinful man and a Holy God. For it to be ripped in two from top to bottom was obviously a supernatural event, and it followed immediately upon Jesus' death—which means that the Father considered that Jesus had solved the problem. Hebrews 10:20 makes the connection. Because of Jesus' death we can now have access to the Father.">r</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Matthew 27:54; Luke 23:47–48; Matthew 27:43"> Well when the centurion, who was standing opposite Him, saw that He breathed out His spirit after giving such a loud shout,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-s" id="fnref-s" data-fn="A mere handful (0.4%) of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, omit ‘after giving a loud shout’, to be followed by NASB and LB.">s</a></sup>he said, “This man really was God's Son!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-t" id="fnref-t" data-fn="Any centurion would be a hardened soldier, who had seen no end of crucifixions. He knew that a cross killed by asphyxiation. Hanging from the hands, with the arms stretched out, pushes the diaphragm against the lungs so you can't breathe. Nailing the feet was a sadistic procedure to prolong the agony—even though painful, the victim would push up so he could get a breath, until finally too worn out to do so. Breaking the legs would put an end to that expedient, and the person died within a few minutes, asphyxiated. Someone who is dying asphyxiated does not shout. Since Jesus gave a loud shout, but then immediately died, the centurion knew beyond the shadow of a doubt that the cross had not killed Jesus (later, when Joseph asks for the body, Pilate is surprised that Jesus could already be dead). But who can just tell his spirit to leave? Putting two and two together, the centurion concluded that Jesus was a supernatural being. Just so!">t</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="Luke 23:49; Luke 8:2–3; Matthew 27:55–56"> There were also women looking on from a distance, among whom were Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James the Less and of Joses, and Salome</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="Luke 8:2–3; Matthew 27:55–56"> —they used to follow Him and minister to Him when He was in Galilee—and many other women who had come up with Him to Jerusalem.</span>

## The burial

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="Luke 23:50–56; John 19:38–42; John 19:31"> Now when evening had come, because it was Preparation Day (that is, the day before the Sabbath),</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43" data-xref="Luke 2:38; Luke 2:25; Luke 23:51"> Joseph of Arimathea, a prominent council member who also himself was waiting for the Kingdom of God, came and boldly went in to Pilate and asked for the body of Jesus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44" data-xref="John 19:31–37"> Well Pilate was surprised that He was already dead; and summoning the centurion he asked him when He had died.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-u" id="fnref-u" data-fn="As soon as Jesus died, the centurion most probably had left the scene, going back to headquarters. He probably felt he should inform Pilate about the unusual events, but somehow Joseph got ahead of him. Well, Joseph was evidently primed for action; watching from a distance, as soon as Jesus dismissed His spirit Joseph headed for Pilate.">u</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v45" data-v="45" data-xref="Matthew 27:58; John 19:38; Mark 15:39"> Upon the centurion's confirmation he granted the body to Joseph.</span>
<span className="v" id="v46" data-v="46" data-xref="Isaiah 53:9; Matthew 27:59–60; Isaiah 22:16"> Having bought linen, he took Him down, wrapped Him in the linen and laid Him in a tomb that had been cut out of rock; then he rolled a stone against the door of the tomb.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-v" id="fnref-v" data-fn="If he rolled it, it was in the form of a wheel; there would be a track in which it rolled, with a bit of incline, so that he and Nicodemus could roll it down into place, where it would stop; but it would take several men to roll it back up and away (16:3-4). Obviously all of this had been planned and prepared in advance—the donkey owner, the upper room owner, the sepulcher owner all knew in advance just what part they were to play.">v</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v47" data-v="47" data-xref="Mark 15:40; Mark 16:1; Luke 23:55–2"> Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses observed where He was placed.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-w" id="fnref-w" data-fn="They had seen where Jesus was placed, but obviously had not looked in the tomb—there were 100 pounds of spices in there, with enough linen to tie it all in.">w</a></sup></span>
</div>
