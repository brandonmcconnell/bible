<li id="fn-w"><a href="#fnref-w" className="fnb">w</a> <span className="fnr">10:40</span> If Jesus Himself is at the Father's right, then it follows that the Father is on Jesus' left, so only one of the spots is ‘available’, except that the occupant is already known.</li>
<li id="fn-x"><a href="#fnref-x" className="fnb">x</a> <span className="fnr">10:41</span> Now just why, do you suppose, were they upset?</li>
<li id="fn-y"><a href="#fnref-y" className="fnb">y</a> <span className="fnr">10:45</span> The Lord Jesus is our example.</li>
<li id="fn-z"><a href="#fnref-z" className="fnb">z</a> <span className="fnr">10:46</span> The parallel account in Luke has Jesus entering Jericho, not leaving it. At that time, there were two Jerichos, about a kilometer apart. What better place for an enterprising beggar than between the two towns? For a detailed discussion of the three accounts (also Matthew), please see the Appendix: Entering, or leaving Jericho?</li>
<li id="fn-aa"><a href="#fnref-aa" className="fnb">aa</a> <span className="fnr">10:46</span> Mark is the only account that gives the man's name—evidently he was known (or at least became known).</li>
<li id="fn-ab"><a href="#fnref-ab" className="fnb">ab</a> <span className="fnr">10:47</span> The Text has ‘Natsorean’, not ‘Nazarene (Natsarene)’. Natsorean is presumably a reference to Isaiah 11:1, the Branch—Jesus was the Branch-man. Natsarene just means that He came from Natsareth.</li>
<li id="fn-ac"><a href="#fnref-ac" className="fnb">ac</a> <span className="fnr">10:47</span> Bartimaeus addresses Jesus as the Messiah.</li>
<li id="fn-ad"><a href="#fnref-ad" className="fnb">ad</a> <span className="fnr">10:50</span> Now why did he abandon his cloak? He expected to be healed—being able to see he could easily find it (and who else would want a beggar's cloak?).</li>
<li id="fn-ae"><a href="#fnref-ae" className="fnb">ae</a> <span className="fnr">10:51</span> Does God ever take anything for granted from us? He knows what we need before we ask, but we must ask.</li>
<li id="fn-af"><a href="#fnref-af" className="fnb">af</a> <span className="fnr">10:51</span> Some 65% of the Greek manuscripts spell the word with two ‘b’s; I follow the best line of transmission, but there is no difference in meaning: ‘my teacher/master’.</li>
