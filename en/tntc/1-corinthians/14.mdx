## Prophecy is better than Tongues

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:39; 1 Timothy 4:14; 1 Corinthians 12:1"> Pursue love, and desire the spiritual gifts, but especially that you may prophesy.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Mark 16:17; Acts 19:6; Acts 10:46"> Because he who speaks in a ‘language’ is not speaking to people but to God, since no one understands; in spirit he speaks mysteries.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:12; 1 Timothy 4:13; Jude 1:20"> But he who prophesies speaks edification and exhortation and comfort to people.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Prophecy is not only for foretelling the future, but all true prophecy comes directly from God. Many churches today give ‘prophesy/prophecy’ an unbiblical meaning, wherein the people tell each other all the good things they hope will happen to them. That is just wishful thinking, not true prophecy.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:22; 1 Corinthians 14:26; Mark 16:17"> The one speaking in a ‘language’ edifies himself, but he who prophesies edifies the congregation.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Corinthians 12:28–30; 1 Corinthians 12:10; Mark 16:17"> I could wish<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="The verb here is ambiguous as to mode; it could be either Indicative or Subjunctive. Since Paul has already stated that not everyone receives ‘languages’, and that the Holy Spirit distributes as He chooses, the Subjunctive is the correct choice.">b</a></sup>that you all spoke in ‘languages’, but even more that you might prophesy; because the one prophesying is greater than the one speaking in ‘languages’<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="For someone to argue that he is being humble in seeking and using the lesser gift will not pass muster, because in 12:31 we are commanded to seek the best gifts.">c</a></sup>(unless he interprets),<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="According to verse 27 below, only one interpreter should perform his function during a given meeting. So if someone says he is going to interpret his own language, he must have the genuine gift and be able to interpret any other languages. (I have witnessed no end of people faking a language and then faking its interpretation.)">d</a></sup>so that the congregation may receive edification.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Messages need to be intelligible

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Ephesians 1:17; Romans 6:17; 2 Timothy 3:16"> Now then, brothers, what good will I do you if I come to you speaking in ‘languages’ instead of addressing you with revelation, or with knowledge, or with prophecy, or with teaching?</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:8; Luke 7:32; 1 Corinthians 13:1"> Take lifeless things like a flute or a harp; if they make no distinction in the notes when they produce sound, how will it be known what is being piped or harped?</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Numbers 10:9; Ezekiel 33:3–6; Jeremiah 4:19"> Also a trumpet; if it gives an indistinct sound, who will prepare for battle?</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Corinthians 9:26"> So it is with you: if you do not deliver an intelligible message with the ‘language’, how will it be known what is being said? You will just be speaking into the air.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> There are probably a great many kinds of sounds in the world, and none of them is without significance.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 28:2; Romans 1:14; Colossians 3:11"> But if I do not know the force of the sound, I will be a foreigner to the speaker, and he will be a foreigner to me.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:26; 1 Corinthians 14:3–4; 1 Corinthians 14:1"> And you too: since you are zealous for spiritual things, aim at the edification of the congregation, that you may all grow.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Mark 11:24; 1 Corinthians 12:30; John 14:13–14"> Therefore the one speaking in a ‘language’ should pray that he may interpret.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="I do not understand this, since someone with the gift of interpretation does not need to ask. If no interpreter is present, perhaps the speaker could ask God to give him the interpretation.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:2; 1 Corinthians 14:15–16; 1 Corinthians 14:19"> For if I pray in a ‘language’, my spirit prays, but my mind is unfruitful.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Colossians 3:16; John 4:23–24; Ephesians 6:18"> So what then? I will pray with the spirit, but I will also pray with the mind; I will sing with the spirit, but I will also sing with the mind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Chronicles 16:36; Psalms 106:48; Revelation 5:14"> Otherwise, if you bless with the spirit, how will he who occupies the place of the outsider say the “Amen” at your giving of thanks, since he does not know what you are saying?</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:4; 1 Corinthians 14:6"> You, of course, give thanks quite well, but the other is not edified.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Paul's example

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:4–6; 1 Corinthians 4:7"> I thank my God speaking in ‘languages’ more than you all,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Since Paul obviously would not use a ‘language’ in public, he made heavy use of them when alone with God. People who follow Paul's example have told me that it recharges their spiritual ‘battery’ in short order. No wonder Paul did it!">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:21–22; 1 Corinthians 14:4"> but in the congregation I would rather speak five words with my understanding, precisely so as to instruct others, than ten thousand words in a ‘language’.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Peter 2:2; 1 Corinthians 13:11; Romans 16:19"> Brothers, stop thinking like children—well, in malice be ‘infants’, but in thinking be adults.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Isaiah 28:11–12; Jeremiah 5:15; Deuteronomy 28:49"> In the law it stands written:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“I will speak to this people in foreign languages</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">and with different ‘lips’,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">but not even then will they listen to me,”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="See Isaiah 28:11-12 and Deuteronomy 28:49.">g</a></sup><br /><span className="pl pl-1">says the Lord.</span></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 2:6–12; Mark 16:17; 1 Corinthians 14:1"> Therefore the ‘languages’ are for a sign, not to believers but to unbelievers;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Like on the day of Pentecost, to be a ‘sign’ a ‘language’ would have to be one that the unbeliever knew, but that the speaker would have no way of knowing. If the unbeliever thinks you are raving (verse 23), where is the ‘sign’? To argue that ‘tongues’ is the sign that you have been ‘baptized in the Spirit’ does not follow from this verse, since that would be for believers.">h</a></sup>while prophesying<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="The term here can mean either ‘prophecy’ or ‘prophesying’; the context calls for the second option. God normally speaks through believers, not unbelievers.">i</a></sup>is not for unbelievers but for believers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Acts 2:13; Acts 26:24; 1 Corinthians 11:18"> So if the whole congregation comes together and all are speaking in ‘languages’, but outsiders or unbelievers come in, will they not say that you are raving?</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 2:37; 1 Corinthians 2:15; Hebrews 4:12–13"> But if everyone is prophesying, and an unbeliever or outsider comes in, he is reproved by all, he is examined by all.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Isaiah 45:14; Zechariah 8:23; Psalms 72:11"> And thus the secrets of his heart are exposed, and so, falling on his face he will worship God, affirming, “Truly God is among you!”</span>
</div>
</div>

## There are limits

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Ephesians 5:19; Romans 14:19; 1 Corinthians 12:7–10"> So what goes on, brothers? Whenever you come together, each of you has a psalm, has a teaching, has a ‘language’, has a revelation, has an interpretation.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Can it be that there was a little competition going on?">j</a></sup>Let all things be done for edification.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:26"> If anyone speaks in a ‘language’, let it be two—at the most three—and in turn, and let one interpret.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Since someone with the gift of interpreting can interpret any and all such utterances, there should be only one interpreter at work during a particular session.">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> But if there is no interpreter, let him keep silent in church; let him speak to himself and to God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="1 Corinthians 12:10; 1 Thessalonians 5:19–21; 1 John 4:1–3"> Let two or three prophets speak, and let the others evaluate.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Prophecy must always be evaluated.">l</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 5:19–20; Job 33:31–33; Job 32:11"> But if another who is sitting by receives a revelation, the first should stop speaking.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 5:14; 1 Corinthians 14:3; 1 Thessalonians 5:11"> For you can all prophesy one by one, so that all may learn and all may be encouraged.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Acts 4:19–20; 1 John 4:1; 1 Samuel 10:10–13"> Yes, spirits of prophets are subordinate to prophets.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="A prophet cannot control what messages he receives from God, but he can control when and how he delivers them (and may even decide not to).">m</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="James 3:17–18; 1 Corinthians 14:40; 2 Thessalonians 3:16"> Further, God is not a God of disorder but of peace. Wives are not to speak<br />As in all the congregations of the saints,</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="1 Timothy 2:11–12; Genesis 3:16; Numbers 30:3–13"> your wives<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="The eclectic Greek text currently in vogue omits ‘your’ (following some 3% of the Greek manuscripts), which allows the translation ‘women’ rather than ‘wives’, as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc. Since ‘wives’ excludes single women, the difference is significant.">n</a></sup>should keep silent in the assemblies, for they are not permitted to speak, but to be in subordination, as the law also says.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Ephesians 5:12; 1 Peter 3:7; Ephesians 5:25–27"> If they want to learn about something, let them ask their own husbands at home, for it is shameful for women to speak in church.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="The crucial factor here is authority, and the underlying need is to protect the structure of the home, the foundational unit of society, including the church. If a woman teaches her husband in church, crossing the street to their house will not make her want to submit to him; the human being just does not work that way. That is why the Text does not allow for female pastors or teachers, since to teach is to exercise authority. But here in verses 34 and 35 Paul goes even further; to protect the husbands' authority, wives should not publicly request an explanation from pastor or teacher, since that could allow a wife to play the teacher against her husband in the home. If a wife requests an explanation from her husband, and he does not know the answer, he can consult the pastor and then transmit the explanation—this preserves the authority structure in the home (which goes back to Genesis 3:16).">o</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="Acts 18:1–17; Zechariah 14:8; Acts 17:15"> Or was it from you that the Word of God went forth? Or was it only to you that it came?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="Either of these situations would constitute a special privilege, which could give rise to a little spiritual pride.">p</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="1 John 4:6; 2 Corinthians 10:7; 2 Peter 3:2"> If anyone thinks that he is a prophet or spiritual, let him acknowledge that the things I write to you are the Lord's commands.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="Was Paul on an ego trip, or was he aware that he was writing under inspiration? Since he says something similar in a number of his letters, it is clear that he believed he was writing Scripture.">q</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="2 Timothy 4:3–4; Hosea 4:17; Revelation 22:11–12"> But if anyone is ignorant, let him remain so.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="I do not understand this. ## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">14:3</span> Prophecy is not only for foretelling the future, but all true prophecy comes directly from God. Many churches today give ‘prophesy/prophecy’ an unbiblical meaning, wherein the people tell each other all the good things they hope will happen to them. That is just wishful thinking, not true prophecy.</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">14:5</span> The verb here is ambiguous as to mode; it could be either Indicative or Subjunctive. Since Paul has already stated that not everyone receives ‘languages’, and that the Holy Spirit distributes as He chooses, the Subjunctive is the correct choice.</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">14:5</span> For someone to argue that he is being humble in seeking and using the lesser gift will not pass muster, because in 12:31 we are commanded to seek the best gifts.</li>
<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">14:5</span> According to verse 27 below, only one interpreter should perform his function during a given meeting. So if someone says he is going to interpret his own language, he must have the genuine gift and be able to interpret any other languages. (I have witnessed no end of people faking a language and then faking its interpretation.)</li>
<li id="fn-e"><a href="#fnref-e" className="fnb">e</a> <span className="fnr">14:13</span> I do not understand this, since someone with the gift of interpretation does not need to ask. If no interpreter is present, perhaps the speaker could ask God to give him the interpretation.</li>
<li id="fn-f"><a href="#fnref-f" className="fnb">f</a> <span className="fnr">14:18</span> Since Paul obviously would not use a ‘language’ in public, he made heavy use of them when alone with God. People who follow Paul's example have told me that it recharges their spiritual ‘battery’ in short order. No wonder Paul did it!</li>
<li id="fn-g"><a href="#fnref-g" className="fnb">g</a> <span className="fnr">14:21</span> See Isaiah 28:11-12 and Deuteronomy 28:49.</li>
<li id="fn-h"><a href="#fnref-h" className="fnb">h</a> <span className="fnr">14:22</span> Like on the day of Pentecost, to be a ‘sign’ a ‘language’ would have to be one that the unbeliever knew, but that the speaker would have no way of knowing. If the unbeliever thinks you are raving (verse 23), where is the ‘sign’? To argue that ‘tongues’ is the sign that you have been ‘baptized in the Spirit’ does not follow from this verse, since that would be for believers.</li>
<li id="fn-i"><a href="#fnref-i" className="fnb">i</a> <span className="fnr">14:22</span> The term here can mean either ‘prophecy’ or ‘prophesying’; the context calls for the second option. God normally speaks through believers, not unbelievers.</li>
<li id="fn-j"><a href="#fnref-j" className="fnb">j</a> <span className="fnr">14:26</span> Can it be that there was a little competition going on?</li>
<li id="fn-k"><a href="#fnref-k" className="fnb">k</a> <span className="fnr">14:27</span> Since someone with the gift of interpreting can interpret any and all such utterances, there should be only one interpreter at work during a particular session.</li>
<li id="fn-l"><a href="#fnref-l" className="fnb">l</a> <span className="fnr">14:29</span> Prophecy must always be evaluated.</li>
<li id="fn-m"><a href="#fnref-m" className="fnb">m</a> <span className="fnr">14:32</span> A prophet cannot control what messages he receives from God, but he can control when and how he delivers them (and may even decide not to).</li>
<li id="fn-n"><a href="#fnref-n" className="fnb">n</a> <span className="fnr">14:34</span> The eclectic Greek text currently in vogue omits ‘your’ (following some 3% of the Greek manuscripts), which allows the translation ‘women’ rather than ‘wives’, as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc. Since ‘wives’ excludes single women, the difference is significant.</li>
<li id="fn-o"><a href="#fnref-o" className="fnb">o</a> <span className="fnr">14:35</span> The crucial factor here is authority, and the underlying need is to protect the structure of the home, the foundational unit of society, including the church. If a woman teaches her husband in church, crossing the street to their house will not make her want to submit to him; the human being just does not work that way. That is why the Text does not allow for female pastors or teachers, since to teach is to exercise authority. But here in verses 34 and 35 Paul goes even further; to protect the husbands' authority, wives should not publicly request an explanation from pastor or teacher, since that could allow a wife to play the teacher against her husband in the home. If a wife requests an explanation from her husband, and he does not know the answer, he can consult the pastor and then transmit the explanation—this preserves the authority structure in the home (which goes back to Genesis 3:16).</li>
<li id="fn-p"><a href="#fnref-p" className="fnb">p</a> <span className="fnr">14:36</span> Either of these situations would constitute a special privilege, which could give rise to a little spiritual pride.</li>
<li id="fn-q"><a href="#fnref-q" className="fnb">q</a> <span className="fnr">14:37</span> Was Paul on an ego trip, or was he aware that he was writing under inspiration? Since he says something similar in a number of his letters, it is clear that he believed he was writing Scripture.</li>
<li id="fn-r"><a href="#fnref-r" className="fnb">r</a> <span className="fnr">14:38</span> I do not understand this. If the definition of “ignorant” here is one who does not do the acknowledging required in verse 37, then Paul may be saying that such a person is not worth arguing with.</li>
<li id="fn-s"><a href="#fnref-s" className="fnb">s</a> <span className="fnr">14:39</span> Here is the inspired conclusion to the extended discussion of the charismatic gifts. Isn't it strange that most churches disobey this verse, in one way or another? ‘Traditional’ churches tend to forbid not only tongues but also prophecy (to ‘seek’ it is totally out of the question). ‘Pentecostal’ churches generally elevate tongues above prophecy, which is plainly contrary to the Text.</li>
