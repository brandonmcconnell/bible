## Not with human wisdom but with God's power

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:17; 1 Corinthians 2:13; 1 Corinthians 2:4"> So when I came to you, brothers, I did not come proclaiming God's testimony<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Instead of “testimony”, less than 5% of the Greek manuscripts have ‘mystery’, to be followed by the eclectic Greek text currently in vogue (as in some versions).">a</a></sup>to you with a ‘superior’ speech or wisdom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Galatians 6:14; John 17:3; 1 Corinthians 1:22–25"> For I determined to ‘know’ nothing while among you except Jesus Christ and Him crucified.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Corinthians 13:4; 1 Corinthians 4:10–13; 2 Corinthians 12:5–10"> Also, I was with you in weakness, in fear, and with much trembling.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Corinthians 4:20; 1 Thessalonians 1:5; 1 Corinthians 1:17"> Yes, my message and my preaching were not with persuasive words of human wisdom, but with a demonstration of the Spirit and of power,</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Corinthians 4:7; 2 Corinthians 12:9; 2 Corinthians 6:7"> so that your faith not be in men's wisdom, but in God's power.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="How many missionaries and pastors today are following Paul's example? How many of us know how to demonstrate God's power? Faith is to be based on power, not human wisdom. If we don't know how to use God's power, we had better cry out to Him until we learn how. Certain theological systems virtually condemn their adherents to never use God's power.">b</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Wisdom for the mature

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Hebrews 5:14; James 3:15; 2 Corinthians 4:4"> However, we do speak wisdom among the mature,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="This is important. Paul is not championing ignorance or stupidity. In teaching Christians he certainly used God's wisdom. It was in his evangelizing that he avoided ‘human wisdom’.">c</a></sup>albeit not the wisdom of this age, nor of the rulers of this age, who are being set aside;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="‘Being set aside’ how, and when? If God's people would really be ‘salt’ and ‘light’ (Matthew 5:13-14) they could transform any culture.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Colossians 1:26–27; 2 Peter 1:3; Matthew 11:25"> but we speak God's wisdom in a mystery, the hidden<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="In the Bible a ‘mystery’ is something hidden, something yet to be revealed, not something ‘mysterious’.">e</a></sup>wisdom that God ordained before the ages for our glory,</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Acts 13:27; James 2:1; Acts 7:2"> that none of the rulers of this age have understood (because if they had understood, they would not have crucified the Lord of the glory).</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Isaiah 64:4; Psalms 31:19; James 1:12"> However, as it is written:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“Things no eye has seen and no ear has heard,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">and no heart of man has imagined,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">such things has God prepared for those who love Him.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Wonderful! Thank you, God! See Isaiah 64:4.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="John 14:26; Ephesians 3:5; Luke 10:21"> But to us God has revealed them by His Spirit;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="To us has been granted the privilege of having a fuller understanding of God's purposes than was granted to OT saints.">g</a></sup>because the Spirit searches all things, yes, the deep things of God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Proverbs 20:27; 1 Corinthians 2:10; Romans 11:33–34"> Now who among men knows the things of a man except the spirit of the man that is in him? So also no one knows the things of God except the Spirit of God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 John 2:27; Romans 8:5–6; 1 John 2:20"> We have not received the spirit of the world<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="‘The spirit of the world’ evidently must exist. If the reference is not to Satan directly, he is certainly behind it.">h</a></sup>but the Spirit who is from God, so that we might know the things that have been freely given to us by God;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Evidently one of the things the Holy Spirit wants to do is to illumine us on the subject of God's free gifts—if we would only listen!">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Corinthians 2:4; 1 Corinthians 2:14; 1 Corinthians 1:17"> which things we also expound, not in words taught by human wisdom, but in those taught by the Holy Spirit,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Note that Paul is plainly stating that he receives instruction from the Holy Spirit. If he could do it, we can too. Perhaps 4% of the Greek manuscripts omit “Holy”, to be followed by NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.">j</a></sup>interpreting spiritual things to spiritual people.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:18; Romans 8:5–8; Matthew 13:11–17"> Now a soulish<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="That is what the Text says. A ‘soulish’ person cannot understand spiritual things, which sounds rather like a congenital defect, no spirit—the reference might be to humanoids à la Genesis 6 (Matthew 24:37). (I would say that the correct translation of Jude 19 is: “they are ‘soulish’, not having a spirit”. See the footnote at that point.)">k</a></sup>man does not receive the things of the Spirit of God, for they are foolishness to him; indeed, he cannot understand them, because they are spiritually discerned.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 5:21; Proverbs 28:5; Hebrews 5:14"> But he who is spiritual evaluates everything, while not being himself subject to anyone's judgment.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="The same verb is used three times, to which I have given different renderings—‘discerned’, ‘evaluates’, ‘subject to judgment’ (the middle one is in the active voice, the other two in the passive). The king of interpretation is the context, and the verb (ανακρινω) covers a considerable semantic area.">l</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Isaiah 40:13–14; Romans 11:34; John 16:13–16"> For “who has known the mind of the Lord, that he may instruct Him?”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="See Isaiah 40:13. Compare John 3:8, where the Lord Jesus says that the Spirit controlled person is like the wind, unpredictable—other people will have trouble figuring him out; when they expect him to ‘zig’, he will ‘zag’.">m</a></sup>But we have the mind of Christ.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Well now, do we or don't we? How many people do you know who think like Jesus did?">n</a></sup></span>
</div>

<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">2:6</span> ‘Being set aside’ how, and when? If God's people would really be ‘salt’ and ‘light’ (Matthew 5:13-14) they could transform any culture.</li>
<li id="fn-e"><a href="#fnref-e" className="fnb">e</a> <span className="fnr">2:7</span> In the Bible a ‘mystery’ is something hidden, something yet to be revealed, not something ‘mysterious’.</li>
<li id="fn-f"><a href="#fnref-f" className="fnb">f</a> <span className="fnr">2:9</span> Wonderful! Thank you, God! See Isaiah 64:4.</li>
<li id="fn-g"><a href="#fnref-g" className="fnb">g</a> <span className="fnr">2:10</span> To us has been granted the privilege of having a fuller understanding of God's purposes than was granted to OT saints.</li>
<li id="fn-h"><a href="#fnref-h" className="fnb">h</a> <span className="fnr">2:12</span> ‘The spirit of the world’ evidently must exist. If the reference is not to Satan directly, he is certainly behind it.</li>
<li id="fn-i"><a href="#fnref-i" className="fnb">i</a> <span className="fnr">2:12</span> Evidently one of the things the Holy Spirit wants to do is to illumine us on the subject of God's free gifts—if we would only listen!</li>
<li id="fn-j"><a href="#fnref-j" className="fnb">j</a> <span className="fnr">2:13</span> Note that Paul is plainly stating that he receives instruction from the Holy Spirit. <li id="fn-k"><a href="#fnref-k" className="fnb">k</a> <span className="fnr">2:14</span> That is what the Text says.
