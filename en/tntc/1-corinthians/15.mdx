## A review

<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Galatians 1:6–12; 1 Peter 5:12; 1 Corinthians 15:3–11"> Now, brothers, I want to add information to the Gospel that I proclaimed to you, that you also received, and in which you stand;</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Hebrews 3:6; Hebrews 2:1; Hebrews 3:14"> through which also you are saved, if you hold fast that word that I proclaimed to you—unless you believed in vain.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Is this just a ‘scarecrow’?">a</a></sup></span>

<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Hosea 6:2; Matthew 12:40; Psalms 16:10–11"> and that He was buried, and that He was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures,</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Mark 16:14; 1 Corinthians 1:12; John 1:42"> and that He was seen by Cephas, then by the Twelve.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Evidently ‘the Twelve’ became like a technical term to refer to the recognized group of apostles. <span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Philippians 4:13; 2 Corinthians 3:5; Philippians 2:13"> But by the grace of God I am what I am, and His grace toward me has not been in vain; in fact, I have worked harder than all of them—well, not I, but the grace of God that was with me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Corinthians 15:3–4; 1 Corinthians 2:2"> So whether it was I or they, thus we preached and thus you believed.</span>

## Resurrection is a fact

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 26:8; Acts 17:32; 2 Timothy 2:18"> Now if Christ is being proclaimed as having been raised from among the dead, how can some among you say that there is no resurrection of the dead?</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="John 11:25–26; Romans 8:11; 1 Thessalonians 4:14"> If there is no resurrection of the dead, not even Christ has been raised.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="1 Corinthians 15:17; 1 Thessalonians 4:14; Galatians 2:2"> And if Christ has not been raised, then our preaching is empty, and so is your faith!</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Acts 2:24; Job 13:7–10; Acts 2:32"> Further, we are even discovered to be false witnesses of God, because we have testified about God that He raised the Christ, whom He did not raise, if in fact the dead are not raised.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> For if no dead are raised, neither has Christ been raised.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Romans 4:25; 1 Peter 1:3; 1 Corinthians 15:14"> And if Christ has not been raised, your faith is useless; you are still in your sins.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 4:13–14; Revelation 14:13; 1 Thessalonians 4:16"> Then those also who have fallen asleep in Christ are lost.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 Peter 1:21; Revelation 14:13; 2 Timothy 1:12"> If it is only for this life that we have hoped in Christ, we are of all men most pitiable.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="We would be ‘pitiable’ because of contrast between reality and our expectations—our high hopes would be dashed; the higher the hopes, the greater the disappointment. ## The sequence of events

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Corinthians 15:23; Acts 26:23; Revelation 1:5"> But indeed, Christ has been raised from the dead; He became the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="John 11:25; Romans 5:12–17; 1 Corinthians 15:22"> For since death came through a man, resurrection of the dead also came through a man.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Although Jesus was God, it was as a man that He defeated Satan.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Romans 5:12–21; 1 Corinthians 15:45–49; John 5:21–29"> For as all in Adam are dying, so also all in the Christ will be made alive.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Since all human beings are in Adam, all are dying; but it is only those who are in Christ who will be made alive.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Isaiah 26:19; Galatians 3:29; 1 Corinthians 15:52"> But each one in his own order: Christ the firstfruits, then those who belong to Christ at His coming;</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Daniel 7:27; Daniel 7:14; Matthew 28:18"> then the end,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="The ‘first’ resurrection refers to that of the saved (the ‘second’ being that of the lost), and apparently occurs in three stages: 1) Christ, the firstfruits, and those He took with Him at that time; 2) the Rapture; 3) after the Millennium.">h</a></sup>when He hands over the Kingdom to the God and Father, whenever He puts an end to every ruler and all authority and power.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Hebrews 10:12–13; Psalms 110:1; Matthew 22:44"> Because it is necessary that He reign until He has put all the enemies under His feet.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Timothy 1:10; 1 Corinthians 15:55; Revelation 21:4"> The last enemy that will be destroyed is death.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Death is definitely our enemy, having been introduced into human experience by sin.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Psalms 8:6; Hebrews 2:8; Matthew 28:18"> For “He has put all things under His feet.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="See Psalm 8:6.">j</a></sup>But when it says that all things have been subordinated, it is obvious that He who put all things under Him is excepted.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Colossians 3:11; 1 Corinthians 3:23; Ephesians 1:23"> Now whenever all things have been subjected to Him, then the Son Himself will also be subjected to Him who subjected all things to Him, so that God may be all in all.</span>

## Consequences of denial

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Romans 6:3–4; 1 Corinthians 15:16; Matthew 20:22"> Otherwise, what will they do who are being baptized in place of the dead? If the dead are not raised at all, just why are they being baptized in the place of the dead?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="To be ‘dead’, they were once alive, and will be judged on the basis of what they did while alive; once dead their account is closed. So Paul is here presumably referring to those who are replacing the dead in the ranks of believers by being baptized. If there is no resurrection, what is the point of doing so, especially if all you are going to get is persecution?">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="2 Corinthians 6:9; Galatians 5:11; 2 Corinthians 4:7–12"> And why do we face danger every hour?</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 Corinthians 4:10–11; Romans 8:36; 1 Corinthians 4:9–13"> I die every day<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Paul could not have been referring to actual physical death, obviously, since Hebrews 9:27 affirms that it is appointed to men to die only once (no reincarnation). He may well have faced possible death often enough, but I suppose he is referring to dying to himself, to his own ambitions, ideas and wishes, so as to embrace God's will.">l</a></sup>—so I affirm by the boasting about you that I have in Christ Jesus our Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Isaiah 22:13; Isaiah 56:12; Luke 12:19"> If I fought human ‘animals’ in Ephesus,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Many versions have Paul actually fighting wild animals, but had he done so as a punishment in an arena, he would have lost his Roman citizenship, which he claimed to have until the end. If the dead are not raised, “Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="See Isaiah 22:13.">n</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Proverbs 13:20; Matthew 24:4; 1 Corinthians 5:6"> Stop kidding yourselves: evil associations corrupt good habits.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="1 Corinthians 6:5; Romans 13:11; Hebrews 5:11–12"> Sober up righteously, and stop sinning, for some have no knowledge of God. A clean break would require an explanation.">o</a></sup></span>

## A philosophical objection

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Ezekiel 37:3; 1 Corinthians 15:38–53; Romans 9:19"> But someone will say, “How are the dead raised? With what kind of body do they come?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="John 12:24; Luke 11:40; Ephesians 5:15"> Ignorant, what you plant is not brought to life unless it dies.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37"> And what you plant is a bare seed (perhaps of wheat or some one of the others); you do not plant the body that it will become.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="1 Corinthians 3:7; Isaiah 61:11; Mark 4:26–29"> But God gives it a body just as He determined, to each of the seeds its own body.</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Genesis 1:20–26"> All flesh is not the same flesh: that of humans is one kind, and the flesh of animals is another,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="Human meat is not the same as animal meat!">p</a></sup>that of fish is another, that of birds is yet another.</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40"> There are also celestial bodies and terrestrial bodies; but the glory of the heavenly is one, while that of the earthly is another.</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="Isaiah 24:23; Psalms 148:3–5; Deuteronomy 4:19"> There is one splendor of the sun, another splendor of the moon, and another splendor of the stars; and star differs from star in brightness.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="Matthew 13:43; Daniel 12:3; Romans 8:21"> The resurrection of the dead is like that: the body is ‘planted’ in deterioration and it is raised in incorruptibility;</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43" data-xref="1 Corinthians 6:14; Colossians 3:4; Philippians 3:20–21"> it is ‘planted’ in dishonor and it is raised in glory; it is ‘planted’ in weakness and it is raised in power;</span>
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44" data-xref="1 Corinthians 15:50; Luke 24:31; John 20:19"> it is ‘planted’ a natural body and it is raised a spiritual body. There is a natural body and there is a spiritual body.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="The spiritual body is still a body. Verses 42-44 are describing the saved, not the lost.">q</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v45" data-v="45" data-xref="John 5:21; Genesis 2:7; Romans 8:2"> So also it is written: “The first man, Adam, became a living being”;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="See Genesis 2:7.">r</a></sup>the last Adam became a life-giving spirit.</span>
<span className="v" id="v46" data-v="46" data-xref="Colossians 3:9–10; Romans 6:6; Ephesians 4:22–24"> However, the spiritual is not first, but the natural, and then the spiritual.</span>
<span className="v" id="v47" data-v="47" data-xref="John 3:31; Genesis 2:7; Genesis 3:19"> The first man was of the earth, of soil; the second man was the Lord<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-s" id="fnref-s" data-fn="The eclectic Greek text currently in vogue omits ‘the Lord’ (following 2.4% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality); and so NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc. do the same—now really, to omit ‘the Lord’ is clearly an inferior reading, and to do so on such slender evidence is irresponsible and reprehensible.">s</a></sup>from heaven.</span>
<span className="v" id="v48" data-v="48" data-xref="Genesis 5:3; Romans 5:12–21; John 3:6"> As was the earth-man, just so are the earth-people; and as was the heaven-man, just so are the heaven-people.</span>
<span className="v" id="v49" data-v="49" data-xref="Romans 8:29; 2 Corinthians 3:18; Genesis 5:3"> And just as we have borne the image of the earth-man, we should also bear the image of the heaven-man.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-t" id="fnref-t" data-fn="Since both the Textus Receptus and the eclectic Greek text currently in vogue have the Indicative here (‘we will bear’), almost all versions so read. But over 80% of the Greek manuscripts, including the best line of transmission, have the Subjunctive, which I have followed. It is not automatic; it is up to us.">t</a></sup></span>

## Our turn is coming

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v50" data-v="50" data-xref="John 3:3–6; Matthew 16:17; Ephesians 4:17"> Now this I say, brothers, that flesh and blood cannot inherit the Kingdom of God; neither will deterioration inherit incorruptibility.</span>
<span className="v" id="v51" data-v="51" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 4:14–17; Philippians 3:21; 1 Corinthians 15:20"> And now, I tell you a mystery: we will not all sleep, but we will all be changed</span>
<span className="v" id="v52" data-v="52" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 4:16–17; Matthew 24:31; John 5:28"> —in a split second, in an eye twinkle, at the last trumpet—the trumpet will blast and the dead will be raised incorruptible, and we will be changed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v53" data-v="53" data-xref="2 Corinthians 5:2–4; 1 John 3:2; Romans 2:7"> Because this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal put on immortality.</span>
<span className="v" id="v54" data-v="54" data-xref="Isaiah 25:8; Revelation 20:14; Hebrews 2:14–15"> So whenever this corruptible puts on incorruption and this mortal puts on immortality, then this written word will happen: “Death has been swallowed down into victory.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-u" id="fnref-u" data-fn="See Isaiah 25:8. This whole paragraph is addressed to “brothers” (verse 50).">u</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v55" data-v="55" data-xref="Hosea 13:14; Psalms 89:48; Acts 2:27"> <span className="pl pl-1">“Where, O Death, is your sting?</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">Where, O Hades, is your victory?”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-v" id="fnref-v" data-fn="See Hosea 13:14. Hades is not the Lake of fire. Less than 2% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, have ‘death’, instead of “Hades”, to be followed by NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.">v</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v56" data-v="56" data-xref="Romans 4:15; Galatians 3:10–13; Romans 7:5–13"> The stinger of death is sin, and the adjunct of sin is the law.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v57" data-v="57" data-xref="2 Corinthians 2:14; Romans 8:37; 1 John 5:4–5"> Now thanks be to God who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ!</span>
<span className="v" id="v58" data-v="58" data-xref="Galatians 6:9; 2 Chronicles 15:7; Hebrews 6:10"> So then, my dear brothers, be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, knowing that your labor is not in vain in the Lord.</span>
