## John eats a little book

<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Ezekiel 1:28; Matthew 17:2; Revelation 1:15–16"> (I saw a mighty angel descending out of heaven,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="John is evidently back on earth at this point.">a</a></sup>clothed with a cloud, and the rainbow<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="That is what the Text says, “the” rainbow; our rainbows are caused by the sun shining through rain, which was not the case here—perhaps God has a prototype.">b</a></sup>on his head; his face was like the sun and his feet like pillars of fire;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Does the description remind you of anyone?">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Ezekiel 2:9–10; Revelation 10:5; Revelation 5:1–5"> and he had a little book open in his hand. He placed his right foot on the sea and his left on the land,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Isaiah 31:4; Revelation 4:5; Joel 3:16"> and he cried out with a loud voice, just like a lion roars. And when he cried out, the seven thunders uttered their own voices.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="That is what the Text says, the seven thunders, and each had a voice and could speak intelligible words. <span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Ephesians 3:3–9; Romans 16:25; Luke 24:44–47"> but in the days<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="“The days”—the events associated with a given trumpet (and probably seal and bowl) cover a period of time; they are not instantaneous.">f</a></sup>of the blast of the seventh angel, whenever he should trumpet, the mystery of God which was announced to His slaves the prophets would be finished.</span>

## Footnotes

<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">10:1</span> That is what the Text says, “the” rainbow; our rainbows are caused by the sun shining through rain, which was not the case here—perhaps God has a prototype.</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">10:1</span> Does the description remind you of anyone?</li>
<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">10:3</span> That is what the Text says, the seven thunders, and each had a voice and could speak intelligible words. Obviously these “thunders” exist quite apart from climactic conditions on earth. Interesting! (I suspect that what we do not know exceeds what we think we do know, and by a considerable margin.)</li>
<li id="fn-e"><a href="#fnref-e" className="fnb">e</a> <span className="fnr">10:4</span> The evidence is divided here, but I take it that two of the three main independent lines of transmission, including the best group, have “You write after these things” rather than ‘do not write them’. This text has been generally misunderstood since the beginning. Having been told to seal what the thunders uttered, why would John be told he would write it later? So many copyists altered the text, reinforcing the prohibition. However, I take it that the voice was giving two unrelated instructions: not to record the thunders; and to put his notebook aside until later—he is going to be busy interacting with the angel and eating a book, and the voice does not want him to be distracted by trying to write at the same time.</li>
<li id="fn-f"><a href="#fnref-f" className="fnb">f</a> <span className="fnr">10:7</span> “The days”—the events associated with a given trumpet (and probably seal and bowl) cover a period of time; they are not instantaneous.</li>
<li id="fn-g"><a href="#fnref-g" className="fnb">g</a> <span className="fnr">10:11</span> The action in verses 9-11 is presumably symbolic, but of what? Like why was the little book open, and why the stomach ache, etc.? I imagine that the book contained the prophecy mentioned by the angel, and being open would indicate that the prophecy was to be understandable. The book inside John suggests inspiration to me, and the terms of the prophecy would be mainly bitter.</li>
</ol>
