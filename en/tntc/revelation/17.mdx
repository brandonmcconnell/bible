## A woman and a beast

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Revelation 12:3; Revelation 13:1–6; Revelation 12:6"> So he took me away in spirit to a wilderness. And I saw a woman sitting on a scarlet beast full of blasphemous names, having seven heads and ten horns.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Revelation 18:16; Jeremiah 51:7; Revelation 19:2"> And the woman was clothed in purple and scarlet, adorned with gold and precious stones and pearls, having in her hand a golden cup full of abominations and the filthiness of her fornication.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Revelation 14:8; Revelation 18:2; Revelation 17:7"> And on her forehead a name was written:<br />“Mystery,<br />Babylon the Great,<br />the Mother of the prostitutes<br />and the abominations of the earth.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="In Hebrew usage a ‘son’ of something was characterized by that something. <li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">17:8</span> Well, the origin matches 11:7 but the description matches 13:1, so maybe the three passages refer to a single personage. If “sea” refers to human origin and “Abyss” to demonic, could it be that Satan will imitate God in producing the Antichrist?</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">17:8</span> Here is a strong statement of election or predestination—how else could God know what names to put in the Book? At the same time the seven letters (chaps. 2 &amp; 3) are strong on human responsibility (including the possibility of a name being erased). Likewise the whore, represented by a city, may well involve people from around the earth. “Is the great city”—when John wrote, Rome was the ruling city.</li>
