## The sixth trumpet

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Revelation 8:3–5; Exodus 30:2–3; Revelation 9:1"> So the sixth angel trumpeted, and I heard a voice from the four horns of the golden altar<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="I find it to be curious that this voice comes from the altar [and why did the altars have horns?], which is usually associated with obtaining God's mercy. <span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Revelation 16:12; Revelation 9:15; 2 Samuel 8:3"> saying to the sixth angel who had the trumpet, “Release the four angels who are bound at the great river Euphrates.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Revelation 9:18; Revelation 8:7; Revelation 8:9"> So the four angels were released—they had been prepared for the hour and the day and month and year—so that they might kill a third of mankind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Revelation 7:4; Psalms 68:17; Daniel 7:10"> And the number of the mounted troops was a hundred million<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Some ¾ of the Greek manuscripts, including the best group, do not have ‘two’.
