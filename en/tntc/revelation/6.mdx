## The Lamb opens the seals


## The first seal

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Revelation 6:5; Revelation 5:1; Revelation 6:7"> And I saw that the Lamb opened one of the seven seals, and I heard one of the four living beings saying, like a voice of thunder, “Come!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="The evidence is badly divided here, but I take it that two of the three main independent lines of transmission, including the best one, read as I have translated. The “Come!” was directed to the horse, not to John (since he was right there, there would be no need to address him with a thunderous roar). The same comment applies in verses 5 and 7.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Zechariah 6:3–8; Zechariah 1:8; Psalms 45:3–5"> And I looked and, wow, a white horse!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="You had better believe that that was some horse!">b</a></sup>And he who sat on it had a bow. And a crown was given to him; and he went out conquering, that is, in order to conquer.</span>
</div>
</div>

## The second seal

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Revelation 4:7; Revelation 6:1"> And when He opened the second seal I heard the second living being saying, “Come!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Over 80% of the Greek manuscripts do not have “and see”, as in KJV. Each of the living beings called out a horse, saying to it, “Come!”">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Zechariah 6:2; Matthew 10:34; Revelation 13:10"> And another horse went out, fiery red, and it was granted to him who sat on it to take the peace from the earth, so that they would slaughter each other; also, a huge sword was given to him.</span>
</div>
</div>

## The third seal

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Zechariah 6:2; Ezekiel 4:16; Zechariah 6:6"> And when He opened the third seal I heard the third living being saying, “Come!” And I looked and, wow, a black horse! And he who sat on it had a pair of scales in his hand.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Revelation 9:4; Revelation 4:6; Revelation 7:3"> And I heard a voice in the midst of the four living beings saying: “A ‘quart’ of wheat for a denarius<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="A denarius was the daily wage for manual labor. To work a full day for a quart of wheat means bare survival.">d</a></sup>and three ‘quarts’ of barley for a denarius; but do not harm the olive oil and the wine.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Perhaps the oil and wine are spared for their medicinal value.">e</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## The fourth seal

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Revelation 4:7; Revelation 6:3; Revelation 6:1"> And when He opened the fourth seal I heard a voice from the fourth living being saying, “Come!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Hosea 13:14; Jeremiah 15:2–3; Revelation 1:18"> And I looked and, wow, a sickly pale horse! And as for the one sitting upon it, his name is Death, and Hades follows with him.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="The Text does not say that Hades was on a horse. John is stating a fact of human existence: Hades follows death—so it has been for 6,000 years.">f</a></sup>And authority was given to him<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Less than 30% of the Greek manuscripts read ‘them’ for “him”, but they are followed by most (if not all) versions in English. However, it is Death that does the killing; Hades just collects the dead. The 70% are doubtless correct (involving two of the three main independent lines of transmission, including the best one).">g</a></sup>over a fourth of the earth, to kill by sword and by famine and by death, also by the wild animals of the earth.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="‘The four horsemen of the Apocalypse’ have received quite a bit of press, off and on, but they are just the opening gambit. The really heavy stuff comes later.">h</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## The fifth seal

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Revelation 20:4; John 16:2; Revelation 1:9"> And when He opened the fifth seal I saw underneath the altar the souls of the people who had been slaughtered on account of the Word of God and on account of the testimony of the Lamb which they held.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="These “souls” have evidently not yet been reunited with their bodies, which means that they have not participated in any resurrection, including the Rapture (see 3:10, 4:1 and 5:9). See notes at 7:13-14. “Of the people” excludes animals, that also have souls.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Revelation 19:2; Psalms 9:12; Revelation 16:5–7"> And they cried out with a loud voice saying: “How long, O Sovereign, Holy and True, until You judge and avenge our blood on those who dwell on the earth?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Revelation 14:13; Revelation 7:9; Revelation 3:4–5"> So a white robe was given to each of them, and they were told that they should rest a while longer, until both their fellow slaves and their brothers,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="“Slaves” and “brothers” evidently refer to two distinct categories of people, but I don't know the explanation (unless ‘slaves’ refers to Israel, see 7:3 below and Galatians 4:25, in which case ‘brothers’ would refer to the Church).">j</a></sup>who were about to be killed just like they were, should complete the number.</span>
</div>

## The sixth seal

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Matthew 24:29; Joel 2:10; Acts 2:19–20"> And I saw, just when He opened the sixth seal—there was a severe earthquake, and the sun became black as sackcloth of hair, and the moon became like blood.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Isaiah 34:4; Matthew 24:29; Revelation 9:1"> And the stars of heaven fell to the earth,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="I suppose this refers to fallen angels, since a single literal star would obliterate our planet.">k</a></sup>like a fig tree drops its late figs when shaken by a strong wind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Isaiah 34:4; Revelation 16:20; 2 Peter 3:10"> And the sky was split, like a scroll being rolled up, and every mountain and island was moved out of its place.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Now it starts to get heavy! <span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Malachi 3:2; Joel 2:11; Psalms 76:7"> Because the great day of His wrath has come, and who is able to stand?”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Note that it is the Lamb's wrath—those who reject His sacrifice can look for wrath. ## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">6:1</span> The evidence is badly divided here, but I take it that two of the three main independent lines of transmission, including the best one, read as I have translated. The “Come!” was directed to the horse, not to John (since he was right there, there would be no need to address him with a thunderous roar). The same comment applies in verses 5 and 7.</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">6:2</span> You had better believe that that was some horse!</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">6:3</span> Over 80% of the Greek manuscripts do not have “and see”, as in KJV. Each of the living beings called out a horse, saying to it, “Come!”</li>
<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">6:6</span> A denarius was the daily wage for manual labor. To work a full day for a quart of wheat means bare survival.</li>
<li id="fn-e"><a href="#fnref-e" className="fnb">e</a> <span className="fnr">6:6</span> Perhaps the oil and wine are spared for their medicinal value.</li>
<li id="fn-f"><a href="#fnref-f" className="fnb">f</a> <span className="fnr">6:8</span> The Text does not say that Hades was on a horse. John is stating a fact of human existence: Hades follows death—so it has been for 6,000 years.</li>
<li id="fn-g"><a href="#fnref-g" className="fnb">g</a> <span className="fnr">6:8</span> Less than 30% of the Greek manuscripts read ‘them’ for “him”, but they are followed by most (if not all) versions in English. However, it is Death that does the killing; Hades just collects the dead. The 70% are doubtless correct (involving two of the three main independent lines of transmission, including the best one).</li>
<li id="fn-h"><a href="#fnref-h" className="fnb">h</a> <span className="fnr">6:8</span> ‘The four horsemen of the Apocalypse’ have received quite a bit of press, off and on, but they are just the opening gambit. The really heavy stuff comes later.</li>
<li id="fn-i"><a href="#fnref-i" className="fnb">i</a> <span className="fnr">6:9</span> These “souls” have evidently not yet been reunited with their bodies, which means that they have not participated in any resurrection, including the Rapture (see 3:10, 4:1 and 5:9). See notes at 7:13-14. “Of the people” excludes animals, that also have souls.</li>
<li id="fn-j"><a href="#fnref-j" className="fnb">j</a> <span className="fnr">6:11</span> “Slaves” and “brothers” evidently refer to two distinct categories of people, but I don't know the explanation (unless ‘slaves’ refers to Israel, see 7:3 below and Galatians 4:25, in which case ‘brothers’ would refer to the Church).</li>
<li id="fn-k"><a href="#fnref-k" className="fnb">k</a> <span className="fnr">6:13</span> I suppose this refers to fallen angels, since a single literal star would obliterate our planet.</li>
<li id="fn-l"><a href="#fnref-l" className="fnb">l</a> <span className="fnr">6:14</span> Now it starts to get heavy! I have never been in a serious earthquake, but a former colleague was, and the trauma was so severe that years later he still had trouble talking about it.</li>
<li id="fn-m"><a href="#fnref-m" className="fnb">m</a> <span className="fnr">6:15</span> The wrath of God is a great leveler; the social classes all of a sudden do not make much difference—they are all trying to hide.</li>
<li id="fn-n"><a href="#fnref-n" className="fnb">n</a> <span className="fnr">6:17</span> Note that it is the Lamb's wrath—those who reject His sacrifice can look for wrath. Notice that there is no hint of repentance from the people.</li>
</ol>
