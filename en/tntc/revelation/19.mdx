## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">19:3</span> The whore's eternal punishment is a cause for praise to God.</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">19:9</span> Presumably the speaker is the angel of 17:1.</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">19:10</span> It surprises me that an angel could or would call himself “your brother”. I do not know what to make of it, unless this was really a human being that looked like an angel. Since the Bible records angels appearing in human form, I suppose a glorified human could appear in angelic form. Why not?</li>
<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">19:10</span> I do not understand this statement either, unless it is Jesus who is testifying to the prophets, giving them their messages.</li>
<li id="fn-e"><a href="#fnref-e" className="fnb">e</a> <span className="fnr">19:12</span> Well over half of the Greek manuscripts, two of the three main independent lines of transmission (including the best one) read “having names written”—presumably these names were on the diadems. I would imagine that each diadem had a name.</li>
<li id="fn-f"><a href="#fnref-f" className="fnb">f</a> <span className="fnr">19:15</span> The Lord Jesus Christ paid the price for our sins all by Himself (Hebrews 1:3), and here He treads “the winepress of the fury of the wrath” all by Himself. Those who think of the Lord only in terms of ‘loving, meek, mild’ need to understand that there is a whole lot more to the story. Those who reject His sacrifice can look for “the fury of the wrath”—none of us can understand how terrible was the price the Son had to pay, and He will not take kindly to having that price despised!</li>
<li id="fn-g"><a href="#fnref-g" className="fnb">g</a> <span className="fnr">19:17</span> It is mainly birds of prey and scavengers that fly high in the sky; songbirds and game birds usually stay fairly close to the ground (except when migrating).</li>
<li id="fn-h"><a href="#fnref-h" className="fnb">h</a> <span className="fnr">19:20</span> Apparently these are the first two residents in the Lake, and they will have it all to themselves for a full one thousand years!</li>
<li id="fn-i"><a href="#fnref-i" className="fnb">i</a> <span className="fnr">19:21</span> If the Captain did all the killing, then His followers were just along for the ride. Evidently all the Captain had to do was speak.</li>
<li id="fn-j"><a href="#fnref-j" className="fnb">j</a> <span className="fnr">19:21</span> The birds will eat all they can, but there will presumably be plenty of flesh left over. Ezekiel 39:12 says it will take seven months to cleanse the land of all the remains!</li>
</ol>
