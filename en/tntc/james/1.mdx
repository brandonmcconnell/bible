## About wisdom

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Proverbs 2:3–6; John 15:7; Proverbs 3:5–7"> But if any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask from the giving God,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="One of God's characteristics is that He gives, and evidently wisdom is something that He especially enjoys giving. What better time to ask for wisdom than when we are in the middle of a test?">f</a></sup>who gives to all liberally and does not reproach, and it will be given to him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Matthew 21:21–22; Hebrews 11:6; Mark 11:22–24"> However, let him ask in faith, with no doubting, because he who doubts is like a sea wave driven and tossed by the wind—</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="James 4:3; Isaiah 58:3–4; Proverbs 21:27"> such a man should not suppose that he will receive anything from the Lord,</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Kings 18:21; Matthew 6:24; James 4:8"> being a double-minded man,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="In verse 7 we have the generic term for man, but here the term refers to males.">g</a></sup>unstable in all his ways.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Jeremiah 9:23–24; James 2:5–6; Proverbs 19:1"> Now the lowly brother should glory in his high position,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="I assume that the reference is to his position in Christ, in spite of his low social standing.">h</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Peter 1:24; 1 John 2:17; Job 14:2"> but the rich brother in his humiliation,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="I take the point to be that he has already been humbled; otherwise he probably would not have trusted in Christ.">i</a></sup>because he will pass away like a flower of wild grass.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Presumably this is true of a lowly person as well, but they are usually less arrogant, more aware of their vulnerability.">j</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Isaiah 40:7–8; Psalms 37:35–36; Psalms 49:6–14"> For the sun rises with its burning heat and withers the grass, and its flower falls off and the beauty of its face perishes. Just so the rich man will be withered in his undertakings.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Sovereign Jesus said that someone who trusts in riches cannot be saved (Mark 10:24); not infrequently God uses financial reverses to bring people to Himself.">k</a></sup></span>

## About testings

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Peter 5:10; 1 Peter 1:6–8; Revelation 2:10"> A man who endures testing is blessed, because upon becoming approved<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="So what happens if we squirm out from under the testing and are not approved?">l</a></sup>he will receive the crown of the life that the Lord<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="The so-called ‘critical’ text (really ‘eclectic’) omits ‘the Lord’, following 2% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality. <span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Habakkuk 2:12–13; James 1:12; Romans 9:19–20"> Let no one, upon being tempted,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="The same Greek word can mean ‘test’ or ‘tempt’ (to tempt = to test in a moral context), the translator's choice being guided by the context. <span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Mark 7:21–22; Ephesians 4:22; Matthew 15:18"> But each one is tempted by his own craving, being taken in tow and enticed.</span>

<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Galatians 6:7; James 1:19; Philippians 2:12"> Do not be deceived, my beloved brothers:</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="John 3:27; Matthew 7:11; Hebrews 13:8"> every good giving and every perfect gift is from Above,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="Gifts coming from other sources may certainly strike us as pleasant and inviting, but will presumably have a negative effect upon our spiritual life.">p</a></sup>coming down from the Father of the lights,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="That is what the Text says, presumably referring to the sun, moon, etc. (shadows caused by those lights move and change).">q</a></sup>with whom there is no variation or shadow of turning.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Romans 8:29–31; 1 Peter 1:23; John 3:3–5"> Having willed it, He brought us forth by a word of truth,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="For someone to be born again (born from Above) he must be exposed to a word of truth. We are special!">s</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Proverbs 17:27; Proverbs 21:23; Proverbs 14:29"> So then,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-t" id="fnref-t" data-fn="Instead of “so then”, some 4% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, read “know!” (as in NIV, TEV, LB, NASB, etc.).">t</a></sup>my beloved brothers, let every man be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger;</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Timothy 2:24–25; Ephesians 4:26; James 3:17–18"> because a man's anger does not achieve God's righteousness.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-u" id="fnref-u" data-fn="“A man's anger”, not God's anger. ## About obeying the Word

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Ephesians 4:22; Ephesians 1:13; 1 Peter 2:11"> Therefore, laying aside all filthiness and overflow of evil,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-v" id="fnref-v" data-fn="Evidently James is not laboring under any exaggerated notions of their saintliness—he refers to rather gross manifestations of the old nature.">v</a></sup>receive with meekness the implanted Word which is able to save your souls.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-w" id="fnref-w" data-fn="This sounds like a process—the Word has been implanted, but needs to be ‘received’ with meekness, and then must be practiced.">w</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Romans 2:13; Luke 6:46–48; John 13:17"> Further, become word doers and not just hearers, deceiving yourselves.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Matthew 7:26–27; Ezekiel 33:31–32; James 2:14–26"> Because if anyone is a law<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-x" id="fnref-x" data-fn="I render “law”, instead of ‘word’, following some 30% of the Greek manuscripts including what is clearly the best line of transmission, in my opinion. <span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="2 Peter 3:11; Luke 1:66; Luke 7:39"> for he observes himself, then goes away and promptly forgets what he is like.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Luke 11:28; John 13:17; 2 Corinthians 3:17–18"> But he who looks carefully into the perfect law of liberty and continues in it,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-y" id="fnref-y" data-fn="This is reminiscent of John 8:31.">y</a></sup>he—not being a forgetful hearer but a work doer—he will be blessed in what he does.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-z" id="fnref-z" data-fn="Notice the emphasis on doing. <span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Ephesians 4:29; Psalms 141:3; 1 Peter 3:10"> If anyone among you thinks he is religious, not bridling his tongue but deceiving his heart, his religion is useless.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-aa" id="fnref-aa" data-fn="This topic will be developed further in 3:1-12.">aa</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Isaiah 1:16–17; 1 John 3:17–19; Galatians 6:9–10"> Pure and undefiled religion before God and Father<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-ab" id="fnref-ab" data-fn="The phrase “God and Father” works like a proper noun, referring to one being.">ab</a></sup>is this: to care for orphans and widows in their adversity;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-ac" id="fnref-ac" data-fn="James is writing to Jews, who tended to be quite religious, tithing being an important feature.
