## Rich oppressors will be judged

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Proverbs 11:28; 1 Timothy 6:9–10; Proverbs 11:4"> Come now, you rich, howl as you weep over your distresses that are coming upon you!</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Matthew 6:19–20; Job 13:28; Luke 12:33"> Your wealth has decayed and your clothes have become moth-eaten.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Romans 2:5; James 5:7–8; Revelation 21:8"> Your gold and silver are corroded,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="For some reason they just hoarded their wealth; they did not invest it, did not put it to work. They are doubly at fault—they acquired the wealth unjustly, and then just sat on it.">a</a></sup>and their corrosion will be a witness against you and will eat your flesh like fire—you stored it up for the last days.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="They probably thought they were hoarding if for a future ‘rainy day’, but they were actually hoarding it against themselves at the Day of Judgment.">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Leviticus 19:13; Jeremiah 22:13; Deuteronomy 24:14–15"> Look, the wages of the workers who cut your fields, that were unjustly held back by you, cry out, and the loud cries of the reapers have entered the ears of the Lord of Hosts.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Timothy 5:6; Luke 16:25; Luke 16:19"> You have lived on the earth in self-indulgence and luxury, you fattened your hearts as in a day of slaughter.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Back in the old days, when an animal was slaughtered you got to eat more than usual; things like liver, heart, tongue could not be preserved, so had to be eaten up.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Peter 2:22–23; John 19:9–11; Isaiah 53:7"> You condemned, you murdered the righteous—he offers you no resistance.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="The tense of the verb changes from past to present, and I do not understand why. How can he offer resistance after he is dead? Or is this giving the rationale of the murderer, ‘might makes right’; someone who offers no resistance is like a sheep asking to be shorn.">d</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Patience and perseverance

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Hebrews 12:1–3; James 5:8–9; Joel 2:23"> Therefore, brothers, be patient until the coming of the Lord. Consider, the farmer waits for the precious fruit of the earth, being patient over it until it receives the early and late rain.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="James 5:9; Galatians 5:22; Psalms 37:7"> You also be patient. Keep your hearts firm, because the coming of the Lord has approached.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Leviticus 19:18; 1 Corinthians 4:5; James 4:11"> Do not groan against one another, brothers, lest you be judged. Look, the Judge is standing at your doors!</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Hebrews 11:32–38; Matthew 5:11–12; Hebrews 13:7"> Brothers, take as an example the patient bearing of hardship and the steadfastness of the prophets who spoke in the name of the Lord.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="According to the Record, the OT prophets generally did not have an easy time of it.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="James 1:12; Exodus 34:6; Job 1:21–22"> Indeed we count as blessed those who endure—you have heard of the perseverance of Job and have seen the final outcome given by the Lord, that He is very compassionate and merciful.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="See Job 42:10-13. God gave him double what he had lost.">f</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Matthew 5:33–37; Matthew 23:16–22; 2 Corinthians 1:17–20"> But above all, my brothers, do not swear, either by heaven or by earth or any other oath. Let your “Yes” be “Yes”, and your “No”, “No”, lest you fall into pretense.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Some 5% of the Greek manuscripts read ‘judgment’ instead of ‘pretense’ (as in NIV, TEV, LB, NASB, etc.). The more we talk, the more likely we are to say things that are not precisely true.">g</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Righteous prayer is powerful

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Psalms 50:15; Jonah 2:2; Acts 16:24–25"> Is anyone among you suffering hardship? Let him pray. Is anyone cheerful? Let him sing praise.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Mark 6:13; Acts 28:8; Mark 16:18"> Is anyone among you sick? Let him call for the elders of the congregation, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 John 5:14–16; James 5:13; James 5:16"> And the prayer of faith will deliver the one who is sick,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="The implication is that the person is really sick, confined to a bed (that is why the elders have to go to him). Note that it is the faith of the elders.">h</a></sup>and the Lord will raise him up; and if he has committed sin, it will be forgiven him.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="The clear implication is that some sickness is the result of sin, but not all: “and if”.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Jeremiah 29:12–13; Proverbs 15:29; Matthew 21:22"> Confess your transgressions to one another and pray for one another, so that you may be healed.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="In my own experience, praying by myself for my own healing does not always work. When I ask others to pray for me, the Lord usually answers. God likes to promote ‘mutuality’ or interdependence.">j</a></sup>The prayer of a righteous one, being operative, is very powerful.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Just because we ourselves do not see, or are not allowed to see, an immediate result to our praying, does not mean that God is not answering. ‘The just shall live by faith’, and that includes praying.">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Kings 17:1; Luke 4:25; Revelation 11:6"> Elijah was a man with a nature like ours, and he prayed earnestly for it not to rain; and it did not rain on the land for three years and six months.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Kings 18:42–45; Jeremiah 14:22; 1 Kings 18:18"> And he prayed again, and the heaven gave rain and the earth produced its fruit.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Help the wanderer

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Galatians 6:1; Matthew 18:15; James 5:20"> Brothers, if anyone among you should wander away from the truth, and someone should turn him back,</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Peter 4:8; Proverbs 10:12; 1 Timothy 4:16"> let him know that the one who turns a sinner back from the error of his way will deliver a soul from death<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="In spite of the demands of our doctrinal packages, I don't see how this can be referring to mere physical death. Apart from the Rapture, all Christians die physically.">l</a></sup>and will remove from sight a multitude of sins.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Most of the letter has been about sin, sinning and sinners, one way or another, so this is a fitting conclusion. As we help each other, it will reduce the level or incidence of sin, and therefore of the consequences thereof.">m</a></sup></span>
</div>
