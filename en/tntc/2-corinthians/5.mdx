## We live by faith

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Job 19:25–26; 1 John 3:2; 2 Peter 1:13–14"> Now then, we know that though our earthly, tent-like ‘house’ may be destroyed, we have a building from God (not a handmade house), eternal in the heavens.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Romans 8:23; 1 Corinthians 15:53–54; 2 Corinthians 5:3–4"> Further, in this one we do groan, longing to be clothed with our heavenly habitation;</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Genesis 3:7–11; Revelation 16:15; Revelation 3:18"> since, obviously, once clothed we will not be found naked.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Corinthians 15:53–54; 2 Corinthians 5:2; Isaiah 25:8"> Yes, being in this ‘tent’ is a burden, so we groan—not enough to want to strip, but to be really clothed; that what is mortal may be swallowed up by the Life.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="This paragraph relates only to the redeemed; only we have the prospect of improving our ‘clothing’. As for the lost, the mortal will be swallowed up by the Death, and they will be left naked (but not cease to exist).">a</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Corinthians 1:22; Ephesians 1:13–14; Romans 8:23"> Now it is God who has prepared us for this very purpose, who also has given us the down payment of the Spirit.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="If you don't have the Holy Spirit, you don't have a ‘contract’. He is the down payment on ‘the Life’ (verse 4); eternal life being a quality of life, we can start living it down here.">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Corinthians 5:1; Philippians 3:20–21; Hebrews 11:13"> So we are always confident, even knowing that while we are at home in the body we are away from the Lord's home</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Hebrews 11:1–27; 2 Corinthians 4:18; Romans 8:24–25"> —we walk by faith, not by sight—</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Philippians 1:20–24; 2 Corinthians 5:6; John 14:3"> indeed, we are confident in preferring to be away from the body and at home with the Lord.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Paul is talking about facing death with confidence, knowing it is the doorway to something much better than this life. Of course this only applies to those who belong to Sovereign Jesus.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Colossians 1:10; 2 Peter 3:14; Romans 14:18"> Yes, that is why we make it our aim (whether at home or away from it) to be well pleasing to Him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Matthew 16:27; Revelation 22:12; Ephesians 6:8"> Because we must all be exposed before Christ's Judgment Seat, that each one may receive his due for the things he did while in the body, whether good or bad.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="If we are wise, like Paul, we will never forget the Accounting. He was ‘confident’ that upon leaving his physical body he would be at home with the Lord. To have the same confidence we also must consciously live so as to be well pleasing to Him. Notice that the emphasis is on what we do.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Jude 1:23; Hebrews 10:31; 2 Timothy 2:24–26"> Therefore, since we know the Lord's intimidation,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="God's holiness is an aggressive quality that attacks anything evil that tries to approach. That holiness is truly intimidating, and we will all be judged by it. Jonathan Edwards preached on that basis, with extraordinary effect, but there are few in our day who follow his example. On a lower level, worldly people tend to feel uncomfortable in the presence of someone who is holy, sometimes to the point of reacting aggressively. As Satan increases his hold on the world, such aggressive reaction will become ever more common.">e</a></sup>we try to convince people. Why Christ died<br />We are well known to God, and I hope in your consciences as well.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Corinthians 3:1; 2 Corinthians 1:14; Galatians 6:12–14"> We are not commending ourselves to you again, but are giving you an opportunity of boasting on our behalf, that you may have an answer for those who boast in appearance and not in heart.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Paul will enlarge on this later on in the letter.">f</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Corinthians 11:16–17; 2 Corinthians 11:1; 2 Corinthians 12:6"> If we are ‘out of our senses’, it is for God; if we are of sound mind, it is for you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Galatians 2:20; Romans 14:7–9; Matthew 10:37–38"> For Christ's love impels us, having concluded this: if One died for all it follows that all died;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="They all died in Him; just like all sinned in Adam.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Romans 14:7–9; 1 Peter 4:2–4; 1 Peter 1:14–15"> and He died for all so that those who now live should no longer live for themselves but for the One who died for all<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="I follow the best line of transmission, along with a minority of the manuscript total (35% here), in reading “all” instead of the familiar ‘them’.">h</a></sup>and was raised again.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="The clearest statements giving why Christ died begin with Romans 14:9, “that He might be Lord of both the dead and the living”; followed by this verse, that we should no longer live for ourselves, but for Him. Philippians 2:8-11 refers to the final victory. Hebrews 2:14 refers to destroying Satan; saving us from hell is a by-product. As it says in Romans 10:9, “if you confess with your mouth, ‘Jesus is Lord…’, you will be saved”.">i</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## We are Christ's ambassadors

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Colossians 3:11; Philippians 3:7–8; John 6:63"> So then, from now on we regard no one from a fleshly perspective—even if we have ‘known’ Christ in this way, we do so no longer<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="The unregenerate are forever trying to explain or understand Jesus in purely humanistic terms. We who are in Christ must not do so. Further, we should not try to understand anyone in such terms—we need to take account of what is happening in the spiritual realm. (As a student Saul of Tarsus had certainly seen and heard Jesus.)">j</a></sup>—</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Ezekiel 36:26; Ephesians 4:22–24; Isaiah 43:18–19"> so then, if anyone is in Christ he is a new creation; the old things have passed on; look, all has become new!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="This is neither immediate nor automatic; it is potential. No benefit of the shed blood of God's Lamb is automatic; it must be appropriated.">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Colossians 1:20–21; 2 Corinthians 5:19–20; Romans 5:10–11"> And that ‘all’ is from the God who has reconciled us to Himself through Jesus<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Perhaps 3% of the Greek manuscripts omit “Jesus”, to be followed by NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.">l</a></sup>Christ and given us the ministry of the reconciliation,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Yes, the reconciliation—no other reconciling compares with reconciling men to God.">m</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Isaiah 43:25; Isaiah 44:22; 1 John 2:1–2"> namely that in Christ God was reconciling the world to Himself,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Wonderful, wonderful, wonderful!">n</a></sup>not imputing their trespasses to them, including committing to us the message of this reconciliation.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Ephesians 6:20; Luke 14:23; Malachi 2:7"> So then, we are Christ's ambassadors, as though God were making His appeal through us. We implore you on Christ's behalf: Be reconciled to God!</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Peter 2:22–24; 1 Peter 3:18; Romans 5:19"> Because He made the One who did not know sin to be sin on our behalf, so that in Him we might become God's righteousness.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="Here we are face to face with the mystery of our redemption. Jesus, God's Son, never sinned, did not ‘know’ sin, experientially, but He was “made to be sin” on our behalf. It is a grim picture, as though on the cross He was changed into a bundle of sin. And in exchange He gave us His righteousness. How can we ‘measure’ or understand such love? It is beyond our comprehension. As Romans 11:33 expresses it: “Oh the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are His judgments and His ways past finding out!”">o</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>
