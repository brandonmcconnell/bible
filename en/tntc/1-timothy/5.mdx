## Honor true widows

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Deuteronomy 27:19; Acts 6:1; Deuteronomy 10:18"> Give financial support to widows who are really ‘widows’.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Ephesians 6:1–3; John 19:26–27; Matthew 15:4–6"> But if any widow has children or grandchildren, let them learn first to demonstrate piety in their own household by repaying their parents, for this is pleasing<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Instead of “is pleasing”, perhaps 15% of the Greek manuscripts have ‘is good and pleasing’ (as in AV and NKJV).">a</a></sup>to God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Timothy 5:3; Luke 2:37; 1 Corinthians 7:32"> Now she who is really a widow and desolate has set her hope on God, and continues in supplications and prayers night and day.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="James 5:5; Revelation 18:7; Luke 15:24"> But she who indulges in pleasure is dead while she lives.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Timothy 4:11; Titus 2:15; 2 Timothy 4:1"> You should even command these things, so that they may be blameless.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Galatians 6:10; 2 Corinthians 12:14; Luke 11:11–13"> But if anyone does not provide for his own, and especially for those of his household, he has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="This is reminiscent of our Lord's word in Mark 7:9-13.">b</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Timothy 3:2; 1 Timothy 5:11; Luke 2:36–37"> No widow should be enrolled unless she is at least sixty, a one man wife,</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Timothy 6:18; Titus 3:8; Acts 9:36"> known for good works—she brought up children, she lodged strangers, she washed saints' feet, she relieved the afflicted, she devoted herself to every good work.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Wow! How is that for a ‘job description’?">c</a></sup></span>

## About younger widows

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Timothy 5:14; Hosea 13:6; James 5:5"> As for younger widows, do not enroll them; because whenever they are controlled by sensual desires rather than by Christ, they want to marry,</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Galatians 1:6; James 3:1; 1 Corinthians 11:34"> coming under judgment because they have broken their first commitment.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="What “first commitment”? Looking carefully at verses 11 and 12, I take it that Paul is arguing as if some younger ones have already been enrolled—in order to be enrolled they would have had to commit themselves to a certain life style, which they would subsequently break.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Proverbs 20:19; 2 Thessalonians 3:6–11; Acts 20:30"> Not only that, they learn to be idle, going around from house to house; and not only idle, but also gossips and busybodies, saying things they ought not to.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="The young have more energy than the old, and if that energy is not put to good use, it will be put to bad use; and the congregation should not finance such bad use.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Titus 2:5; 1 Timothy 6:1; Titus 2:8"> So I want the younger widows to marry, to bear children, to manage the home, to give the adversary no occasion for caustic comments.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="2 Peter 2:2; Jude 1:4–5; 2 Peter 2:20–22"> (In fact, some have already turned aside after Satan.)<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="It is not clear to me to whom the “some” refers, although the first ‘candidate’ would appear to some younger widows.">f</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Timothy 5:3–5; 1 Timothy 5:8"> If any man or<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Some 2% of the Greek manuscripts, of objectively inferior quality, omit “man or” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.). The omission is obviously an inferior reading, and could be an easy case of ‘similar beginning’.">g</a></sup>woman who is a believer has widows, let them relieve them and not burden the congregation, so that it may relieve those who are widows indeed.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Here is the ‘bottom line’ in this discussion about widows. The congregation would have limited material resources, and these should be reserved for the more needy cases. (Any semblance to a ‘gravy train’ is to be avoided.)">h</a></sup></span>
</div>

## Honor elders

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Hebrews 13:17; 1 Thessalonians 5:12–13; Galatians 6:6"> Let the elders who lead well be counted worthy of a double honorarium, especially those who labor in word and teaching.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Deuteronomy 25:4; 1 Corinthians 9:14; 1 Corinthians 9:7–10"> For the Scripture says: “You shall not muzzle an ox while it treads out grain,” and “The worker is worthy of his wages.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="The part about the ox is a quote from Deuteronomy 25:4, definitely Scripture, but the part about the worker is a quote from Luke 10:7! Now this is very instructive. Paul, a former Pharisee, presumably ascribed the highest level of inspiration to the five books of the Law, so we expect him to call Deuteronomy Scripture. But for him to place Luke on a par with Moses is little short of incredible. Although there may have been close to twenty years between the ‘publishing’ of Luke and the writing of 1 Timothy, Luke was recognized and declared by apostolic authority to be Scripture not long after it came off the press, so to speak. But if Paul wrote this letter under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, as I believe, then God Himself is declaring Luke to be Scripture!">i</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Matthew 18:16; Deuteronomy 19:15; 2 Corinthians 13:1"> Do not entertain an accusation against an elder except on the basis of two or three witnesses.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Timothy 4:2; Titus 1:13; 1 Timothy 1:20"> Those who are sinning rebuke publicly, so that the rest also may be in fear.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="It appears that Timothy was to act as a ‘deputy apostle’ (something like a deputy sheriff).">j</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Watch your step

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Peter 2:4; 2 Timothy 4:1; 1 Timothy 6:13"> I charge you in the presence of God and Sovereign<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Perhaps 5% of the Greek manuscripts omit “Sovereign” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">k</a></sup>Jesus Christ and the elect angels<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="I wonder what the angels have to do with it. Might the “elect” angels be a special class?">l</a></sup>that you observe these things without prejudgment, doing nothing by partiality.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Ephesians 5:11; 1 Timothy 3:10; 1 Timothy 3:6"> Do not lay hands &#91;in ordination&#93; hastily on anyone, do not thereby participate in the sins of others.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="The point seems to be that if we are responsible for ordaining somebody, we become accomplices of what he subsequently does, at least in part.">m</a></sup>Keep yourself pure.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Psalms 104:15; Proverbs 31:4–7; 1 Timothy 4:4"> Stop drinking only water; use a little wine because of your stomach and your frequent infirmities.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="A little homemade wine can be effective against diarrhea, which was presumably Timothy's ‘infirmity’. <span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="2 Timothy 4:10; Acts 8:18; Galatians 5:19–21"> The sins of some men are obvious, leading the way into judgment,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="I take the point to be that some sins are judged already in this life, but all will be judged at the final accounting.">o</a></sup>while those of others trail behind.</span>
