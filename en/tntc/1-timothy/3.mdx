## Of overseers

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">

<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Titus 1:6–9; 2 Timothy 2:24; Romans 12:13"> Now then, it is obligatory for the overseer to be above reproach, a one woman man,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="That is what the Text says, strictly speaking, emphasizing the quality—a man who has only one wife, but has a wandering eye, would not qualify. The term “man” here refers exclusively to males; there is no room here for a female overseer.">b</a></sup>temperate, sensible, respectable, hospitable, good at teaching,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Titus 1:7; Ephesians 5:18; Titus 3:2"> not a drinker, not a bully, not corrupt &#91;financially&#93;,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Some 30% of the Greek manuscripts omit “not corrupt [financially]” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.). <span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Titus 1:6; Genesis 18:19; 1 Timothy 3:12"> one who rules his own house well, having children<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Presumably adults who have left the ‘nest’ are not in view here.">d</a></sup>who obey him with due respect</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Samuel 3:13; Ephesians 5:24; Acts 20:28"> (for if a man does not know how to rule his own house, how can he take care of God's congregation?);</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Timothy 6:4; Hebrews 5:12–13; 1 Peter 2:2"> not a recent convert, lest being puffed up he fall into the same judgment that the devil did.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Pride brought about Lucifer's downfall (Isaiah 14:13-14).">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Corinthians 8:21; 2 Timothy 2:26; Colossians 4:5"> Also, it is necessary for him to have a good reputation with those who are outside the congregation, so as not to fall into reproach and the devil's snare.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="When a pastor is well known and respected in a community, any attempt to discredit him through false accusations will probably fail; the people know it isn't true. ## Of ‘deaconesses’

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Titus 2:3; Titus 3:2; 1 Timothy 3:2"> Similarly, women<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="That is what the Text says, just “women”—no article and no possessive pronoun. Because Paul returns to the deacons in verse 12, most versions take the reference here to be to their wives, but the grammatical construction of verse 11 is parallel to that of verse 8, which is parallel to verse 2. I take it that the grammar obliges us to see a third office in the congregation, one filled by women—deaconesses, or something of the sort. Counseling women can be dangerous for a man; certain matters are best handled by a mature, sanctified woman; if she has an official standing in the congregation, so much the better.">h</a></sup>must be respectable, not slanderers, temperate, trustworthy in everything.</span>

## God in flesh

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="1 Corinthians 11:34; Philemon 1:22; 2 Corinthians 1:15–17"> Although I hope to come to you shortly, I am writing these things to you</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Peter 2:5; Ephesians 2:21–22; 1 Thessalonians 1:9"> in case I am delayed, so that you may know how it is necessary to conduct oneself in God's household, which is the Church of the living God, pillar and foundation of the truth.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="My first impression would be that the truth should be sustaining the Church, not vice versa. But it is the Church that has the responsibility to promote and defend the truth in the society at large—in education, health, commerce, government, everywhere.">j</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="John 1:14; Hebrews 1:3; Mark 16:19"> Yes, the mystery of our religion is confessedly great:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">God was manifested in flesh,</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Instead of ‘God’, 1% of the Greek manuscripts (of objectively inferior quality) read ‘who’, and most modern versions follow this 1%. But ‘who’ is nonsensical (in the context), so most of them take evasive action: NEB and NASB have ‘he who’; Phillips has ‘the one’; NRSV, Jerusalem, TEV and NIV render ‘he’. Berkley actually has ‘who’! In the Greek Text the relative pronoun has no antecedent, so it is a grammatical ‘impossibility’, besides being a stupidity—what is so mysterious about someone being manifested in flesh? All human beings have bodies. In the absence of concrete evidence, the claim that this is a note lifted from a known hymn or poem becomes no more than a desperate attempt to ‘save’ a choice that besides being stupid is also perverse (because of the theological consequences). The pronoun can be accounted for as an easy transcriptional error, a simple copying mistake, so why not stay with the 98.5% (there are other variants)? “God was manifested in flesh”—now there you have a mystery! For a more detailed discussion, please see my book, The Identity of the New Testament Text IV, footnote 3, on pages 115-117.">k</a></sup><br /><span className="pl pl-2">was vindicated in spirit,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">was revealed to angels,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">was proclaimed among nations,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">was believed in the world,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">was received up in glory!</span></span>
</div>
