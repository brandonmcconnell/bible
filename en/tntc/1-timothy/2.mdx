## About prayer

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Thessalonians 1:3; Matthew 6:9–10; James 5:16"> Therefore I exhort first of all that supplications, prayers, intercessions and thanksgivings be made on behalf of all men<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="The term “men” here is generic and can include women and children = all people. <span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Peter 2:9–13; Jeremiah 29:7; Ezra 6:10"> —on behalf of kings and all who are in authority, that we may live a tranquil and quiet life in all godliness and dignity.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="This is what God would like to see. <span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Hebrews 13:16; 1 Timothy 1:1; 1 Peter 2:20"> Because this is good and pleasing to our Savior God,</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Ezekiel 18:32; 2 Peter 3:9; Ezekiel 18:23"> who desires all men to be saved and to come into a real knowledge of Truth.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="When people are in the middle of distress and turmoil it is difficult to pause and reflect on spiritual things. <span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Matthew 20:28; 1 Peter 3:18; 1 John 2:1–2"> who gave Himself as a ransom on behalf of all people (to be testified in its own seasons),<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Although Jesus Christ “gave Himself as a ransom on behalf of all people”, obviously the exact time when a given people heard about it has varied considerably down through the last two millennia, and some have yet to hear! ## About women

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:34–35; Ephesians 5:22–24; 1 Corinthians 11:3"> Let a woman learn in silence, in full subordination.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:34"> I do not permit a woman to teach or to have authority over a man, but to be in silence.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="The crucial factor here is authority, and the underlying need is to protect the structure of the home, the foundational unit of society, including the church. If a woman teaches her husband in church, crossing the street to their house will not make her want to submit to him; human nature does not work that way.">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Genesis 2:18; 1 Corinthians 11:8–9; Genesis 1:27"> For Adam was formed first, then Eve.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Paul appeals to God's purpose in Creation. God created the man first and gave him the responsibility to administer the earth. Later on He created the woman, using a part of the man's body, for the declared purpose of being his assistant (Genesis 2:18). An assistant does not give orders to the boss; nor does he make policy decisions on his own. The first woman did make a policy decision on her own, with catastrophic consequence.">l</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Corinthians 11:3; Genesis 3:6; Genesis 3:12–13"> Also, Adam was not deceived; rather, the woman, being deceived, became a transgressor.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="As a general rule (there are exceptions) a man tends to think things through before reaching a decision, so he can defend his choice on the basis of logical argument. A woman tends to be more intuitive and will decide on that basis, without being able to defend the choice logically (which tends to frustrate men no end). Whether that was why Eve could be so easily deceived, I am not prepared to say, but Paul uses that susceptibility as an argument in his case against allowing women to teach. Doctrine must be based on reason, not intuition—to teach is to define. Paul affirms that Adam was not deceived, so his choice was deliberate. To disobey the Creator was a policy decision that Eve, as assistant, had no business making—she rebelled against the role that the Creator assigned her. Her fait accompli left Adam with a difficult decision—let her die alone, or die with her. It is idle to speculate on why he decided as he did, but it was Adam's choice (not Eve's) that condemned the race to ‘death through sin’ (Romans 5:12-21).">m</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Titus 2:12; Genesis 3:15–16; 1 Peter 4:7"> However, she will be saved through the Childbirth<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Note that ‘she’ refers to Eve, she is the antecedent. Neither Eve nor any other woman is saved by bearing a child. ‘Childbirth’ is accompanied by the definite article, in the Text, so it is “the childbirth”. There is only one childbirth that could result in salvation for Eve, and the rest of us, the birth of the Messiah.">n</a></sup>—if they<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="Paul breaks the rules of grammar and switches from ‘she’ to ‘they’ in the middle of the sentence—what is true of Eve is applied to all women. Well, strictly speaking, since “they” has no antecedent I suppose it could include men as well, everybody (unless someone wants to argue that women are saved on a different basis than men [which I think would run afoul of other passages]). Still, the paragraph is about women. Any sisters in Christ who have been troubled by this verse, thinking they must bear a child, may relax on that score.">o</a></sup>continue in faith, love and holiness, with self-control.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="So what happens if they do not “continue”?">p</a></sup></span>
</div>
