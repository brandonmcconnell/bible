## God is light

<div className="bp">

<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Ephesians 5:8; Romans 13:12; Revelation 1:5"> But if we walk in the light just as He is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus Christ<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Perhaps 5% of the Greek manuscripts omit “Christ” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).">f</a></sup>His Son cleanses us from all sin.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Cleansing is not the same as forgiveness. ## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">1:1</span> John could hardly be more emphatic as to the certainty of what he is going to write—he is an eye-witness, an ear-witness, a hand-witness, a mind-witness…</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">1:1</span> The text has the definite article with “life”, and from the next verse it seems clear that the reference is to the Son (compare John 1:4).</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">1:3</span> Note that the fellowship is to be based on historical fact, not someone's subculture.</li>
<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">1:4</span> The manuscript evidence is badly divided between “our” and ‘your’, roughly 60:40%. The best line of evidence, in my view, is with the majority in favor of “our”; it is easy to see how many copyists could make the change (a change in only one letter). The more people we bring into the fellowship, the greater our joy.</li>
<li id="fn-e"><a href="#fnref-e" className="fnb">e</a> <span className="fnr">1:5</span> I suppose that “darkness” here refers to sin and Satan; presumably it follows that there is no light in Satan. Also, “walking in the darkness” (next verse) refers to living according to the world's values, a world controlled by Satan.</li>
<li id="fn-f"><a href="#fnref-f" className="fnb">f</a> <span className="fnr">1:7</span> Perhaps 5% of the Greek manuscripts omit “Christ” (as in NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc.).</li>
<li id="fn-g"><a href="#fnref-g" className="fnb">g</a> <span className="fnr">1:7</span> Cleansing is not the same as forgiveness. If a mother forgives her boy for playing in the mud, he escapes punishment, but that does not wash the clothes. Forgiveness takes care of the consequences of our sin in heaven, but for the consequences down here we need cleansing (although some consequences cannot be undone). As we walk in the light we will be progressively cleansed.</li>
<li id="fn-h"><a href="#fnref-h" className="fnb">h</a> <span className="fnr">1:8</span> I take the reference here to be to the sin nature; of course someone without a sin nature would have no need of a Savior, nor of regeneration. Anyone who really thinks that he does not have one is most certainly self-deceived, if not satanically deceived.</li>
<li id="fn-i"><a href="#fnref-i" className="fnb">i</a> <span className="fnr">1:9</span> If He forgave without cleansing us, we would still be dirty. But He only forgives when we confess. (Do not forget Proverbs 29:1).</li>
<li id="fn-j"><a href="#fnref-j" className="fnb">j</a> <span className="fnr">1:10</span> The reference here is to individual acts of sin. To contradict God, who affirms that we do in fact commit sins, is to make Him out to be a liar—probably not a good idea.</li>
</ol>
