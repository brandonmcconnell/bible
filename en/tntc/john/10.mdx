## Jesus the Shepherd


## A true shepherd

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Matthew 7:15; John 10:8–10; Romans 16:18"> “Most assuredly I say to you, he who does not enter the sheepfold by the door, but climbs up some other way, the same is a thief and a robber.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="John 10:14; Acts 20:28; 1 Peter 5:4"> But he who enters by the door is a shepherd<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“A shepherd”—the fold was communal, so the sheep belonged to a variety of shepherds. <span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Ezekiel 34:11–16; 2 Timothy 2:19; John 10:4"> To him the doorkeeper opens, and the sheep hear his voice; and he calls his own sheep by name and leads them out.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Deuteronomy 1:30; John 10:16; John 12:26"> And whenever he takes out his own sheep he goes ahead of them,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="He ‘leads’, not ‘drives’. <span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 John 4:5–6; Colossians 2:6–10; Mark 4:24"> But they will not follow a stranger; rather they will run away from him, because they do not know the voice of strangers.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Isaiah 56:11; Psalms 82:5; Proverbs 28:5"> Jesus gave them this illustration, but they did not understand what He was telling them.</span>

<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Ezekiel 34:2; Jeremiah 23:1; John 10:1"> All who ever came before me are thieves and robbers,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Although some 50% of the Greek manuscripts (also the three ancient versions) omit “before me”, we are virtually obliged to understand this, in any case, since presumably Jesus means pretending to be the Messiah. ## The Good Shepherd

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="John 10:14–15; Psalms 23:1; John 15:13"> “I am the good shepherd.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Here Jesus changes the figure—as the good shepherd He really cares about the sheep, to the point of dying for them.">h</a></sup>The good shepherd lays down his life on behalf of the sheep.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 20:29; Zechariah 11:16–17; Ezekiel 34:2–6"> But the hired man, not being the shepherd and not owning the sheep, sees the wolf coming and abandons the sheep, and runs away; and the wolf snatches the sheep and scatters them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Philippians 2:20; John 12:6; Acts 18:17"> Now the hired man runs away because he is a hired man and it does not matter to him about the sheep.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="John 10:27; John 10:11; 2 Timothy 2:19"> “I am the good shepherd, and I know my own sheep, and I am known by them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Galatians 1:4; John 10:11; Isaiah 53:10"> Just as the Father knows me, I also know the Father, and I lay down my life on behalf of the sheep.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Isaiah 56:8; John 11:52; 1 Peter 2:25"> I also have other sheep, that are not of this fold;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="“This fold” presumably refers to Israel, so the “other sheep” would be Gentiles. Paul develops this theme in Ephesians 2:11-22.">i</a></sup>I must bring them also, and they will listen to my voice; and there will be one flock, one shepherd.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="John 10:18; John 10:11; Hebrews 2:9"> “Because of this the Father loves me, in that I lay down my life so that I may take it up again.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Hebrews 2:9; Titus 2:14; John 2:19–21"> No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of myself.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="This is important! The cross did not kill Jesus; He dismissed His spirit—see John 19:30 and Matthew 27:50. In Mark 15:39 the loud shout followed immediately by death convinced the centurion. He had seen many crucifixions and was doubtless hardened to it. He knew that a person on a cross dies from asphyxiation. The weight of the body pushes the diaphragm against the lungs and you cannot breathe. Nailing the feet was a sadistic procedure that prolonged the agony—rather than die they would push against the nail to get a breath. Finally, when too weak to do that they would die for lack of air. (That is why they broke the legs of the two thieves; they then died within a few minutes.) Obviously, if you are dying without air you cannot shout! The centurion knew that the cross had not killed Jesus. But what mere human can just tell his spirit to leave? 2 + 2 = 4. Jesus had to be the Son of God.">j</a></sup>I have authority to lay it down, and I have authority to take it up again. This command I have received from my Father.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="The whole procedure was part of the Plan. An ordinary human being can kill himself, all right, but cannot change his mind later and come back. To Jesus the Father gave the ability to reunite spirit and body—presumably a unique occurrence.">k</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="John 9:16; 1 Corinthians 3:3; John 7:40–43"> Therefore there was another division among the Jews because of these words.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="John 7:20; Mark 3:21; John 8:47–48"> Many of them were saying: “He has a demon and is raving mad. Why do you listen to him?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Exodus 4:11; Isaiah 35:5–6; Psalms 94:9"> Others were saying: “These are not the sayings of someone who is demonized. A demon cannot open blind people's eyes, can it?”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="So they thought, but on the basis of what happens in Spiritist and Satanist circles, it appears that if a demon causes blindness (which they can), the same or a stronger one can remove it.">l</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## “My sheep hear my voice”

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> Now it was the Feast of Dedication in Jerusalem;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Chapter 8 happened on Oct. 18, 29 (I think). Their target was interior Judea, in contrast to the twelve, whose target was Galilee.">m</a></sup>and it was winter.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Acts 5:12; Acts 3:11"> And Jesus was walking about in the temple,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="It was probably cold and He was walking to stay warm.">n</a></sup>in Solomon's porch.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Luke 22:67–70; John 1:19; John 8:25"> Then the Jews surrounded Him and said to Him: “How long will you keep us in suspense? If you are the Christ, tell us plainly.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="John 10:38; John 8:58; John 14:11"> Jesus answered them: “I did tell you, and you do not believe.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="Quite right. <span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="John 8:47; 2 Corinthians 4:3–4; John 12:37–40"> But you do not believe because you are not among my sheep, just like I told you.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="When was that? <span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="John 6:37; John 17:2; John 17:9"> My Father, who has given them to me, is greater than all;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="About 0.5% of the Greek manuscripts (with variations), of objectively inferior quality, read “What my Father has given me is greater than all” (as in TEV, etc.).">r</a></sup>and no one is able to snatch out of my Father's hand.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="John 1:1–2; John 14:9; John 17:21–23"> I and the Father are one.”</span>

<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="John 5:18; John 8:59; 1 Samuel 30:6"> Therefore the Jews picked up stones<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-s" id="fnref-s" data-fn="The temple courts were paved and presumably swept with some regularity. <span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Psalms 82:6–7; Exodus 7:1; Psalms 82:1"> Jesus answered them: “Is it not written in your law, ‘I said, you are gods’?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-t" id="fnref-t" data-fn="The reference is to Psalm 82:6, where the speaker is God (verse 1). <span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Matthew 24:35; Romans 13:1; Luke 16:17"> If He called them ‘gods’ to whom the Word of God came—and the Scripture cannot be broken<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-u" id="fnref-u" data-fn="The Lord inserts this as an aside, but it is an important statement in itself—Scripture cannot be broken. <span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="John 4:41; John 2:23; John 7:31"> And many of the people there believed into Him.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-x" id="fnref-x" data-fn="We are back in Bithabara (see John 1:28).
