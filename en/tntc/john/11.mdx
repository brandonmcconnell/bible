After all the discussion is over, we have two options: rebel or submit.</li>
<li id="fn-l"><a href="#fnref-l" className="fnb">l</a> <span className="fnr">11:26</span> The verb ‘die’ in verse 25 refers to physical death, in verse 26 it refers to spiritual death, while the verb ‘live’ refers to spiritual life (although one might translate the Text as ‘will not die forever’, rather than “will never ever die”, indicating that physical death is temporary). Note that ‘lives’ and ‘believes into’ are in the present tense, and then note the word ‘both’. Once you have life in Christ, you need to keep on believing.</li>
<li id="fn-m"><a href="#fnref-m" className="fnb">m</a> <span className="fnr">11:27</span> Martha was not a theologian; she may not have understood it all, but she knew who Jesus was! Like Martha, we don't have to understand everything, but we do need to know who Jesus is.</li>
<li id="fn-n"><a href="#fnref-n" className="fnb">n</a> <span className="fnr">11:32</span> I see a significant contrast between the sisters. Both of them say the same thing, but in a different tone. Mary is given over to her grief, she has no hope. Martha goes on to declare her confidence in Jesus; she still has hope.</li>
<li id="fn-o"><a href="#fnref-o" className="fnb">o</a> <span className="fnr">11:33</span> Why? What troubled Him? He would not be inconsistent with Himself and His commitment to the Father's will, the Plan. Seeing Mary's despair, perhaps He felt distress at the suffering the Plan was causing—He did not enjoy the fact that He was responsible for that suffering.</li>
<li id="fn-p"><a href="#fnref-p" className="fnb">p</a> <span className="fnr">11:35</span> The verb rendered ‘wept’ is really ‘shed tears’, different from the verb in verse 33, which is often associated with mourning. Jesus was not mourning, knowing what He was about to do. So why did He weep? Perhaps He was empathizing with their grief. But I imagine that their hopeless perspective also troubled Him—their view of death was inadequate.</li>
<li id="fn-q"><a href="#fnref-q" className="fnb">q</a> <span className="fnr">11:39</span> That is just what she said. Hey, at that moment Martha was not thinking about how it would read 2,000 years later! She reacted instinctively and blurted it out. Being a practical person she wanted to avoid further distress.</li>
<li id="fn-r"><a href="#fnref-r" className="fnb">r</a> <span className="fnr">11:40</span> When had He said that? The Text does not say, but I imagine it was during their earlier conversation—to me it is obvious that they said more than is recorded. In any event, we have here an important declaration. Have you seen the glory of God lately?</li>
<li id="fn-s"><a href="#fnref-s" className="fnb">s</a> <span className="fnr">11:41</span> He is saying in so many words that He and the Father had already talked about the plan of action.</li>
<li id="fn-t"><a href="#fnref-t" className="fnb">t</a> <span className="fnr">11:42</span> He was talking out loud on purpose, so that the people around would hear what He said.</li>
<li id="fn-u"><a href="#fnref-u" className="fnb">u</a> <span className="fnr">11:43</span> That is what the Text says. Of course to shout is to use a loud voice, so the point seems to be that He really put His lungs into it—you could have heard Him a mile away! Well, Lazarus heard Him in Hades, which is in the middle of the earth somewhere (Matthew 12:40, Ephesians 4:9). Yes, but, could He not have done it with a whisper? I suppose so. So why did He yell? I do not know; I was not there. However, there may have been some symbolism involved—the longer one is dead the harder it is to bring him back.</li>
<li id="fn-v"><a href="#fnref-v" className="fnb">v</a> <span className="fnr">11:43</span> As someone has said, if the Lord hadn't specified ‘Lazarus’ He might have emptied the graveyard!</li>
<li id="fn-w"><a href="#fnref-w" className="fnb">w</a> <span className="fnr">11:44</span> He must have come jumping out like a kangaroo—with his feet tied he could not walk. It must have been an interesting sight. And with his eyes covered he could not see, so obviously he had supernatural help.</li>
<li id="fn-x"><a href="#fnref-x" className="fnb">x</a> <span className="fnr">11:44</span> Would you have wanted to be the first one to start untying? I bet it was Martha. Notice that Jesus did not do what they could do; removing the stone and unwrapping Lazarus was up to them.</li>
<li id="fn-y"><a href="#fnref-y" className="fnb">y</a> <span className="fnr">11:47</span> There is no definite article with ‘council’, so I doubt that it was the full Sanhedrin; they would not want Nicodemus, or any others who were sympathetic to Jesus, to be there.</li>
<li id="fn-z"><a href="#fnref-z" className="fnb">z</a> <span className="fnr">11:48</span> This was their ‘hidden agenda’. Jesus had more than substantiated His claim to be the Messiah, but they did not want the political consequences of installing Him.</li>
<li id="fn-aa"><a href="#fnref-aa" className="fnb">aa</a> <span className="fnr">11:49</span> Dear me, he wasn't very nice! But John says he was prophesying, so it may have been God telling them what He thought of them.</li>
<li id="fn-ab"><a href="#fnref-ab" className="fnb">ab</a> <span className="fnr">11:50</span> For “us” perhaps 2% of the Greek manuscripts, of inferior quality, read “you” (as in NIV, NASB, TEV etc. &#91;LB omits&#93;). Since this is a prophecy, the difference is significant.</li>
<li id="fn-ac"><a href="#fnref-ac" className="fnb">ac</a> <span className="fnr">11:51</span> The ‘nation’ in question would be the Jewish nation. I find it interesting that God would choose to speak through Caiaphas. He was not the real high priest (who was Annas) but reflected a Roman imposition whereby the Jews had to name a new one every year, and that year it was Caiaphas. Maybe from God's point of view Annas was worse.</li>
<li id="fn-ad"><a href="#fnref-ad" className="fnb">ad</a> <span className="fnr">11:52</span> The actual terms of the prophecy seem to be limited to the one nation, so I take it that verse 52 is an added application offered by John.</li>
<li id="fn-ae"><a href="#fnref-ae" className="fnb">ae</a> <span className="fnr">11:53</span> They have been wanting to kill Him for some time, but at this point it becomes top priority.</li>
<li id="fn-af"><a href="#fnref-af" className="fnb">af</a> <span className="fnr">11:54</span> The time available was probably not more than two weeks, if that. It was a last bit of rest before the final storm, but the Lord doubtless took advantage of the opportunity to teach the disciples, without the distraction of the crowds.</li>
<li id="fn-ag"><a href="#fnref-ag" className="fnb">ag</a> <span className="fnr">11:56</span> I think this refers back to verse 53—it is the leaders who are conversing among themselves.</li>
</ol>
