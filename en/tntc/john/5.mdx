Here in John Jesus challenges their authority over the Sabbath.</li>

<li id="fn-m"><a href="#fnref-m" className="fnb">m</a> <span className="fnr">5:19</span> I find this statement to be amazing, revealing and challenging. Jesus only did what He saw the Father doing; so how about us? I would say that my main ‘ministry’ problem is that I often don't know what the Father is doing, and so I waste a lot of time and effort.</li>
<li id="fn-n"><a href="#fnref-n" className="fnb">n</a> <span className="fnr">5:20</span> To my surprise the verb here is φιλεω, not αγαπαω—the Father is emotionally attached to the Son. Well, since we have emotions God must have had them first. God affirms that He has soul (Isaiah 42:1, Matthew 12:18, Hebrews 10:38).</li>
<li id="fn-o"><a href="#fnref-o" className="fnb">o</a> <span className="fnr">5:22</span> I suppose this means that it will be the Son who sits on the ‘great white throne’ (Revelation 20:11).</li>
<li id="fn-p"><a href="#fnref-p" className="fnb">p</a> <span className="fnr">5:23</span> Of course someone who does not honor the Father will not honor the Son either.</li>
<li id="fn-q"><a href="#fnref-q" className="fnb">q</a> <span className="fnr">5:23</span> I take it that the Lord Jesus is saying that monotheistic people (Jews, Muslims) who claim to honor the Father, but do not honor the Son, are not really doing so. (Perhaps that would not apply to those who have never heard about Jesus.)</li>
<li id="fn-r"><a href="#fnref-r" className="fnb">r</a> <span className="fnr">5:24</span> Most versions render ‘hear’ instead of ‘listen’, both here and in the next verse, but from the context it seems clear that Jesus is not talking about sound striking the eardrum; He is talking about acting on the basis of what is heard.</li>
<li id="fn-s"><a href="#fnref-s" className="fnb">s</a> <span className="fnr">5:24</span> “The death” refers to Satan's kingdom; “the life” refers to Christ's kingdom (and they are the only alternatives in this world).</li>
<li id="fn-t"><a href="#fnref-t" className="fnb">t</a> <span className="fnr">5:25</span> It seems clear that the picture here is different from that in verses 28-29. Here Jesus says “now is”, so He is not talking about future judgment; so the dead here are spiritually dead, and those who listen to Him receive spiritual life. In verse 28 the reference is to the physically dead, who are raised to face the final judgment.</li>
<li id="fn-u"><a href="#fnref-u" className="fnb">u</a> <span className="fnr">5:27</span> We as human beings will be judged by another human being, someone who knows what it's like. There is no definite article with either “Son” or “Man”, so this is not the title He normally used.</li>
<li id="fn-v"><a href="#fnref-v" className="fnb">v</a> <span className="fnr">5:29</span> Notice again the emphasis on doing, because what you do reflects what you really believe, and what you really believe is what you are—and you cannot kid God. “All those in the graves” means everyone who has died; all will be raised, and all must face the final Accounting.</li>
<li id="fn-w"><a href="#fnref-w" className="fnb">w</a> <span className="fnr">5:31</span> According to the Law at least two witnesses were required, so if Jesus had only His own word it would not be enough, would not be valid (even though He told the truth).</li>
<li id="fn-x"><a href="#fnref-x" className="fnb">x</a> <span className="fnr">5:32</span> I take it that this witness must be the Father, since in verse 34 Jesus discounts John the Baptizer.</li>
<li id="fn-y"><a href="#fnref-y" className="fnb">y</a> <span className="fnr">5:34</span> Since Jesus has three supernatural witnesses—the Father, His own works and the Scriptures—He does not need John. I do not believe Jesus was angry with John or otherwise dissatisfied with his testimony; it is just that the Jews had already rejected John's testimony about Jesus (as they had rejected Jesus Himself). As He says, He would like for them to turn around and be saved, so He organizes His argument in that attempt.</li>
<li id="fn-z"><a href="#fnref-z" className="fnb">z</a> <span className="fnr">5:36</span> Nicodemus recognized this.</li>
<li id="fn-aa"><a href="#fnref-aa" className="fnb">aa</a> <span className="fnr">5:38</span> The Lord declares their spiritual bankruptcy. For a religious leader to have never heard God's voice is pretty poor, but much worse is to study God's Word without its ever finding a place in your heart.</li>
<li id="fn-ab"><a href="#fnref-ab" className="fnb">ab</a> <span className="fnr">5:40</span> Jesus had proved that He was the Messiah, but He threatened everything that they stood for.</li>
<li id="fn-ac"><a href="#fnref-ac" className="fnb">ac</a> <span className="fnr">5:42</span> That is what the Text says, “I have come to know”—presumably He is referring to knowledge gained by observation; they have clearly demonstrated in His presence that in fact they do not have God's love.</li>
<li id="fn-ad"><a href="#fnref-ad" className="fnb">ad</a> <span className="fnr">5:43</span> Might this be a prophetic reference to the Antichrist?</li>
<li id="fn-ae"><a href="#fnref-ae" className="fnb">ae</a> <span className="fnr">5:44</span> Here I follow what I consider to be by far the best line of transmission, although attested by less than 30% of the Greek manuscripts.</li>
<li id="fn-af"><a href="#fnref-af" className="fnb">af</a> <span className="fnr">5:44</span> This is serious! Apparently one's attitude toward glory affects his ability to believe. If the approval of people is more important to you than the approval of God, you probably will not believe into Jesus.</li>
<li id="fn-ag"><a href="#fnref-ag" className="fnb">ag</a> <span className="fnr">5:47</span> The Lord is affirming the inspiration and authority of Moses' writings; in effect He places them on a level with His own word, for which He claims eternal authority (Luke 21:33).</li>
</ol>
