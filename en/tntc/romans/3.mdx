## Advantage

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Romans 2:25–29; Genesis 25:32; Ecclesiastes 6:11"> So what advantage does the Jew have, or what is the profit of circumcision?</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Romans 9:4; Acts 7:38; 1 Corinthians 9:17"> Much in every way!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="For a fuller list of the advantages, see Romans 9:1-5. To be born into a culture that has been blessed with God's Light is certainly an advantage.">a</a></sup>First of all, because they were entrusted with the oracles of God.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Job was written by a pre-Jew, but all the rest of the Old Testament, which is what Paul is referring to, was written by Jews. For that matter, all the books of the New Testament were also written by Jews (with the possible exception of Luke). “The oracles of God” are God's written Revelation to the human race.">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Timothy 2:13; Hebrews 4:2; Romans 9:6"> So, what if some did not believe? Their unbelief will not nullify God's faithfulness, will it?</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Psalms 51:4; Psalms 116:11; Galatians 2:17"> Of course not! Rather, let God be found true, but every man a liar; just as it is written:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“That you may be justified in your words,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">and may overcome when you are judged.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="See Psalm 51:4. “If we are faithless, He remains faithful—He is incapable of denying Himself” (2 Timothy 2:13).">c</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## A conundrum

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Romans 6:19; Galatians 3:15; Romans 9:18–20"> Now if our unrighteousness highlights God's righteousness, what shall we say? God is not unrighteous for inflicting His wrath, is He? (I speak as a man.)</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Genesis 18:25; Acts 17:31; Job 8:3"> Of course not! Otherwise, how will God judge the world?</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Romans 3:4; Romans 9:19–20; Acts 13:27–29"> Still, if the truth of God abounded to His glory by my falsehood, just why am I still judged as a sinner?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="If I choose falsehood, it is an evil choice, and I deserve to be punished, quite apart from any highlighting of God's righteousness. Any ‘highlighting’ does not alter the nature of my choice.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Romans 6:1; Romans 6:15; Romans 5:20"> However, we must not say, “Let us do evil so that good may come” (as some people slanderously claim that we do)—their condemnation is well deserved!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="I suppose that part of the point is that all evil has consequences in this life and in this world, and any eventual ‘good’ does not undo those consequences, and neither does it compensate for them.">e</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## All are under sin

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Galatians 3:22; Romans 6:15; Isaiah 65:5"> What then? Are we any better? Not at all! For we have already charged both Jews and Greeks<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Paul uses ‘Greeks’ as a cover term for all non-Jews.">f</a></sup>that they are all under sin.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Psalms 53:1–3; Psalms 14:1–3; Romans 3:23"> Just as it is written:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“No one is righteous, not even one;</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Psalms 14:2–4; Psalms 53:2; Titus 3:3"> <span className="pl pl-2">there is no one who understands, no one who seeks God.</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Psalms 14:3; Ecclesiastes 7:20; Isaiah 64:6"> <span className="pl pl-1">All have turned aside, together they have been made useless;</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">there is no one who shows kindness,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">no, not so much as one.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="See Psalm 14:1-3, 53:1-3; Ecclesiastes 7:20.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Psalms 5:9; Psalms 140:3; Matthew 23:27–28"> <span className="pl pl-1">“Their throat is an opened grave;</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">they habitually deceive with their tongues.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="See Psalm 5:9.">h</a></sup><br /><span className="pl pl-1">“Viper's venom is under their lips”;</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="See Psalm 140:3.">i</a></sup><br /></span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Psalms 10:7; Psalms 109:17–18; Psalms 59:12"> <span className="pl pl-1">“whose mouth is full of cursing and bitterness.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="See Psalm 10:7.">j</a></sup><br /></span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Isaiah 59:7–8; Proverbs 1:16; Proverbs 6:18"> <span className="pl pl-1">“Their feet are swift to shed blood;</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> <span className="pl pl-2">ruin and wretchedness are in their paths,</span><br /></span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Isaiah 59:8; Romans 5:1; Luke 1:79"> <span className="pl pl-2">and the way of peace they have not known.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="See Isaiah 59:7-8.">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Psalms 36:1; Proverbs 23:17; Proverbs 16:6"> <span className="pl pl-1">“There is no fear of God before their eyes.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="See Psalm 36:1. The picture painted in verses 10-18 is not pretty! The description includes both Jews and Gentiles. Since the Creator is the only one who is competent to dictate moral values, someone who denies His authority (or worse, His existence) will not be bound by such values. We have already commented on what Satan does with a ‘son of the disobedience’; the result is ‘ruin and wretchedness’. Why would any sane person want that?">l</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Galatians 3:10; Galatians 3:22–23; Romans 2:12–18"> Now we know that whatever the law says, it speaks to those who are under the law, so that every mouth may be shut and the whole world become accountable to God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Galatians 2:16; Acts 13:39; Romans 3:28"> It follows that no flesh will be justified in His sight by the works of the law, because through the law comes the real knowledge of sin.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="The final accounting is based on our works, what we did—but no one will be justified on that basis. Our justification comes through the shed blood of God's Lamb.">m</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Righteousness by faith

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Acts 10:43; Jeremiah 33:16; Philippians 3:9"> But now, apart from law, a righteousness from God has been revealed, being attested by the Law and the Prophets,</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Romans 10:12; Galatians 3:28; Romans 8:1"> namely, a righteousness from God through faith in Jesus Christ, into all and upon all<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="That righteousness is available to all, but is only applied to those who believe. Perhaps 7% of the Greek MSS omit “and upon all”, to be followed by NIV, NASB, TEV, LB, etc.—an inferior proceeding (it is an easy case of ‘similar ending’—the eye of the copyist skipped from one ‘all’ to the next).">n</a></sup>those who believe. There is no difference:</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 7:20; 1 John 1:8–10; Romans 3:9"> all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="What do you think ‘the glory of God’ refers to? In this context it can scarcely be brilliance or effulgence. I suggest that God's true glory is what He Himself is, His essence—truth, holiness, justice, love, among others. Although pristine Adam presumably reflected that essence quite well, the Fall resulted in a barrier that man cannot cross, apart from divine assistance. I suspect that modern man is but a poor reflection of what pristine Adam once was (due to accumulated congenital weaknesses). Alas, we fall far short!">o</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Peter 1:18–19; Titus 3:5–7; Colossians 1:14"> being justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus;</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Romans 3:23–24; 1 Peter 1:18–20; 1 John 4:10"> whom God has openly made available as a propitiation through the faith in His blood, to demonstrate His righteousness because of the passing over, in God's forbearance, of the previously committed sins;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="“Without the shedding of blood there is no remission” (Hebrews 9:22). “When I see the blood, I will pass over you” (Exodus 12:13, 22-23). “Behold! The Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!” (John 1:29). 1 Peter 1:18-20 states that the Lamb, with His blood shed, was so foreknown before the foundation of the world. All the animal blood shed in the Old Testament only covered sin; it did not pay for it. All those sacrifices looked forward to Christ's perfect sacrifice. But all of that was only validated by the death of God's Lamb. So as verse 25 here states, the true propitiation is now available “through the faith in His blood”. This propitiation also benefits the Old Testament saints.">p</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Deuteronomy 32:4; Romans 4:5; Romans 3:30"> to demonstrate His righteousness at this present time, so as to be just, Himself, and the justifier of him who is of the Faith of Jesus.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="I take the reference to be to the Faith that revolves around the person and work of Jesus.">q</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Romans 4:2; Romans 2:23; 1 Corinthians 1:29–31"> Where then is the boasting? It is excluded. By what kind of law, that of works? No, but by a law of faith.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Galatians 2:16; Galatians 3:8; Titus 3:7"> Therefore we conclude that a person is justified by faith apart from works of law.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Colossians 3:11; Ephesians 3:6; Zechariah 2:11"> Or is God for Jews only? Is He not also for Gentiles? Yes, also for Gentiles,</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Galatians 3:8; Galatians 3:20; Philippians 3:3"> since indeed there is only one God, who will justify the circumcised by faith and the uncircumcised through the Faith.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="How could a ritual performed on a baby justify that baby? A circumcised person had to demonstrate faith in God by shedding animal blood to cover his sin; this before the perfect Sacrifice. Now justification comes through the Faith.">r</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Galatians 2:21; Matthew 5:17; Romans 13:8–10"> Do we then nullify law through the Faith? Of course not! Rather, we uphold law.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-s" id="fnref-s" data-fn="The proper function of the law is recognized within the Faith.">s</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>
