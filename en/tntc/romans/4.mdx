## Abraham

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Isaiah 51:2; Romans 4:16; 2 Corinthians 11:22"> What then shall we say that our father Abraham discovered, as a man?</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:31; 2 Corinthians 11:30; Philippians 3:9"> If Abraham was really justified by works, he has a boast—but not before God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Genesis 15:6; James 2:23; Galatians 3:6–8"> So what does the Scripture say? “Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="See Genesis 15:6.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Romans 11:6; Romans 9:32; Romans 11:35"> Now to him who works, the pay is not counted as a gift but as an obligation.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Philippians 3:9; Romans 3:22; Titus 3:3–7"> But to him who does not work<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="We work because we have been justified, not in order to be justified (Ephesians 2:10). See further James 2:21-23—a living faith does things.">b</a></sup>but believes on Him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is credited as righteousness.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Timothy 1:9; Philippians 3:9; 1 Corinthians 1:30"> Just as David speaks of the blessing of the man to whom God credits righteousness apart from works:</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Psalms 32:1–2; Psalms 130:3–4; Luke 7:47–50"> <span className="pl pl-1">“Blessed are they whose lawless deeds have been forgiven,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">and whose sins have been covered up.</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Psalms 32:2; 1 Peter 2:24; 2 Corinthians 5:19–20"> <span className="pl pl-1">Blessed is the man to whom the Lord will not impute sin!”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="See Psalm 32:1-2. The negative is emphasized.">c</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Before circumcision

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Galatians 3:14; Colossians 3:11; Galatians 3:26–28"> Is this blessing then only for the circumcised, or also for the uncircumcised? Since we say that faith was credited to Abraham as righteousness,</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Galatians 6:15; Galatians 5:6; Genesis 17:10"> how then was it credited? After he was circumcised or while still uncircumcised? Not circumcised, but uncircumcised!</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Romans 3:22; Luke 19:9; 2 Corinthians 1:22"> And he received the sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness from his faith while still uncircumcised, so that he might be the father of all those who believe while uncircumcised, in order that this righteousness may be credited to them also,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="We non-Jews may wonder why Paul is spending so much time on the subject of circumcision, but to the Jews of that day it was BIG.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Song of Solomon 1:8; John 8:39–40; Proverbs 2:20"> and the father of the circumcised—not only those who are circumcised, but especially those who follow in the footsteps of the faith of our father Abraham while still uncircumcised.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="The point is that physical circumcision is not enough; one must believe.">e</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## The promise

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Galatians 3:29; Genesis 22:17–18; Genesis 28:14"> Now the promise that he would be the heir of the world<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Now what might ‘heir of the world’ mean? “In you all the families of the earth shall be blessed” (Genesis 12:3). Galatians 3:16 makes clear that the Seed, singular, refers to the Christ (Genesis 12:7 and 24:7). Psalm 2:7-8 also clearly refers to the Christ: “I will declare the decree: the Lord has said to Me, ‘You are My Son, today I have begotten You. Ask of Me, and I will give You the nations for your inheritance, and the ends of the earth for your possession.’ ” It might be argued that in some sense this is happening through the Church, Christ's body—in Matthew 5:13-14 the Christ called His disciples “the salt of the earth” and “the light of the world”. Paul states, “all things are yours… and you are Christ's… and Christ is God's” (1 Corinthians 3:21-23). But during the millennial Messianic Kingdom the whole world will obey the Christ, who will rule the nations with ‘a rod of iron’ (Revelation 19:15).">f</a></sup>was not to Abraham, or his seed, through law, but through the righteousness of faith.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Galatians 3:18–24; Philippians 3:9; Romans 4:16"> For if those who are of law are heirs, the faith is made empty and the promise has been invalidated;</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Romans 5:13; Galatians 3:10; Romans 3:19–20"> because the law produces wrath, since where there is no law neither is there transgression.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="How can you cross a line that is not there? So until someone draws a line (law) there is no transgression.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Romans 3:24–26; Ephesians 2:8; Galatians 3:22"> For this reason it is of faith so that it may be according to grace, in order that the promise be guaranteed to all the seed—not only to those of law, but also to those of the faith of Abraham, who is the father of us all;</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:28; Genesis 17:4–5; John 5:21"> just as it is written: “I have made you a father of many ethnic nations”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="See Genesis 17:5.">h</a></sup>—in the presence of Him whom he believed: God, who gives life to the dead and calls the nonexistent things as though they did exist.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="God can create just by speaking: if He says, “Let there be…”, there is.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Romans 8:24; Genesis 15:5–6; Acts 27:25"> Contrary to hope, Abraham in hope believed, so as to become a father of many ethnic nations, according to what had been spoken: “So shall your seed be.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="See Genesis 15:5.">j</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Hebrews 11:11–19; Romans 4:20–21; Genesis 17:17"> And not being weak in faith, he did not<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Perhaps 3% of the Greek manuscripts (of objectively inferior quality) omit “not”, to be followed by NIV, NASB, TEV, LB, etc.—an inferior proceeding.">k</a></sup>consider his own body, already dead<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Paul declares that Abraham had become impotent before begetting Isaac. That means that he had started slowing down long before, and no one would be more aware of that than Sarah. That may explain why she offered Hagar—she was aware of the Promise and Abraham needed a descendant.">l</a></sup>(being about a hundred years old), or the deadness of Sarah's womb.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Luke 1:45; 1 Corinthians 16:13; Daniel 10:19"> He did not waver at God's promise in unbelief; rather, he was strengthened by the faith, giving glory to God,</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Luke 1:37; Jeremiah 32:27; Genesis 18:14"> being fully convinced that what He had promised He was also able to perform<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="“Being fully convinced” is a basic ingredient in true faith.">m</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Romans 4:6; Romans 4:3"> —that is why it was credited to him as righteousness.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Corinthians 10:11; Romans 15:4; 1 Corinthians 10:6"> Now it was not only for his sake that it was written that it was credited to him,</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 2:24; 1 Peter 1:21; Acts 13:30"> but for our sake also, to whom it will be credited, we who believe on Him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead,</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="1 John 4:9–10; Isaiah 53:5–6; 2 Corinthians 5:21"> who was delivered up because of our transgressions, and was raised because of our justification.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="The grammatical construction is the same in both clauses: ‘because’, ‘because’. The resurrection was the proof that the Father had accepted the price paid for our justification. Thank you, Jesus!">n</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>
