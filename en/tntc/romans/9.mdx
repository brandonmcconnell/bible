<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Ephesians 2:12; Psalms 147:19; Romans 9:6"> the Israelites.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Wow, Paul, do you really mean it? ## Children of the promise

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Romans 2:28–29; Galatians 6:16; Romans 11:1–2"> However, it is not as though the Word of God has failed!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="The covenants and the promises involve human participation, so they only come to fruition for those who obey God (almost always a minority of the population).">c</a></sup>For not all who have descended from Israel are ‘Israel’.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Genesis 21:12; Hebrews 11:18; John 8:37–39"> Neither are they all ‘children’ because they are Abraham's descendants; but, “in Isaac will your seed be called.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="See Genesis 21:12.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="John 1:13; Romans 4:11–16; Galatians 3:26–29"> That is, it is not the physical children who are God's ‘children’, but the children of the promise are regarded as ‘seed’.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Genesis 18:10; Genesis 18:14; Genesis 17:21"> For this is the word of promise: “At this season I will come, and Sarah will have a son.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="See Genesis 18:10,14.">e</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Genesis 25:21–23; Luke 16:26; Romans 5:11"> Not only that, but also when Rebecca had conceived twins by our forefather Isaac</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Timothy 1:9; Ephesians 1:4–5; 2 Peter 1:10"> (though they had not yet been born, not having done anything good or bad, so that God's purpose according to election might stand, not of works but of Him who calls),</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Genesis 25:22–23; 2 Samuel 8:14; 1 Kings 22:47"> it was said to her, “The older will serve the younger.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="See Genesis 25:23.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Malachi 1:2–3; Deuteronomy 21:15; Genesis 29:33"> As it is written: “Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="See Malachi 1:2,3. Of course Malachi was written long after the two had demonstrated their characters by their lives. Further, the ‘hated’ is not an active hate, but a disregard.">g</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## It is pointless to fight God's Sovereignty!

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Chronicles 19:7; Psalms 145:17; Deuteronomy 32:4"> So what shall we say, there is no injustice with God, is there? Of course not!</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Exodus 33:19; Romans 9:16; Micah 7:18"> For He says to Moses:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“I will have mercy on whomever I have mercy,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">and I will have compassion on</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">whomever I have compassion.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="See Exodus 33:19.">h</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Ephesians 2:8; Matthew 11:25–26; 1 Peter 2:9–10"> So then, it is not of him who wills nor of him who strives, but of God who shows mercy.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Exodus 9:16; Proverbs 16:4; Exodus 14:17–18"> For the Scripture says to Pharaoh: “I raised you up for this very purpose, that I might display my power in you, and that my name might be proclaimed in all the earth.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="See Exodus 9:16. If you check the record, Pharaoh hardened his own heart the first five times; after that God did the hardening.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Joshua 11:20; Exodus 4:21; Romans 9:15–16"> So then, He has mercy on whom He wishes, and He hardens whom He wishes.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Daniel 4:35; 2 Chronicles 20:6; 1 Corinthians 15:12"> You will say to me then, “Why does He still find fault? For who has ever resisted His will?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Isaiah 29:16; Isaiah 45:9–11; Isaiah 64:8"> Really now, just who are you, O man, to talk back to God? What is formed will not say to the one who formed it, “Why did you make me like this,” will it?</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Isaiah 64:8; Proverbs 16:4; 2 Timothy 2:20–21"> Or has the potter no right over the clay, to make from the same lump one vessel for honor and another for dishonor?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="We can argue, and complain, and squirm, but after everything is said and done, when faced with God's Sovereignty we have just two options: rebel or submit, giving due regard to the consequences. That said, however, this text is concentrating on God's Sovereignty. Due regard must also be given to all the texts that deal with human responsibility.">j</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Proverbs 16:4; Jude 1:4; Romans 9:21"> What if God, wishing to display His wrath and to make His power known, endured with much patience vessels of wrath prepared for destruction,</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Romans 2:4; 1 Peter 1:2–5; Colossians 1:27"> just in order to make known the riches of His glory on vessels of mercy,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Presumably by way of contrast.">k</a></sup>which He prepared beforehand for glory,</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Colossians 3:11; Acts 13:47–48; Ephesians 3:6–8"> even us whom He called, not only from the Jews but also from the Gentiles?</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Hosea 2:23; 1 Peter 2:10; John 16:27"> As He also says in Hosea:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“I will call the ‘not my people’, ‘my people’,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">and the ‘not loved’ (f), ‘loved’.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="See Hosea 2:23.">l</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Hosea 1:9–10; 1 John 3:1–3; Galatians 3:26"> <span className="pl pl-1">“And it will be in the place where it was said to them,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">‘You are not my people,’</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">there they will be called sons of the living God.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="See Hosea 1:10.">m</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Isaiah 10:20–23; Romans 11:4–6; Isaiah 1:9"> And Isaiah cries out concerning Israel:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“Though the number of the children of Israel be as the sand of the sea, only the remnant will be saved;</span><br /></span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Isaiah 10:23; Revelation 19:11; Acts 17:31"> <span className="pl pl-1">because He is finishing and cutting short an accounting in righteousness; yes, the Lord will impose a condensed accounting</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="“In righteousness; yes, the Lord will impose a condensed accounting” is omitted by less than 3% of the Greek manuscripts, to be followed by NIV, NASB, TEV, LB, etc.—an inferior proceeding.">n</a></sup><br /><span className="pl pl-1">upon the earth.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="See Isaiah 10:22-23. It has always been the case that those who really want to walk with God represent a small minority of the population sample.">o</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Isaiah 1:9; Isaiah 13:19; Amos 4:11"> And as Isaiah had said previously:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“Unless the Lord of Sabaoth had left us a seed,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">we would have become like Sodom,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">and we would have been made like Gomorrah.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="See Isaiah 1:9. To be like Sodom would be complete destruction.">p</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Their present condition

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Philippians 3:9; Hebrews 11:7; Galatians 2:16"> So what shall we say? That Gentiles, who did not pursue righteousness, attained to righteousness, namely the righteousness that comes from faith;</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Romans 11:7; Romans 3:20; James 2:10–11"> while Israel, pursuing a standard of righteousness, did not attain to that standard.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:23; 1 Peter 2:8; Luke 7:23"> Why not? Because it was not from faith, but as from works of the law. They stumbled over the ‘stumbling stone’;</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Isaiah 28:16; Isaiah 8:14–15; Romans 10:11"> as it is written:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“Look! I am placing in Zion a stumbling stone</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">and a rock of offense,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">and no one who believes on Him will be put to shame.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="See Isaiah 8:14 and 28:16. They had to ‘believe on’ the ‘stumbling block’, the Christ, but He did not perform the way they expected that the Messiah would perform.">q</a></sup></span>
</div>
