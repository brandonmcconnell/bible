## Abraham’s Faith Counted as Righteousness

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Isaiah 51:2; Romans 4:16; 2 Corinthians 11:22"> What then shall we say that Abraham, our ancestor according to the flesh, has found?</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:31; 2 Corinthians 11:30; Philippians 3:9"> For if Abraham was justified by works, he has something to boast about, but not before God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Genesis 15:6; James 2:23; Galatians 3:6–8"> For what does the scripture say? “And Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him for righteousness.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="A quotation from Gen 15:6">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Romans 11:6; Romans 9:32; Romans 11:35"> Now to the one who works, his pay is not credited according to grace, but according to his due.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Philippians 3:9; Romans 3:22; Titus 3:3–7"> But to the one who does not work, but who believes in the one who justifies the ungodly, his faith is credited for righteousness,</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Timothy 1:9; Philippians 3:9; 1 Corinthians 1:30"> just as David also speaks about the blessing of the person to whom God credits righteousness apart from works:</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Psalm 32:1–2; Psalm 130:3–4; Luke 7:47–50"> <span className="pl pl-1">“Blessed are they whose lawless deeds have been forgiven,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-2">and whose sins are covered over.</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Psalm 32:2; 1 Peter 2:24; 2 Corinthians 5:19–20"> <span className="pl pl-1">Blessed is the person against whom the Lord will never count sin.”</span><sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="A quotation from Ps 32:1–2">b</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Galatians 3:14; Colossians 3:11; Galatians 3:26–28"> Therefore, is this blessing for ⸤those who are circumcised⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Literally “the circumcision”">c</a></sup>, or also for ⸤those who are uncircumcised⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Literally “the uncircumcision”">d</a></sup>? For we say, “Faith was credited to Abraham for righteousness.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="A quotation from Gen 15:6">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Galatians 6:15; Galatians 5:6; Genesis 17:10"> How then was it credited? While he was ⸤circumcised⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Literally “in circumcision”">f</a></sup>or ⸤uncircumcised⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Literally “in uncircumcision”">g</a></sup>? Not ⸤while circumcised⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Literally “in circumcision”">h</a></sup>but ⸤while uncircumcised⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Literally “in uncircumcision”">i</a></sup>!</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Romans 3:22; Luke 19:9; 2 Corinthians 1:22"> And he received the sign of circumcision as a seal<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Or “confirmation”">j</a></sup>of the righteousness by faith which he had ⸤while uncircumcised⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Literally “in uncircumcision”">k</a></sup>, so that he could be the father of all who believe ⸤although they are uncircumcised⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Literally “through uncircumcision”">l</a></sup>, so that righteousness could be credited to them,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Some manuscripts have “could be credited to them also”">m</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Song of Solomon 1:8; John 8:39–40; Proverbs 2:20"> and the father ⸤of those who are circumcised⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Literally “of the circumcision”">n</a></sup>to those who are not only from the circumcision, but who also follow in the footsteps of the faith of our father Abraham ⸤which he had while uncircumcised⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="Literally “of the in uncircumcision faith of our father Abraham”">o</a></sup>.</span>
</div>
</div>

## The Promise to Abraham Secured through Faith

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Galatians 3:29; Genesis 22:17–18; Genesis 28:14"> For the promise to Abraham or to his descendants, that he would be heir of the world, was not through the law, but through the righteousness by faith.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Galatians 3:18–24; Philippians 3:9; Romans 4:16"> For if those of the law are heirs, faith is rendered void and the promise is nullified.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Romans 5:13; Galatians 3:10; Romans 3:19–20"> For the law produces wrath, but where there is no law, neither is there transgression.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Romans 3:24–26; Ephesians 2:8; Galatians 3:22"> Because of this, it is by faith, in order that it may be according to grace, so that the promise may be secure to all the descendants, not only to those of the law, but also to those of the faith of Abraham, who is the father of us all</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:28; Genesis 17:4–5; John 5:21"> (just as it is written, “I have made you the father of many nations”)<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="A quotation from Gen 17:5">p</a></sup>before God, in whom he believed, the one who makes the dead alive and who calls the things that are not as though they are,</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Romans 8:24; Genesis 15:5–6; Acts 27:25"> who against hope believed in hope, so that he became the father of many nations, according to what was said, “so will your descendants be.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="A quotation from Gen 15:5">q</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Hebrews 11:11–19; Romans 4:20–21; Genesis 17:17"> And not being weak in faith, he considered his own body as good as dead,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="Some manuscripts have “already as good as dead”">r</a></sup>because he was approximately a hundred years old, and the deadness of Sarah’s womb.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Luke 1:45; 1 Corinthians 16:13; Daniel 10:19"> And he did not waver in unbelief at the promise of God, but was strengthened in faith, giving glory to God</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Luke 1:37; Jeremiah 32:27; Genesis 18:14"> and being fully convinced that what he had promised, he was also able to do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Romans 4:6; Romans 4:3"> Therefore<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-s" id="fnref-s" data-fn="Some manuscripts have “Therefore, indeed,”">s</a></sup>it was credited to him for righteousness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Corinthians 10:11; Romans 15:4; 1 Corinthians 10:6"> But it was not written for the sake of him alone that it was credited to him,</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 2:24; 1 Peter 1:21; Acts 13:30"> but also for the sake of us to whom it is going to be credited, to those who believe in the one who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead,</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="1 John 4:9–10; Isaiah 53:5–6; 2 Corinthians 5:21"> who was handed over on account of our trespasses, and was raised up in the interest of our justification.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-t" id="fnref-t" data-fn="Or “vindication”; or “acquittal”">t</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>
