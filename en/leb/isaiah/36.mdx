## Sennacherib Threatens Jerusalem

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Chronicles 32:1; 2 Kings 18:13; 2 Kings 18:17"> And this happened: In ⸤the fourteenth year⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally “four ten year”">a</a></sup>of King Hezekiah, Sennacherib king of Assyria went up against all the fortified cities of Judah, and he captured them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Chronicles 32:9–23; Isaiah 7:3; 2 Kings 18:17–37"> And the king of Assyria sent Rabshakeh<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Rabshekah is the title of a high Assyrian official">b</a></sup>from Lachish to Jerusalem, to King Hezekiah, with a large army, and he stood by the conduit of the upper pool on the highway of the field of the washer.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:16–17; Isaiah 22:15–21; 2 Samuel 20:24–25"> And Eliakim son of Hilkiah, who ⸤was in charge of the palace⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Literally “was over the house”">c</a></sup>came out to him, and Shebna the secretary, and Joah son of Asaph, the reminder.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Kings 18:19–37; Jude 1:16; Proverbs 16:18"> And Rabshakeh said to them, “Now say to Hezekiah, ‘Thus says the great king, the king of Assyria: “What is this confidence in which you trust?</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Kings 18:7; Jeremiah 52:3; Proverbs 21:30–31"> I said, ‘Only a word of lips! ⸤War has power and a plan⸥!’<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="The Hebrew here is awkward; literally “Plan and power for war”">d</a></sup>Now, in whom do you trust, that you have rebelled against me?</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Ezekiel 29:6–7; Jeremiah 37:5–8; 2 Kings 18:21"> Look, you trust in the staff of this broken reed, on Egypt, which if a man leans on it, goes into his hand and bores through it! Such is Pharaoh, king of Egypt, to all those who trust in him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Kings 18:4–5; Psalm 42:10–11; Psalm 42:5"> And if you say to me, ‘We trust in Yahweh our God,’ was it not he whose high places and altars Hezekiah removed? And he said to Judah and to Jerusalem, ‘You shall bow down in the ⸤presence⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Literally “face”">e</a></sup>of this altar.’ ”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Samuel 17:40–43; 1 Kings 20:10; Nehemiah 4:2–5"> And now please make a wager with my master the king of Assyria, and I will give you two thousand horses, that is, if you are able ⸤put⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Literally “give”">f</a></sup>riders for yourself on them!</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Isaiah 10:8; Isaiah 30:16–17; Isaiah 36:6"> But how can you drive back ⸤one governor among the least of my master’s servants⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Literally “the face of the governor of the one of the insignificant servants of my master”">g</a></sup>when<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Or “and”">h</a></sup>you trust in Egypt for chariots<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Hebrew “chariot”">i</a></sup>and horsemen?</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Kings 18:25; Isaiah 10:5–7; 1 Kings 13:18"> And now was it without Yahweh that I have come up against this land to destroy it? Yahweh said to me, “Go up against this land and destroy it!” ’ ”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ezra 4:7; Daniel 2:4; 2 Kings 18:26–27"> And Eliakim, Shebna, and Joah said to Rabshakeh, “Please speak to your servants in Aramaic, for we can understand<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Or “hear”">j</a></sup>it, and you must not speak to us in Judean in the ⸤hearing⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Literally “ear”">k</a></sup>of the people who are on the wall.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Kings 18:27; Leviticus 26:29; Lamentations 4:9–10"> But<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Or “And”">l</a></sup>Rabshakeh said, “Has my master sent me to speak these words to your masters and you? Was it not for the people who sit on the wall, to eat their dung and drink their urine<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="So Masoretic Hebrew text (Kethib ); the reading tradition (Qere ) has “feet-water”">m</a></sup>with you?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Chronicles 32:18; Psalm 73:8–9; 2 Kings 18:28–32"> Then<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Or “And”">n</a></sup>Rabshakeh stood and called in a great voice in Judean and said, “Hear the words of the great king, the king of Assyria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Thessalonians 2:4; Revelation 13:5–6; Daniel 6:20"> Thus says the king: ‘Do not let Hezekiah deceive you, for he will not be able to deliver you!</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Isaiah 37:23–24; Matthew 27:43; Psalm 71:9–11"> And do not let Hezekiah make you rely on Yahweh, saying, “Surely Yahweh will deliver us; this city will not be given into the hand of the king of Assyria!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Kings 4:25; Zechariah 3:10; Micah 4:4"> You must not listen to Hezekiah, for thus says the king of Assyria: “Make a blessing<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="That is, a gesture of surrender">o</a></sup>with me, and come out to me, and each one will eat from his vine and from his fig tree and drink water from<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="Or “of”">p</a></sup>his cistern,</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Kings 18:32; Deuteronomy 8:7–9; Job 20:17"> until ⸤I come⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="Literally “my coming”">q</a></sup>and take you to a land like your land, a land of grain and new wine, a land of bread and vineyards,</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Isaiah 37:17–18; Isaiah 36:15; Isaiah 37:10"> lest Hezekiah mislead you, saying, ‘Yahweh will save us!’ Did the gods of the nations each save his land from the hand of the king of Assyria?</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Isaiah 10:9–11; Jeremiah 49:23; 2 Samuel 8:9"> Where are the gods of Hamath and Arpad? Where are the gods of Sepharvaim? Indeed, have they delivered Samaria from my hand?</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Psalm 50:21; 2 Chronicles 32:19; Isaiah 37:23–29"> Who are there among all the gods of these countries who have saved their land from my hand, that Yahweh should save Jerusalem from my hand?” ’ ”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Proverbs 26:4; Psalm 38:13–15; Matthew 7:6"> But<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="Or “And”">r</a></sup>they were silent and did not answer him a word, for the command of the king was, “You must not answer him.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Isaiah 36:3; Isaiah 33:7; Matthew 26:65"> Then<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-s" id="fnref-s" data-fn="Or “And”">s</a></sup>Eliakim son of Hilkiah, who was over the ⸤palace⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-t" id="fnref-t" data-fn="Literally “house”">t</a></sup>Shebna the secretary, and Joah son of Asaph, the reminder, came to Hezekiah with torn garments and told him the words of Rabshakeh.</span>
</div>
</div>
