## The Final Invasion of Nebuchadnezzar

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:4–11; 1 Chronicles 6:15; Jeremiah 34:1–6"> It happened that in the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, on the tenth of the month, Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon came, he and his army, against Jerusalem. He encamped against it and built siege works against it all around.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2"> So the city came under siege until the eleventh year of the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:6; Ezekiel 5:12; Jeremiah 38:2"> In the ninth month, the famine became severe in the city, and there was no food for the people of the land.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Ezekiel 33:21; 2 Kings 25:5; Jeremiah 39:2–7"> Then the city was breached, and all of the men of war entered by night by way of the gate between the wall which was by the garden of the king, and the Chaldeans were against the city all around, so he<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="That is, Zedekiah">a</a></sup>left by the way of the Arabah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Isaiah 30:16; Jeremiah 39:5; Amos 2:14–16"> But the army of the Chaldeans pursued the king, and they overtook him in the Arabah of Jericho, and all of his army scattered from him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Kings 23:33; Jeremiah 34:21–22; Jeremiah 21:7"> So they captured the king and brought him up to the king of Babylon at Riblah, and ⸤they passed sentence on him⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Literally “they spoke justice with him”">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Jeremiah 32:4–5; Judges 16:21; Jeremiah 39:6–7"> They slaughtered the sons of Zedekiah before his eyes; then they blinded the eyes of Zedekiah and bound him in bronze fetters and brought him to Babylon.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Jerusalem Is Sacked and the Temple Burned

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:12–16; Jeremiah 40:1–4; Lamentations 4:12"> In the fifth month, on the seventh of the month, that is, the nineteenth year of King Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon, Nebuzaradan, a commander of the imperial guard, a servant of the king of Babylon, came to Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Amos 2:5; Psalm 74:3–7; 1 Kings 9:8"> He burned the temple of Yahweh, the palace of the king, and all of the houses of Jerusalem; every large house he burned with fire.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Nehemiah 1:3; Jeremiah 39:8; Jeremiah 52:14–23"> He and all the army of the Chaldeans who were with the imperial guard tore down the wall of Jerusalem all around.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ezekiel 5:2; Jeremiah 15:1–2; Jeremiah 39:9"> The remainder of the people left in the city, the deserters who had deserted to the king of Babylon, and the remainder of the multitude, Nebuzaradan the commander of the imperial guard deported.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Jeremiah 40:7; 2 Kings 24:14; Ezekiel 33:24"> But the poor of the land the commander of the imperial guard left for the vineyards and for tilling.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Plunder Taken by the Chaldeans

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Chronicles 36:18; Lamentations 1:10; 2 Kings 20:17"> The bronze pillars which were in the temple<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Or “house”">c</a></sup>of Yahweh, the water carts, and the bronze sea that was in the temple of Yahweh, the Chaldeans broke into pieces and carried their bronze to Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Exodus 27:3; 1 Kings 7:47–50; Exodus 38:3"> The pots, the shovels, the snuffers, the dishes, and the vessels of bronze with which they served there, they took.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Daniel 5:2–3; Numbers 7:13–14; 1 Kings 7:48–51"> The firepans and the basins, whatever was gold, the commander of the imperial guard took for the gold and whatever was silver, for the silver.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Kings 7:47"> The two pillars, the one sea, and the water cart which Solomon had made for the temple of Yahweh, there was no weighing to the bronze of all of these vessels.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Kings 7:15–22; Jeremiah 52:21–23"> The height of the one pillar was eighteen cubits; a bronze capital was on it, with the height of the capital being three cubits. The latticework and pomegranates on the capital all around were bronze, and likewise on the latticework for the second pillar.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Jeremiah 29:25; Ezra 7:1; Jeremiah 21:1"> Then the commander of the imperial guard took Seraiah the chief priest, Zephaniah the second priest, and three of the threshold keepers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Esther 1:14"> From the city he took one court official who was chief officer over the men of war, five men ⸤from the king’s council⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Literally “from those who saw the face of the king”">d</a></sup>who were found in the city, the secretary of the commander of the army who mustered the people of the land, and sixty men from the people of the land being found in the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Lamentations 4:16; Jeremiah 52:26–27; 2 Kings 23:33"> Nebuzaradan commander of the imperial guard took them and brought them to the king of Babylon at Riblah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Deuteronomy 28:64; 2 Kings 23:27; Deuteronomy 4:26"> Then the king of Babylon struck them down and killed them at Riblah in the land of Hamath; thus Judah was removed from its land.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Gedaliah Appointed Governor

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Jeremiah 39:14; 2 Kings 22:12; Jeremiah 41:2"> Now as far as the people left in Judah whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon left behind, he appointed Gedaliah the son of Ahikam the son of Shaphan over them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Jeremiah 40:7–9; Jeremiah 40:11–12; Joshua 18:26"> When all of the commanders of the troops heard, they and the men, that the king of Babylon had appointed Gedaliah, they came to Gedaliah at Mizpah, even Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, Johanan the son of Kareah, Seriah the son of Tanhumeth the Netophathite, Jaazaniah the son of the Maacathite, they and their men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Jeremiah 40:9–10; Jeremiah 43:6; 2 Samuel 19:23"> Gedaliah swore to them and to their men, and he said to them, “You must not be afraid because of the Chaldeans. Settle in the land and serve the king of Babylon, and may it go well with you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Zechariah 7:5; Zechariah 8:19; Jeremiah 40:15"> But it happened in the seventh month that Ishmael the son of Nethaniah the son of Elishama from the offspring of the kingship came, and ten men with him, and they struck down Gedaliah so that he died with the Judeans and with the Chaldeans who were with him at Mizpah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Jeremiah 43:4–7; Jeremiah 41:16–18; Jeremiah 42:14–22"> Then all the people, from youngest to oldest, and the commanders of the troops, went to Egypt, for they were afraid of the presence of the Chaldeans.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Elderly Jehoiachin Cared for in Babylon

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:31–34; Genesis 40:20; Genesis 40:13"> It happened in the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin king of Judah, in the twelfth month on the twenty-seventh of the month, lifted Evil-Merodach king of Babylon in the year that he became king, the head of Jehoiachin king of Judah from the house of imprisonment.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Daniel 2:37; Jeremiah 27:6–11; Daniel 5:18–19"> He spoke ⸤kindly⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Literally “good things”">e</a></sup>to him, and he gave him a better seat than the seat of the kings who were with him in Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="2 Samuel 9:7; Isaiah 61:3; Genesis 41:42"> So he changed the clothes of his imprisonment, and he ate food continually in his presence all the days of his life.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Luke 11:3; Matthew 6:11; Nehemiah 11:23"> His allowance was continually given to him from the king, ⸤a portion every day⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Literally “a thing of day on his day”">f</a></sup>all the days of his life.</span>
</div>
