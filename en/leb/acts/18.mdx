## Paul, Silas, and Timothy in Corinth

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:2; 2 Timothy 4:20; 2 Corinthians 1:1"> After these things he departed from Athens and went to Corinth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Romans 16:3–4; 1 Corinthians 16:19; 2 Timothy 4:19"> And he found a certain Jew ⸤named⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally “by name”">a</a></sup>Aquila, ⸤a native⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Literally “by nationality”">b</a></sup>of Pontus who had arrived recently from Italy ⸤along with⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Literally “and”">c</a></sup>Priscilla his wife, because Claudius had ordered all the Jews to depart from Rome, and he went to them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Corinthians 4:12; 1 Thessalonians 2:9; 2 Corinthians 11:9"> And because he was practicing the same trade, he stayed with them and worked, for they were tentmakers by trade.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Corinthians 5:11; Acts 17:17; Acts 28:23"> And he argued in the synagogue every Sabbath, attempting to persuade both Jews and Greeks.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Corinthians 5:14; Acts 18:28; Acts 20:21"> Now when both Silas and Timothy came down from Macedonia, Paul began to be occupied with the message, solemnly testifying to the Jews that the Christ<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Or “Messiah”">d</a></sup>was Jesus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Ezekiel 33:4; 2 Samuel 1:16; Ezekiel 33:8–9"> And when they resisted and reviled him, he shook out his clothes and said to them, “Your blood be on your own heads! I am guiltless! From now on I will go to the Gentiles!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Acts 16:14; Colossians 4:11; Acts 10:2"> And leaving there, he entered into the house of someone ⸤named⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Literally “by name”">e</a></sup>Titius Justus, a worshiper<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Or “a God-fearer”">f</a></sup>of God whose house was next door to the synagogue.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:13–17; Romans 10:14–17; Mark 16:15–16"> And Crispus, the ruler of the synagogue, believed in the Lord together with his whole household. And many of the Corinthians, when they heard about it, believed and were baptized.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 23:11; Ephesians 6:19–20; Acts 27:23–25"> And the Lord said to Paul by a vision in the night, “Do not be afraid, but speak and do not keep silent,</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Corinthians 12:9; Exodus 4:12; Isaiah 41:10"> because I am with you and no one will attack you to harm you, because many people are mine in this city.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 14:3; Acts 19:10; Acts 20:31"> So he stayed a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Paul Accused Before the Proconsul Gallio

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 13:50; Acts 18:27; Acts 13:7"> Now when Gallio was proconsul of Achaia, the Jews rose up with one purpose against Paul and brought him before the judgment seat,</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Acts 25:8; Acts 24:5–6; Acts 6:13"> saying, “This man is persuading people to worship God contrary to the law!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="1 Peter 3:14–15; Acts 25:11; Mark 9:19"> But when Paul was about to open his mouth, Gallio said to the Jews, “If it was some crime or wicked villainy, O Jews, ⸤I would have been justified in accepting⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Literally “with respect to a motive I would have accepted”">g</a></sup>your complaint.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Acts 23:29; Acts 25:19; 1 Timothy 6:4"> But if it is questions concerning a word and names and ⸤your own law⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Literally “the according to you law”">h</a></sup>see to it yourselves! I do not wish to be a judge of these things.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Revelation 12:16; Psalm 76:10; Romans 13:3–4"> And he drove them away from the judgment seat.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:1; Amos 6:6; Acts 17:32"> So they all seized Sosthenes, the ruler of the synagogue, and began beating him in front of the judgment seat. And none of these things was a concern to Gallio.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Paul Returns to Antioch in Syria

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Acts 21:24; Numbers 6:18; Numbers 6:5–9"> So Paul, after remaining many days longer, said farewell to the brothers and sailed away to Syria, and with him Priscilla and Aquila. He shaved his head at Cenchrea, because he had taken a vow.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Ephesians 1:1; 1 Timothy 1:3; 2 Timothy 1:18"> So they arrived at Ephesus, and those he left behind there, but he himself entered into the synagogue and discussed with the Jews.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Mark 1:37–38; 1 Corinthians 16:12; Acts 20:16"> And when they asked him to stay for a longer time, he did not give his consent,</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Corinthians 4:19; James 4:15; Romans 15:32"> but saying farewell and telling them, “I will return to you again if God wills,” he set sail from Ephesus.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 8:40; Acts 15:4; Acts 23:23"> And when he arrived at Caesarea, he went up and greeted the church, and went down to Antioch.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Acts 14:22; Acts 16:6; Acts 15:41"> And after spending some time there, he departed, traveling through one place after another in the Galatian region and Phrygia, strengthening all the disciples.</span>
</div>
</div>

## The Early Ministry of Apollos

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Corinthians 4:6; Titus 3:13; 1 Corinthians 3:5–6"> Now a certain Jew ⸤named⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Literally “by name”">i</a></sup>Apollos, ⸤a native⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Literally “by nationality”">j</a></sup>Alexandrian, arrived in Ephesus—an eloquent man who was well-versed in the scriptures.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Acts 19:3; Romans 12:11; Hosea 14:9"> This man had been instructed in the way of the Lord, and being enthusiastic in spirit, he was speaking and teaching accurately the things about Jesus, although he knew only the baptism of John.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 18:25; Acts 14:3; 1 Corinthians 12:21"> And he began to speak boldly in the synagogue, but when Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they took him aside and explained the way of God to him more accurately.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 Thessalonians 2:13–14; James 1:16–18; 1 Peter 1:2–3"> And when he wanted to cross over to Achaia, the brothers encouraged him and wrote to the disciples to welcome him. When he arrived, ⸤he⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Literally “who”">k</a></sup>assisted greatly those who had believed through grace.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Acts 18:5; 1 Corinthians 15:3–4; Acts 9:22"> For he was vigorously refuting the Jews in public, demonstrating through the scriptures that the Christ<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Or “Messiah”">l</a></sup>was Jesus.</span>
</div>
