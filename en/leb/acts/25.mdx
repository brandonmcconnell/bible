## Paul Appeals to Caesar

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 23:34; Acts 25:5; Acts 8:40"> Now when Festus set foot in the province, after three days he went up to Jerusalem from Caesarea.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Acts 25:15; Proverbs 4:16; Acts 24:1"> And the chief priests and the most prominent men of the Jews brought charges against Paul to him, and were urging him,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Acts 9:24; Acts 23:12–15; 1 Samuel 23:19–21"> asking for a favor against him, that he summon him to Jerusalem, because they were preparing an ambush to do away with him along the way.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Acts 24:23"> Then Festus replied that Paul was being kept at Caesarea, and he himself was about to go there in a short time.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Acts 25:25; John 18:29–30; Acts 25:18–19"> So he said, “Let those among you who are prominent go down with me, and if there is any wrong in the man, let them bring charges against him.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 25:17; Acts 25:10; Matthew 27:19"> And after he had stayed among them not more than eight or ten days, he went down to Caesarea. On the next day he sat down on the judgment seat and gave orders for Paul to be brought.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Acts 24:13; Luke 23:10; Acts 25:24"> And when he arrived, the Jews who had come down from Jerusalem stood around him, bringing many and serious charges that they were not able to prove,</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Acts 24:12; Acts 28:17; 2 Corinthians 1:12"> while Paul said in his defense, “Neither against the law of the Jews nor against the temple nor against Caesar have I sinned with reference to anything!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 24:27; Acts 12:3; Acts 25:20"> But Festus, because he wanted to do a favor for the Jews, answered and said to Paul, “Are you willing to go up to Jerusalem to be tried before me there concerning these things?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Matthew 27:18; 2 Corinthians 4:2; Acts 28:18"> But Paul said, “I am standing before the judgment seat of Caesar, where it is necessary for me to be judged. I have done no wrong to the Jews, as you also know very well.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 28:19; Acts 26:32; Acts 25:25"> If then I am doing wrong<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Or “I am in the wrong”">a</a></sup>and have done anything deserving death, I am not trying to avoid dying. But if there is nothing true of the things which these people are accusing me, no one can give me up to them. I appeal to Caesar!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 19:21; Philippians 1:12–14; Philippians 1:20"> Then Festus, after discussing this with his council, replied, “You have appealed to Caesar—to Caesar you will go!”</span>
</div>
</div>

## Festus Asks King Agrippa for Advice

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Acts 8:40; Acts 26:1; Acts 26:27–28"> Now after some days had passed, King Agrippa and Bernice arrived at Caesarea to welcome Festus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Acts 24:27"> And while they were staying there many days, Festus laid out the case against Paul to the king, saying, “There is a certain man left behind by Felix as a prisoner,</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Acts 25:1–3; Esther 3:9; Luke 18:3–5"> concerning whom when I was in Jerusalem the chief priests and the elders of the Jews presented evidence, asking for a sentence of condemnation against him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="John 7:51; Deuteronomy 19:17–18; Acts 23:30"> To ⸤them⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Literally “whom”">b</a></sup>I replied that it was not the custom of the Romans to give up any man before the one who had been accused met his accusers face to face and received an opportunity for a defense concerning the accusation.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Acts 25:6; Acts 25:10"> Therefore, when they had assembled here, I made no delay; on the next day I sat down on the judgment seat and gave orders for the man to be brought.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> When they stood up, his accusers began bringing no charge concerning ⸤him⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Literally “whom”">c</a></sup>of the evil deeds that I was suspecting,</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Acts 23:29; Acts 18:15; 1 Corinthians 15:3–4"> but they had some issues with him concerning their own religion, and concerning a certain Jesus, who was dead, whom Paul claimed to be alive.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Acts 25:9"> And because I was at a loss with regard to the investigation concerning these things, I asked if he was willing to go to Jerusalem and to be judged there concerning these things.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Timothy 4:16; Luke 2:1; Acts 25:10–11"> But when Paul appealed that he be kept under guard for the decision of His Majesty the Emperor, I gave orders for him to be kept under guard until I could send him to Caesar.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 9:15; Matthew 10:18; Isaiah 52:15"> So Agrippa said to Festus, “I want to hear the man myself also.” “Tomorrow,” he said, “you will hear him.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Acts 26:30; Acts 25:13; 1 John 2:16"> So on the next day, Agrippa and Bernice came with great pageantry and entered into the audience hall, along with military tribunes and the most prominent men of the city. And when Festus gave the order, Paul was brought in.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 22:22; Acts 25:7; Acts 25:2–3"> And Festus said, “King Agrippa and all who are present with us, you see this man about whom the whole population of the Jews appealed to me, both in Jerusalem and here, shouting that he must not live any longer.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Acts 23:29; Acts 25:11–12; Luke 23:4"> But I understood that he had done nothing deserving death himself, and when this man appealed to His Majesty the Emperor, I decided to send him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 26:2–3"> I do not have anything definite to write to my lord about ⸤him⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Literally “whom”">d</a></sup>Therefore I have brought him before you all—and especially before you, King Agrippa—so that after this preliminary hearing has taken place, I may have something to write.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="John 7:51; Proverbs 18:13"> For it seems unreasonable to me to send a prisoner and not to indicate the charges against him.”</span>
</div>
</div>
