## Paul and His Associates Sail for Rome

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 25:25; Acts 25:12; Acts 10:1"> And when it was decided that we would sail away to Italy, they handed over Paul and some other prisoners to a centurion ⸤named⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally “by name”">a</a></sup>Julius of the Augustan<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="The meaning and significance of the title “Augustan” is highly debated, as is the precise identification of this military unit; it may be an honorary unit designation given to auxiliary or provincial troops">b</a></sup>Cohort.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Acts 19:29; Philemon 1:24; Colossians 4:10"> And we went aboard a ship from Adramyttium that was about to sail to the places along the coast of Asia<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="A reference to the Roman province of Asia (modern Asia Minor)">c</a></sup>and put out to sea. Aristarchus, a Macedonian from Thessalonica, was with us.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Acts 24:23; Acts 28:16; Acts 27:43"> And on the next day, we put in at Sidon. And Julius, treating Paul kindly, allowed him to go to his friends ⸤to be cared for⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Literally “to experience care”">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Acts 4:36; Acts 15:39; Acts 13:4"> And from there we put out to sea and sailed under the lee of Cyprus, because the winds were against us.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Acts 6:9; Acts 13:13; Acts 15:41"> And after we had sailed across the open sea along Cilicia and Pamphylia, we put in at Myra in Lycia.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 28:11; Acts 27:1; Acts 6:9"> And there the centurion found an Alexandrian ship sailing for Italy and put us ⸤on board⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Literally “into”">e</a></sup>it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Acts 27:21; Titus 1:5; Titus 1:12"> And sailing slowly, in many days and with difficulty we came to Cnidus. Because the wind did not permit us to go further, we sailed under the lee of Crete off Salmone.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> And sailing along its coast with difficulty, we came to a certain place called Fair Havens, near which was the town of Lasea.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Numbers 29:7; Leviticus 23:27–29; Leviticus 16:29–31"> And because considerable time had passed and the voyage was now dangerous because even the Fast<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="A reference to the Jewish Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur) which occurs in mid-autumn">f</a></sup>was already over, Paul strongly recommended,</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Daniel 2:30; Acts 27:20–26; Acts 27:41–44"> saying to them, “Men, I perceive that the voyage is going ⸤to end⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Literally “to be”">g</a></sup>with disaster and great loss, not only of the cargo and the ship, but also of our lives!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ezekiel 33:4; Hebrews 11:7; Ezekiel 3:17–18"> But the centurion was convinced even more by the shipmaster and the shipowner than by what was said by Paul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Psalm 107:30; Acts 27:8"> And because the harbor was unsuitable for spending the winter in, the majority decided on a plan to put out to sea from there, if somehow they could arrive at Phoenix, a harbor of Crete facing toward the southwest and toward the northwest, to spend the winter there.</span>
</div>
</div>

## A Violent Storm at Sea

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Luke 12:55; Song of Solomon 4:16; Job 37:17"> And when a southwest wind began to blow gently, because they thought they could accomplish their purpose, they weighed anchor and sailed close along Crete.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Mark 4:37; Psalm 107:25–27; Exodus 14:21–27"> But not long afterward a wind like a hurricane, called the northeaster,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Literally “Euraquilo,” a violent northern wind">h</a></sup>rushed down from it.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="That is, from the island of Crete">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="James 3:4; Acts 27:27"> And when the ship was caught and was not able to head into the wind, we gave way and were driven along.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> And running under the lee of a certain small island called Cauda, we were able with difficulty to get the ship’s boat under control.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Acts 27:29; Acts 27:26; Acts 27:41"> After hoisting ⸤it up⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Literally “which”">j</a></sup>they made use of supports to undergird the ship. And because they were afraid lest they run aground on the Syrtis, they lowered the sea anchor and thus were driven along.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Jonah 1:5; Acts 27:38; Matthew 16:26"> And because we were violently battered by the storm, on the next day ⸤they began⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Literally “they began to carry out”">k</a></sup>jettisoning the cargo,</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Job 2:4; Luke 9:24–25; Mark 8:35–37"> and on the third day they threw overboard the gear of the ship with their own hands.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Matthew 24:29; Matthew 8:24–25; Jeremiah 2:25"> But when neither sun nor stars appeared for many days, and with not a little bad weather confronting us, finally all hope was abandoned that we would be saved.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Genesis 42:22; Psalm 107:5–6; Acts 27:7"> And because many were experiencing lack of appetite, at that time Paul stood up in their midst and said, “Men, you ought to have followed my advice not to put out to sea from Crete, and thus avoided this damage and loss!</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 27:25; Acts 27:36; 2 Corinthians 4:8–9"> And now I urge you to cheer up, for there will be no loss of life from among you, but only of the ship.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Acts 23:11; Romans 1:9; 2 Timothy 4:17"> For this night an angel of the God whose I am and whom I serve came to me,</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 23:11; 2 Timothy 4:16–17; Matthew 10:28"> saying, ‘Do not be afraid, Paul! It is necessary for you to stand before Caesar, and behold, God has graciously granted you all who are sailing with you.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Romans 4:20–21; Luke 1:45; Numbers 23:19"> Therefore keep up your courage, men, for I believe God that it will be like this—according to ⸤the⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Literally “which”">l</a></sup>way it was told to me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 28:1; Acts 27:17; Acts 27:29"> But it is necessary that we run aground on some island.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Jonah 1:6; Acts 27:30; Revelation 18:17"> And when the fourteenth night had come, as we were being driven in the Adriatic Sea about the middle of the night, the sailors suspected ⸤they were approaching some land⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Literally “some land was approaching them”">m</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> And taking soundings, they found twenty fathoms. So going on a little further and taking soundings again, they found fifteen fathoms.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Acts 27:17; Hebrews 6:19; Deuteronomy 28:67"> And because they were afraid lest somewhere we run aground against rough places, they threw down four anchors from the stern and prayed for day to come.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Acts 27:16; Acts 27:32"> And when the sailors were seeking to escape from the ship and were lowering the ship’s boat into the sea, pretending as if they were going to lay out anchors from the bow,</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 Thessalonians 2:13–14; Psalm 91:11–12; Jeremiah 29:11–13"> Paul said to the centurion and the soldiers, “Unless these men remain with the ship, you cannot be saved!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Philippians 3:7–9; Luke 16:8"> Then the soldiers cut away the ropes of the ship’s boat and let it fall away.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Or “let it drift away”">n</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33"> And until the day was about to come, Paul was urging them all to take some food, saying, “Today is the fourteenth day you have waited anxiously, and you have continued without eating, having taken nothing.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Matthew 10:30; 1 Kings 1:52; Luke 21:18"> Therefore I urge you to take some food, for this is necessary for your preservation. For not a hair from your head will be lost.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Matthew 15:36; 1 Corinthians 10:30–31; 1 Timothy 4:3–4"> And after he said these things and took bread, he gave thanks to God in front of them all, and after breaking it, he began to eat.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="2 Corinthians 1:4–6; Acts 27:22; Psalm 27:14"> So they all were encouraged and partook of food themselves.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="Acts 2:41; Romans 13:1; 1 Peter 3:20"> (Now we were in all two hundred seventy six persons on the ship.)</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="Jonah 1:5; Matthew 6:25; Acts 27:18–19"> And when they had eaten their fill of food, they lightened the ship by throwing the wheat<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="Or “grain”">o</a></sup>into the sea.</span>
</div>
</div>

## The Shipwreck

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Acts 28:1"> Now when day came, they did not recognize the land, but they noticed a certain bay having a beach, onto which they decided to run the ship ashore if they could.</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="Isaiah 33:23; Acts 27:29–30"> And slipping the anchors, they left them in the sea, at the same time loosening the ropes<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="Or “bands” (referring to the linkage that tied the steering oars together)">p</a></sup>of the steering oars. And hoisting the foresail to the wind that was blowing, they held course for the beach.</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="2 Corinthians 11:25–26; Ezekiel 27:26; Acts 27:26–29"> But falling into a place of crosscurrents,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="Or “a reef”; literally “a place of two seas,” an expression of uncertain meaning but most likely a nautical technical term for some adverse sea condition">q</a></sup>they ran the ship aground. And the bow stuck fast and stayed immovable, but the stern was being broken up by the violence.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="Some manuscripts have “the violence of the waves”">r</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="Mark 15:15–20; Ecclesiastes 9:3; Psalm 74:20"> Now the plan of the soldiers was that they would kill the prisoners lest any escape by swimming away,</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43" data-xref="Acts 27:3; Proverbs 16:7; Acts 27:11"> but the centurion, because he wanted to save Paul, prevented them ⸤from doing what they intended⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-s" id="fnref-s" data-fn="Literally “of the intention”">s</a></sup>and gave orders that those who were able to swim should jump in first to get to the land,</span>
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44" data-xref="Acts 27:22; Psalm 107:28–30; John 6:39–40"> and then the rest, some of whom floated on planks and some of whom on anything that was from the ship. And in this way all were brought safely to the land.</span>
</div>
</div>
