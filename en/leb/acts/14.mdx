## Preaching in Iconium

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 18:4; Acts 13:51; Acts 13:46"> Now it happened that in Iconium they entered ⸤together⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally “according to the same”">a</a></sup>into the synagogue of the Jews and spoke in such a way that a large number of both Jews and Greeks believed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Acts 13:50; Acts 21:27–30; Acts 14:19"> But the Jews who were disobedient stirred up and ⸤poisoned the minds⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Literally “embittered the souls”">b</a></sup>of the Gentiles against the brothers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Hebrews 2:4; Ephesians 6:18–20; Acts 20:32"> So they stayed there for a considerable time, speaking boldly for the Lord, who testified to the message of his grace, granting signs and wonders to be performed through their hands.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Acts 28:24; Acts 14:14; Luke 11:21–23"> But the population of the city was divided, and ⸤some⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Literally “those on the one hand”">c</a></sup>were with the Jews ⸤and some⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Literally “those on the other hand”">d</a></sup>with the apostles.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Acts 14:19; 2 Timothy 3:11; Acts 17:5"> So when an inclination took place on the part of both the Gentiles and the Jews, together with their rulers, to mistreat them and to stone them,</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 14:20–21; Matthew 10:23; 2 Timothy 3:11"> they became aware of it and fled to the Lycaonian cities—Lystra and Derbe and the surrounding region.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Acts 14:21; Acts 16:10; 1 Thessalonians 2:2"> And there they were continuing to proclaim the good news.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Mistaken for Gods in Lystra

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Acts 3:2; John 9:1–2; John 5:3–5"> And in Lystra a certain man was sitting powerless in his feet, lame from ⸤birth⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Literally “his mother’s womb”">e</a></sup>who had never walked.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 3:4; Matthew 15:28; Matthew 9:28–29"> This man listened while Paul was speaking. ⸤Paul⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Literally “who”">f</a></sup>looking intently at him and seeing that he had faith to be healed,</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Isaiah 35:6; John 5:8–9; John 14:12"> said with a loud voice, “Stand upright on your feet!” And he leaped up and began walking.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 28:6; Acts 8:10; Acts 12:22"> And when the crowds saw what Paul had done, they raised their voices in the Lycaonian language, saying, “The gods have become like men and have come down to us!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 19:35"> And they began calling Barnabas Zeus and Paul Hermes, because he was the ⸤principal speaker⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Literally “leader of the message”">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Daniel 2:46; Acts 10:25"> And the priest of the temple of Zeus that was just outside the city brought bulls and garlands to the gates and was wanting to offer sacrifice, along with the crowds.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Matthew 26:65; 1 Corinthians 9:5–6; 2 Kings 5:7"> But when the apostles Barnabas and Paul heard about it, they tore their clothing and rushed out into the crowd, shouting</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Acts 10:26; 1 Corinthians 8:4; James 5:17"> and saying, “Men, why are you doing these things? We also are men with the same nature as you, proclaiming the good news that you should turn from these worthless things to the living God, who made the heaven and the earth and the sea and all the things that are in them—</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Acts 17:30; Psalm 81:12; Romans 1:28"> who in generations that are past permitted all the nations<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Or “Gentiles”; the same Greek word can be translated “nations” or “Gentiles” depending on the context">h</a></sup>to go their own ways.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Romans 1:19–20; Joel 2:23; Job 5:10"> And yet he did not leave himself without witness by doing good, giving you rain from heaven and fruitful seasons, satisfying you with food and your hearts with gladness.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Jeremiah 44:16–17; Genesis 19:9; Genesis 11:6"> And although they said these things, only with difficulty did they dissuade the crowds from offering sacrifice to them.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Corinthians 11:25; 2 Timothy 3:11; Acts 13:50–51"> But Jews arrived from Antioch and Iconium, and when they had won over the crowds and stoned Paul, they dragged him outside the city, thinking he was dead.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Corinthians 1:9–10; 2 Corinthians 6:9; Acts 14:6"> But after the disciples surrounded him, he got up and went into the city. And on the next day he departed with Barnabas for Derbe.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Paul and Barnabas Return to Antioch in Syria

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Acts 14:19; Acts 14:1; Acts 13:51"> And after they had proclaimed the good news in that city and made many disciples, they returned to Lystra and to Iconium and to Antioch,</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="1 Peter 5:10; 2 Timothy 3:12; John 16:33"> strengthening the souls of the disciples, encouraging them to continue in the faith and saying, “Through many persecutions<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Or “afflictions”">i</a></sup>it is necessary for us to enter into the kingdom of God.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Titus 1:5; Acts 11:30; James 5:14"> And when they had appointed elders for them in every church, after praying with fasting, they entrusted them to the Lord, in whom they had believed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 13:13–14; Acts 15:38"> And they passed through Pisidia and came to Pamphylia.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> And after they proclaimed the message in Perga, they went down to Attalia,</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 15:40; Acts 11:19; 2 Corinthians 1:12"> and from there they sailed away to Antioch where they had been commended to the grace of God for the work that they had completed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="1 Corinthians 16:9; Acts 15:12; Colossians 4:3"> And when they arrived and called the church together, they reported all that God had done with them, and that he had opened a door of faith for the Gentiles.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Or “nations”; the same Greek word can be translated “nations” or “Gentiles” depending on the context">j</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Acts 11:26; Acts 15:35"> And they stayed no little time with the disciples.</span>
</div>
</div>
