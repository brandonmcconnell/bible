## Paul Before the Sanhedrin

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 24:16; 2 Timothy 1:3; 2 Corinthians 1:12"> And looking intently at the Sanhedrin, Paul said, “Men and brothers, I have lived my life in all good conscience before God to this day.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="John 18:22; Acts 24:1; Micah 5:1"> So the high priest Ananias ordered those standing near him to strike his mouth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Deuteronomy 25:1–2; John 7:51; Matthew 23:27–28"> Then Paul said to him, “God is going to strike you, you whitewashed wall! And are you sitting there judging me according to the law, and acting contrary to the law do you order me to be struck?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> And those who stood nearby said, “Are you reviling the high priest of God?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Exodus 22:28; Ecclesiastes 10:20; Jude 1:8–9"> And Paul said, “I did not know, brothers, that he was high priest. For it is written, ‘You must not speak evil of a ruler of your people.’ ”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="A quotation from Exod 22:28">a</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 24:15; Acts 24:21; Acts 26:5–8"> Now when Paul realized that one part were Sadducees and the other Pharisees, he shouted out in the Sanhedrin, “Men and brothers! I am a Pharisee, a son of Pharisees! I am being judged concerning the hope and the resurrection of the dead!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Psalm 55:9; John 7:40–43; Matthew 10:34"> And when he said this, a dispute developed between the Pharisees and Sadducees, and the assembly was divided.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Luke 20:27; Matthew 22:23; Mark 12:18"> (For the Sadducees say there is no resurrection or angel or spirit, but the Pharisees acknowledge them all.)</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 22:7; Acts 22:17–18; John 12:29"> And there was loud shouting, and some of the scribes from the party of the Pharisees stood up and contended sharply, saying, “We find nothing wrong with this man! But what if a spirit or an angel has spoken to him?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="James 1:19; James 3:14–2; Acts 22:24"> And when the dispute became severe, the military tribune, fearing lest Paul be torn apart by them, ordered the detachment to go down, take him away from their midst, and bring him into the barracks.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Or “headquarters”">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 18:9; Acts 19:21; 2 Timothy 4:17"> And the next night the Lord stood by him and said, “Have courage, for as you have testified about me in Jerusalem, so you must also testify in Rome.”</span>
</div>
</div>

## A Conspiracy to Kill Paul

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 23:30; Isaiah 8:9–10; Acts 23:21"> And when it was day, the Jews made a conspiracy and bound themselves under a curse, saying they would<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Literally “saying neither to eat nor to drink”; the words “they would” are supplied for smoother English style">c</a></sup>neither eat nor drink until they had killed Paul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="John 16:2; 2 Samuel 15:31; 2 Samuel 15:12"> Now there were more than forty who had made this conspiracy,</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Acts 23:12; Jeremiah 8:12; Isaiah 3:9"> who went to the chief priests and the elders and said, “We have bound ourselves under a curse to partake of nothing until we have killed Paul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Isaiah 59:7; Psalm 37:32–33; Proverbs 1:16"> Therefore, now you along with the Sanhedrin explain to the military tribune that he should bring him down to you, as if you were going to determine more accurately the things concerning him. And we are ready to do away with him before he comes near.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Acts 23:10; 1 Corinthians 3:19; Acts 23:32"> But when the son of Paul’s sister heard about the ambush, he came and entered into the barracks<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Or “headquarters”">d</a></sup>and reported it to Paul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Proverbs 22:3; Matthew 8:8–9; Matthew 10:16"> So Paul called one of the centurions and said, “Bring this young man to the military tribune, because he has something to report to him.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Ephesians 3:1; Ephesians 4:1; Acts 28:17"> So he took him and brought him to the military tribune and said, “The prisoner Paul called me and asked me to bring this young man to you because he has something to tell you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Jeremiah 31:32; Esther 9:12; Esther 7:2"> And the military tribune, taking hold of his hand and withdrawing privately, asked, “What is it that you have to report to me?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Acts 23:14–15; Acts 23:1; Psalm 12:2"> And he said, “The Jews have agreed to ask you that you bring Paul down to the Sanhedrin tomorrow, as if they were going to inquire somewhat more accurately concerning him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Acts 23:12–14; Acts 14:5–6; 2 Corinthians 11:26"> You therefore do not be persuaded by them, because more than forty men of ⸤their number⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Literally “them”">e</a></sup>are lying in wait for him, who have bound themselves under a curse neither to eat nor to drink until they have done away with him. And now they are ready, waiting for ⸤you to agree⸥.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Literally “the assurance of agreement from you”">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Mark 1:44; Joshua 2:14"> So the military tribune sent the young man away, directing him, “Tell no one that you have revealed these things to me.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Matthew 14:25; Acts 23:33; Luke 12:38"> And he summoned two of the centurions and said, “Make ready from the third hour of the night two hundred soldiers and seventy horsemen and two hundred spearmen,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="A word of uncertain meaning, probably a military technical term">g</a></sup>in order that they may proceed as far as Caesarea.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 25:14; Acts 23:26; Acts 24:10"> And provide mounts so that they can put Paul on them and bring him safely to Felix the governor.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> ⸤He wrote⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Literally “writing”">h</a></sup>a letter that had this form:<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Or “content”">i</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 15:23; Acts 26:25; Luke 1:3"> Claudius Lysias.<br />To his excellency Governor Felix. Greetings!</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Acts 22:25–29; Acts 21:31–33; Acts 24:6"> This man was seized by the Jews and was about to be killed by them when I came upon them with the detachment and rescued him, because I learned that he was a Roman citizen.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Acts 22:30"> And because I wanted to know the charge for which they were accusing him, I brought him down to their Sanhedrin.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Or “council”">j</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Acts 26:31; Acts 25:25; Acts 18:15"> I found ⸤he⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Literally “whom”">k</a></sup>was accused concerning controversial questions of their law, but having no charge deserving death or imprisonment.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Acts 23:35; Acts 25:16; Acts 25:5–6"> And when it was made known to me there would be a plot against the man, I sent him to you immediately, also ordering his accusers to speak against him<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Some manuscripts have “to state the charges against him” (literally, “to speak the things against him”)">l</a></sup>before you.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 Timothy 2:3–4; Luke 7:8; Acts 23:23–24"> Therefore the soldiers, in accordance with ⸤their orders⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Literally “what was ordered to them”">m</a></sup>took Paul and brought him to Antipatris during the night.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Acts 23:23; Acts 23:10"> And on the next day they let the horsemen go on with him, and they returned to the barracks.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Or “headquarters”">n</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Acts 23:26; Acts 23:23–24; Acts 28:16"> ⸤The horsemen⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="Literally “who”">o</a></sup>when they came to Caesarea and delivered the letter to the governor, also presented Paul to him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Acts 21:39; Acts 25:1; Daniel 2:49"> So after reading the letter and asking what province he was from, and learning that he was from Cilicia,</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Acts 23:30; Acts 25:16; Matthew 27:27"> he said, “I will give you a hearing whenever your accusers arrive also,” giving orders for him to be guarded in the praetorium<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="The “praetorium” of Herod refers to the palace of Herod the Great in Caesarea Maritima">p</a></sup>of Herod.</span>
</div>
</div>
