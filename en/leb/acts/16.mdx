## Timothy Accompanies Paul and Silas

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Timothy 1:5; 2 Timothy 1:2; 1 Corinthians 4:17"> And he came also to Derbe and to Lystra. And behold, a certain disciple was there ⸤named⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally “by name”">a</a></sup>Timothy, the son of a believing Jewish woman but of a Greek father,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Timothy 5:10; 2 Timothy 3:15; Acts 14:21"> who was well spoken of by the brothers in Lystra and Iconium.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Galatians 2:3; 1 Corinthians 9:20; Galatians 5:6"> Paul wanted this one to go with him, and he took him and circumcised him because of the Jews who were in those places, for they all knew that his father was Greek.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Acts 15:28–29; Acts 15:2; Acts 11:30"> And as they went through the towns, they passed on to them to observe the rules that had been decided by the apostles and elders who were in Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Acts 9:31; Acts 2:47; Acts 6:7"> So the churches were being strengthened in the faith and were growing in number every day.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Paul’s Vision of a Man of Macedonia

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 18:23; Acts 16:7; Acts 13:2–4"> And they traveled through the Phrygian and Galatian region, having been prevented by the Holy Spirit from speaking the message in Asia.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="A reference to the Roman province of Asia (modern Asia Minor)">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Romans 8:9; Philippians 1:19; Galatians 4:6"> And when they came to Mysia, they attempted to go into Bithynia, and the Spirit of Jesus did not permit them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Corinthians 2:12; 2 Timothy 4:13; Acts 16:11"> So going through Mysia, they went down to Troas.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 9:10–12; Acts 19:21; Acts 18:5"> And a vision appeared to Paul during the night: a certain Macedonian man was standing there and imploring him and saying, “Come over to Macedonia and help us!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Corinthians 2:12–13; Acts 14:7; Acts 16:10–17"> And when he had seen the vision, we wanted at once to go away to Macedonia, concluding that God had called us to proclaim the good news to them.</span>
</div>
</div>

## The Conversion of Lydia at Philippi

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 21:1; Acts 16:8"> So putting out to sea from Troas, we sailed a straight course to Samothrace, and on the following day to Neapolis,</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 2:2; Philippians 1:1; Acts 20:6"> and from there to Philippi, which is a leading city of that district of Macedonia, a Roman colony. And we were staying in this city for some days.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Acts 13:14; Colossians 1:23; Acts 17:2"> And on the day of the Sabbath, we went outside the city gate beside the river, where we thought there was a place of prayer, and we sat down and spoke to the women assembled there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Ephesians 1:17–18; Luke 24:45; Isaiah 50:5"> And a certain woman ⸤named⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Literally “by name”">c</a></sup>Lydia from the city of Thyatira, a merchant dealing in purple cloth who showed reverence for God, was listening. The Lord opened ⸤her⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Literally “whose”">d</a></sup>heart to pay attention to what was being said by Paul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Acts 11:14; Hebrews 13:2; Acts 16:33"> And after she was baptized, and her household, she urged us, saying, “If you consider me to be a believer in the Lord, come to my house and stay.” And she prevailed upon us.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Paul and Silas Imprisoned

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Leviticus 19:31; Deuteronomy 18:9–11; Isaiah 8:19"> And it happened that as we were going to the place of prayer, a certain female slave who had a spirit of divination<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Literally “a spirit of Python”; Python was the name of the serpent or dragon that guarded the Delphic oracle at the foot of Mt. Parnassus and the word eventually came to be used for a spirit of divination">e</a></sup>met us, who was bringing a large profit to her owners by fortune-telling.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="John 14:6; 1 Peter 2:16; Mark 5:7"> She followed Paul and us and was crying out, saying, “These men are slaves of the Most High God, who are proclaiming to you the way of salvation!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Mark 16:17; Luke 10:17–19; Acts 3:6"> And she was doing this for many days. But Paul, becoming greatly annoyed and turning around, said to the spirit, “I command you in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her!” And it came out ⸤immediately⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Literally “that same hour”">f</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Acts 17:6; Acts 21:30; Acts 8:3"> But when her owners saw that their hope of profit was gone, they seized Paul and Silas and dragged them into the marketplace before the rulers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="James 4:4; John 15:18–20; Acts 28:22"> And when they had brought them to the chief magistrates, they said, “These men are throwing our city into confusion, being Jews,</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Esther 3:8; Acts 26:3; Jeremiah 10:3"> and are proclaiming customs that are not permitted for us to accept or to practice, because we are Romans!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 2:2; 2 Corinthians 6:5; 2 Corinthians 11:23–25"> And the crowd joined in attacking them, and the chief magistrates tore off their clothing and gave orders to beat them with rods.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Revelation 2:10; 2 Timothy 2:9; 1 Samuel 23:22–23"> And after they had inflicted many blows on them, they threw them into prison, giving orders to the jailer to guard them securely.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Job 13:27; Job 33:11; Jeremiah 29:26"> Having received such an order, ⸤he⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Literally “who”">g</a></sup>put them in the inner prison and fastened their feet in the stocks.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Or possibly “to the block of wood,” referring to a log to which the prisoners were chained or tied">h</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## The Conversion of the Philippian Jailer

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 5:16–18; Psalm 119:62; James 1:2"> Now about midnight, Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were listening to them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 4:31; Acts 5:19; Acts 12:10"> And suddenly there was a great earthquake, so that the foundations of the prison were shaken. And immediately all the doors were opened and all the bonds<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Or “chains”">i</a></sup>were unfastened.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Acts 12:19; Matthew 27:5; Judges 9:54"> And after the jailer was awake and saw the doors of the prison open, he drew his sword and was about to kill himself, because he thought the prisoners had escaped.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 5:15; Luke 23:34; Luke 6:27–28"> But Paul called out with a loud voice, saying, “Do no harm to yourself, for we are all here!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Revelation 3:9; Acts 24:25; Isaiah 60:14"> And demanding lights, he rushed in and, ⸤beginning to tremble⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Literally “became trembling”">j</a></sup>fell down at the feet of Paul and Silas.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Acts 2:37; John 6:27–29; Isaiah 58:9"> And he brought them outside and said, “Sirs, what must I do so that I can be saved?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="John 1:12; Acts 2:38–39; John 3:36"> And they said, “Believe in the Lord Jesus and you will be saved, you and your household!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Romans 1:16; Colossians 1:27–28; 1 Timothy 1:13–16"> And they spoke the message of the Lord to him, together with all those in his house.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:16; Acts 16:15; Acts 16:25"> And he took them at that very hour of the night and washed their wounds, and he himself was baptized at once, and all those of his household.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Romans 15:13; Acts 2:46; Romans 5:11"> And he brought them up into his house and set a meal before them, and rejoiced greatly that he had believed in God with his whole household.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Paul and Silas Receive an Official Apology

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Jeremiah 5:22; Psalm 76:10; Acts 5:40"> And when it was day, the chief magistrates sent the police officers, saying, “Release those men.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="Acts 15:33; John 14:27; 1 Samuel 1:17"> And the jailer reported these words to Paul: “The chief magistrates have sent an order that you should be released. So come out now and go in peace!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="Acts 22:25–29; Psalm 58:1–2; Matthew 10:16"> But Paul said to them, “They beat us in public without due process—men who are Roman citizens—and threw us into prison, and now they are wanting to release us secretly? Certainly not! Rather let them come themselves and bring us out!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="Acts 22:29; Matthew 21:46; Matthew 14:5"> So the police officers reported these words to the chief magistrates, and they were afraid when they heard that they were Roman citizens.</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Matthew 8:34; Mark 5:17; Daniel 6:23"> And they came and apologized to<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Or “reassured”; or “conciliated”">k</a></sup>them, and after they brought them out they asked them to depart from the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 3:2–3; Acts 16:14; Acts 14:22"> And when they came out of the prison, they went to Lydia and when they saw them, they encouraged the brothers and departed.</span>
</div>
