## David Commits Adultery with Bathsheba

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Chronicles 20:1; 1 Kings 20:26; 1 Kings 20:22"> ⸤It came about in the spring⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally “And it happened at the turn of the year”">a</a></sup>at the time ⸤kings⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="According to the reading tradition (Qere) ; Kethib has “angels” or “messengers”">b</a></sup>go out, David sent Joab and his servants with him and all of Israel. They ravaged all of the ⸤Ammonites⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Literally “sons/children of Ammon”">c</a></sup>and besieged Rabbah, but David was remaining in Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Matthew 5:28; Psalm 119:37; Job 31:1"> It happened ⸤late one afternoon⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Literally “at the time of the evening”">d</a></sup>that David got up from his bed and walked about on the roof of the king’s house, and he saw a woman bathing on her<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Hebrew “the”">e</a></sup>roof. Now the woman ⸤was very beautiful⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Literally “very good of appearance”">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Samuel 23:39; 1 Chronicles 3:5; James 1:14–15"> David sent and inquired about the woman, and someone said, “Is this not Bathsheba the daughter of Eliam, the wife of Uriah the Hittite?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Leviticus 18:19; James 1:14–15; Psalm 51:1–19"> Then David sent messengers and took her, and she came to him, and he slept with her. (Now she had been purifying herself from her uncleanness.) And she returned to her house.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Deuteronomy 22:22; Proverbs 6:34"> The woman became pregnant, and she sent and told David, and she said, “I am pregnant.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Proverbs 28:13; Genesis 4:7; 1 Samuel 15:30"> So David sent to Joab, “Send Uriah the Hittite to me.” So Joab sent Uriah to David.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Genesis 37:14; 1 Samuel 17:22; Genesis 29:6"> Uriah came to him, and David asked ⸤how Joab and the army fared and how the war was going⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Literally “as far as the peace of Joab, as far as the peace of the army, and as far as the peace of the battle”">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Genesis 18:4; Genesis 43:34; Isaiah 29:15"> David said to Uriah, “Go down to your house, and wash your feet.” So Uriah went out from the king’s house, and a gift from the king went out after him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Proverbs 21:30; Job 5:12–14"> But Uriah slept at the entrance of the king’s house with all the servants of his master and did not go down to his house.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> They told David, “Uriah did not go down to his house.” David said to Uriah, “Are you not coming from a journey? Why did you not go down to your house?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Samuel 7:2; 2 Samuel 20:6; 2 Samuel 7:6"> Uriah said to David, “The ark and Israel and Judah are living in the booths; and my lord Joab and the servants of my lord are camping on the surface of the open field; and I, shall I go to my house to eat and to drink and to sleep with my wife? By your life and the life of your soul, I surely will not do this thing.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Jeremiah 2:37; Jeremiah 2:22–23"> David said to Uriah, “Remain here ⸤today⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Literally “also the day”">h</a></sup>and tomorrow I will send you away.” So Uriah remained in Jerusalem on that day and the next.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Habakkuk 2:15; Genesis 19:32–35; Proverbs 20:1"> David invited him, and he ate and drank in his presence ⸤so that he became drunk⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Literally “and he made him drunk”">i</a></sup>and he went out in the evening to lie on his bed with the servants of his lord, but he did not go down to his house.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Jeremiah 17:9; 1 Kings 21:8–10; Psalm 19:13"> And it happened in the morning, David wrote a letter to Joab, and he sent it by the hand of Uriah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="2 Samuel 12:9; Psalm 51:4; Jeremiah 20:13"> He had written in the letter, “Put Uriah in the front, in the face of the fiercest fighting, then draw back from behind him so that he may be struck down and die.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Proverbs 29:12; 1 Samuel 22:17–19; Acts 5:29"> ⸤When Joab was besieging⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Literally “And it happened at the besieging of Joab”">j</a></sup>the city, he put Uriah toward the place which he knew ⸤there were valiant warriors⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Literally “there were men of ability”">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Samuel 12:9; Psalm 51:14"> The men of the city came out and fought with Joab. Some from the army from the servants of David fell; Uriah the Hittite also died.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> Joab sent and told David all of the news of the battle.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> He instructed the messenger, saying, “As you are finishing to speak all the news of the battle to the king,</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> if the anger of the king rises and he says to you, ‘Why did you go near the city to fight? Did you not know that they would shoot from atop the wall?</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Judges 7:1; Judges 9:50–54; 2 Samuel 3:27"> Who killed Abimelech the son of Jerub-bosheth,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="In putting words in David’s mouth, Joab alludes to the story of Abimelech the son of Gideon from Judg 9:52–55. Though Gideon was also known as Jerub-ba’al, Joab conventionally substitutes bosheth (shame) for Ba’al to avoid naming the Canaanite deity">l</a></sup>if not a woman who threw an upper millstone on him from atop the wall and he died at Thebez? Why did you go near the wall?’ Then you shall say, ‘Your servant Uriah the Hittite also died.’ ”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> Then the messenger left, and he came and told David all that Joab had sent him to say.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> The messenger said to David, “Because ⸤the men overpowered us⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Literally “the men were superior over us”">m</a></sup>the men came out to us in the field, but ⸤we forced them back⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Literally “we were upon them”">n</a></sup>to the entrance of the gate.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24"> The archers shot at your servant from atop the wall, and some of the servants of the king died; your servant Uriah the Hittite also died.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Samuel 12:26; Joshua 7:8–9; Ecclesiastes 9:1–3"> Then David said to the messenger, “Thus you shall say to Joab, ‘⸤Do not feel badly about this matter⸥;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="Literally “Do not let his matter be evil in your eyes”">o</a></sup>⸤now one and then another⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="Literally “for as this and as this”">p</a></sup>the sword will devour. Intensify your attack on the city and overthrow it.’ ” And he encouraged him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Samuel 14:2; Genesis 27:41; 2 Samuel 3:31"> When the wife of Uriah heard that Uriah her husband was dead, she mourned over her husband.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Psalm 51:4–5; 2 Samuel 12:9; Deuteronomy 22:29"> When the mourning was over, David sent and brought her to his household, and she became his wife and bore him a son. But the thing which David had done was evil in the eyes of Yahweh.</span>
</div>
