## Ziba Brings Provisions

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Samuel 25:18; 2 Samuel 15:32; 2 Samuel 9:2–13"> Now David passed a little from beyond the summit, and suddenly Ziba the servant of Mephibosheth was there to meet him with a pair of saddled donkeys; on them were two hundred loaves of bread and a hundred raisin cakes, with a hundred summer fruits and a skin of wine.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Samuel 17:29; Judges 10:4; Judges 5:10"> The king said to Ziba, “⸤What do you want to accomplish by bringing these⸥?”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally “What are these to you?”">a</a></sup>And Ziba said, “The donkeys are for the king’s household to ride on; the bread and the summer fruit are for the young men to eat, and the wine is for the faint in the wilderness to drink.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Samuel 9:9–10; Psalm 15:3; 2 Samuel 19:24–30"> Then the king said, “Where is the son of your lord?” And Ziba said to the king, “He is living in Jerusalem for he said, ‘Today the house of Israel shall return the kingdom of my father to me.’ ”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Proverbs 18:13; Proverbs 18:17; Proverbs 19:2"> The king said to Ziba, “Look, all that was Mephibosheth’s is yours.” Ziba said, “I hereby do obeisance; may I find favor in your eyes, my lord the king.”</span>
</div>
</div>

## Shimei Curses David

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Samuel 3:16; 1 Samuel 17:43; Exodus 22:28"> King David came up to Bahurim and suddenly a man from there was coming out from the family of the house of Saul, and his name was Shimei the son of Gera. ⸤He was cursing as he came out⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Literally “He was coming out, coming out and cursing”">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> And he threw stones at David and at all the servants of King David and at all the people and at all the mighty warriors on his right and on his left.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Deuteronomy 13:13; 1 Samuel 25:17; 1 Kings 21:10"> Shimei said while cursing him, “Go out, go out, you ⸤man of bloodshed⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Literally “man of bloods”">c</a></sup>you ⸤man of wickedness⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Literally “and man of the wickedness”">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Samuel 1:16; 2 Samuel 3:28–29; 1 Kings 2:32–33"> Yahweh has returned on you all the blood of the household of Saul ⸤whom you have supplanted as king⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Literally “who you have reigned in place of him”">e</a></sup>and Yahweh has given the kingship into the hand of Absalom your son. Look, you are in disaster for you are a man of blood.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Exodus 22:28; 2 Samuel 9:8; 1 Samuel 24:14"> Then Abishai the son of Zeruiah said to the king, “Why should this dead dog curse my lord the king? Please let me go over and take off his head.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Samuel 19:22; Romans 9:20; John 18:11"> The king said, “⸤What do we have in common⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Literally “What is for me and what is for you”">f</a></sup>sons of Zeruiah? If<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Hebrew “Because”">g</a></sup>he curses because Yahweh has said to him ‘Curse David,’ who can say, “Why have you done this?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Samuel 17:1–4; Genesis 45:5; 2 Samuel 12:11–12"> David said to Abishai and to all his servants, “Look, my son who came out of my loins<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Hebrew “intestines”">h</a></sup>is seeking my life. Now ⸤as far as⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Literally “and even that now”">i</a></sup>this Benjaminite, leave him alone and let him curse, for Yahweh has spoken to him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Deuteronomy 23:5; Hebrews 12:10; Psalm 25:18"> Perhaps Yahweh will look ⸤in my eye⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="According to the reading tradition (Qere) ; Kethib has “at my guilt”">j</a></sup>and repay good for me in place of his curse this day.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Acts 23:23; 2 Samuel 16:5–6"> Then David and his men went on the road, with Shimei going on the side of the hill beside him, ⸤cursing as he went⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Literally “going and cursing”">k</a></sup>He threw stones beside him and threw<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Hebrew “flung”">l</a></sup>dust in the air.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> When the king and all of the people who were with him arrived, he was weary, so he recovered there.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Hushai Comes to Absalom

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="2 Samuel 15:37; 2 Samuel 15:12"> Now Absalom and all the people, the men of Israel, had come to Jerusalem, and Ahithophel was with him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Kings 11:12; 1 Samuel 10:24; 1 Kings 1:25"> When Hushai the Arkite the friend of David came to Absalom, Hushai said to Absalom, “Long live the king, long live the king!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Samuel 19:25; 2 Samuel 15:32–37; Deuteronomy 32:6"> Absalom said to Hushai, “This is your loyal love with your friend? Why did you not go with your friend?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Samuel 16:13; 2 Samuel 5:1–3"> Then Hushai said to Absalom, “No, rather, whom Yahweh and this people and all the men of Israel have chosen, to him<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="According to the reading tradition (Qere ); Kethib has “no”">m</a></sup>I will be, and with him I will remain.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Samuel 15:34; Psalm 55:21; 1 Samuel 28:2"> ⸤Furthermore⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Literally “And the second thing ”">n</a></sup>for whom have I served? Is it not in the presence of his son that I have served before your father? ⸤So shall I serve you⸥!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="Literally “So shall I be in your presence”">o</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Isaiah 8:10; Psalm 37:12–13; Isaiah 29:15"> Then Absalom said to Ahithophel, “Give your advice. What shall we do?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Samuel 20:3; 2 Samuel 15:16; Zechariah 8:13"> And Ahithophel said to Absalom, “Go to the concubines of your father whom he left behind to watch over the house, then all of Israel will hear that you made yourself odious to your father, and ⸤all of your followers will be motivated⸥!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="Literally “the hands of all who are with you will be strengthened”">p</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Samuel 12:11–12; 2 Samuel 15:16; 2 Samuel 20:3"> Then they pitched a tent for Absalom on the roof, and he went in to the concubines of his father before the eyes of all Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 Samuel 15:12; 2 Samuel 17:14; 2 Samuel 17:23"> The counsel that Ahithophel gave in those days was regarded as when a man<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="Qere reads “a man inquired”; Kethib omits “a man”">q</a></sup>inquired of the word of God, so all the counsel of Ahithophel was esteemed both by David and by Absalom.</span>
</div>
