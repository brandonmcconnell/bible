## Qohelet’s Investigation of Self-Indulgence

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Luke 12:19; Ecclesiastes 8:15; James 5:5"> I said ⸤to myself⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally “to my heart”">a</a></sup>“Come! I will test<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="The MT reads “I will test you,” but the BHS editors propose “I will test …” Whether or not one adopts MT, Qohelet is speaking to himself">b</a></sup>pleasure ⸤to see whether it is worthwhile⸥.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Literally “and look at goodness”; this idiom refers to the enjoyment of life">c</a></sup>But look, “This also is vanity!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Proverbs 14:13; Ecclesiastes 7:2–6; 1 Peter 4:2–4"> I said of laughter, “It is folly!” and of pleasure, “⸤What does it accomplish?⸥”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Literally “What does it give?”">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Proverbs 20:1; Ecclesiastes 1:17; Proverbs 31:4–5"> I also ⸤explored⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Literally “I searched in my mind”">e</a></sup>⸤the effects of indulging my flesh⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Literally “to cheer my flesh”">f</a></sup>with wine. My mind guiding me with wisdom, ⸤I investigated⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Literally “laid hold of”">g</a></sup>folly so that I might discover what is good under heaven<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Follows MT; two medieval Hebrew manuscripts, LXX, Peshitta read, “under the sun,” cf. 1:3, 9, etc.">h</a></sup>for ⸤humans⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Literally “the sons of the man”">i</a></sup>to do ⸤during the days of their lives⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Literally “the number of the days of their lives”">j</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## Qohelet’s Investigation of Personal Accomplishment

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Kings 7:1–12; 2 Chronicles 8:11; 2 Chronicles 26:10"> ⸤I accomplished great things⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Literally “I made great my works”">k</a></sup>I built for myself houses; I planted for myself vineyards.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Luke 17:27–29; Song of Solomon 4:12–1; Jeremiah 39:4"> I made for myself gardens and parks, and I planted all sorts of fruit trees in them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Nehemiah 2:14; Psalm 1:3; Jeremiah 17:8"> I made for myself pools of water from which to irrigate a grove of flourishing trees.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Kings 3:4; Genesis 15:3; 1 Kings 4:23"> I acquired male slaves and female slaves, as well as children born in my house. I also had livestock, cattle, and flocks more than anyone who was before me in Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Kings 10:10; 1 Kings 9:28; 2 Samuel 19:35"> I also gathered to myself silver and gold—the royal and provincial treasuries. I acquired for myself male and female singers, as well as the delight of ⸤men⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Literally “the sons of the man”">l</a></sup>⸤voluptuous concubines⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Literally “a breast and breasts,” as a synecdoche for beautiful women in the king’s harem">m</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Chronicles 29:25; Ecclesiastes 1:16; 1 Kings 10:23"> Thus, ⸤I accomplished far more⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Literally “I became great and I surpassed”">n</a></sup>than anyone who was before me in Jerusalem—indeed, my wisdom stood by me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 9:9; Ecclesiastes 5:18; Ecclesiastes 3:22"> I neither withheld anything from my eyes that they desired, nor did I deprive any pleasure from my heart. My heart rejoiced in all my toil, for this was my reward from all my toil.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 1:14; 1 John 2:16–17; Ecclesiastes 1:3"> Yet when I considered<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="Or “turned to”">o</a></sup>all the effort which I expended and the toil with which I toiled to do, then behold, “Everything is vanity and chasing wind! There is nothing profitable under the sun!”</span>
</div>
</div>

## The Living Must Abandon the Work of their Hands to Others at Death

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 1:17; Ecclesiastes 7:25; Ecclesiastes 1:9–10"> Next, I considered wisdom, as well as delusion and folly. What can anyone do who will come after the king that has not already been done?</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Ephesians 5:8; Ecclesiastes 7:11–12; Matthew 6:23"> I realized that wisdom has an advantage over folly, just as light has an advantage over darkness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Psalm 49:10; Proverbs 17:24; Ecclesiastes 9:11"> ⸤The wise man can see where he is walking⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="Literally “The eyes of the wise are in his head”">p</a></sup>but the fool walks in darkness. Yet I also realized that both of them suffer the same fate.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 6:8; Ecclesiastes 1:14; Ecclesiastes 1:2"> So I said ⸤to myself⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="Literally “in my heart”">q</a></sup>“⸤If I also suffer the same fate as the fool⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="Literally “Just as the fate of the fool—so it will happen to me!”">r</a></sup>⸤what advantage is my great wisdom⸥?”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-s" id="fnref-s" data-fn="Literally “why have I been so exceedingly wise?”">s</a></sup>So I said ⸤to myself⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-t" id="fnref-t" data-fn="Literally “in my heart”">t</a></sup>“This also is vanity!”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 1:11; Ecclesiastes 9:5; Psalm 88:12"> Certainly no one will remember the wise man or the fool in ⸤future generations⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-u" id="fnref-u" data-fn="Literally “the futures”">u</a></sup>When future days come, both will have been forgotten already. How is it that the wise man dies the same as the fool?</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 2:11; Jonah 4:8; Ecclesiastes 4:2"> So I hated life because the work done under the sun is grievous to me. For everything is vanity and chasing wind!</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Psalm 39:6; Psalm 49:10; Luke 16:27–28"> So I hated all my toil with which I have toiled under the sun, for I must leave it behind to someone who will be after me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="James 3:17; Ecclesiastes 3:22; Ecclesiastes 9:13"> And who knows whether he will be wise or foolish? Yet he will exercise control of all the fruit of my toil with which I toiled wisely under the sun. This also is vanity!</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Genesis 43:14; Job 17:11–15; 2 Corinthians 1:8–10"> So ⸤I began to despair⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-v" id="fnref-v" data-fn="Literally “I myself turned to cause my heart to despair”">v</a></sup>of all the toil with which I toiled under the sun.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 2:17–18; 2 Chronicles 31:20–21; 2 Chronicles 34:2"> For although a person may toil with great wisdom and skill, he must leave his reward to someone who has not toiled for it. This also is vanity and a great calamity.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Psalm 127:2; Ecclesiastes 1:3; Philippians 4:6"> For what does a person receive for all his toil and in the longing of his heart with which he toils under the sun?</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Job 14:1; Job 5:7; Psalm 6:6–7"> All his days are painful, his labor brings grief, and his heart cannot rest at night. This also is vanity!</span>
</div>
</div>

## It is Best to Simply Enjoy the Passing Pleasures of Life as Reward for Pleasing God

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 3:22; Ecclesiastes 8:15; 1 Timothy 6:17"> There is nothing better for a person than to eat and drink and ⸤find delight⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-w" id="fnref-w" data-fn="Literally “to see good”">w</a></sup>in his toil. For I also realized that this is from the hand of God!</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 2:1–12; 1 Kings 4:21–24"> For who can eat and drink, and who can enjoy life apart from him?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-x" id="fnref-x" data-fn="The MT reads “more than me,” which is supported by Aramaic Targum and Latin Vulgate, but several medieval Hebrew manuscripts read “from him”">x</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Proverbs 13:22; Job 27:16–17; Proverbs 28:8"> For to the person who is good in his eyes, he gives wisdom, knowledge, and joy; but to the sinner he gives the task of gathering and heaping up only to give it to him who is pleasing to him. This also is vanity and chasing wind!</span>
</div>
</div>
