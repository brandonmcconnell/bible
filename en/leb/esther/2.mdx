## Esther is Chosen Queen

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Daniel 6:14–18; Esther 7:10; Esther 1:12–21"> After these things, when the anger of King Ahasuerus subsided, he remembered Vashti and what she had done and what had been decreed against her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Kings 1:2; Genesis 12:14; Esther 6:14"> And the king’s servants attending him said, “Let them seek attractive young virgins for the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Esther 2:12–15; Esther 2:8–9; Esther 1:1–2"> Let the king appoint chief officers in all the provinces of his kingdom, and let them gather every attractive young virgin to the ⸤harem⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally “house of the women”">a</a></sup>in the citadel of Susa ⸤under the care of⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Literally “to the hand of”">b</a></sup>Hegai, the king’s eunuch, who is in charge of the women; and let him give them their beauty treatment.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Matthew 14:6; Matthew 20:16; Esther 1:21"> And let the young woman ⸤who is pleasing⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Literally “who is good”">c</a></sup>in the king’s eyes become queen in place of Vashti.” The thing was good in the king’s eyes, and he acted accordingly.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Samuel 16:5; 1 Samuel 9:1; Esther 10:3"> There was a Jew in the citadel of Susa whose name was Mordecai son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, a Benjaminite,</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Kings 24:14–15; 2 Chronicles 36:20; Jeremiah 24:1"> who was deported<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Or “exiled”">d</a></sup>from Jerusalem with the exiles<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Hebrew “exile”">e</a></sup>who were deported<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Or “exiled”">f</a></sup>with Jeconiah<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="A variant spelling of “Jehoiachin”">g</a></sup>the king of Judah, whom King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon had deported.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Or “exiled”">h</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Esther 2:15; Esther 1:11; 2 Corinthians 6:18"> He<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="That is, Mordecai">i</a></sup>was raising Hadassah, that is Esther, his uncle’s daughter,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="That is, his cousin">j</a></sup>for she did not have a father or a mother; the young woman had a beautiful figure and was very attractive. When her father and mother died, Mordecai had taken her as his daughter.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Esther 2:3; Esther 2:15"> And it happened, at the proclaiming of the edict of the king and his law, when many young women were being gathered to the citadel of Susa ⸤under Hegai’s care⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Literally “to the hand of Hegai”">k</a></sup>Esther was taken to the ⸤king’s palace⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Literally “house of the king”">l</a></sup>⸤under the care of⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Literally “to the hand of”">m</a></sup>Hegai who was in charge of the women.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Genesis 39:21; Proverbs 16:7; Esther 2:12"> ⸤The young woman pleased him⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Literally “the young woman was good in his eyes”">n</a></sup>and she won favor in his presence, and he quickly provided for her beauty treatment and her portion of food, with seven chosen maids to give to her from the ⸤king’s palace⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="Literally “house of the king”">o</a></sup>and he advanced her and her maids to the best part of the ⸤harem⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="Literally “house of the women”">p</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Esther 2:20; Matthew 10:16; Esther 3:8"> Esther did not disclose her people and her family because Modecai had charged her that she must not tell.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Genesis 37:14; 1 Samuel 17:18; Acts 15:36"> And every day Modecai would walk up and down in front of the courtyard of the ⸤harem⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="Literally “house of the women”">q</a></sup>to learn ⸤how Esther was doing⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-r" id="fnref-r" data-fn="Literally “the peace of Esther and how it does/does with her”">r</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 4:4–5; Proverbs 7:17; Isaiah 57:9"> When the turn came for each girl to go to King Ahasuerus, after the end of twelve months of being under the regulations of the women—for the days of their beauty treatments had to be filled, six months with the oil of myrrh and six months with perfumes and women’s cosmetics—</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> in this way, the girl goes to the king and all that she asks is given to her ⸤to take⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-s" id="fnref-s" data-fn="Literally “to go”">s</a></sup>with her from the ⸤harem⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-t" id="fnref-t" data-fn="Literally “house of the women”">t</a></sup>to the ⸤king’s palace⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-u" id="fnref-u" data-fn="Literally “house of the king”">u</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Esther 4:11; Genesis 34:19; Isaiah 62:4–5"> In the evening she would go and in the morning she would return to the ⸤second harem⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-v" id="fnref-v" data-fn="Literally “second house of the women”">v</a></sup>⸤under the care of⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-w" id="fnref-w" data-fn="Literally “to the hand of”">w</a></sup>Shaashgaz, the king’s eunuch in charge of the concubines. She would not go back to the king unless the king delighted in her and she was called by name.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Song of Solomon 6:9; Esther 2:3; Esther 9:29"> When the turn came near for Esther daughter of Abihail the uncle of Mordecai, who had taken her as a daughter, to go to the king, she did not ask anything except what Hegai the eunuch of the king who was in charge of the women, advised. And Esther carried favor in the eyes of everyone that saw her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Esther 8:9; Esther 2:3; Esther 2:1"> Esther was taken to King Ahasuerus, to his ⸤palace⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-x" id="fnref-x" data-fn="Literally “house of the reign” or “house of the kingdom”">x</a></sup>in the tenth month that is Tebeth in the seventh year of his reign.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Psalm 113:7–8; Luke 1:48–52; Ezekiel 17:24"> And the king loved Esther more than all the women, and she won his favor and loyalty more than all the virgins, so he put a ⸤royal crown⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-y" id="fnref-y" data-fn="Literally “crown of the kingdom”">y</a></sup>on her head and made her queen instead of Vashti.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Song of Solomon 3:11; Luke 14:8; Judges 14:10–17"> And the king gave a great banquet, Esther’s banquet, for all his officials and servants. And he granted a tax amnesty<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-z" id="fnref-z" data-fn="See HALOT 252–253 s.v.">z</a></sup>to the provinces and he gave gifts with royal liberality.</span>
</div>
</div>

## A Plot Against the King

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Esther 2:21; Esther 2:3–4; Esther 5:13"> When the virgins were gathered a second time, Mordecai was sitting at the gate of the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Ephesians 6:1–3; Esther 2:10; Esther 2:7"> Esther had not made known her family and her people, just as Mordecai had instructed her; for Esther ⸤did what Mordecai told her⸥,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-aa" id="fnref-aa" data-fn="Literally “did the word of Mordecai”">aa</a></sup>just as when she was brought up by him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Esther 6:2; 1 Kings 16:9; 2 Kings 21:23"> In those days Mordecai was sitting at the gate of the king. Bigthana and Teresh, two of the king’s eunuchs from the keepers of the threshold, became angry and they conspired ⸤to assassinate⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-ab" id="fnref-ab" data-fn="Literally “to send a hand against”">ab</a></sup>King Ahasuerus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Esther 6:1–2; Romans 11:33; Ecclesiastes 10:20"> And the matter became known to Mordecai and he told it to Queen Esther, and Esther told it to the king in the name of Mordecai.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Genesis 40:22; Genesis 40:19; Esther 10:2"> And the matter was investigated and found to be so; and the two of them were hanged on the gallows, and it was written in the scroll of the events of the days before the presence of the king.</span>
</div>
