## The Parable of the Sower

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Luke 5:1–3; Mark 2:13; Luke 8:4–10"> And again he began to teach beside the sea,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="That is, the Sea of Galilee">a</a></sup>and a very large crowd was gathered to him, so that he got into a boat and sat on the sea, and the whole crowd was at the sea on the land.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Matthew 13:34–35; Mark 3:23; Mark 4:11"> And he began to teach<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="The imperfect tense has been translated as ingressive here (“began to teach”)">b</a></sup>them many things in parables, and was saying to them in his teaching,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="James 2:5; Matthew 13:26; Mark 4:26–29"> “Listen! Behold, the sower went out to sow.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Matthew 13:19; Mark 4:15; Matthew 13:4"> And it happened that while he was sowing, ⸤some seed⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Literally “some of which”">c</a></sup>fell on the side of the path, and the birds came and devoured it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Ezekiel 36:26; Luke 8:6; Luke 8:13"> And other seed fell on the rocky ground where it did not have much soil, and it sprang up at once, because it did not have any depth of soil.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="James 1:11; Jeremiah 17:5–8; Jonah 4:8"> And when the sun rose it was scorched, and because it did not have enough root, it withered.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Jeremiah 4:3; Luke 8:14; Genesis 3:17–18"> And other seed fell among the thorn plants, and the thorn plants came up and choked it, and it did not produce grain.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Literally “fruit,” describing here the grain harvested from the healthy plants; in contemporary English this would more naturally be expressed by terms like “grain” or “crop”">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="John 15:5; Colossians 1:6; Mark 4:20"> And other seed fell on the good soil, and produced grain,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Literally “fruit,” describing here the grain harvested from the healthy plants; in contemporary English this would more naturally be expressed by terms like “grain” or “crop”">e</a></sup>coming up and increasing, and it bore a crop—one thirty and one sixty and one a hundred times as much.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="The phrase “times as much ” is not in the Greek text but is implied">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Matthew 11:15; Mark 4:23–24; Revelation 3:22"> And he said, “Whoever has ears to hear, let him hear!”</span>
</div>
</div>

## The Parable of the Sower Interpreted

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Revelation 3:19; Matthew 13:51–52; Mark 7:17–18"> And he said to them, “Do you not understand this parable? And how will you understand all the parables?</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Acts 8:4; Matthew 13:19; Matthew 13:37"> The sower sows the word.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Peter 5:8; Matthew 13:19; 2 Corinthians 2:11"> And these are the ones beside the path where the word is sown, and whenever they hear it, immediately Satan comes and takes away the word that was sown in them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Mark 6:20; Luke 8:13; Matthew 13:20–21"> And these are like<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Some manuscripts omit “like”">k</a></sup>the ones sown on the rocky ground, who whenever they hear the word immediately receive it with joy.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Matthew 11:6; 1 Thessalonians 3:3–5; Matthew 12:31"> And they have no root in themselves, but are temporary. Then when affliction or persecution comes because of the word, immediately they fall away.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Luke 8:14; Jeremiah 4:3; Matthew 13:22"> And others are the ones sown among the thorn plants—these are the ones who hear the word,</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 John 2:15–17; 1 Timothy 6:9–10; 1 Timothy 6:17"> and the cares of the world and the deceitfulness of wealth and the desires for other things come in and choke the word and it becomes unproductive.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Colossians 1:10; Luke 8:15; John 15:4–5"> And those are the ones sown on the good soil, who hear the word and receive it and bear fruit—one thirty and one sixty and one a hundred times as much.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="The phrase “times as much ” is not in the Greek text but is implied">l</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>

## The Parable of the Lamp

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Matthew 5:15; Luke 11:33; Luke 8:16"> And he said to them, “Surely a lamp is not brought so that it may be put under a bushel basket or under a bed, is it?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="The negative construction in Greek anticipates a negative answer here, indicated in the translation by the phrase “is it ”">m</a></sup>Is it not<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="The negative construction in Greek anticipates a positive answer here">n</a></sup>so that it may be put on a lampstand?</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Luke 8:17; Ecclesiastes 12:14; Matthew 10:26–27"> For nothing is secret except so that it may be revealed, nor has become hidden except so that it will come to light.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Mark 4:9; Matthew 11:15; Revelation 2:29"> If anyone has ears to hear, let him hear!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Hebrews 2:1; Luke 8:18; Matthew 7:2"> And he said to them, “Take care what you hear! With the measure by which you measure out, it will be measured out to you, and will be added to you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Matthew 13:12; Luke 8:18; Matthew 25:28–29"> For whoever has, more will be given to him, and whoever does not have, even what he has will be taken away from him.”</span>
</div>

## The Parable of the Seed that Grows by Itself

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Matthew 13:24; 1 Corinthians 3:6–9; Luke 8:11"> And he said, “The kingdom of God is like this: like a man scatters seed on the ground.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 11:5; 2 Thessalonians 1:3; 1 Corinthians 15:37–38"> And he sleeps and gets up, night and day, and the seed sprouts and grows—⸤he does not know how⸥.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="Literally “in such a way as he does not know”">o</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Mark 4:31–32; Hosea 6:3; Philippians 1:9–11"> By itself the soil produces a crop: first the grass, then the head of grain, then the full grain in the head.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Joel 3:13; Matthew 13:30; Matthew 13:40–43"> But when the crop permits, he sends in the sickle right away, because the harvest has come.”</span>
</div>
</div>

## The Parable of the Mustard Seed

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Matthew 13:31–32; Luke 13:18–21; Lamentations 2:13"> And he said, “With what can we compare the kingdom of God, or by what parable can we present it?</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Acts 19:20; Luke 13:18–19; Daniel 2:44–45"> It is like a mustard seed that when sown on the ground, although it is the smallest of all the seeds that are on the ground,</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Daniel 4:20–22; Daniel 4:10–14; Ezekiel 31:3–10"> but when it is sown it grows up and becomes the largest of all the garden herbs, and sends out large branches so that the birds of the sky are able to nest in its shade.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Matthew 13:34–35; John 16:12; Hebrews 5:11–14"> And with many parables such as these he was speaking the word to them, as they were able to hear it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="John 16:25; Mark 4:10; Matthew 15:15–20"> And he did not speak to them without a parable, but in private he explained everything to his own disciples.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Calming of a Storm

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Matthew 8:18; Luke 8:22; Luke 8:25"> And on that day, when it was evening, he said to them, “Let us cross over to the other side.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="Mark 3:9; Mark 4:1; Mark 5:2"> And leaving the crowd, they took him along, as he was, in the boat. And other boats were with him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="Matthew 8:23–24; Job 1:19; Luke 8:22–23"> And a great storm of wind developed, and the waves were breaking into the boat, so that the boat was already being filled with water.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="1 Peter 5:7; Hebrews 4:15; Isaiah 54:6–8"> And he was in the stern sleeping on the cushion, and they woke him up and said to him, “Teacher, is it not a concern to you that we are perishing?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Psalm 107:29; Psalm 89:9; Psalm 65:7"> And he woke up and rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, “Be quiet! Be silent!” And the wind abated and there was a great calm.</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="Luke 8:25; Matthew 8:26; Matthew 14:31"> And he said to them, “Why are you fearful? Do you not yet have faith?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="Luke 8:25; Job 38:11; Psalm 89:7"> And ⸤they were terribly frightened⸥<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="Literally “they feared a great fear”">p</a></sup>and began to say<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="The imperfect tense has been translated as ingressive here (“began to say”)">q</a></sup>to one another, “Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?”</span>
