<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Matthew 12:42; Luke 11:31; 1 Kings 10:1–13"> When the queen of Sheba heard of the fame of Solomon, she came to test Solomon with hard questions at Jerusalem, with a very great caravan, including camels that bore spices, gold in abundance, and precious stones. When she had come to Solomon, she talked with him about all that was in her heart.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Yochanan 15:15; 1 Kings 3:12; Mark 4:34"> Solomon answered all her questions. There wasn’t anything hidden from Solomon which he didn’t tell her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Chronicles 3:1–4; 1 Kings 10:3; 1 Kings 6:1–7"> When the queen of Sheba had seen the wisdom of Solomon, the house that he had built,</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Luke 12:37; 1 Kings 4:22–23; Psalms 143:7"> the food of his table, the seating of his servants, the attendance of his ministers, their clothing, his cup bearers and their clothing, and his ascent by which he went up to the LORD’s house, there was no more spirit in her.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="or, she was breathless.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Kings 10:6"> She said to the king, “It was a true report that I heard in my own land of your acts and of your wisdom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Kings 10:7; 2 Chronicles 9:5; 1 Kings 4:34"> However I didn’t believe their words until I came, and my eyes had seen it; and behold half of the greatness of your wisdom wasn’t told me. You exceed the fame that I heard!</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Psalms 27:4; Proverbs 3:14; Proverbs 10:21"> Happy are your men, and happy are these your servants, who stand continually before you and hear your wisdom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Chronicles 2:11; 1 Chronicles 29:23; Isaiah 32:1–2"> Blessed be the LORD your God, who delighted in you and set you on his throne to be king for the LORD your God, because your God loved Israel, to establish them forever. Therefore he made you king over them, to do justice and righteousness.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Chronicles 9:1; Psalms 72:10; Psalms 72:15"> She gave the king one hundred and twenty talents<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="A talent is about 30 kilograms or 66 pounds or 965 Troy ounces, so 120 talents is about 3.6 metric tons">b</a></sup>of gold, spices in great abundance, and precious stones. There was never before such spice as the queen of Sheba gave to King Solomon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Chronicles 8:18; 1 Kings 10:11; 1 Kings 10:22"> The servants of Huram and the servants of Solomon, who brought gold from Ophir, also brought algum trees<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="possibly Indian sandalwood, which has nice grain and a pleasant scent and is good for woodworking">c</a></sup>and precious stones.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Kings 10:12; 1 Chronicles 23:5; Psalms 92:1–3"> The king used algum tree wood to make terraces for the LORD’s house and for the king’s house, and harps and stringed instruments for the singers. There were none like these seen before in the land of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Kings 10:13; Ephesians 3:20; Psalms 20:4"> King Solomon gave to the queen of Sheba all her desire, whatever she asked, more than that which she had brought to the king. So she turned and went to her own land, she and her servants.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Psalms 72:15; Psalms 68:29; 1 Kings 10:14–28"> Now the weight of gold that came to Solomon in one year was six hundred sixty-six talents<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="A talent is about 30 kilograms or 66 pounds or 965 Troy ounces, so 666 talents is about 20 metric tons">d</a></sup>of gold,</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Psalms 68:29; Psalms 72:10"> in addition to that which the traders and merchants brought. All the kings of Arabia and the governors of the country brought gold and silver to Solomon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Kings 10:16–17; 2 Chronicles 12:9–10"> King Solomon made two hundred large shields of beaten gold. Six hundred shekels<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="A shekel is about 10 grams or about 0.32 Troy ounces, so 600 shekels was about 6 kilograms or about 192 Troy ounces.">e</a></sup>of beaten gold went to one large shield.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Kings 7:2"> He made three hundred shields of beaten gold. Three hundred shekels<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="A shekel is about 10 grams or about 0.32 Troy ounces, so 300 shekels was about 3 kilograms or about 96 Troy ounces.">f</a></sup>of gold went to one shield. The king put them in the House of the Forest of Lebanon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Kings 10:18–20; Psalms 45:8; Revelation 20:11"> Moreover the king made a great throne of ivory, and overlaid it with pure gold.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Numbers 24:9; Revelation 5:5; Numbers 23:24"> There were six steps to the throne, with a footstool of gold, which were fastened to the throne, and armrests on either side by the place of the seat, and two lions standing beside the armrests.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Matthew 19:28; Revelation 21:12"> Twelve lions stood there on the one side and on the other on the six steps. There was nothing like it made in any other kingdom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Kings 10:21; Esther 1:7; 2 Chronicles 9:27"> All King Solomon’s drinking vessels were of gold, and all the vessels of the House of the Forest of Lebanon were of pure gold. Silver was not considered valuable in the days of Solomon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Kings 22:48; Job 39:13; 1 Kings 10:22"> For the king had ships that went to Tarshish with Huram’s servants. Once every three years, the ships of Tarshish came bringing gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Chronicles 1:12; Psalms 89:27; 1 Kings 4:30–31"> So King Solomon exceeded all the kings of the earth in riches and wisdom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Jacob 1:5; Jacob 3:17; Proverbs 2:6"> All the kings of the earth sought the presence of Solomon to hear his wisdom, which God had put in his heart.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Kings 10:25; Job 42:11; Psalms 72:10"> They each brought tribute: vessels of silver, vessels of gold, clothing, armor, spices, horses, and mules every year.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="1 Kings 4:26; 1 Kings 10:26; 2 Chronicles 1:14"> Solomon had four thousand stalls for horses and chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen that he stationed in the chariot cities and with the king at Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="1 Kings 4:21; Genesis 15:18; 1 Kings 4:24"> He ruled over all the kings from the River even to the land of the Philistines, and to the border of Egypt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 Chronicles 1:15–17; Isaiah 9:10; 1 Kings 10:27–29"> The king made silver as common in Jerusalem as stones, and he made cedars to be as abundant as the sycamore trees that are in the lowland.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 Chronicles 1:16; 1 Kings 10:28; Isaiah 31:1"> They brought horses for Solomon out of Egypt and out of all lands.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="1 Chronicles 29:29; 1 Kings 11:41–43; 1 Kings 11:29"> Now the rest of the acts of Solomon, first and last, aren’t they written in the history of Nathan the prophet, and in the prophecy of Ahijah the Shilonite, and in the visions of Iddo the seer concerning Jeroboam the son of Nebat?</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="1 Kings 11:42–43"> Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel forty years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="1 Kings 2:10; 2 Samuel 7:12; 1 Kings 1:21"> Solomon slept with his fathers, and he was buried in his father David’s city; and Rehoboam his son reigned in his place.</span>
