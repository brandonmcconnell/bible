<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Kings 18:17; Jeremiah 25:29–30; Amos 1:8"> In the year in which Tartan, being sent by Sargon, the king of Assyria, came to Ashdod, and fought against Ashdod, and took it,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Micah 1:8; Matthew 3:4; 1 Samuel 19:24"> at that time spake Jehovah through Isaiah the son of Amoz in this manner: Go, and loose the sackcloth from thy loins, and put off thy shoes from thy feet. And he did so, walking naked and barefoot.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Isaiah 8:18; Numbers 14:34; Ezekiel 4:5–6"> And Jehovah said: As my servant Isaiah hath walked naked and barefoot three years, a sign and a token for Egypt and Ethiopia,</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Isaiah 19:4; Jeremiah 13:26; Jeremiah 13:22"> so shall the king of Assyria lead the captives of Egypt, and prisoners of Ethiopia, young and old, naked and barefoot, with their hind parts uncovered, to the shame of the Egyptians.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Isaiah 30:5; Isaiah 30:3; 2 Kings 18:21"> Then shall they be afraid and ashamed on account of Ethiopia their trust, and of Egypt their glory.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Matthew 23:33; 1 Thessalonians 5:3; Hebrews 2:3"> The inhabitant of this coast shall say in that day, “Behold, so is it with them in whom we trusted, and to whom we fled for help, that we might be delivered from the king of Assyria. How then shall we escape?”</span>
