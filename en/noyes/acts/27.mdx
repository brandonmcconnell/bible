<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 25:25; Acts 25:12; Acts 10:1"> And when it was determined that we should sail for Italy, they delivered Paul and certain other prisoners to a centurion named Julius, of the Augustan band.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Acts 19:29; Philemon 1:24; Colossians 4:10"> And going on board a ship of Adramyttium, about to sail along the coasts of Asia, we put to sea; Aristarchus, a Macedonian of Thessalonica, being with us.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Acts 24:23; Acts 28:16; Acts 27:43"> And the next day we landed at Sidon; and Julius treated Paul kindly, and gave him leave to go to his friends, and receive their care.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Acts 4:36; Acts 15:39; Acts 13:4"> And thence putting to sea, we sailed under Cyprus, because the winds were contrary.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Acts 6:9; Acts 13:13; Acts 15:41"> And having sailed over the sea along Cilicia and Pamphylia, we came to Myra, a city of Lycia.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 28:11; Acts 27:1; Acts 6:9"> And there the centurion, finding a ship of Alexandria about to sail for Italy, put us on board of it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Acts 27:21; Titus 1:5; Titus 1:12"> And sailing slowly some days, and having with difficulty arrived over against Cnidus, the wind not permitting us to put in, we sailed under Crete, over against Salmone;</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> and coasting along it with difficulty we came to a place called Fair Havens, near which was the city Lasea.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Numbers 29:7; Leviticus 23:27–29; Leviticus 16:29–31"> And much time having been spent, and the voyage being now dangerous, because the Fast had already gone by, Paul advised them,</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Daniel 2:30; Acts 27:20–26; Acts 27:41–44"> saying, Sirs, I perceive that this voyage will be with injury and much loss, not only of the lading and the ship, but also of our lives.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ezekiel 33:4; Hebrews 11:7; Ezekiel 3:17–18"> But the centurion believed the master and the owner of the ship more than what was spoken by Paul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Psalms 107:30; Acts 27:8"> And as the harbor was not well situated for wintering, the greater part advised to sail thence also, if by any means they might reach Phoenix, a harbor of Crete, looking toward the southwest and northwest, and there winter.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Luke 12:55; Song of Solomon 4:16; Job 37:17"> And when a south wind blew gently, supposing that they had obtained their purpose, they weighed anchor, and coasted along close by Crete.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Mark 4:37; Psalms 107:25–27; Exodus 14:21–27"> But not long after, there rushed against it a tempestuous wind, called Euroclydon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="James 3:4; Acts 27:27"> And the ship being caught, and unable to face the wind, we gave up to it, and were driven along.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> And running under a certain small island called Clauda, we were hardly able to get possession of the boat;</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Acts 27:29; Acts 27:26; Acts 27:41"> which when they had taken up, they used helps, undergirding the ship; and fearing lest they should run into the Syrtis, they lowered the sail and so were driven.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Jonah 1:5; Acts 27:38; Matthew 16:26"> And as we were violently tempest-tossed, the next day they began to lighten the vessel;</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Job 2:4; Luke 9:24–25; Mark 8:35–37"> and the third day we cast out with our own hands the movables of the ship.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Matthew 24:29; Matthew 8:24–25; Jeremiah 2:25"> And as neither sun nor stars had appeared for many days, and no small tempest lay on us, thenceforward all hope that we should be saved was taken away.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Genesis 42:22; Psalms 107:5–6; Acts 27:7"> And after there had been much abstinence from food, Paul stood up in the midst of them and said, Sirs, ye should have hearkened to me, and not have put to sea from Crete, nod thus brought upon yourselves this injury and loss.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 27:25; Acts 27:36; 2 Corinthians 4:8–9"> And now I exhort you to be of good cheer; for there will be no loss of life among you, but only of the ship.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Acts 23:11; Romans 1:9; 2 Timothy 4:17"> For there stood by me this night an angel of God, whose I am, and whom I serve,</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 23:11; 2 Timothy 4:16–17; Matthew 10:28"> saying, Fear not, Paul! Thou must stand before Caesar; and lo, God hath given thee all those that sail with thee.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Romans 4:20–21; Luke 1:45; Numbers 23:19"> Wherefore, sirs, be of good cheer; for I believe God, that it will be just as it hath been told me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 28:1; Acts 27:17; Acts 27:29"> But we must be cast upon some island.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Jonah 1:6; Acts 27:30; Revelation 18:17"> And when the fourteenth night had come on, as we were driven onward in the Adriatic sea, about midnight the sailors suspected that they were near some country;</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> and sounding, they found twenty fathoms; and having gone a little further and sounded again, they found fifteen fathoms;</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Acts 27:17; Hebrews 6:19; Deuteronomy 28:67"> then fearing lest we should fall upon rocks, they cast four anchors out of the stern, and wished for day.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Acts 27:16; Acts 27:32"> And as the sailors were seeking means to escape from the ship, when they had lowered the boat into the sea, under the pretence that they were about to carry out anchors from the foreship,</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 Thessalonians 2:13–14; Psalms 91:11–12; Jeremiah 29:11–13"> Paul said to the centurion and to the soldiers, If these men do not stay in the ship, ye cannot be saved.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Philippians 3:7–9; Luke 16:8"> Then the soldiers cut off the ropes of the boat, and let it fall off.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33"> And while the day was coming on, Paul exhorted them all to take food; saying, This is the fourteenth day that ye have waited, and continued fasting, having taken nothing.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Matthew 10:30; 1 Kings 1:52; Luke 21:18"> Wherefore I exhort you to take food; for this is for your safety; for there shall not a hair be lost from the head of one of you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Matthew 15:36; 1 Corinthians 10:30–31; 1 Timothy 4:3–4"> And having thus spoken, he took bread, and gave thanks to God in presence of them all, and having broken it, he began to eat.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="2 Corinthians 1:4–6; Acts 27:22; Psalms 27:14"> Then were they all of good cheer, and they also took food.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="Acts 2:41; Romans 13:1; 1 Peter 3:20"> And there were of us in the ship in all two hundred and seventy-six souls.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="Jonah 1:5; Matthew 6:25; Acts 27:18–19"> And when they had eaten enough, they lightened the ship, casting out the grain into the sea.</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Acts 28:1"> And when it was day, they did not know the land; but they observed a certain creek having a beach, into which they determined, if possible, to run the ship.</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="Isaiah 33:23; Acts 27:29–30"> And cutting away the anchors, they left them in the sea, and at the same time unfastening the rudder-bands, and hoisting the foresail to the wind, they made toward the beach.</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="2 Corinthians 11:25–26; Ezekiel 27:26; Acts 27:26–29"> And falling into a place having the sea on both sides, they ran the ship aground; and the prow stuck fast, and remained immovable, but the stern was breaking to pieces with the violence &#91;&#91;of the waves&#93;&#93;.</span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="Mark 15:15–20; Ecclesiastes 9:3; Psalms 74:20"> And on the part of the soldiers there was a plan to kill the prisoners, lest any one should swim out and escape.</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43" data-xref="Acts 27:3; Proverbs 16:7; Acts 27:11"> But the centurion, wishing to save Paul, kept them from their purpose, and ordered those who could swim to cast themselves first into the sea, and get to land,</span>
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44" data-xref="Acts 27:22; Psalms 107:28–30; John 6:39–40"> and the rest, some on boards, and others on something from the ship. And in this way it came to pass, that they all escaped safe to land.</span>
