<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> Surely vain are all men by nature, who are ignorant of God, and could not out of the good things that are seen know him that is: neither by considering the works did they acknowledge the workmaster;</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2"> But deemed either fire, or wind, or the swift air, or the circle of the stars, or the violent water, or the lights of heaven, to be the gods which govern the world.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3"> With whose beauty if they being delighted took them to be gods; let them know how much better the Lord of them is: for the first author of beauty has created them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> But if they were astonished at their power and virtue, let them understand by them, how much mightier he is that made them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> For by the greatness and beauty of the creatures proportionably the maker of them is seen.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> But yet for this they are the less to be blamed: for they perhaps err, seeking God, and desirous to find him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7"> For being conversant in his works they search him diligently, and believe their sight: because the things are beautiful that are seen.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> Howbeit neither are they to be pardoned.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> For if they were able to know so much, that they could aim at the world; how did they not sooner find out the Lord thereof?</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> But miserable are they, and in dead things is their hope, who call them gods, which are the works of men's hands, gold and silver, to show are in, and resemblances of beasts, or a stone good for nothing, the work of an ancient hand.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> Now a carpenter that fells timber, after he has sawn down a tree meet for the purpose, and taken off all the bark skilfully round about, and has wrought it handsomely, and made a vessel thereof fit for the service of man's life;</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> And after spending the refuse of his work to dress his meat, has filled himself;</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> And taking the very refuse amongst those which served to no use, being a crooked piece of wood, and full of knots, has carved it diligently, when he had nothing else to do, and formed it by the skill of his understanding, and fashioned it to the image of a man;</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> Or made it like some vile beast, laying it over with vermilion, and with paint colouring it red, and covering every spot therein;</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> And when he had made a convenient room for it, set it in a wall, and made it fast with iron:</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> For he provided for it that it might not fall, knowing that it was unable to help itself; for it is an image, and has need of help:</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> Then makes he prayer for his goods, for his wife and children, and is not ashamed to speak to that which has no life.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> For health he calls upon that which is weak: for life prays to that which is dead; for aid humbly implores that which has least means to help: and for a good journey he asks of that which can’t set a foot forward:</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> And for gaining and getting, and for good success of his hands, asks ability to do of him, that is most unable to do any thing.</span>
