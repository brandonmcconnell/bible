<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> After three years was Judas informed, that Demetrius the son of Seleucus, having entered by the haven of Tripolis with a great power and navy,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2"> Had taken the country, and killed Antiochus, and Lysias his protector.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3"> Now one Alcimus, who had been high priest, and had defiled himself wilfully in the times of their mingling with the Gentiles, seeing that by no means he could save himself, nor have any more access to the holy altar,</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> Came to king Demetrius in the hundred and one and fifties year, presenting to him a crown of gold, and a palm, and also of the boughs which were used solemnly in the temple: and so that day he held his peace.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> Howbeit having gotten opportunity to further his foolish enterprise, and being called into counsel by Demetrius, and asked how the Jews stood affected, and what they intended, he answered thereto:</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> Those of the Jews that he called Assideans, whose captain is Judas Maccabeus, nourish war and are seditious, and will not let the rest be in peace.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7"> Therefore I, being deprived of my ancestors' honour, I mean the high priesthood, am now come here:</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> First, verily for the unfeigned care I have of things pertaining to the king; and secondly, even for that I intend the good of my own countrymen: for all our nation is in no small misery through the unadvised dealing of them aforesaid.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> Therefore, O king, seeing know all these things, be careful for the country, and our nation, which is pressed on every side, according to the clemency that you readily show to all.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> For as long as Judas lives, it is not possible that the state should be quiet.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> This was no sooner spoken of him, but others of the king's friends, being maliciously set against Judas, did more incense Demetrius.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> And forthwith calling Nicanor, who had been master of the elephants, and making him governor over Judea, he sent him forth,</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> Commanding him to kill Judas, and to scatter them that were with him, and to make Alcimus high priest of the great temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> Then the heathen, that had fled out of Judea from Judas, came to Nicanor by flocks, thinking the harm and calamities of the Jews to be their welfare.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> Now when the Jews heard of Nicanor's coming, and that the heathen were up against them, they cast earth upon their heads, and made supplication to him that had established his people for ever, and who always helps his portion with manifestation of his presence.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> So at the commandment of the captain they removed straightways from thence, and came near to them at the town of Dessau.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> Now Simon, Judas' brother, had joined battle with Nicanor, but was somewhat discomfited through the sudden silence of his enemies.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> Nevertheless Nicanor, hearing of the manliness of them that were with Judas, and the courageousness that they had to fight for their country, dared not try the matter by the sword.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> Therefore he sent Posidonius, and Theodotus, and Mattathias, to make peace.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> So when they had taken long advisement thereupon, and the captain had made the multitude acquainted therewith, and it appeared that they were all of one mind, they consented to the covenants,</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> And appointed a day to meet in together by themselves: and when the day came, and stools were set for either of them,</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> Judas placed armed men ready in convenient places, lest some treachery should be suddenly practised by the enemies: so they made a peaceful conference.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> Now Nicanor abode in Jerusalem, and did no hurt, but sent away the people that came flocking to him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24"> And he would not willingly have Judas out of his sight: for he love the man from his heart</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> He prayed him also to take a wife, and to beget children: so he married, was quiet, and took part of this life.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> But Alcimus, perceiving the love that was between them, and considering the covenants that were made, came to Demetrius, and told him that Nicanor was not well affected towards the state; for that he had ordained Judas, a traitor to his realm, to be the king's successor.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27"> Then the king being in a rage, and provoked with the accusations of the most wicked man, wrote to Nicanor, signifying that he was much displeased with the covenants, and commanding him that he should send Maccabeus prisoner in all haste to Antioch.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> When this came to Nicanor's hearing, he was much confounded in himself, and took it grievously that he should make void the articles which were agreed upon, the man being in no fault.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29"> But because there was no dealing against the king, he watched his time to accomplish this thing by policy.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30"> Notwithstanding, when Maccabeus saw that Nicanor began to be churlish to him, and that he entreated him more roughly than he was wont, perceiving that such sour behaviour came not of good, he gathered together not a few of his men, and withdrew himself from Nicanor.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31"> But the other, knowing that he was notably prevented by Judas' policy, came into the great and holy temple, and commanded the priests, that were offering their usual sacrifices, to deliver him the man.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32"> And when they swore that they could not tell where the man was whom he sought,</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33"> He stretched out his right hand towards the temple, and made an oath in this manner: If you⌃ will not deliver me Judas as a prisoner, I will lay this temple of God even with the ground, and I will break down the altar, and erect a notable temple to Bacchus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34"> After these words he departed. Then the priests lifted up their hands towards heaven, and implored him that was ever a defender of their nation, saying in this manner;</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35"> You, O Lord of all things, who have need of nothing, was pleased that the temple of your habitation should be amongst us:</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36"> Therefore now, O holy Lord of all holiness, keep this house ever undefiled, which lately was cleansed, and stop every unrighteous mouth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37"> Now was there accused to Nicanor one Razis, one of the elders of Jerusalem, a lover of his countrymen, and a man of very good report, who for his kindness was called a father of the Jews.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38"> For in the former times, when they mingled not themselves with the Gentiles, he had been accused of Judaism, and did boldly jeopard his body and life with all vehemency for the religion of the Jews.</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39"> So Nicanor, willing to declare the hate that he bare to the Jews, sent above five hundred men of war to take him:</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40"> For he thought by taking him to do the Jews much hurt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41"> Now when the multitude would have taken the tower, and violently broken into the outer door, and bade that fire should be brought to burn it, he being ready to be taken on every side fell upon his sword;</span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42"> Choosing rather to die manfully, than to come into the hands of the wicked, to be abused otherwise than beseemed his noble birth:</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43"> But missing his stroke through haste, the multitude also rushing within the doors, he ran boldly up to the wall, and cast himself down manfully amongst the thickest of them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44"> But they quickly giving back, and a space being made, he fell down into the midst of the void place.</span>
<span className="v" id="v45" data-v="45"> Nevertheless, while there was yet breath within him, being inflamed with anger, he rose up; and though his blood gushed out like spouts of water, and his wounds were grievous, yet he ran through the midst of the throng; and standing upon a steep rock,</span>
<span className="v" id="v46" data-v="46"> When as his blood was now quite gone, he plucked out his bowels, and taking them in both his hands, he cast them upon the throng, and calling upon the Lord of life and spirit to restore him those again, he thus died.</span>
