## Othniel: A Model Leader

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Exodus 34:13; 2 Kings 23:6; 1 Kings 18:19"> The Israelites did evil in the Lord’s sight. They forgot the Lord their God and worshiped the Baals and the Asherahs.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Judges 2:14; Habakkuk 3:7; Judges 4:9"> The Lord was furious with Israel and turned them over to King Cushan-Rishathaim of Aram-Naharaim. They were Cushan-Rishathaim’s subjects for eight years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Judges 3:15; Judges 1:13; Psalms 22:5"> When the Israelites cried out for help to the Lord, he raised up a deliverer for the Israelites who rescued them. His name was Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb’s younger brother.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Judges 13:25; 1 Samuel 11:6; Judges 14:19"> The Lord’s spirit empowered him and he led Israel. When he went to do battle, the Lord handed over to him King Cushan-Rishathaim of Aram and he overpowered him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Judges 5:31; Judges 8:28; Joshua 11:23"> The land had rest for forty years; then Othniel son of Kenaz died.</span>

## Deceit, Assassination, and Deliverance

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Samuel 12:9; Judges 2:19; Judges 2:14"> The Israelites again did evil in the Lord’s sight. The Lord gave King Eglon of Moab control over Israel because they had done evil in the Lord’s sight.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Judges 1:16; Deuteronomy 34:3; Judges 5:14"> Eglon formed alliances with the Ammonites and Amalekites. He came and defeated Israel, and they seized the City of Date Palm Trees.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Deuteronomy 28:47–48; Deuteronomy 28:40; Leviticus 26:23–25"> The Israelites were subject to King Eglon of Moab for eighteen years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Judges 3:9; Psalms 78:34; Isaiah 36:16"> When the Israelites cried out for help to the Lord, he raised up a deliverer for them. His name was Ehud son of Gera the Benjaminite, a left-handed man. The Israelites sent him to King Eglon of Moab with their tribute payment.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Song of Solomon 3:8; Revelation 2:12; Psalms 149:6"> Ehud made himself a sword – it had two edges and was eighteen inches long. He strapped it under his coat on his right thigh.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Ezekiel 34:20; Job 15:27; Jeremiah 50:11"> He brought the tribute payment to King Eglon of Moab. (Now Eglon was a very fat man.)</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> After Ehud brought the tribute payment, he dismissed the people who had carried it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Joshua 4:20; Judges 3:20; Acts 23:18–19"> But he went back once he reached the carved images at Gilgal. He said to Eglon, “I have a secret message for you, O king.” Eglon said, “Be quiet!” All his attendants left.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Jeremiah 10:7; Amos 3:15; Psalms 29:1"> When Ehud approached him, he was sitting in his well-ventilated upper room all by himself. Ehud said, “I have a message from God for you.” When Eglon rose up from his seat,</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Zechariah 13:3; Job 20:25; 1 Samuel 15:33"> Ehud reached with his left hand, pulled the sword from his right thigh, and drove it into Eglon’s belly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> The handle went in after the blade, and the fat closed around the blade, for Ehud did not pull the sword out of his belly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> As Ehud went out into the vestibule, he closed the doors of the upper room behind him and locked them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Samuel 24:3"> When Ehud had left, Eglon’s servants came and saw the locked doors of the upper room. They said, “He must be relieving himself in the well-ventilated inner room.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> They waited so long they were embarrassed, but he still did not open the doors of the upper room. Finally they took the key and opened the doors. Right before their eyes was their master, sprawled out dead on the floor!</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> Now Ehud had escaped while they were delaying. When he passed the carved images, he escaped to Seirah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Judges 6:34; 1 Samuel 13:3; Judges 5:14"> When he reached Seirah, he blew a trumpet in the Ephraimite hill country. The Israelites went down with him from the hill country, with Ehud in the lead.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Judges 12:5; Judges 7:9; Judges 7:15"> He said to them, “Follow me, for the Lord is about to defeat your enemies, the Moabites!” They followed him, captured the fords of the Jordan River opposite Moab, and did not let anyone cross.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Deuteronomy 32:15; Judges 3:17; Psalms 17:10"> That day they killed about ten thousand Moabites – all strong, capable warriors; not one escaped.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Judges 3:11; Judges 5:31"> Israel humiliated Moab that day, and the land had rest for eighty years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Judges 5:6; 1 Corinthians 1:17; 1 Samuel 17:50"> After Ehud came Shamgar son of Anath; he killed six hundred Philistines with an oxgoad and, like Ehud, delivered Israel.</span>
</div>
