<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Nehemiah 2:20; Matthew 10:16; 2 Chronicles 36:22–3"> But Zerubbabel, Jeshua, and the rest of the leaders of Israel said to them, “You have no right to help us build the temple of our God. <span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Ezra 3:3; Nehemiah 6:9; Jeremiah 38:4"> Then the local people began to discourage the people of Judah and to dishearten them from building.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Nahum 1:11; Ezra 5:5–18; Psalms 2:1–2"> They were hiring advisers to oppose them, so as to frustrate their plans, throughout the time of King Cyrus of Persia until the reign of King Darius of Persia.</span>

## Official Complaints Are Lodged Against the Jews

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Esther 1:1; Daniel 9:1; Acts 24:5–9"> At the beginning of the reign of Ahasuerus they filed an accusation against the inhabitants of Judah and Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Kings 18:26; Daniel 2:4; Isaiah 36:11"> And during the reign of Artaxerxes, Bishlam, Mithredath, Tabeel, and the rest of their colleagues wrote to King Artaxerxes of Persia. This letter was first written in Aramaic but then translated.<br />&#91;Aramaic:&#93;</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Ezra 4:9; 2 Samuel 8:17; 2 Samuel 20:25"> Rehum the commander and Shimshai the scribe wrote a letter concerning Jerusalem to King Artaxerxes as follows:</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Ezra 5:6; Ezra 6:6; 2 Kings 17:24"> From Rehum the commander, Shimshai the scribe, and the rest of their colleagues – the judges, the rulers, the officials, the secretaries, the Erechites, the Babylonians, the people of Susa (that is, the Elamites),</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Ezra 4:17; Ezra 4:11; Ezra 7:12"> and the rest of nations whom the great and noble Ashurbanipal deported and settled in the cities of Samaria and other places in Trans-Euphrates.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> (This is a copy of the letter they sent to him:)<br />“To King Artaxerxes, from your servants in Trans-Euphrates:</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Ezra 5:9; 2 Chronicles 36:13; Ezra 5:3"> Now let the king be aware that the Jews who came up to us from you have gone to Jerusalem. They are rebuilding that rebellious and odious city. They are completing its walls and repairing its foundations.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Ezra 7:24; Romans 13:6–7; Nehemiah 5:4"> Let the king also be aware that if this city is built and its walls are completed, no more tax, custom, or toll will be paid, and the royal treasury will suffer loss.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="John 19:12–15; Ezekiel 33:31; John 12:5–6"> In light of the fact that we are loyal to the king, and since it does not seem appropriate to us that the king should sustain damage, we are sending the king this information</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Ezra 4:12; Esther 3:5–8; 2 Kings 24:20–1"> so that he may initiate a search of the records of his predecessors and discover in those records that this city is rebellious and injurious to both kings and provinces, producing internal revolts from long ago. It is for this very reason that this city was destroyed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Ezra 4:20; 1 Kings 4:24; 2 Samuel 8:3"> We therefore are informing the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls are completed, you will not retain control of this portion of Trans-Euphrates.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Romans 1:7; Ezra 5:7; Ezra 4:9–11"> The king sent the following response:<br />“To Rehum the commander, Shimshai the scribe, and the rest of their colleagues who live in Samaria and other parts of Trans-Euphrates: Greetings!</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Nehemiah 8:8"> The letter you sent to us has been translated and read in my presence.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Kings 18:7; Proverbs 25:2; Deuteronomy 13:14"> So I gave orders, and it was determined that this city from long ago has been engaging in insurrection against kings. It has continually engaged in rebellion and revolt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Genesis 15:18; 1 Kings 4:21; 1 Chronicles 18:3"> Powerful kings have been over Jerusalem who ruled throughout the entire Trans-Euphrates and who were the beneficiaries of tribute, custom, and toll.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> Now give orders that these men cease their work and that this city not be rebuilt until such time as I so instruct.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Esther 7:3–4; Esther 3:8–9; Ezra 4:13"> Exercise appropriate caution so that there is no negligence in this matter. Why should danger increase to the point that kings sustain damage?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Romans 3:15; Micah 2:1; Ezra 4:17"> Then, as soon as the copy of the letter from King Artaxerxes was read in the presence of Rehum, Shimshai the scribe, and their colleagues, they proceeded promptly to the Jews in Jerusalem and stopped them with threat of armed force.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Haggai 1:15; Nehemiah 6:9; Ezra 5:5"> So the work on the temple of God in Jerusalem came to a halt. It remained halted until the second year of the reign of King Darius of Persia.</span>
</div>
</div>

</div>
