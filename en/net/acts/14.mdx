<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Acts 28:24; Acts 14:14; Luke 11:21–23"> But the population of the city was divided; some sided with the Jews, and some with the apostles.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Acts 14:19; 2 Timothy 3:11; Acts 17:5"> When both the Gentiles and the Jews (together with their rulers) made an attempt to mistreat them and stone them,</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 14:20–21; Matthew 10:23; 2 Timothy 3:11"> Paul and Barnabas learned about it and fled to the Lycaonian cities of Lystra and the surrounding region.</span>

## Paul and Barnabas at Lystra

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Acts 3:2; John 9:1–2; John 5:3–5"> In Lystra sat a man who could not use his feet, lame from birth, who had never walked.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 3:4; Matthew 15:28; Matthew 9:28–29"> This man was listening to Paul as he was speaking. When Paul stared intently at him and saw he had faith to be healed,</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Isaiah 35:6; John 5:8–9; John 14:12"> he said with a loud voice, “Stand upright on your feet.” And the man leaped up and began walking.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 28:6; Acts 8:10; Acts 12:22"> So when the crowds saw what Paul had done, they shouted in the Lycaonian language, “The gods have come down to us in human form!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 19:35"> They began to call Barnabas Zeus and Paul Hermes, because he was the chief speaker.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Daniel 2:46; Acts 10:25"> The priest of the temple of Zeus, located just outside the city, brought bulls and garlands to the city gates; he and the crowds wanted to offer sacrifices to them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Matthew 26:65; 1 Corinthians 9:5–6; 2 Kings 5:7"> But when the apostles Barnabas and Paul heard about it, they tore their clothes and rushed out into the crowd, shouting,</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Acts 10:26; 1 Corinthians 8:4; James 5:17"> “Men, why are you doing these things? We too are men, with human natures just like you! We are proclaiming the good news to you, so that you should turn from these worthless things to the living God, who made the heaven, the earth, the sea, and everything that is in them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Acts 17:30; Psalms 81:12; Romans 1:28"> In past generations he allowed all the nations to go their own ways,</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Romans 1:19–20; Joel 2:23; Job 5:10"> yet he did not leave himself without a witness by doing good, by giving you rain from heaven and fruitful seasons, satisfying you with food and your hearts with joy.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Jeremiah 44:16–17; Genesis 19:9; Genesis 11:6"> Even by saying these things, they scarcely persuaded the crowds not to offer sacrifice to them.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Corinthians 11:25; 2 Timothy 3:11; Acts 13:50–51"> But Jews came from Antioch and Iconium, and after winning the crowds over, they stoned Paul and dragged him out of the city, presuming him to be dead.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Corinthians 1:9–10; 2 Corinthians 6:9; Acts 14:6"> But after the disciples had surrounded him, he got up and went back into the city. On the next day he left with Barnabas for Derbe.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Paul and Barnabas Return to Antioch in Syria

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Acts 14:19; Acts 14:1; Acts 13:51"> After they had proclaimed the good news in that city and made many disciples, they returned to Lystra,</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="1 Peter 5:10; 2 Timothy 3:12; John 16:33"> They strengthened the souls of the disciples and encouraged them to continue in the faith, saying, “We must enter the kingdom of God through many persecutions.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Titus 1:5; Acts 11:30; James 5:14"> When they had appointed elders for them in the various churches, with prayer and fasting they entrusted them to the protection of the Lord in whom they had believed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 13:13–14; Acts 15:38"> Then they passed through Pisidia and came into Pamphylia,</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> and when they had spoken the word in Perga, they went down to Attalia.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 15:40; Acts 11:19; 2 Corinthians 1:12"> From there they sailed back to Antioch, where they had been commended to the grace of God for the work they had now completed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="1 Corinthians 16:9; Acts 15:12; Colossians 4:3"> When they arrived and gathered the church together, they reported all the things God had done with them, and that he had opened a door of faith for the Gentiles.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Acts 11:26; Acts 15:35"> So they spent considerable time with the disciples.</span>
</div>
</div>
