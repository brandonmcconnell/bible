## Zechariah’s Reign over Israel

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Kings 14:16–17; 2 Kings 14:29–1; 2 Kings 14:21"> In the thirty-eighth year of King Azariah’s reign over Judah, Jeroboam’s son Zechariah became king over Israel. He reigned in Samaria for six months.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Kings 14:24; 2 Kings 13:2; 2 Kings 13:11"> He did evil in the sight of the Lord, as his ancestors had done. He did not repudiate the sinful ways of Jeroboam son of Nebat who encouraged Israel to sin.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Kings 15:28; 2 Kings 15:14; 2 Kings 9:24"> Shallum son of Jabesh conspired against him; he assassinated him in Ibleam and took his place as king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Kings 14:28"> The rest of the events of Zechariah’s reign are recorded in the scroll called the Annals of the Kings of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Kings 10:30; Mark 13:31; 2 Kings 13:13"> His assassination brought to fulfillment the Lord’s word to Jehu, “Four generations of your descendants will rule over Israel.” That is exactly what happened.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Kings 15:1; Matthew 1:8–9; 2 Kings 15:8"> Shallum son of Jabesh became king in the thirty-ninth year of King Uzziah’s reign over Judah. He reigned for one month in Samaria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="1 Kings 14:17; 1 Kings 16:17; 1 Kings 16:15"> Menahem son of Gadi went up from Tirzah to Samaria and attacked Shallum son of Jabesh. He killed him and took his place as king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Kings 14:19; 1 Kings 14:29; 2 Kings 15:11"> The rest of the events of Shallum’s reign, including the conspiracy he organized, are recorded in the scroll called the Annals of the Kings of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Kings 8:12; 1 Kings 4:24; Hosea 13:16"> At that time Menahem came from Tirzah and attacked Tiphsah. He struck down all who lived in the city and the surrounding territory, because they would not surrender. He even ripped open the pregnant women.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Menahem’s Reign over Israel

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> In the thirty-ninth year of King Azariah’s reign over Judah, Menahem son of Gadi became king over Israel. He reigned for twelve years in Samaria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> He did evil in the sight of the Lord; he did not repudiate the sinful ways of Jeroboam son of Nebat who encouraged Israel to sin.<br />During his reign,</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Kings 14:5; 1 Chronicles 5:25–26; 2 Kings 18:16"> Pul king of Assyria invaded the land, and Menahem paid him a thousand talents of silver to gain his support and to solidify his control of the kingdom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Kings 18:14–17; 2 Kings 23:35; 2 Kings 17:3–4"> Menahem got this silver by taxing all the wealthy men in Israel; he took fifty shekels of silver from each one of them and paid it to the king of Assyria. Then the king of Assyria left; he did not stay there in the land.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> The rest of the events of Menahem’s reign, including all his accomplishments, are recorded in the scroll called the Annals of the Kings of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> Menahem passed away and his son Pekahiah replaced him as king.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Pekahiah’s Reign over Israel

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Kings 15:25; 1 Kings 22:51; Job 20:5"> In the fiftieth year of King Azariah’s reign over Judah, Menahem’s son Pekahiah became king over Israel. He reigned in Samaria for two years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="2 Kings 15:9; 2 Kings 15:18"> He did evil in the sight of the Lord; he did not repudiate the sinful ways of Jeroboam son of Nebat who encouraged Israel to sin.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Chronicles 28:6; 2 Kings 9:14; 1 Kings 16:9"> His officer Pekah son of Remaliah conspired against him. He and fifty Gileadites assassinated Pekahiah, as well as Argob and Arieh, in Samaria in the fortress of the royal palace. Pekah then took his place as king.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> The rest of the events of Pekahiah’s reign, including all his accomplishments, are recorded in the scroll called the Annals of the Kings of Israel.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Pekah’s Reign over Israel

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Isaiah 7:4; Isaiah 7:1; 2 Kings 15:23"> In the fifty-second year of King Azariah’s reign over Judah, Pekah son of Remaliah became king over Israel. He reigned in Samaria for twenty years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 Kings 15:18; 2 Kings 13:6; 2 Kings 15:9"> He did evil in the sight of the Lord; he did not repudiate the sinful ways of Jeroboam son of Nebat who encouraged Israel to sin.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="1 Chronicles 5:26; 2 Kings 17:6; 2 Kings 16:7"> During Pekah’s reign over Israel, King Tiglath-pileser of Assyria came and captured Ijon, Abel Beth Maacah, Janoah, Kedesh, Hazor, Gilead, and Galilee, including all the territory of Naphtali. He deported the people to Assyria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="2 Kings 17:1; 2 Chronicles 28:4–6; 2 Kings 16:1"> Hoshea son of Elah conspired against Pekah son of Remaliah. He assassinated him and took his place as king, in the twentieth year of the reign of Jotham son of Uzziah.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31"> The rest of the events of Pekah’s reign, including all his accomplishments, are recorded in the scroll called the Annals of the Kings of Israel.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Jotham’s Reign over Judah

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="1 Chronicles 3:12; 2 Kings 15:7; 2 Chronicles 27:1–9"> In the second year of the reign of Israel’s King Pekah son of Remaliah, Uzziah’s son Jotham became king over Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="2 Chronicles 27:1"> He was twenty-five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned for sixteen years in Jerusalem. His mother was Jerusha the daughter of Zadok.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="2 Chronicles 26:4–5; 2 Kings 15:3–4; 2 Chronicles 27:2"> He did what the Lord approved, just as his father Uzziah had done.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="2 Kings 12:3; 2 Chronicles 23:20; 2 Chronicles 27:3–9"> But the high places were not eliminated; the people continued to offer sacrifices and burn incense on the high places. He built the Upper Gate to the Lord’s temple.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="2 Chronicles 27:4–9; 2 Kings 15:6–7"> The rest of the events of Jotham’s reign, including his accomplishments, are recorded in the scroll called the Annals of the Kings of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="2 Kings 16:5; Isaiah 7:1; Luke 21:28"> In those days the Lord prompted King Rezin of Syria and Pekah son of Remaliah to attack Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="1 Kings 14:20; 1 Kings 14:31; 2 Kings 16:1"> Jotham passed away and was buried with his ancestors in the city of his ancestor David. His son Ahaz replaced him as king.</span>
</div>
