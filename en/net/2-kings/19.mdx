<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Kings 21:27; 2 Kings 18:37; 2 Chronicles 32:20–22"> When King Hezekiah heard this, he tore his clothes, put on sackcloth, and went to the Lord’s temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Isaiah 1:1; Isaiah 2:1; Matthew 4:14"> He sent Eliakim the palace supervisor, Shebna the scribe, and the leading priests, clothed in sackcloth, with this message to the prophet Isaiah son of Amoz:</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Jeremiah 30:5–7; Isaiah 66:9; Hosea 13:13"> “This is what Hezekiah says: ‘This is a day of distress, insults, and humiliation, as when a baby is ready to leave the birth canal, but the mother lacks the strength to push it through.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Samuel 16:12; Romans 9:27; Joshua 14:12"> Perhaps the Lord your God will hear all these things the chief adviser has spoken on behalf of his master, the king of Assyria, who sent him to taunt the living God. When the Lord your God hears, perhaps he will punish him for the things he has said. So pray for this remnant that remains.’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> When King Hezekiah’s servants came to Isaiah,</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Kings 18:35; 2 Kings 18:17; Isaiah 37:6–38"> Isaiah said to them, “Tell your master this: ‘This is what the Lord says: “Don’t be afraid because of the things you have heard – these insults the king of Assyria’s servants have hurled against me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Obadiah 1:1; 2 Kings 7:6; Psalms 18:14–15"> Look, I will take control of his mind; he will receive a report and return to his own land. I will cut him down with a sword in his own land.”’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Kings 18:14; Joshua 10:29; Joshua 12:11"> When the chief adviser heard the king of Assyria had departed from Lachish, he left and went to Libnah, where the king was campaigning.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Samuel 23:27; Isaiah 37:9; 2 Kings 18:17"> The king heard that King Tirhakah of Ethiopia was marching out to fight him. He again sent messengers to Hezekiah, ordering them:</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Kings 18:5; 2 Kings 18:29–30; 2 Chronicles 32:15–19"> “Tell King Hezekiah of Judah this: ‘Don’t let your God in whom you trust mislead you when he says, “Jerusalem will not be handed over to the king of Assyria.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Isaiah 10:8–11; 2 Chronicles 32:13–14; 2 Kings 19:17–18"> Certainly you have heard how the kings of Assyria have annihilated all lands. Do you really think you will be rescued?</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Kings 17:6; Genesis 11:31; Ezekiel 27:23"> Were the nations whom my ancestors destroyed – the nations of Gozan, Haran, Rezeph, and the people of Eden in Telassar – rescued by their gods?</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Kings 18:34; 2 Kings 17:24; Isaiah 11:11"> Where are the king of Hamath, the king of Arpad, and the king of Lair, Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivvah?’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Isaiah 37:14; Psalms 91:1–2; Psalms 123:1–4"> Hezekiah took the letter from the messengers and read it. Then Hezekiah went up to the Lord’s temple and spread it out before the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Isaiah 44:6; 2 Kings 5:15; Daniel 4:34–35"> Hezekiah prayed before the Lord: “Lord God of Israel, who is enthroned on the cherubs! You alone are God over all the kingdoms of the earth. You made the sky and the earth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Chronicles 6:40; Psalms 31:2; 2 Kings 19:4"> Pay attention, Lord, and hear! Open your eyes, Lord, and observe! Listen to the message Sennacherib sent and how he taunts the living God!</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Chronicles 5:26; Job 9:2; 1 Corinthians 14:25"> It is true, Lord, that the kings of Assyria have destroyed the nations and their lands.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Acts 17:29; Isaiah 44:9–20; Jeremiah 10:3–9"> They have burned the gods of the nations, for they are not really gods, but only the product of human hands manufactured from wood and stone. That is why the Assyrians could destroy them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Psalms 83:18; 1 Kings 8:43; Psalms 67:1–2"> Now, O Lord our God, rescue us from his power, so that all the kingdoms of the earth will know that you, Lord, are the only God.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Kings 20:5; 2 Chronicles 32:20–21; Jeremiah 33:3"> Isaiah son of Amoz sent this message to Hezekiah: “This is what the Lord God of Israel says: ‘I have heard your prayer concerning King Sennacherib of Assyria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Lamentations 2:13; Job 16:4; Jeremiah 14:17"> This is what the Lord says about him:<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“The virgin daughter Zion</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">despises you, she makes fun of you;</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">Daughter Jerusalem</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">shakes her head after you.</span><br /></span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Isaiah 5:24; Psalms 71:22; Isaiah 30:11–12"> <span className="pl pl-1">Whom have you taunted and hurled insults at?</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">At whom have you shouted,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">and looked so arrogantly?</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">At the Holy One of Israel!</span><br /></span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 Kings 18:17; Psalms 20:7; Isaiah 10:18"> <span className="pl pl-1">Through your messengers you taunted the sovereign master,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">‘With my many chariots</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">I climbed up the high mountains,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">the slopes of Lebanon.</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">I cut down its tall cedars,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">and its best evergreens.</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">I invaded its most remote regions,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">its thickest woods.</span><br /></span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Isaiah 19:6; Exodus 15:9; 2 Samuel 17:13"> <span className="pl pl-1">I dug wells and drank</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">water in foreign lands.</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">With the soles of my feet I dried up</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">all the rivers of Egypt.’</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Isaiah 45:7; Isaiah 10:5–6; Isaiah 37:26–27"> <br /><span className="pl pl-1">Certainly you must have heard!</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">Long ago I worked it out,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">In ancient times I planned it;</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">and now I am bringing it to pass.</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">The plan is this:</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">Fortified cities will crash</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">into heaps of ruins.</span><br /></span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Jeremiah 51:30; Psalms 102:11; Jeremiah 51:32"> <span className="pl pl-1">Their residents are powerless,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">they are terrified and ashamed.</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">They are as short-lived as plants in the field,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">or green vegetation.</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">They are as short-lived as grass on the rooftops</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">when it is scorched by the east wind.</span><br /></span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Isaiah 37:28–29; Jeremiah 23:23–24; Psalms 139:1–11"> <span className="pl pl-1">I know where you live,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">and everything you do.</span><br /></span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Ezekiel 29:4; Ezekiel 38:4; 2 Kings 19:33"> <span className="pl pl-1">Because you rage against me,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">and the uproar you create has reached my ears;</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">I will put my hook in your nose,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">and my bridle between your lips,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">and I will lead you back the way</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">you came.”</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Leviticus 25:20–22; 2 Kings 20:8–9; Exodus 3:12"> This will be your confirmation that I have spoken the truth: This year you will eat what grows wild, and next year what grows on its own from that. But in the third year you will plant seed and harvest crops; you will plant vines and consume their produce.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="2 Kings 19:4; Isaiah 27:6; 2 Chronicles 32:22–23"> Those who remain in Judah will take root in the ground and bear fruit.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Isaiah 9:7; Romans 11:5; Romans 9:27"> <span className="pl pl-1">For a remnant will leave Jerusalem;</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">survivors will come out of Mount Zion.</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">The intense devotion of the sovereign Lord to his people will accomplish this.</span><br /></span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Isaiah 8:7–10; 2 Samuel 20:15; Isaiah 37:33–35"> <span className="pl pl-1">So this is what the Lord says about the king of Assyria:</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">“He will not enter this city,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">nor will he shoot an arrow here.</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">He will not attack it with his shield-carrying warriors,</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">nor will he build siege works against it.</span><br /></span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="2 Kings 19:28; 2 Kings 19:36"> <span className="pl pl-1">He will go back the way he came.</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">He will not enter this city,” says the Lord.</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="1 Kings 11:12–13; 2 Kings 20:6; 1 Kings 15:4"> I will shield this city and rescue it for the sake of my reputation and because of my promise to David my servant.’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="2 Chronicles 32:21–22; 2 Samuel 24:16; Exodus 12:29–30"> That very night the Lord’s messenger went out and killed 185,000 men in the Assyrian camp. When they got up early the next morning, there were all the corpses.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="Jonah 1:2; 2 Kings 19:7; 2 Kings 19:33"> So King Sennacherib of Assyria broke camp and went on his way. He went home and stayed in Nineveh.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="Ezra 4:2; Genesis 8:4; 2 Kings 19:7"> One day, as he was worshiping in the temple of his god Nisroch, his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer struck him down with the sword. They escaped to the land of Ararat; his son Esarhaddon replaced him as king.</span>
