They built siege ramps all around it. He arrived on the tenth day of the tenth month in the ninth year of Zedekiah’s reign.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2"> The city remained under siege until King Zedekiah’s eleventh year.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:6; Ezekiel 5:12; Jeremiah 38:2"> By the ninth day of the fourth month the famine in the city was so severe the residents had no food.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Ezekiel 33:21; 2 Kings 25:5; Jeremiah 39:2–7"> The enemy broke through the city walls, and all the soldiers tried to escape. They left the city during the night. They went through the gate between the two walls that is near the king’s garden. (The Babylonians were all around the city.) Then they headed for the Jordan Valley.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Isaiah 30:16; Jeremiah 39:5; Amos 2:14–16"> But the Babylonian army chased after the king. They caught up with him in the plains of Jericho, and his entire army deserted him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Kings 23:33; Jeremiah 34:21–22; Jeremiah 21:7"> They captured the king and brought him up to the king of Babylon at Riblah, where he passed sentence on him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Jeremiah 32:4–5; Judges 16:21; Jeremiah 39:6–7"> Zedekiah’s sons were executed while Zedekiah was forced to watch. The king of Babylon then had Zedekiah’s eyes put out, bound him in bronze chains, and carried him off to Babylon.</span>

## Nebuchadnezzar Destroys Jerusalem

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:12–16; Jeremiah 40:1–4; Lamentations 4:12"> On the seventh day of the fifth month, in the nineteenth year of King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon, Nebuzaradan, the captain of the royal guard who served the king of Babylon, arrived in Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Amos 2:5; Psalms 74:3–7; 1 Kings 9:8"> He burned down the Lord’s temple, the royal palace, and all the houses in Jerusalem, including every large house.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Nehemiah 1:3; Jeremiah 39:8; Jeremiah 52:14–23"> The whole Babylonian army that came with the captain of the royal guard tore down the walls that surrounded Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ezekiel 5:2; Jeremiah 15:1–2; Jeremiah 39:9"> Nebuzaradan, the captain of the royal guard, deported the rest of the people who were left in the city, those who had deserted to the king of Babylon, and the rest of the craftsmen.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Jeremiah 40:7; 2 Kings 24:14; Ezekiel 33:24"> But he left behind some of the poor of the land and gave them fields and vineyards.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Chronicles 36:18; Lamentations 1:10; 2 Kings 20:17"> The Babylonians broke the two bronze pillars in the Lord’s temple, as well as the movable stands and the big bronze basin called the “The Sea.” They took the bronze to Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Exodus 27:3; 1 Kings 7:47–50; Exodus 38:3"> They also took the pots, shovels, trimming shears, pans, and all the bronze utensils used by the priests.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Daniel 5:2–3; Numbers 7:13–14; 1 Kings 7:48–51"> The captain of the royal guard took the golden and silver censers and basins.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Kings 7:47"> The bronze of the items that King Solomon made for the Lord’s temple – including the two pillars, the big bronze basin called “The Sea,” the twelve bronze bulls under “The Sea,” and the movable stands – was too heavy to be weighed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Kings 7:15–22; Jeremiah 52:21–23"> Each of the pillars was about twenty-seven feet high. The bronze top of one pillar was about four and a half feet high and had bronze latticework and pomegranate shaped ornaments all around it. The second pillar with its latticework was like it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Jeremiah 29:25; Ezra 7:1; Jeremiah 21:1"> The captain of the royal guard took Seraiah the chief priest and Zephaniah, the priest who was second in rank, and the three doorkeepers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Esther 1:14"> From the city he took a eunuch who was in charge of the soldiers, five of the king’s advisers who were discovered in the city, an official army secretary who drafted citizens for military service, and sixty citizens from the people of the land who were discovered in the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Lamentations 4:16; Jeremiah 52:26–27; 2 Kings 23:33"> Nebuzaradan, captain of the royal guard, took them and brought them to the king of Babylon at Riblah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Deuteronomy 28:64; 2 Kings 23:27; Deuteronomy 4:26"> The king of Babylon ordered them to be executed at Riblah in the territory of Hamath. So Judah was deported from its land.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Gedaliah Appointed Governor

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Jeremiah 39:14; 2 Kings 22:12; Jeremiah 41:2"> Now King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon appointed Gedaliah son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan, as governor over the people whom he allowed to remain in the land of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Jeremiah 40:7–9; Jeremiah 40:11–12; Joshua 18:26"> All of the officers of the Judahite army and their troops heard that the king of Babylon had appointed Gedaliah to govern. So they came to Gedaliah at Mizpah. The officers who came were Ishmael son of Nethaniah, Johanan son of Kareah, Seraiah son of Tanhumeth the Netophathite, and Jaazaniah son of the Maacathite.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Jeremiah 40:9–10; Jeremiah 43:6; 2 Samuel 19:23"> Gedaliah took an oath so as to give them and their troops some assurance of safety. He said, “You don’t need to be afraid to submit to the Babylonian officials. Settle down in the land and submit to the king of Babylon. Then things will go well for you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Zechariah 7:5; Zechariah 8:19; Jeremiah 40:15"> But in the seventh month Ishmael son of Nethaniah, son of Elishama, who was a member of the royal family, came with ten of his men and murdered Gedaliah, as well as the Judeans and Babylonians who were with him at Mizpah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Jeremiah 43:4–7; Jeremiah 41:16–18; Jeremiah 42:14–22"> Then all the people, from the youngest to the oldest, as well as the army officers, left for Egypt, because they were afraid of what the Babylonians might do.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Jehoiachin in Babylon

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:31–34; Genesis 40:20; Genesis 40:13"> In the thirty-seventh year of the exile of King Jehoiachin of Judah, on the twenty-seventh day of the twelfth month, King Evil-Merodach of Babylon, in the first year of his reign, pardoned King Jehoiachin of Judah and released him from prison.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Daniel 2:37; Jeremiah 27:6–11; Daniel 5:18–19"> He spoke kindly to him and gave him a more prestigious position than the other kings who were with him in Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="2 Samuel 9:7; Isaiah 61:3; Genesis 41:42"> Jehoiachin took off his prison clothes and ate daily in the king’s presence for the rest of his life.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Luke 11:3; Matthew 6:11; Nehemiah 11:23"> He was given daily provisions by the king for the rest of his life until the day he died.</span>
</div>
</div>
