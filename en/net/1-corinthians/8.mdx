<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Corinthians 8:7; Isaiah 5:21; Romans 14:3"> With regard to food sacrificed to idols, we know that “we all have knowledge.” Knowledge puffs up, but love builds up.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Corinthians 13:12; 1 Corinthians 3:18; Proverbs 26:12"> If someone thinks he knows something, he does not yet know to the degree that he needs to know.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Revelation 3:8–9; Exodus 33:17; Nahum 1:7"> But if someone loves God, he is known by God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Deuteronomy 4:39; Isaiah 41:24; 1 Corinthians 10:19–20"> With regard then to eating food sacrificed to idols, we know that “an idol in this world is nothing,” and that “there is no God but one.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Thessalonians 2:4; Jeremiah 2:28; Galatians 4:8"> If after all there are so-called gods, whether in heaven or on earth (as there are many gods and many lords),</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Romans 11:36; John 1:3; Malachi 2:10"> yet for us there is one God, the Father, from whom are all things and for whom we live, and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things and through whom we live.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Romans 14:14; 1 Corinthians 10:28–29; Romans 14:22–23"> But this knowledge is not shared by all. And some, by being accustomed to idols in former times, eat this food as an idol sacrifice, and their conscience, because it is weak, is defiled.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Romans 14:17; Hebrews 13:9; 1 Corinthians 6:13"> Now food will not bring us close to God. We are no worse if we do not eat and no better if we do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Galatians 5:13; Romans 14:20–21; 1 Peter 2:16"> But be careful that this liberty of yours does not become a hindrance to the weak.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Corinthians 10:28–29; 1 Corinthians 10:20–21; Romans 14:23"> For if someone weak sees you who possess knowledge dining in an idol’s temple, will not his conscience be “strengthened” to eat food offered to idols?</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Romans 14:15; Romans 15:1–3; 1 Corinthians 8:13"> So by your knowledge the weak brother or sister, for whom Christ died, is destroyed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Matthew 18:6; Matthew 25:45; 1 Corinthians 12:12"> If you sin against your brothers or sisters in this way and wound their weak conscience, you sin against Christ.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Romans 14:21; 2 Corinthians 6:3; 2 Corinthians 11:29"> For this reason, if food causes my brother or sister to sin, I will never eat meat again, so that I may not cause one of them to sin.</span>
