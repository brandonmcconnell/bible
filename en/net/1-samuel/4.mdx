The Ark of the Covenant is Lost to the Philistines Then the Israelites went out to fight the Philistines. They camped at Ebenezer, and the Philistines camped at Aphek.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Lamentations 3:40; 1 Samuel 17:8; Joshua 7:5–8"> The Philistines arranged their forces to fight Israel. As the battle spread out, Israel was defeated by the Philistines, who killed about four thousand men in the battle line in the field.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Timothy 3:5; Numbers 10:35; 1 Corinthians 10:1–5"> When the army came back to the camp, the elders of Israel said, “Why did the Lord let us be defeated today by the Philistines? Let’s take with us the ark of the covenant of the Lord from Shiloh. When it is with us, it will save us from the hand of our enemies.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Samuel 6:2; Numbers 7:89; Psalms 80:1"> So the army sent to Shiloh, and they took from there the ark of the covenant of the Lord of hosts who sits between the cherubim. Now the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phineas, were there with the ark of the covenant of God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Joshua 6:5; Job 20:5; Amos 6:3"> When the ark of the covenant of the Lord arrived at the camp, all Israel shouted so loudly that the ground shook.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Exodus 32:17–18"> When the Philistines heard the sound of the shout, they said, “What is this loud shout in the camp of the Hebrews?” Then they realized that the ark of the Lord had arrived at the camp.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Deuteronomy 32:30; Exodus 14:25; Exodus 15:14–16"> The Philistines were scared because they thought that gods had come to the camp. They said, “Too bad for us! We’ve never seen anything like this!</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Exodus 9:14; Exodus 7:5; Psalms 78:43–51"> Too bad for us! Who can deliver us from the hand of these mighty gods? These are the gods who struck the Egyptians with all sorts of plagues in the desert!</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Corinthians 16:13; Judges 13:1; 2 Samuel 10:12"> Be strong and act like men, you Philistines, or else you will wind up serving the Hebrews the way they have served you! Act like men and fight!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Leviticus 26:17; 2 Kings 14:12; Deuteronomy 28:25"> So the Philistines fought. Israel was defeated; they all ran home. The slaughter was very great; thirty thousand foot soldiers fell in battle.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Samuel 2:34; Psalms 78:64; 1 Samuel 2:32"> The ark of God was taken, and the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phineas, were killed.</span>

## Eli Dies

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Joshua 7:6; Nehemiah 9:1; 2 Samuel 15:32"> On that day a Benjaminite ran from the battle lines and came to Shiloh. His clothes were torn and dirt was on his head.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Samuel 1:9; 1 Samuel 4:18; Joshua 7:9"> When he arrived in Shiloh, Eli was sitting in his chair watching by the side of the road, for he was very worried about the ark of God. As the man entered the city to give his report, the whole city cried out.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> When Eli heard the outcry, he said, “What is this commotion?” The man quickly came and told Eli.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Samuel 3:2; Psalms 90:10; 1 Kings 14:4"> Now Eli was ninety-eight years old and his eyes looked straight ahead; he was unable to see.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Samuel 1:4; 1 Samuel 3:6; Joshua 7:19"> The man said to Eli, “I am the one who came from the battle lines! Just today I fled from the battle lines!” Eli asked, “How did things go, my son?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Samuel 3:11; 1 Samuel 4:10–11"> The messenger replied, “Israel has fled from the Philistines! The army has suffered a great defeat! Your two sons, Hophni and Phineas, are dead! The ark of God has been captured!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Samuel 4:21–22; 1 Peter 4:17–18; Psalms 69:9"> When he mentioned the ark of God, Eli fell backward from his chair beside the gate. He broke his neck and died, for he was old and heavy. He had judged Israel for forty years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> His daughter-in-law, the wife of Phineas, was pregnant and close to giving birth. When she heard that the ark of God was captured and that her father-in-law and her husband were dead, she doubled over and gave birth. But her labor pains were too much for her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Genesis 35:17–18; Psalms 77:2; John 16:21"> As she was dying, the women who were there with her said, “Don’t be afraid! You have given birth to a son!” But she did not reply or pay any attention.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Psalms 26:8; Jeremiah 2:11; Psalms 78:61"> She named the boy Ichabod, saying, “The glory has departed from Israel,” referring to the capture of the ark of God and the deaths of her father-in-law and her husband.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Psalms 137:5–6; John 2:17"> She said, “The glory has departed from Israel, because the ark of God has been captured.”</span>
</div>
