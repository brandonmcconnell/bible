<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Isaiah 51:2; Romans 4:16; 2 Corinthians 11:22"> What then shall we say that Abraham, our ancestor according to the flesh, has discovered regarding this matter?</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:31; 2 Corinthians 11:30; Philippians 3:9"> For if Abraham was declared righteous by the works of the law, he has something to boast about – but not before God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Genesis 15:6; James 2:23; Galatians 3:6–8"> For what does the scripture say? “Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness&#42;.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Romans 11:6; Romans 9:32; Romans 11:35"> Now to the one who works, his pay is not credited due to grace but due to obligation.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Philippians 3:9; Romans 3:22; Titus 3:3–7"> But to the one who does not work, but believes in the one who declares the ungodly righteous, his faith is credited as righteousness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Timothy 1:9; Philippians 3:9; 1 Corinthians 1:30"> So even David himself speaks regarding the blessedness of the man to whom God credits righteousness apart from works:</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Psalms 32:1–2; Psalms 130:3–4; Luke 7:47–50"> <span className="pl pl-1">“Blessed are those whose lawless deeds are forgiven, and whose sins are covered;</span><br /></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Psalms 32:2; 1 Peter 2:24; 2 Corinthians 5:19–20"> <span className="pl pl-1">blessed is the one against whom the Lord will never count sin&#42;.”</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Galatians 3:14; Colossians 3:11; Galatians 3:26–28"> Is this blessedness then for the circumcision or also for the uncircumcision? For we say, “faith was credited to Abraham as righteousness.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Galatians 6:15; Galatians 5:6; Genesis 17:10"> How then was it credited to him? Was he circumcised at the time, or not? No, he was not circumcised but uncircumcised!</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Romans 3:22; Luke 19:9; 2 Corinthians 1:22"> And he received the sign of circumcision as a seal of the righteousness that he had by faith while he was still uncircumcised, so that he would become the father of all those who believe but have never been circumcised, that they too could have righteousness credited to them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Song of Solomon 1:8; John 8:39–40; Proverbs 2:20"> And he is also the father of the circumcised, who are not only circumcised, but who also walk in the footsteps of the faith that our father Abraham possessed when he was still uncircumcised.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Galatians 3:29; Genesis 22:17–18; Genesis 28:14"> For the promise to Abraham or to his descendants that he would inherit the world was not fulfilled through the law, but through the righteousness that comes by faith.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Galatians 3:18–24; Philippians 3:9; Romans 4:16"> For if they become heirs by the law, faith is empty and the promise is nullified.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Romans 5:13; Galatians 3:10; Romans 3:19–20"> For the law brings wrath, because where there is no law there is no transgression either.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Romans 3:24–26; Ephesians 2:8; Galatians 3:22"> For this reason it is by faith so that it may be by grace, with the result that the promise may be certain to all the descendants – not only to those who are under the law, but also to those who have the faith of Abraham, who is the father of us all</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:28; Genesis 17:4–5; John 5:21"> (as it is written, “I have made you the father of many nations”). He is our father in the presence of God whom he believed – the God who makes the dead alive and summons the things that do not yet exist as though they already do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Romans 8:24; Genesis 15:5–6; Acts 27:25"> Against hope Abraham believed in hope with the result that he became the father of many nations according to the pronouncement, “so will your descendants be.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Hebrews 11:11–19; Romans 4:20–21; Genesis 17:17"> Without being weak in faith, he considered his own body as dead (because he was about one hundred years old) and the deadness of Sarah’s womb.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Luke 1:45; 1 Corinthians 16:13; Daniel 10:19"> He did not waver in unbelief about the promise of God but was strengthened in faith, giving glory to God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Luke 1:37; Jeremiah 32:27; Genesis 18:14"> He was fully convinced that what God promised he was also able to do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Romans 4:6; Romans 4:3"> So indeed it was credited to Abraham as righteousness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Corinthians 10:11; Romans 15:4; 1 Corinthians 10:6"> But the statement it was credited to him was not written only for Abraham’s sake,</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 2:24; 1 Peter 1:21; Acts 13:30"> but also for our sake, to whom it will be credited, those who believe in the one who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="1 John 4:9–10; Isaiah 53:5–6; 2 Corinthians 5:21"> He was given over because of our transgressions and was raised for the sake of our justification.</span>
