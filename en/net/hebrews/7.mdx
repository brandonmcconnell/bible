<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Genesis 14:20; Acts 2:29; Acts 7:8–9"> But see how great he must be, if Abraham the patriarch gave him a tithe of his plunder.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Leviticus 27:30–33; 2 Chronicles 31:4–6; Numbers 18:21–32"> And those of the sons of Levi who receive the priestly office have authorization according to the law to collect a tithe from the people, that is, from their fellow countrymen, although they too are descendants of Abraham.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Romans 4:13; Hebrews 7:3–4; Genesis 13:14–17"> But Melchizedek who does not share their ancestry collected a tithe from Abraham and blessed the one who possessed the promise.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Chronicles 30:27; Genesis 48:15–20; 1 Timothy 3:16"> Now without dispute the inferior is blessed by the superior,</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Hebrews 6:20; Hebrews 5:6; Revelation 1:18"> and in one case tithes are received by mortal men, while in the other by him who is affirmed to be alive.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Romans 5:12; Genesis 14:20; Hebrews 7:4"> And it could be said that Levi himself, who receives tithes, paid a tithe through Abraham.</span>

## Jesus and the Priesthood of Melchizedek

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Hebrews 8:7; Galatians 2:21; Galatians 4:9"> So if perfection had in fact been possible through the Levitical priesthood – for on that basis the people received the law – what further need would there have been for another priest to arise, said to be in the order of Melchizedek and not in Aaron’s order?</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Jeremiah 31:31–34; Isaiah 66:21; Ezekiel 16:61"> For when the priesthood changes, a change in the law must come as well.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Hebrews 7:11; Hebrews 7:14; Numbers 17:5"> Yet the one these things are spoken about belongs to a different tribe, and no one from that tribe has ever officiated at the altar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Micah 5:2; Isaiah 11:1; Luke 3:33"> For it is clear that our Lord is descended from Judah, yet Moses said nothing about priests in connection with that tribe.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Psalms 110:4; Hebrews 7:17–21; Hebrews 7:3"> And this is even clearer if another priest arises in the likeness of Melchizedek,</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Revelation 1:18; Colossians 2:20; Hebrews 7:28"> who has become a priest not by a legal regulation about physical descent but by the power of an indestructible life.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Psalms 110:4; Hebrews 5:6; Hebrews 7:21"> For here is the testimony about him: “You are a priest forever in the order of Melchizedek.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Romans 8:3; Acts 13:39; Galatians 4:9"> On the one hand a former command is set aside because it is weak and useless,</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Acts 13:39; Hebrews 4:16; Hebrews 9:9"> for the law made nothing perfect. On the other hand a better hope is introduced, through which we draw near to God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Numbers 18:7; Hebrews 5:6; Psalms 110:4"> And since this was not done without a sworn affirmation – for the others have become priests without a sworn affirmation,</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Psalms 110:4; Hebrews 7:17; Hebrews 6:16–18"> but Jesus did so with a sworn affirmation by the one who said to him, “The Lord has sworn and will not change his mind, ‘You are a priest forever’” –</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="1 Corinthians 11:25; Genesis 44:32; Hebrews 8:6–12"> accordingly Jesus has become the guarantee of a better covenant.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Nehemiah 12:10–11; 1 Chronicles 6:3–14; Hebrews 7:8"> And the others who became priests were numerous, because death prevented them from continuing in office,</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="John 12:34; Hebrews 7:28; Isaiah 9:6–7"> but he holds his priesthood permanently since he lives forever.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Romans 8:34; 1 John 2:1–2; 1 Timothy 2:5"> So he is able to save completely those who come to God through him, because he always lives to intercede for them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Corinthians 5:21; Hebrews 4:14–15; 1 Peter 2:22"> For it is indeed fitting for us to have such a high priest: holy, innocent, undefiled, separate from sinners, and exalted above the heavens.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Hebrews 9:28; Hebrews 9:12; Hebrews 9:14"> He has no need to do every day what those priests do, to offer sacrifices first for their own sins and then for the sins of the people, since he did this in offering himself once for all.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Hebrews 2:10; Hebrews 7:21; Hebrews 1:2"> For the law appoints as high priests men subject to weakness, but the word of solemn affirmation that came after the law appoints a son made perfect forever.</span>
</div>
