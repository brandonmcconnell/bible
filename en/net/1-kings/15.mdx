<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Chronicles 13:2; 2 Chronicles 11:20–22; 1 Kings 15:13"> He ruled for three years in Jerusalem. His mother was Maacah, the daughter of Abishalom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Kings 11:4; Psalms 119:80; 1 Kings 3:14"> He followed all the sinful practices of his father before him. He was not wholeheartedly devoted to the Lord his God, as his ancestor David had been.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Kings 11:36; 2 Chronicles 21:7; Isaiah 37:35"> Nevertheless for David’s sake the Lord his God maintained his dynasty in Jerusalem by giving him a son to succeed him and by protecting Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Kings 14:8; 2 Samuel 11:15–17; 1 Kings 15:3"> He did this because David had done what he approved and had not disregarded any of his commandments his entire lifetime, except for the incident involving Uriah the Hittite.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Kings 14:30"> Rehoboam and Jeroboam were continually at war with each other throughout Abijah’s lifetime.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Chronicles 13:2–22; 1 Kings 14:29"> The rest of the events of Abijah’s reign, including all his accomplishments, are recorded in the scroll called the Annals of the Kings of Judah. Abijah and Jeroboam had been at war with each other.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Chronicles 14:1; 1 Kings 14:31; Matthew 1:7–8"> Abijah passed away and was buried in the city of David. His son Asa replaced him as king.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Asa’s Reign over Judah

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> In the twentieth year of Jeroboam’s reign over Israel, Asa became the king of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Kings 15:2; 1 Kings 15:13; 2 Chronicles 13:2"> He ruled for forty-one years in Jerusalem. His grandmother was Maacah daughter of Abishalom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Chronicles 14:2; 2 Chronicles 15:17; 1 Kings 15:3"> Asa did what the Lord approved like his ancestor David had done.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Kings 22:46; 1 Kings 14:23–24; 2 Chronicles 14:2–5"> He removed the male cultic prostitutes from the land and got rid of all the disgusting idols his ancestors had made.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Exodus 32:20; Deuteronomy 7:5; Zechariah 13:3"> He also removed Maacah his grandmother from her position as queen because she had made a loathsome Asherah pole. Asa cut down her Asherah pole and burned it in the Kidron Valley.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="1 Kings 22:43; 1 Kings 8:61; 1 Kings 15:3"> The high places were not eliminated, yet Asa was wholeheartedly devoted to the Lord throughout his lifetime.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Kings 7:51; 2 Chronicles 14:13; 2 Chronicles 15:18"> He brought the holy items that he and his father had made into the Lord’s temple, including the silver, gold, and other articles.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Kings 15:32; 1 Kings 14:30; 2 Chronicles 16:1–6"> Now Asa and King Baasha of Israel were continually at war with each other.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Kings 12:27; 2 Chronicles 16:1–6; Joshua 18:25"> King Baasha of Israel attacked Judah and established Ramah as a military outpost to prevent anyone from leaving or entering the land of King Asa of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Kings 14:26; 2 Kings 12:18; 1 Kings 11:23–24"> Asa took all the silver and gold that was left in the treasuries of the Lord’s temple and of the royal palace and handed it to his servants. He then told them to deliver it to Ben Hadad son of Tabrimmon, the son of Hezion, king of Syria, ruler in Damascus, along with this message:</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Romans 1:31; 2 Chronicles 16:7; Romans 3:8"> “I want to make a treaty with you, like the one our fathers made. See, I have sent you silver and gold as a present. Break your treaty with King Baasha of Israel, so he will retreat from my land.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Kings 15:29; Judges 18:29; Joshua 11:2"> Ben Hadad accepted King Asa’s offer and ordered his army commanders to attack the cities of Israel. They conquered Ijon, Dan, Abel Beth Maacah, and all the territory of Naphtali, including the region of Kinnereth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Kings 14:17; 1 Kings 16:15–18; 2 Chronicles 16:5"> When Baasha heard the news, he stopped fortifying Ramah and settled down in Tirzah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Joshua 21:17; Joshua 18:24; Joshua 18:26"> King Asa ordered all the men of Judah (no exemptions were granted) to carry away the stones and wood that Baasha had used to build Ramah. King Asa used the materials to build up Geba (in Benjamin) and Mizpah.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 Chronicles 16:11–14; 1 Kings 14:29–31; 1 Kings 15:7–8"> The rest of the events of Asa’s reign, including all his successes and accomplishments, as well as a record of the cities he built, are recorded in the scroll called the Annals of the Kings of Judah. Yet when he was very old he developed a foot disease.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Matthew 1:8; 1 Kings 22:41–43; 2 Chronicles 17:1–9"> Asa passed away and was buried with his ancestors in the city of his ancestor David. His son Jehoshaphat replaced him as king.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Nadab’s Reign over Israel

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="1 Kings 14:20; 1 Kings 14:12"> In the second year of Asa’s reign over Judah, Jeroboam’s son Nadab became the king of Israel; he ruled Israel for two years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="1 Kings 14:16; 1 Kings 15:34; 1 Kings 15:30"> He did evil in the sight of the Lord. He followed in his father’s footsteps and encouraged Israel to sin.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Joshua 19:44; Joshua 21:23; 1 Kings 14:14"> Baasha son of Ahijah, from the tribe of Issachar, conspired against Nadab and assassinated him in Gibbethon, which was in Philistine territory. This happened while Nadab and all the Israelite army were besieging Gibbethon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Deuteronomy 32:35"> Baasha killed him in the third year of Asa’s reign over Judah and replaced him as king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="1 Kings 14:9–16; 2 Kings 19:25; 2 Kings 10:10–11"> When he became king, he executed Jeroboam’s entire family. He wiped out everyone who breathed, just as the Lord had predicted through his servant Ahijah the Shilonite.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="1 Kings 15:26; 1 Kings 14:22; 1 Kings 14:9–16"> This happened because of the sins which Jeroboam committed and which he made Israel commit. These sins angered the Lord God of Israel.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="1 Kings 14:19; 1 Kings 16:20; 1 Kings 16:14"> The rest of the events of Nadab’s reign, including all his accomplishments, are recorded in the scroll called the Annals of the Kings of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="1 Kings 15:16"> Asa and King Nadab of Israel were continually at war with each other.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Baasha’s Reign over Israel

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="1 Kings 16:8"> In the third year of Asa’s reign over Judah, Baasha son of Ahijah became king over all Israel in Tirzah; he ruled for twenty-four years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="1 Kings 15:26; 1 Kings 14:16; 1 Kings 12:28–29"> He did evil in the sight of the Lord; he followed in Jeroboam’s footsteps and encouraged Israel to sin.</span>
</div>
</div>
