<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Exodus 18:21; 1 Corinthians 12:28; 2 Samuel 8:15–18"> These were his officials: Azariah son of Zadok was the priest.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:16; Isaiah 62:6; 1 Chronicles 18:15"> Elihoreph and Ahijah, the sons of Shisha, wrote down what happened. Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was in charge of the records.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Kings 2:35; 1 Kings 2:26–27"> Benaiah son of Jehoiada was commander of the army. Zadok and Abiathar were priests.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Chronicles 27:33; 2 Samuel 15:37; 2 Samuel 16:16"> Azariah son of Nathan was supervisor of the district governors. Zabud son of Nathan was a priest and adviser to the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Kings 9:15; 2 Samuel 20:24; 1 Kings 12:18"> Ahishar was supervisor of the palace. Adoniram son of Abda was supervisor of the work crews.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Chronicles 27:1–15"> Solomon had twelve district governors appointed throughout Israel who acquired supplies for the king and his palace. Each was responsible for one month in the year.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Joshua 24:33; Judges 19:1; Judges 17:1"> These were their names: Ben-Hur was in charge of the hill country of Ephraim.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Joshua 21:16; 1 Samuel 6:20; 1 Samuel 6:12"> Ben-Deker was in charge of Makaz, Shaalbim, Beth Shemesh, and Elon Beth Hanan.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Joshua 15:35; Joshua 12:17; Joshua 17:2"> Ben-Hesed was in charge of Arubboth; he controlled Socoh and all the territory of Hepher.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Joshua 11:2; Joshua 12:23; Joshua 17:11"> Ben-Abinadab was in charge of Naphath Dor. (He was married to Solomon’s daughter Taphath.)</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Joshua 17:11; Judges 5:19; Joshua 3:16"> Baana son of Ahilud was in charge of Taanach and Megiddo, as well as all of Beth Shan next to Zarethan below Jezreel, from Beth Shan to Abel Meholah and on past Jokmeam.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Deuteronomy 3:4; Numbers 32:41; 1 Kings 22:3"> Ben-Geber was in charge of Ramoth Gilead; he controlled the tent villages of Jair son of Manasseh in Gilead, as well as the region of Argob in Bashan, including sixty large walled cities with bronze bars locking their gates.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Joshua 13:26; 2 Samuel 17:24; 2 Samuel 2:8"> Ahinadab son of Iddo was in charge of Mahanaim.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Joshua 19:32–39; 2 Samuel 15:27; 1 Samuel 18:18"> Ahimaaz was in charge of Naphtali. (He married Solomon’s daughter Basemath.)</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Joshua 19:24–31; 2 Samuel 15:32"> Baana son of Hushai was in charge of Asher and Aloth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Joshua 19:17–23"> Jehoshaphat son of Paruah was in charge of Issachar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Kings 1:8; Joshua 18:20–28; Zechariah 12:13"> Shimei son of Ela was in charge of Benjamin.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Deuteronomy 2:26–17; Joshua 13:9–12; Numbers 21:21–35"> Geber son of Uri was in charge of the land of Gilead (the territory which had once belonged to King Sihon of the Amorites and to King Og of Bashan). He was sole governor of the area.</span>

## Solomon’s Wealth and Fame

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Genesis 22:17; 1 Kings 3:8; Genesis 32:12"> The people of Judah and Israel were as innumerable as the sand on the seashore; they had plenty to eat and drink and were happy.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Genesis 15:18; Joshua 1:4; Psalms 68:29"> Solomon ruled all the kingdoms from the Euphrates River to the land of the Philistines, as far as the border of Egypt. These kingdoms paid tribute as Solomon’s subjects throughout his lifetime.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> Each day Solomon’s royal court consumed thirty cors of finely milled flour, sixty cors of cereal,</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Nehemiah 5:17–18"> ten calves fattened in the stall, twenty calves from the pasture, and a hundred sheep, not to mention rams, gazelles, deer, and well-fed birds.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Chronicles 22:9; Psalms 72:11; Psalms 72:3"> His royal court was so large because he ruled over all the kingdoms west of the Euphrates River from Tiphsah to Gaza; he was at peace with all his neighbors.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Micah 4:4; Zechariah 3:10; Judges 20:1"> All the people of Judah and Israel had security; everyone from Dan to Beer Sheba enjoyed the produce of their vines and fig trees throughout Solomon’s lifetime.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Chronicles 1:14; 2 Chronicles 9:25; 2 Samuel 8:4"> Solomon had 4,000 stalls for his chariot horses and 12,000 horses.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27"> The district governors acquired supplies for King Solomon and all who ate in his royal palace. Each was responsible for one month in the year; they made sure nothing was lacking.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Esther 8:10; Esther 8:14; Micah 1:13"> Each one also brought to the assigned location his quota of barley and straw for the various horses.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="1 Kings 3:12; James 3:17; 1 Kings 4:20"> God gave Solomon wisdom and very great discernment; the breadth of his understanding was as infinite as the sand on the seashore.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Daniel 5:11–12; Genesis 25:6; Daniel 1:20"> Solomon was wiser than all the men of the east and all the sages of Egypt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="1 Chronicles 2:6; 1 Chronicles 15:19; 1 Kings 3:12"> He was wiser than any man, including Ethan the Ezrahite or Heman, Calcol, and Darda, the sons of Mahol. He was famous in all the neighboring nations.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Song of Solomon 1:1–17; Matthew 13:35; Proverbs 1:1–31"> He composed 3,000 proverbs and 1,005 songs.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="2 Kings 19:23; Psalms 51:7; Psalms 92:12"> He produced manuals on botany, describing every kind of plant, from the cedars of Lebanon to the hyssop that grows on walls. He also produced manuals on biology, describing animals, birds, insects, and fish.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="2 Chronicles 9:23; 1 Kings 10:1; 2 Chronicles 9:1"> People from all nations came to hear Solomon’s display of wisdom; they came from all the kings of the earth who heard about his wisdom.</span>
</div>
</div>
