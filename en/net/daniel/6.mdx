<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Exodus 18:21–22; Daniel 5:31; Esther 1:1"> It seemed like a good idea to Darius to appoint over the kingdom 120 satraps who would be in charge of the entire kingdom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Daniel 2:48–49; Daniel 5:29; Esther 7:4"> Over them would be three supervisors, one of whom was Daniel. These satraps were accountable to them, so that the king’s interests might not incur damage.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Daniel 5:14; Proverbs 22:29; Genesis 41:38–41"> Now this Daniel was distinguishing himself above the other supervisors and the satraps, for he had an extraordinary spirit. In fact, the king intended to appoint him over the entire kingdom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Peter 3:16; Philippians 2:15; 1 Peter 2:12"> Consequently the supervisors and satraps were trying to find some pretext against Daniel in connection with administrative matters. But they were unable to find any such damaging evidence, because he was trustworthy and guilty of no negligence or corruption.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Acts 24:13–16; Esther 3:8; Acts 24:20–21"> So these men concluded, “We won’t find any pretext against this man Daniel unless it is in connection with the law of his God.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Daniel 2:4; Nehemiah 2:3; Daniel 6:21"> So these supervisors and satraps came by collusion to the king and said to him, “O King Darius, live forever!</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Psalms 59:3; Psalms 62:4; Daniel 3:27"> To all the supervisors of the kingdom, the prefects, satraps, counselors, and governors it seemed like a good idea for a royal edict to be issued and an interdict to be enforced. For the next thirty days anyone who prays to any god or human other than you, O king, should be thrown into a den of lions.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Daniel 6:15; Esther 1:19; Daniel 6:12"> Now let the king issue a written interdict so that it cannot be altered, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which cannot be changed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Psalms 146:3; Psalms 118:9; Psalms 62:9–10"> So King Darius issued the written interdict.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Acts 5:29; Psalms 55:17; 1 Thessalonians 5:17–18"> When Daniel realized that a written decree had been issued, he entered his home, where the windows in his upper room opened toward Jerusalem. Three times daily he was kneeling and offering prayers and thanks to his God just as he had been accustomed to do previously.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Psalms 37:32–33; Psalms 10:9; Daniel 6:6"> Then those officials who had gone to the king came by collusion and found Daniel praying and asking for help before his God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Daniel 6:8; Daniel 3:8–12; Esther 1:19"> So they approached the king and said to him, “Did you not issue an edict to the effect that for the next thirty days anyone who prays to any god or human other than to you, O king, would be thrown into a den of lions?” The king replied, “That is correct, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which cannot be changed.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Daniel 3:12; Acts 5:29; Daniel 2:25"> Then they said to the king, “Daniel, who is one of the captives from Judah, pays no attention to you, O king, or to the edict that you issued. Three times daily he offers his prayer.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Mark 6:26; Matthew 27:17–24; 2 Samuel 3:28–29"> When the king heard this, he was very upset and began thinking about how he might rescue Daniel. Until late afternoon he was struggling to find a way to rescue him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Daniel 6:12; Psalms 94:20–21; Daniel 6:8"> Then those men came by collusion to the king and said to him, “Recall, O king, that it is a law of the Medes and Persians that no edict or decree that the king issues can be changed.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Psalms 37:39–40; Job 5:19; Psalms 91:14–16"> So the king gave the order, and Daniel was brought and thrown into a den of lions. The king consoled Daniel by saying, “Your God whom you continually serve will rescue you!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Lamentations 3:53; Acts 12:4; Matthew 27:60–66"> Then a stone was brought and placed over the opening to the den. The king sealed it with his signet ring and with those of his nobles so that nothing could be changed with regard to Daniel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Esther 6:1; Daniel 2:1; 2 Samuel 12:16–17"> Then the king departed to his palace. But he spent the night without eating, and no diversions were brought to him. He was unable to sleep.</span>

## God Rescues Daniel from the Lions

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp poetry">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 3:5; Mark 16:2; Matthew 28:1"> In the morning, at the earliest sign of daylight, the king got up and rushed to the lions’ den.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Chronicles 16:11; Hebrews 7:25; Daniel 6:27"> As he approached the den, he called out to Daniel in a worried voice, “Daniel, servant of the living God, was your God whom you continually serve able to rescue you from the lions?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Daniel 2:4; Daniel 6:6; Nehemiah 2:3"> Then Daniel spoke to the king, “O king, live forever!</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Timothy 4:17; Hebrews 11:33; Acts 12:11"> My God sent his angel and closed the lions’ mouths so that they have not harmed me, because I was found to be innocent before him. Nor have I done any harm to you, O king.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Chronicles 5:20; Mark 9:23; Daniel 3:27–28"> Then the king was delighted and gave an order to haul Daniel up from the den. So Daniel was hauled up out of the den. He had no injury of any kind, because he had trusted in his God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Isaiah 38:13; Esther 7:10; Deuteronomy 24:16"> The king gave another order, and those men who had maliciously accused Daniel were brought and thrown into the lions’ den – they, their children, and their wives. They did not even reach the bottom of the den before the lions overpowered them and crushed all their bones.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Daniel 4:1; Ezra 1:1–2; Esther 3:12"> Then King Darius wrote to all the peoples, nations, and language groups who were living in all the land: “Peace and prosperity!</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Daniel 4:34; Luke 1:33; Daniel 3:29"> I have issued an edict that throughout all the dominion of my kingdom people are to revere and fear the God of Daniel.<br /><span className="pl pl-1">“For he is the living God;</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">he endures forever.</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">His kingdom will not be destroyed;</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">his authority is forever.</span><br /></span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Daniel 4:2–3; Psalms 18:50; Acts 4:30"> <span className="pl pl-1">He rescues and delivers</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">and performs signs and wonders</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">in the heavens and on the earth.</span><br /><span className="pl pl-1">He has rescued Daniel from the power of the lions!”</span></span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Daniel 1:21; 2 Chronicles 36:22–2; Isaiah 44:28–1"> So this Daniel prospered during the reign of Darius and the reign of Cyrus the Persian.</span>
</div>
