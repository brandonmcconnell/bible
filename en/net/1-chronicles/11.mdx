## David Conquers Jerusalem

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Judges 1:21; Genesis 15:21; Joshua 15:8"> David and the whole Israelite army advanced to Jerusalem (that is, Jebus). (The Jebusites, the land’s original inhabitants, lived there.)</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Chronicles 5:2; 1 Kings 8:1; Psalms 87:2"> The residents of Jebus said to David, “You cannot invade this place!” But David captured the fortress of Zion (that is, the City of David).</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:16; 2 Samuel 20:23; 2 Samuel 3:27"> David said, “Whoever attacks the Jebusites first will become commanding general!” So Joab son of Zeruiah attacked first and became commander.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Chronicles 11:5; 2 Samuel 5:7; Psalms 2:6"> David lived in the fortress; for this reason it is called the City of David.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Judges 9:6; 1 Kings 11:27; 2 Kings 12:20"> He built up the city around it, from the terrace to the surrounding walls; Joab restored the rest of the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Samuel 3:1; Isaiah 41:10; 1 Chronicles 9:20"> David’s power steadily grew, for the Lord who commands armies was with him.</span>
</div>

## David’s Warriors

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Chronicles 11:3; 1 Samuel 16:1; 1 Chronicles 12:38"> These were the leaders of David’s warriors who helped establish and stabilize his rule over all Israel, in accordance with the Lord’s word.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Samuel 23:8; 1 Chronicles 27:2; 1 Chronicles 12:18"> This is the list of David’s warriors: Jashobeam, a Hacmonite, was head of the officers. He killed three hundred men with his spear in a single battle.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Chronicles 27:4; 2 Samuel 23:17–19; 2 Samuel 23:23"> Next in command was Eleazar son of Dodo the Ahohite. He was one of the three elite warriors.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Samuel 23:11–12; 1 Samuel 17:1"> He was with David in Pas Dammim when the Philistines assembled there for battle. In an area of the field that was full of barley, the army retreated before the Philistines,</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Psalms 144:10; 2 Kings 5:1; 1 Samuel 14:23"> but then they made a stand in the middle of that area. They defended it and defeated the Philistines; the Lord gave them a great victory.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Chronicles 14:9; 2 Samuel 5:18; Isaiah 17:5"> Three of the thirty leaders went down to David at the rocky cliff at the cave of Adullam, while a Philistine force was camped in the Valley of Rephaim.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Samuel 10:5; 1 Samuel 13:4; 1 Samuel 13:23"> David was in the stronghold at the time, while a Philistine garrison was in Bethlehem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="John 4:14; Isaiah 12:3; 2 Samuel 23:15–16"> David was thirsty and said, “How I wish someone would give me some water to drink from the cistern in Bethlehem near the city gate!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Acts 21:13; 1 Samuel 19:5; Acts 20:24"> So the three elite warriors broke through the Philistine forces and drew some water from the cistern in Bethlehem near the city gate. They carried it back to David, but David refused to drink it. He poured it out as a drink offering to the Lord</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Samuel 23:17; Psalms 72:14; Judges 9:17"> and said, “God forbid that I should do this! Should I drink the blood of these men who risked their lives?” Because they risked their lives to bring it to him, he refused to drink it. Such were the exploits of the three elite warriors.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Samuel 26:6; 1 Chronicles 2:16; 2 Samuel 20:6"> Abishai the brother of Joab was head of the three elite warriors. He killed three hundred men with his spear and gained fame along with the three elite warriors.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Corinthians 15:41; Matthew 13:8"> From the three he was given double honor and he became their officer, even though he was not one of them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Samuel 23:20–23; 2 Samuel 8:18; Joshua 15:21"> Benaiah son of Jehoiada was a brave warrior from Kabzeel who performed great exploits. He struck down the two sons of Ariel of Moab; he also went down and killed a lion inside a cistern on a snowy day.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Samuel 17:7; 1 Samuel 17:4; Deuteronomy 3:11"> He even killed an Egyptian who was seven and a half feet tall. The Egyptian had a spear as big as the crossbeam of a weaver’s loom; Benaiah attacked him with a club. He grabbed the spear out of the Egyptian’s hand and killed him with his own spear.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24"> Such were the exploits of Benaiah son of Jehoiada, who gained fame along with the three elite warriors.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Samuel 20:23"> He received honor from the thirty warriors, though he was not one of the three elite warriors. David put him in charge of his bodyguard.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="1 Chronicles 27:7; 2 Samuel 21:19; 2 Samuel 2:18–23"> The mighty warriors were: Asahel the brother of Joab, Elhanan son of Dodo, from Bethlehem,</span>
