<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Deuteronomy 1:28; John 5:44; Luke 1:51"> Then they said, “Come, let’s build ourselves a city and a tower with its top in the heavens so that we may make a name for ourselves. Otherwise we will be scattered across the face of the entire earth.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Genesis 18:21; Psalms 33:13–14; John 3:13"> But the Lord came down to see the city and the tower that the people had started building.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Psalms 2:1–4; Genesis 3:22; Genesis 11:1"> And the Lord said, “If as one people all sharing a common language they have begun to do this, then nothing they plan to do will be beyond them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Psalms 33:10; Psalms 55:9; Job 5:12–13"> Come, let’s go down and confuse their language so they won’t be able to understand each other.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Luke 1:51; Deuteronomy 32:8; Genesis 10:25"> So the Lord scattered them from there across the face of the entire earth, and they stopped building the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Genesis 10:10; Genesis 10:5; Genesis 10:20"> That is why its name was called Babel – because there the Lord confused the language of the entire world, and from there the Lord scattered them across the face of the entire earth.</span>

## The Genealogy of Shem

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Chronicles 1:17–27; Genesis 10:21–22; Luke 3:34–36"> This is the account of Shem. Shem was 100 old when he became the father of Arphaxad, two years after the flood.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Genesis 9:7; Psalms 144:12; Genesis 1:28"> And after becoming the father of Arphaxad, Shem lived 500 years and had other sons and daughters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Luke 3:36"> When Arphaxad had lived 35 years, he became the father of Shelah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> And after he became the father of Shelah, Arphaxad lived 403 years and had other sons and daughters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> When Shelah had lived 30 years, he became the father of Eber.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> And after he became the father of Eber, Shelah lived 403 years and had other sons and daughters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Genesis 10:21; 1 Chronicles 1:19; Numbers 24:24"> When Eber had lived 34 years, he became the father of Peleg.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> And after he became the father of Peleg, Eber lived 430 years and had other sons and daughters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Luke 3:35"> When Peleg had lived 30 years, he became the father of Reu.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> And after he became the father of Reu, Peleg lived 209 years and had other sons and daughters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Luke 3:35"> When Reu had lived 32 years, he became the father of Serug.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> And after he became the father of Serug, Reu lived 207 years and had other sons and daughters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Joshua 24:2"> When Serug had lived 30 years, he became the father of Nahor.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> And after he became the father of Nahor, Serug lived 200 years and had other sons and daughters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Luke 3:34; Joshua 24:2"> When Nahor had lived 29 years, he became the father of Terah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> And after he became the father of Terah, Nahor lived 119 years and had other sons and daughters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Joshua 24:2; 1 Chronicles 1:26–27; Genesis 22:20–24"> When Terah had lived 70 years, he became the father of Abram, Nahor, and Haran.</span>
</div>
</div>

## The Record of Terah

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Genesis 12:4; Genesis 14:12; Genesis 11:31"> This is the account of Terah. Terah became the father of Abram, Nahor, and Haran. And Haran became the father of Lot.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Genesis 15:7; Nehemiah 9:7; Acts 7:2–4"> Haran died in the land of his birth, in Ur of the Chaldeans, while his father Terah was still alive.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Genesis 17:15; Genesis 22:20; Genesis 24:15"> And Abram and Nahor took wives for themselves. The name of Abram’s wife was Sarai, and the name of Nahor’s wife was Milcah; she was the daughter of Haran, the father of both Milcah and Iscah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Genesis 29:31; Genesis 21:1–2; Luke 1:7"> But Sarai was barren; she had no children.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Nehemiah 9:7; Acts 7:2–4; Genesis 24:10"> Terah took his son Abram, his grandson Lot (the son of Haran), and his daughter-in-law Sarai, his son Abram’s wife, and with them he set out from Ur of the Chaldeans to go to Canaan. When they came to Haran, they settled there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32"> The lifetime of Terah was 205 years, and he died in Haran.</span>
</div>
</div>
