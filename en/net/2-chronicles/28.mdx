<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Chronicles 3:13; Isaiah 1:1; 2 Kings 16:1–20"> Ahaz was twenty years old when he began to reign, and he reigned for sixteen years in Jerusalem. He did not do what pleased the Lord, in contrast to his ancestor David.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Exodus 34:17; Judges 2:11; 2 Chronicles 22:3–4"> He followed in the footsteps of the kings of Israel; he also made images of the Baals.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Chronicles 33:2; 2 Chronicles 33:6; Leviticus 18:21"> He offered sacrifices in the Valley of Ben-Hinnom and passed his sons through the fire, a horrible sin practiced by the nations whom the Lord drove out before the Israelites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Kings 16:4; Deuteronomy 12:2–3; Leviticus 26:30"> He offered sacrifices and burned incense on the high places, on the hills, and under every green tree.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Isaiah 7:1; 2 Chronicles 24:24; 2 Kings 16:5–6"> The Lord his God handed him over to the king of Syria. The Syrians defeated him and deported many captives to Damascus. He was also handed over to the king of Israel, who thoroughly defeated him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Kings 15:27; Joshua 24:20; Jeremiah 15:6"> In one day King Pekah son of Remaliah of Israel killed 120,000 warriors in Judah, because they had abandoned the Lord God of their ancestors.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Genesis 43:15; Genesis 41:43; Esther 10:3"> Zikri, an Ephraimite warrior, killed the king’s son Maaseiah, Azrikam, the supervisor of the palace, and Elkanah, the king’s second-in-command.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Chronicles 11:4; Deuteronomy 28:25; Deuteronomy 28:41"> The Israelites seized from their brothers 200,000 wives, sons, and daughters. They also carried off a huge amount of plunder and took it back to Samaria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Isaiah 47:6; Ezra 9:6; Revelation 18:5"> Oded, a prophet of the Lord, was there. He went to meet the army as they arrived in Samaria and said to them: “Look, because the Lord God of your ancestors was angry with Judah he handed them over to you. You have killed them so mercilessly that God has taken notice.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Leviticus 25:39–46; Matthew 7:2–4; Jeremiah 25:29"> And now you are planning to enslave the people of Judah and Jerusalem. Yet are you not also guilty before the Lord your God?</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="James 2:13; Ezra 10:14; Matthew 5:7"> Now listen to me! Send back those you have seized from your brothers, for the Lord is very angry at you!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Chronicles 28:1; Jeremiah 26:6"> So some of the Ephraimite family leaders, Azariah son of Jehochanan, Berechiah son of Meshillemoth, Jechizkiah son of Shallum, and Amasa son of Hadlai confronted those returning from the battle.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Numbers 32:14; Matthew 23:32; Joshua 22:17–18"> They said to them, “Don’t bring those captives here! Are you planning on making us even more sinful and guilty before the Lord? Our guilt is already great and the Lord is very angry at Israel.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> So the soldiers released the captives and the plunder before the officials and the entire assembly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Proverbs 25:21–22; Deuteronomy 34:3; Judges 1:16"> Men were assigned to take the prisoners and find clothes among the plunder for those who were naked. So they clothed them, supplied them with sandals, gave them food and drink, and provided them with oil to rub on their skin. They put the ones who couldn’t walk on donkeys. They brought them back to their brothers at Jericho, the city of the date palm trees, and then returned to Samaria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Kings 16:5–7; Isaiah 7:17; Isaiah 7:1–9"> At that time King Ahaz asked the king of Assyria for help.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Obadiah 1:10; Obadiah 1:13–14; Leviticus 26:18"> The Edomites had again invaded and defeated Judah and carried off captives.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Ezekiel 16:57; Ezekiel 16:27; 1 Samuel 6:9"> The Philistines had raided the cities of Judah in the lowlands and the Negev. They captured and settled in Beth Shemesh, Aijalon, Gederoth, Soco and its surrounding villages, Timnah and its surrounding villages, and Gimzo and its surrounding villages.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Chronicles 21:2; Job 40:12; Deuteronomy 28:43"> The Lord humiliated Judah because of King Ahaz of Israel, for he encouraged Judah to sin and was very unfaithful to the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Kings 15:29; 1 Chronicles 5:26; Isaiah 30:16"> King Tiglath-pileser of Assyria came, but he gave him more trouble than support.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Kings 16:8–9; 2 Kings 18:15–16; 2 Chronicles 12:9"> Ahaz gathered riches from the Lord’s temple, the royal palace, and the officials and gave them to the king of Assyria, but that did not help.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Isaiah 1:5; Ezekiel 21:13; Jeremiah 5:3"> During his time of trouble King Ahaz was even more unfaithful to the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 Chronicles 25:14; Jeremiah 44:20–28; Habakkuk 1:11"> He offered sacrifices to the gods of Damascus whom he thought had defeated him. He reasoned, “Since the gods of the kings of Damascus helped them, I will sacrifice to them so they will help me.” But they caused him and all Israel to stumble.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="2 Chronicles 29:7; 2 Kings 16:17–18; 2 Chronicles 30:14"> Ahaz gathered the items in God’s temple and removed them. He shut the doors of the Lord’s temple and erected altars on every street corner in Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Chronicles 28:3"> In every city throughout Judah he set up high places to offer sacrifices to other gods. He angered the Lord God of his ancestors.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Kings 16:19–20; 2 Chronicles 20:34; 2 Chronicles 27:7–9"> The rest of the events of Ahaz’s reign, including his accomplishments from start to finish, are recorded in the Scroll of the Kings of Judah and Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 Chronicles 21:20; 2 Chronicles 24:25; Proverbs 10:7"> Ahaz passed away and was buried in the City of David; they did not bring him to the tombs of the kings of Israel. His son Hezekiah replaced him as king.</span>
</div>
