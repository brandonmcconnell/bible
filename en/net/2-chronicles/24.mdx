<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Chronicles 3:11; 2 Kings 11:21–15"> Joash was seven years old when he began to reign. He reigned for forty years in Jerusalem. His mother was Zibiah, who was from Beer Sheba.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Chronicles 26:4–5; 2 Chronicles 25:2; 2 Chronicles 24:17–22"> Joash did what the Lord approved throughout the lifetime of Jehoiada the priest.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Chronicles 24:15; Matthew 19:4–8; Genesis 4:19"> Jehoiada chose two wives for him who gave him sons and daughters.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> Joash was determined to repair the Lord’s temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Kings 12:4–7; 2 Chronicles 34:8–9; 2 Chronicles 29:3"> He assembled the priests and Levites and ordered them, “Go out to the cities of Judah and collect the annual quota of silver from all Israel for repairs on the temple of your God. Be quick about it!” But the Levites delayed.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Exodus 30:12–16; Numbers 1:50; Numbers 17:7–8"> So the king summoned Jehoiada the chief priest, and said to him, “Why have you not made the Levites collect from Judah and Jerusalem the tax authorized by Moses the Lord’s servant and by the assembly of Israel at the tent containing the tablets of the law?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Chronicles 21:17; 2 Chronicles 28:22–24; 2 Kings 12:4"> (Wicked Athaliah and her sons had broken into God’s temple and used all the holy items of the Lord’s temple in their worship of the Baals.)</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Mark 12:41; 2 Kings 12:8–9"> The king ordered a chest to be made and placed outside the gate of the Lord’s temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Chronicles 24:6; Matthew 17:24–27"> An edict was sent throughout Judah and Jerusalem requiring the people to bring to the Lord the tax that Moses, God’s servant, imposed on Israel in the wilderness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Acts 2:45–47; 2 Corinthians 9:7; 1 Chronicles 29:9"> All the officials and all the people gladly brought their silver and threw it into the chest until it was full.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Kings 12:10–12; 1 Corinthians 16:2"> Whenever the Levites brought the chest to the royal accountant and they saw there was a lot of silver, the royal scribe and the accountant of the high priest emptied the chest and then took it back to its place. They went through this routine every day and collected a large amount of silver.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Kings 5:15; 2 Chronicles 34:9–11"> The king and Jehoiada gave it to the construction foremen assigned to the Lord’s temple. They hired carpenters and craftsmen to repair the Lord’s temple, as well as those skilled in working with iron and bronze to restore the Lord’s temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Nehemiah 4:7; Mark 13:1–2; Haggai 2:3"> They worked hard and made the repairs. They followed the measurements specified for God’s temple and restored it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Numbers 28:2–29; Proverbs 27:22; 2 Kings 12:13–14"> When they were finished, they brought the rest of the silver to the king and Jehoiada. They used it to make items for the Lord’s temple, including items used in the temple service and for burnt sacrifices, pans, and various other gold and silver items. Throughout Jehoiada’s lifetime, burnt sacrifices were offered regularly in the Lord’s temple.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Genesis 47:9; 1 Chronicles 23:1; Psalms 90:10"> Jehoiada grew old and died at the age of 130.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Hebrews 6:10; 2 Chronicles 31:20; Nehemiah 13:14"> He was buried in the City of David with the kings, because he had accomplished good in Israel and for God and his temple.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Deuteronomy 31:27; Proverbs 26:28; Acts 20:29–30"> After Jehoiada died, the officials of Judah visited the king and declared their loyalty to him. The king listened to their advice.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Joshua 22:20; 2 Chronicles 19:2; Ephesians 5:6"> They abandoned the temple of the Lord God of their ancestors, and worshiped the Asherah poles and idols. Because of this sinful activity, God was angry with Judah and Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Isaiah 42:23; Luke 20:9–15; Matthew 13:9"> The Lord sent prophets among them to lead them back to him. They warned the people, but they would not pay attention.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Numbers 14:41; Jeremiah 5:25; 2 Chronicles 15:1–2"> God’s Spirit energized Zechariah son of Jehoiada the priest. He stood up before the people and said to them, “This is what God says: ‘Why are you violating the commands of the Lord? You will not be prosperous! Because you have rejected the Lord, he has rejected you!’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Acts 7:58–59; Nehemiah 9:26; Jeremiah 38:4–6"> They plotted against him and by royal decree stoned him to death in the courtyard of the Lord’s temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Genesis 9:5; Luke 11:51; Revelation 6:9–11"> King Joash disregarded the loyalty his father Jehoiada had shown him and killed Jehoiada’s son. As Zechariah was dying, he said, “May the Lord take notice and seek vengeance!”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 Kings 12:17–18; Deuteronomy 32:35; 1 Kings 20:22"> At the beginning of the year the Syrian army attacked Joash and invaded Judah and Jerusalem. They wiped out all the leaders of the people and sent all the plunder they gathered to the king of Damascus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="2 Chronicles 22:8; Leviticus 26:37; Leviticus 26:8"> Even though the invading Syrian army was relatively weak, the Lord handed over to them Judah’s very large army, for the people of Judah had abandoned the Lord God of their ancestors. The Syrians gave Joash what he deserved.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Chronicles 24:21–22; 2 Chronicles 28:27; 2 Chronicles 21:18–20"> When they withdrew, they left Joash badly wounded. His servants plotted against him because of what he had done to the son of Jehoiada the priest. They murdered him on his bed. Thus he died and was buried in the City of David, but not in the tombs of the kings.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Kings 12:21"> The conspirators were Zabad son of Shimeath (an Ammonite woman) and Jehozabad son of Shimrith (a Moabite woman).</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 Chronicles 16:11; 2 Chronicles 13:22; 2 Chronicles 24:12"> The list of Joash’s sons, the many prophetic oracles pertaining to him, and the account of his building project on God’s temple are included in the record of the Scroll of the Kings. His son Amaziah replaced him as king.</span>
</div>
</div>
