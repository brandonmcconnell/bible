<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Leviticus 21:5; Romans 9:8; Galatians 3:26"> You are children of the Lord your God. Do not cut yourselves or shave your forehead bald for the sake of the dead.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Deuteronomy 7:6; Leviticus 20:26; Exodus 19:5–6"> For you are a people holy to the Lord your God. He has chosen you to be his people, prized above all others on the face of the earth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Ezekiel 4:14; Romans 14:14; Leviticus 20:25"> You must not eat any forbidden thing.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Leviticus 11:2–45; Acts 10:14; 1 Kings 4:23"> These are the animals you may eat: the ox, the sheep, the goat,</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> the ibex, the gazelle, the deer, the wild goat, the antelope, the wild oryx, and the mountain sheep.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Proverbs 18:1; 2 Corinthians 6:17; Psalms 1:1–2"> You may eat any animal that has hooves divided into two parts and that chews the cud.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Matthew 7:22–23; Titus 1:16; 2 Timothy 3:5"> However, you may not eat the following animals among those that chew the cud or those that have divided hooves: the camel, the hare, and the rock badger. (Although they chew the cud, they do not have divided hooves and are therefore ritually impure to you).</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Leviticus 11:26–27; Isaiah 66:3; 2 Peter 2:22"> Also the pig is ritually impure to you; though it has divided hooves, it does not chew the cud. You may not eat their meat or even touch their remains.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Leviticus 11:9–12"> These you may eat from among water creatures: anything with fins and scales you may eat,</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> but whatever does not have fins and scales you may not eat; it is ritually impure to you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> All ritually clean birds you may eat.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Leviticus 11:13–19"> These are the ones you may not eat: the eagle, the vulture, the black vulture,</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> the kite, the black kite, the dayyah after its species,</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> every raven after its species,</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Job 30:29"> the ostrich, the owl, the seagull, the falcon after its species,</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> the little owl, the long-eared owl, the white owl,</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> the jackdaw, the carrion vulture, the cormorant,</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> the stork, the heron after its species, the hoopoe, the bat,</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Philippians 3:19; Leviticus 11:20–23"> and any winged thing on the ground are impure to you – they may not be eaten.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> You may eat any clean bird.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Exodus 23:19; Exodus 34:26; Leviticus 22:8"> You may not eat any corpse, though you may give it to the resident foreigner who is living in your villages and he may eat it, or you may sell it to a foreigner. You are a people holy to the Lord your God. Do not boil a young goat in its mother’s milk.</span>

## The Offering of Tribute

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Nehemiah 10:37; Deuteronomy 12:17; Deuteronomy 26:12–15"> You must be certain to tithe all the produce of your seed that comes from the field year after year.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Deuteronomy 12:5–7; Deuteronomy 4:10; Deuteronomy 12:17–18"> In the presence of the Lord your God you must eat from the tithe of your grain, your new wine, your olive oil, and the firstborn of your herds and flocks in the place he chooses to locate his name, so that you may learn to revere the Lord your God always.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Deuteronomy 12:21; Deuteronomy 12:5; Deuteronomy 11:24"> When he blesses you, if the place where he chooses to locate his name is distant,</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> you may convert the tithe into money, secure the money, and travel to the place the Lord your God chooses for himself.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 9:7; Deuteronomy 12:7; Matthew 21:12"> Then you may spend the money however you wish for cattle, sheep, wine, beer, or whatever you desire. You and your household may eat there in the presence of the Lord your God and enjoy it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Numbers 18:20; Deuteronomy 14:29; Deuteronomy 12:12"> As for the Levites in your villages, you must not ignore them, for they have no allotment or inheritance along with you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Deuteronomy 14:22; Amos 4:4; Deuteronomy 26:12–15"> At the end of every three years you must bring all the tithe of your produce, in that very year, and you must store it up in your villages.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Deuteronomy 15:10; Deuteronomy 14:27; Proverbs 19:17"> Then the Levites (because they have no allotment or inheritance with you), the resident foreigners, the orphans, and the widows of your villages may come and eat their fill so that the Lord your God may bless you in all the work you do.</span>
