## Paul Appeals to Caesar

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 23:34; Acts 25:5; Acts 8:40"> Now when Festus had set foot in the province, after three days he went up to Jerusalem from Caesarea.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Acts 25:15; Proverbs 4:16; Acts 24:1"> And the high priest and the prominent men of the Jews made known to him the case against Paul; and they were appealing to him,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Acts 9:24; Acts 23:12–15; 1 Samuel 23:19–21"> asking for favor against him, that he would summon him to Jerusalem—while planning an ambush to kill him along the way.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Acts 24:23"> But Festus answered that Paul should be kept at Caesarea, and that he was about to go there shortly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Acts 25:25; John 18:29–30; Acts 25:18–19"> “Therefore,” he said, “the prominent men among you, let them go with me and accuse this man, if there is anything in this man.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 25:17; Acts 25:10; Matthew 27:19"> And when he had stayed among them more than ten days, he went down to Caesarea. And the next day, sitting on the judgment seat, he commanded Paul to be brought.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Acts 24:13; Luke 23:10; Acts 25:24"> When he arrived, the Jews who had come down from Jerusalem stood around him, bringing many and serious accusations against Paul, which they were not able to prove,</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Acts 24:12; Acts 28:17; 2 Corinthians 1:12"> while he defended himself, saying, “Neither against the law of the Jews, nor against the temple, nor against Caesar did I commit any sin.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 24:27; Acts 12:3; Acts 25:20"> But Festus, desiring to grant a favor to the Jews, answered Paul and said, “Are you willing to go up to Jerusalem and there be judged before me concerning these things?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Matthew 27:18; 2 Corinthians 4:2; Acts 28:18"> But Paul said, “I am standing before the judicial bench of Caesar, by whom it is necessary for me to be judged. I have done no wrong to the Jews, as also you know very well.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 28:19; Acts 26:32; Acts 25:25"> For if I am doing wrong, and have done anything worthy of death, I am not trying to escape a death sentence; but if there is nothing in these things of which these people are accusing me, no one is able to deliver me to them. I appeal to Caesar.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 19:21; Philippians 1:12–14; Philippians 1:20"> Then Festus, having talked together with the council, answered, “Have you appealed to Caesar? Before Caesar you shall go!”</span>
</div>
</div>

## Paul Before Agrippa

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Acts 8:40; Acts 26:1; Acts 26:27–28"> And after some days King Agrippa and Bernice arrived in Caesarea and greeted Festus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Acts 24:27"> And as he was spending many days there, Festus laid before the king the charges against Paul, saying: “There is a certain man who was left a prisoner by Felix,</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Acts 25:1–3; Esther 3:9; Luke 18:3–5"> about whom the chief priests and the elders of the Jews informed me, when I was in Jerusalem, asking for punishment against him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="John 7:51; Deuteronomy 19:17–18; Acts 23:30"> To them I answered, ‘It is not a custom with Romans to deliver any man to destruction before the one being accused may meet his accusers face to face, and may receive an opportunity for defense concerning the charge.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Acts 25:6; Acts 25:10"> Therefore when they had assembled here, having made no delay, on the next day I took my seat at the judicial bench, and I commanded the man to be brought in;</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> concerning whom, when the accusers stood, they brought no accusation of the things which I was supposing,</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Acts 23:29; Acts 18:15; 1 Corinthians 15:3–4"> but had some questions against him about their own religion and about a certain Jesus, who had died, whom Paul professed to be alive.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Acts 25:9"> And being uncertain as to the investigation concerning this, I asked whether he was willing to go to Jerusalem and there be judged concerning these charges.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Timothy 4:16; Luke 2:1; Acts 25:10–11"> But when Paul appealed that he be kept for the decision of His Majesty the Emperor, I commanded him to be kept until I may send him to Caesar.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 9:15; Matthew 10:18; Isaiah 52:15"> Then Agrippa said to Festus, “I also was wishing to hear the man myself.” “Tomorrow,” he said, “you shall hear him.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Acts 26:30; Acts 25:13; 1 John 2:16"> Therefore on the next day, when Agrippa and Bernice had come with all pomp, and had entered into the auditorium together with the commanders and the prominent men of the city, and when Festus had commanded, Paul was brought.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 22:22; Acts 25:7; Acts 25:2–3"> And Festus said: “King Agrippa and all the men who are with us, observe this man about whom the people of the Jews petitioned me, both in Jerusalem and here, crying against him that it is not fitting for him to live any longer.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Acts 23:29; Acts 25:11–12; Luke 23:4"> But when I came to understand him to have done nothing worthy of death, and also he had appealed to His Majesty the Emperor, I decided to send him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 26:2–3"> About whom I have nothing certain to write to my lord. Therefore I have brought him before you, and especially before you King Agrippa, in order that when this hearing is finished I may have something to write.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="John 7:51; Proverbs 18:13"> For it seems unreasonable to me to send a prisoner and not to signify the accusations against him.”</span>
</div>
</div>
