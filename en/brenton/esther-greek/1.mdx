<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> &#91;In the second year of the reign of Artaxerxes the great king, on the first day of Nisan, Mardochæus the son of Jairus, the son of Semeias, the son of Cisæus, of the tribe of Benjamin,</span>
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> a Jew dwelling in the city Susa, a great man, serving in the king's palace, saw a vision.</span>
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> Now he was of the captivity which Nabuchodonosor king of Babylon had carried captive from Jerusalem, with Jechonias the king of Judea.</span>
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> And this was his dream: Behold, voices and a noise, thunders and earthquake, tumult upon the earth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> And, behold, two great serpents came forth, both ready for conflict, and there came from them a great voice,</span>
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> and by their voice every nation was prepared for battle, even to fight against the nation of the just.</span>
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> And, behold, a day of darkness and blackness, tribulation and anguish, affliction and great tumult upon the earth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> And all the righteous nation was troubled, fearing their own afflictions; and they prepared to die, and cried to God:</span>
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> and from their cry there came as it were a great river from a little fountain, even much water.</span>
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> And light and the sun arose, and the lowly were exalted, and devoured the honourable.</span>
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> And Mardochæus who had seen this vision and what God designed to do, having awoke, kept it in his heart, and desired by all means to interpret it, even till night.</span>
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> And Mardochæus rested quiet in the palace with Gabatha and Tharrha the king's two chamberlains, eunuchs who guarded the palace.</span>
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> And he heard their reasonings and searched out their plans, and learnt that they were preparing to lay hands on king Artaxerxes: and he informed the king concerning them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> And the king examined the two chamberlains, and they confessed, and were executed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> And the king wrote these things for a memorial; also Mardochæus wrote concerning these matters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> And the king commanded Mardochæus to attend in the palace, and gave him gifts for this service.</span>
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> And Aman the son of Amadathes the Bugæan was honourable in the sight of the king, and he endeavoured to hurt Mardochæus and his people, because of the two chamberlains of the king.&#93;</span>
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> And it came to pass after these things in the days of Artaxerxes,—(this Artaxerxes ruled over a hundred and twenty-seven provinces from India)—</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2"> in those days, when king Artaxerxes was on the throne in the city of Susa,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3"> in the third year of his reign, he made a feast to his friends, and the other nations, and to the nobles of the Persians and Medes, and the chief of the satraps.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> And after this, after he had shewn to them the wealth of his kingdom, and the abundant glory of his wealth during a hundred and eighty days,</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> when, I say, the days of the marriage feast were completed, the king made a banquet to the nations who were present in the city six days, in the court of the king's house,</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> which was adorned with hangings of fine linen and flax on cords of fine linen and purple, fastened to golden and silver studs, on pillars of Parian marble and stone: there were golden and silver couches on a pavement of emerald stone, and of pearl, and of Parian stone, and open-worked coverings variously flowered, having roses worked round about;</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7"> gold and silver cups, and a small cup of carbuncle set out of the value of thirty thousand talents, abundant and sweet wine, which the king himself drank.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> And this banquet was not according to the appointed law; but so the king would have it: and he charged the stewards to perform his will and that of the company.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> Also Astin the queen made a banquet for the women in the palace where king Artaxerxes dwelt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> Now on the seventh day the king, being merry, told Aman, and Bazan, and Tharrha, and Barazi, and Zatholtha, and Abataza, and Tharaba, the seven chamberlains, servants of king Artaxerxes,</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> to bring in the queen to him, to enthrone her, and crown her with the diadem, and to shew her to the princes, and her beauty to the nations: for she was beautiful.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> But queen Astin hearkened not to him to come with the chamberlains: so the king was grieved and angered.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> And he said to his friends, Thus has Astin spoken: pronounce therefore upon this case law and judgment.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> So Arkesæus, and Sarsathæus, and Malisear, the princes of the Persians and Medes, who were near the king, who sat chief in rank by the king, drew near to him,</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> and reported to him according to the laws how it was proper to do to queen Astin, because she had not done the things commanded of the king by the chamberlains.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> And Muchæus said to the king and to the princes, Queen Astin has not wronged the king only, but also all the king's rulers and princes:</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> for he has told them the words of the queen, and how she disobeyed the king. As then, said he, she refused to obey king Artaxerxes,</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> so this day shall the other ladies of the chiefs of the Persians and Medes, having heard what she said to the king, dare in the same way to dishonour their husbands.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> If then it seem good to the king, let him make a royal decree, and let it be written according to the laws of the Medes and Persians, and let him not alter it: and let not the queen come in to him any more; and let the king give her royalty to a woman better than she.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> And let the law of the king which he shall have made, be widely proclaimed, in his kingdom: and so shall all the women give honour to their husbands, from the poor even to the rich.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> And the saying pleased the king and the princes; and the king did as Muchæus had said,</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> and sent into all his kingdom through the several provinces, according to their language, in order that men might be feared in their own houses.</span>
