<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Esther 3:13; Esther 8:12; Psalm 30:11"> On the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, the month of Adar, the king’s command and edict were to be executed. On this day the enemies of the Jews had hoped to overpower them, but their plan was overturned and the Jews overpowered those who hated them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Esther 8:17; Esther 8:11; Psalm 71:13"> In each of the provinces of King Xerxes, the Jews assembled in their cities to attack those who sought to harm them. No man could withstand them, because the fear of them had fallen upon all peoples.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Ezra 8:36; Esther 8:5; Daniel 6:1–2"> And all the officials of the provinces, the satraps, the governors, and the king’s administrators helped the Jews, because the fear of Mordecai had fallen upon them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Samuel 3:1; 1 Chronicles 11:9; 1 Samuel 2:30"> For Mordecai exercised great power in the palace, and his fame spread throughout the provinces as he became more and more powerful.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Psalm 18:34–40; Psalm 20:7–8; Psalm 149:6–9"> The Jews put all their enemies to the sword, killing and destroying them, and they did as they pleased to those who hated them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Esther 3:15"> In the citadel of Susa, the Jews killed and destroyed five hundred men,</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7"> including Parshandatha, Dalphon, Aspatha,</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> Poratha, Adalia, Aridatha,</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> Parmashta, Arisai, Aridai, and Vaizatha.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Esther 5:11; Esther 8:11; Genesis 14:23"> They killed these ten sons of Haman son of Hammedatha, the enemy of the Jews, but they did not lay a hand on the plunder.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> On that day the number of those killed in the citadel of Susa was reported to the king,</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Esther 7:2; Esther 5:6"> who said to Queen Esther, “In the citadel of Susa the Jews have killed and destroyed five hundred men, including Haman’s ten sons. What have they done in the rest of the royal provinces? Now what is your petition? It will be given to you. And what further do you request? It will be fulfilled.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Esther 8:11; Deuteronomy 21:23; Esther 9:15"> Esther replied, “If it pleases the king, may the Jews in Susa also have tomorrow to carry out today’s edict, and may the bodies of Haman’s ten sons be hanged on the gallows.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> So the king commanded that this be done. An edict was issued in Susa, and they hanged the ten sons of Haman.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Esther 8:11; Esther 9:10; Esther 9:16"> On the fourteenth day of the month of Adar, the Jews in Susa came together again and put to death three hundred men there, but they did not lay a hand on the plunder.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Esther 8:11; Esther 9:2; Leviticus 26:7–8"> The rest of the Jews in the royal provinces also assembled to defend themselves and rid themselves of their enemies. They killed 75,000 who hated them, but they did not lay a hand on the plunder.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Esther 9:21; Esther 9:1; Esther 8:9"> This was done on the thirteenth day of the month of Adar, and on the fourteenth day they rested, making it a day of feasting and joy.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Esther 9:21; Esther 9:1; Esther 9:15"> The Jews in Susa, however, had assembled on the thirteenth and the fourteenth days of the month. So they rested on the fifteenth day, making it a day of feasting and joy.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Esther 8:17; Revelation 11:10; Nehemiah 8:10–12"> This is why the rural Jews, who live in the villages, observe the fourteenth day of the month of Adar as a day of joy and feasting. It is a holiday for sending gifts to one another.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Deuteronomy 31:19–22; 2 Corinthians 1:10–11; Psalm 145:4–12"> Mordecai recorded these events and sent letters to all the Jews in all the provinces of King Xerxes, both near and far,</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> to establish among them an annual celebration on the fourteenth and fifteenth days of the month of Adar</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Psalm 30:11; Isaiah 14:3; Esther 9:19"> as the days on which the Jews gained rest from their enemies and the month in which their sorrow turned to joy and their mourning into a holiday. He wrote that these were to be days of feasting and joy, of sending gifts to one another and to the poor.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> So the Jews agreed to continue the custom they had started, as Mordecai had written to them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Esther 3:5–13; Esther 9:10"> For Haman son of Hammedatha, the Agagite, the enemy of all the Jews, had plotted against the Jews to destroy them and had cast the Pur (that is, the lot) to crush and destroy them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Psalm 7:16; Psalm 140:9; Psalm 141:10"> But when it came before the king, he commanded by letter that the wicked scheme which Haman had devised against the Jews should come back upon his own head, and that he and his sons should be hanged on the gallows.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Esther 9:20; Numbers 16:40; Ezekiel 39:11"> Therefore these days are called Purim, from the word Pur. Because of all the instructions in this letter, and because of all they had seen and experienced,</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Zechariah 2:11; Isaiah 56:6; Esther 8:17"> the Jews bound themselves to establish the custom that they and their descendants and all who join them should not fail to celebrate these two days at the appointed time each and every year, according to their regulation.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Exodus 13:8–9; Joshua 4:7; Psalm 103:2"> These days should be remembered and celebrated by every generation, family, province, and city, so that these days of Purim should not fail to be observed among the Jews, nor should the memory of them fade from their descendants.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Esther 9:20; Esther 2:15; Esther 8:10"> So Queen Esther daughter of Abihail, along with Mordecai the Jew, wrote with full authority to confirm this second letter concerning Purim.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Esther 1:1; Esther 8:9; Zechariah 8:19"> And Mordecai sent letters with words of peace and truth to all the Jews in the 127 provinces of the kingdom of Xerxes,</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Esther 4:3; Esther 4:16; Jonah 3:2–9"> in order to confirm these days of Purim at their appointed time, just as Mordecai the Jew and Queen Esther had established them and had committed themselves and their descendants to the times of fasting and lamentation.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Esther 9:26"> So Esther’s decree confirmed these regulations about Purim, which were written into the record.</span>
