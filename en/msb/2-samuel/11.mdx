<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Chronicles 20:1; 1 Kings 20:26; 1 Kings 20:22"> In the spring, at the time when kings march out to war, David sent out Joab and his servants with the whole army of Israel. They destroyed the Ammonites and besieged Rabbah, but David remained in Jerusalem.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Matthew 5:28; Psalm 119:37; Job 31:1"> One evening David got up from his bed and strolled around on the roof of the palace. And from the roof he saw a woman bathing—a very beautiful woman.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Samuel 23:39; 1 Chronicles 3:5; James 1:14–15"> So David sent and inquired about the woman, and he was told, “This is Bathsheba, the daughter of Eliam and the wife of Uriah the Hittite.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Leviticus 18:19; James 1:14–15; Psalm 51:1–19"> Then David sent messengers to get her, and when she came to him, he slept with her. (Now she had just purified herself from her uncleanness.) Then she returned home.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Deuteronomy 22:22; Proverbs 6:34"> And the woman conceived and sent word to David, saying, “I am pregnant.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Proverbs 28:13; Genesis 4:7; 1 Samuel 15:30"> At this, David sent orders to Joab: “Send me Uriah the Hittite.” So Joab sent him to David.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Genesis 37:14; 1 Samuel 17:22; Genesis 29:6"> When Uriah came to him, David asked how Joab and the troops were doing and how the war was going.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Genesis 18:4; Genesis 43:34; Isaiah 29:15"> Then he said to Uriah, “Go down to your house and wash your feet.” So Uriah left the palace, and a gift from the king followed him.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Proverbs 21:30; Job 5:12–14"> But Uriah slept at the door of the palace with all his master’s servants; he did not go down to his house.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> And David was told, “Uriah did not go home.” “Haven’t you just arrived from a journey?” David asked Uriah. “Why didn’t you go home?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Samuel 7:2; 2 Samuel 20:6; 2 Samuel 7:6"> Uriah answered, “The ark and Israel and Judah are dwelling in tents, and my master Joab and his soldiers are camped in the open field. How can I go to my house to eat and drink and sleep with my wife? As surely as you live, and as your soul lives, I will not do such a thing!”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Jeremiah 2:37; Jeremiah 2:22–23"> “Stay here one more day,” David said to Uriah, “and tomorrow I will send you back.” So Uriah stayed in Jerusalem that day and the next.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Habakkuk 2:15; Genesis 19:32–35; Proverbs 20:1"> Then David invited Uriah to eat and drink with him, and he got Uriah drunk. And in the evening Uriah went out to lie down on his cot with his master’s servants, but he did not go home.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Jeremiah 17:9; 1 Kings 21:8–10; Psalm 19:13"> The next morning David wrote a letter to Joab and sent it with Uriah.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="2 Samuel 12:9; Psalm 51:4; Jeremiah 20:13"> In the letter he wrote: “Put Uriah at the front of the fiercest battle; then withdraw from him, so that he may be struck down and killed.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Proverbs 29:12; 1 Samuel 22:17–19; Acts 5:29"> So as Joab besieged the city, he assigned Uriah to a place where he knew the strongest enemy soldiers were.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Samuel 12:9; Psalm 51:14"> And when the men of the city came out and fought against Joab, some of David’s servants fell, and Uriah the Hittite also died.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> Joab sent to David a full account of the battle</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> and instructed the messenger, “When you have finished giving the king a full account of the battle,</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> if the king’s anger flares, he may ask you, ‘Why did you get so close to the city to fight? Did you not realize they would shoot from atop the wall?</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Judges 7:1; Judges 9:50–54; 2 Samuel 3:27"> Who struck Abimelech son of Jerubbesheth? Was it not a woman who dropped an upper millstone on him from the wall, so that he died in Thebez? Why did you get so close to the wall?’ If he asks you this, then you are to say, ‘Your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead as well.’”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> So the messenger set out and reported to David all that Joab had sent him to say.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> The messenger said to David, “The men overpowered us and came out against us in the field, but we drove them back to the entrance of the gate.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24"> Then the archers shot at your servants from the wall, and some of the king’s servants were killed. And your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead as well.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Samuel 12:26; Joshua 7:8–9; Ecclesiastes 9:1–3"> Then David told the messenger, “Say this to Joab: ‘Do not let this matter upset you, for the sword devours one as well as another. Strengthen your attack against the city and demolish it.’ Encourage him with these words.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Samuel 14:2; Genesis 27:41; 2 Samuel 3:31"> When Uriah’s wife heard that her husband was dead, she mourned for him.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Psalm 51:4–5; 2 Samuel 12:9; Deuteronomy 22:29"> And when the time of mourning was over, David had her brought to his house, and she became his wife and bore him a son. But the thing that David had done was evil in the sight of the LORD.</span>
</div>
</div>
