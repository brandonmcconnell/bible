<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Samuel 16:12–13; 1 Samuel 16:1; 2 Samuel 5:3"> So all the elders of Israel came to the king at Hebron, where David made a covenant with them before the LORD. <span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Judges 1:21; Genesis 15:21; Joshua 15:8"> Then David and all the Israelites marched to Jerusalem (that is, Jebus), where the Jebusites inhabited the land.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Chronicles 5:2; 1 Kings 8:1; Psalm 87:2"> The people of Jebus said to David, “You will never get in here.” Nevertheless, David captured the fortress of Zion (that is, the City of David).</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:16; 2 Samuel 20:23; 2 Samuel 3:27"> Now David had said, “Whoever is the first to strike down a Jebusite will become chief commander.” And Joab son of Zeruiah went up first, and he became the chief.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Chronicles 11:5; 2 Samuel 5:7; Psalm 2:6"> So David took up residence in the fortress; that is why it was called the City of David.</span>

<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Samuel 3:1; Isaiah 41:10; 1 Chronicles 9:20"> And David became greater and greater, for the LORD of Hosts was with him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Chronicles 11:3; 1 Samuel 16:1; 1 Chronicles 12:38"> Now these were the chiefs of David’s mighty men, who, together with all Israel, bolstered and strengthened his kingdom, according to the word of the LORD concerning Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Samuel 23:8; 1 Chronicles 27:2; 1 Chronicles 12:18"> This is the list of David’s mighty men: Jashobeam son of Hachmoni was chief of the officers; he wielded his spear against three hundred men, whom he killed at one time.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Chronicles 27:4; 2 Samuel 23:17–19; 2 Samuel 23:23"> Next in command was Eleazar son of Dodo the Ahohite, one of the three mighty men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Samuel 23:11–12; 1 Samuel 17:1"> He was with David at Pas-dammim when the Philistines gathered there for battle. At a place with a field full of barley, the troops fled from the Philistines.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Psalm 144:10; 2 Kings 5:1; 1 Samuel 14:23"> But Eleazar and David stationed themselves in the middle of the field and defended it. They struck down the Philistines, and the LORD brought about a great victory.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Chronicles 14:9; 2 Samuel 5:18; Isaiah 17:5"> Three of the thirty chief men went down to David, to the rock at the cave of Adullam, while a company of Philistines was encamped in the Valley of Rephaim.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Samuel 10:5; 1 Samuel 13:4; 1 Samuel 13:23"> At that time David was in the stronghold, and the garrison of the Philistines was at Bethlehem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="John 4:14; Isaiah 12:3; 2 Samuel 23:15–16"> David longed for water and said, “Oh, that someone would get me a drink of water from the well near the gate of Bethlehem!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Acts 21:13; 1 Samuel 19:5; Acts 20:24"> So the Three broke through the Philistine camp, drew water from the well at the gate of Bethlehem, and brought it back to David. But he refused to drink it; instead, he poured it out to the LORD,</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Samuel 23:17; Psalm 72:14; Judges 9:17"> saying, “Far be it from me, my God, to do this! How can I drink the blood of these men who risked their lives?” Because they had brought it at the risk of their lives, David refused to drink it. Such were the exploits of the three mighty men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Samuel 26:6; 1 Chronicles 2:16; 2 Samuel 20:6"> Now Abishai, the brother of Joab, was chief of the Three, and he wielded his spear against three hundred men, killed them, and won a name along with the Three.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Corinthians 15:41; Matthew 13:8"> He was doubly honored above the Three, and he became their commander, even though he was not included among the Three.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Samuel 23:20–23; 2 Samuel 8:18; Joshua 15:21"> And Benaiah son of Jehoiada was a man of valor from Kabzeel, a man of many exploits. He struck down two champions of Moab, and on a snowy day he went down into a pit and killed a lion.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Samuel 17:7; 1 Samuel 17:4; Deuteronomy 3:11"> He also struck down an Egyptian, a huge man five cubits tall. Although the Egyptian had a spear like a weaver’s beam in his hand, Benaiah went against him with a club, snatched the spear from his hand, and killed the Egyptian with his own spear.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24"> These were the exploits of Benaiah son of Jehoiada, who won a name along with the three mighty men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Samuel 20:23"> He was most honored among the Thirty, but he did not become one of the Three. And David appointed him over his guard.</span>
