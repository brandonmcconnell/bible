<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 2:6; Zephaniah 3:9; Isaiah 19:18"> Now the whole world had one language and a common form of speech.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Genesis 10:10; Daniel 1:2; Genesis 14:1"> And as people journeyed eastward, they found a plain in the land of Shinar and settled there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Genesis 14:10; Exodus 1:14; Exodus 2:3"> And they said to one another, “Come, let us make bricks and bake them thoroughly.” So they used brick instead of stone, and tar instead of mortar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Deuteronomy 1:28; John 5:44; Luke 1:51"> “Come,” they said, “let us build for ourselves a city with a tower that reaches to the heavens, that we may make a name for ourselves and not be scattered over the face of all the earth.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Genesis 18:21; Psalm 33:13–14; John 3:13"> Then the LORD came down to see the city and the tower that the sons of men were building.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Psalm 2:1–4; Genesis 3:22; Genesis 11:1"> And the LORD said, “If they have begun to do this as one people speaking the same language, then nothing they devise will be beyond them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Psalm 33:10; Psalm 55:9; Job 5:12–13"> Come, let Us go down and confuse their language, so that they will not understand one another’s speech.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Luke 1:51; Deuteronomy 32:8; Genesis 10:25"> So the LORD scattered them from there over the face of all the earth, and they stopped building the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Genesis 10:10; Genesis 10:5; Genesis 10:20"> That is why it is called Babel, for there the LORD confused the language of the whole world, and from that place the LORD scattered them over the face of all the earth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Chronicles 1:17–27; Genesis 10:21–22; Luke 3:34–36"> This is the account of Shem. Two years after the flood, when Shem was 100 years old, he became the father of Arphaxad.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Genesis 9:7; Psalm 144:12; Genesis 1:28"> And after he had become the father of Arphaxad, Shem lived 500 years and had other sons and daughters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Luke 3:36"> When Arphaxad was 35 years old, he became the father of Shelah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> And after he had become the father of Shelah, Arphaxad lived 403 years and had other sons and daughters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> When Shelah was 30 years old, he became the father of Eber.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> And after he had become the father of Eber, Shelah lived 403 years and had other sons and daughters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Genesis 10:21; 1 Chronicles 1:19; Numbers 24:24"> When Eber was 34 years old, he became the father of Peleg.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> And after he had become the father of Peleg, Eber lived 430 years and had other sons and daughters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Luke 3:35"> When Peleg was 30 years old, he became the father of Reu.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> And after he had become the father of Reu, Peleg lived 209 years and had other sons and daughters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Luke 3:35"> When Reu was 32 years old, he became the father of Serug.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> And after he had become the father of Serug, Reu lived 207 years and had other sons and daughters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Joshua 24:2"> When Serug was 30 years old, he became the father of Nahor.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> And after he had become the father of Nahor, Serug lived 200 years and had other sons and daughters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Luke 3:34; Joshua 24:2"> When Nahor was 29 years old, he became the father of Terah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> And after he had become the father of Terah, Nahor lived 119 years and had other sons and daughters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Joshua 24:2; 1 Chronicles 1:26–27; Genesis 22:20–24"> When Terah was 70 years old, he became the father of Abram, Nahor, and Haran.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Genesis 12:4; Genesis 14:12; Genesis 11:31"> This is the account of Terah. Terah became the father of Abram, Nahor, and Haran. And Haran became the father of Lot.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Genesis 15:7; Nehemiah 9:7; Acts 7:2–4"> During his father Terah’s lifetime, Haran died in his native land, in Ur of the Chaldeans.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Genesis 17:15; Genesis 22:20; Genesis 24:15"> And Abram and Nahor took wives for themselves. Abram’s wife was named Sarai, and Nahor’s wife was named Milcah; she was the daughter of Haran, who was the father of both Milcah and Iscah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Genesis 29:31; Genesis 21:1–2; Luke 1:7"> But Sarai was barren; she had no children.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Nehemiah 9:7; Acts 7:2–4; Genesis 24:10"> And Terah took his son Abram, his grandson Lot son of Haran, and his daughter-in-law Sarai the wife of Abram, and they set out from Ur of the Chaldeans for the land of Canaan. But when they arrived in Haran, they settled there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32"> Terah lived 205 years, and he died in Haran.</span>
