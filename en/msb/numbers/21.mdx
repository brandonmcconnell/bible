<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Judges 1:16; Numbers 33:40; Joshua 12:14"> When the Canaanite king of Arad, who lived in the Negev, heard that Israel was coming along the road to Atharim, he attacked Israel and captured some prisoners.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Judges 11:30; Genesis 28:20; 1 Samuel 1:11"> So Israel made a vow to the LORD: “If You will deliver this people into our hands, we will devote their cities to destruction.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Numbers 14:45; Deuteronomy 1:44; Psalm 10:17"> And the LORD heard Israel’s plea and delivered up the Canaanites. Israel devoted them and their cities to destruction; so they named the place Hormah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Exodus 6:9; Judges 11:18; Numbers 32:7"> Then they set out from Mount Hor along the route to the Red Sea, in order to bypass the land of Edom. But the people grew impatient on the journey</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Psalm 78:19; Numbers 16:13–14; Exodus 17:2–3"> and spoke against God and against Moses: “Why have you led us up out of Egypt to die in the wilderness? There is no bread or water, and we detest this wretched food!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Corinthians 10:9; Jeremiah 8:17; Deuteronomy 8:15"> So the LORD sent venomous snakes among the people, and many of the Israelites were bitten and died.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Acts 8:24; Numbers 11:2; Psalm 78:34"> Then the people came to Moses and said, “We have sinned by speaking against the LORD and against you. Intercede with the LORD so He will take the snakes away from us.” So Moses interceded for the people.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="John 3:14; Psalm 106:43–45; Psalm 145:8"> Then the LORD said to Moses, “Make a fiery serpent and mount it on a pole. When anyone who is bitten looks at it, he will live.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="John 3:14–15; Hebrews 12:2; John 6:40"> So Moses made a bronze snake and mounted it on a pole. If anyone who was bitten looked at the bronze snake, he would live.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Numbers 33:43–45"> Then the Israelites set out and camped at Oboth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> They journeyed from Oboth and camped at Iye-abarim in the wilderness opposite Moab to the east.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Deuteronomy 2:13–14"> From there they set out and camped in the Valley of Zered.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Judges 11:18; Numbers 22:36; Isaiah 16:2"> From there they moved on and camped on the other side of the Arnon, in the wilderness that extends into the Amorite territory. Now the Arnon is the border between the Moabites and the Amorites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Joshua 10:13; 2 Samuel 1:18"> Therefore it is stated in the Book of the Wars of the LORD: “Waheb in Suphah and the wadis of the Arnon,</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Numbers 21:28; Deuteronomy 2:18; Deuteronomy 2:9"> even the slopes of the wadis that extend to the site of Ar and lie along the border of Moab.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Numbers 20:8; Isaiah 12:3; Isaiah 41:17–18"> From there they went on to Beer, the well where the LORD said to Moses, “Gather the people so that I may give them water.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="James 5:13; Exodus 15:1–2; Psalm 106:12"> Then Israel sang this song: “Spring up, O well, all of you sing to it!</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Isaiah 33:22; 1 Timothy 6:17–18; James 4:12"> The princes dug the well; the nobles of the people hollowed it out with their scepters and with their staffs.” From the wilderness the Israelites went on to Mattanah,</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> and from Mattanah to Nahaliel, and from Nahaliel to Bamoth,</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Numbers 23:28; Numbers 23:14; Deuteronomy 1:5"> and from Bamoth to the valley in Moab where the top of Pisgah overlooks the wasteland.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Deuteronomy 2:26–28; Numbers 20:14–19; Judges 11:19–21"> Then Israel sent messengers to Sihon king of the Amorites, saying,</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Numbers 20:17"> “Let us pass through your land. We will not turn aside into any field or vineyard, or drink water from any well. We will stay on the King’s Highway until we have passed through your territory.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Judges 11:20; Deuteronomy 29:7–8; Isaiah 15:4"> But Sihon would not let Israel pass through his territory. Instead, he gathered his whole army and went out to confront Israel in the wilderness. When he came to Jahaz, he fought against Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Amos 2:9; Joshua 24:8; Genesis 32:22"> And Israel put him to the sword and took possession of his land, from the Arnon to the Jabbok—but only up to the border of the Ammonites, because it was fortified.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Numbers 32:33–42; Isaiah 16:8–9; Jeremiah 48:2"> Israel captured all the cities of the Amorites and occupied them, including Heshbon and all its villages.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> Heshbon was the city of Sihon king of the Amorites, who had fought against the former king of Moab and taken all his land as far as the Arnon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Isaiah 14:4; Numbers 21:14; Habakkuk 2:6"> That is why the poets say: “Come to Heshbon, let it be rebuilt; let the city of Sihon be restored.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Numbers 21:15; Jeremiah 48:45–46; Isaiah 15:1–2"> For a fire went out from Heshbon, a blaze from the city of Sihon. It consumed Ar of Moab, the rulers of Arnon’s heights.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Judges 11:24; 2 Kings 23:13; Jeremiah 48:46"> Woe to you, O Moab! You are destroyed, O people of Chemosh! He gave up his sons as refugees, and his daughters into captivity to Sihon king of the Amorites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Jeremiah 48:18; Isaiah 15:2; Jeremiah 48:22"> But we have overthrown them; Heshbon is destroyed as far as Dibon. We demolished them as far as Nophah, which reaches to Medeba.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Joshua 13:8–12; Numbers 32:33–42; Deuteronomy 3:16–17"> So Israel lived in the land of the Amorites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Jeremiah 48:32; Numbers 32:1; Numbers 32:35"> After Moses had sent spies to Jazer, Israel captured its villages and drove out the Amorites who were there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Joshua 13:12; Deuteronomy 1:4; Deuteronomy 29:7"> Then they turned and went up the road to Bashan, and Og king of Bashan and his whole army came out to meet them in battle at Edrei.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Psalm 135:10–11; Joshua 8:7; Deuteronomy 3:2–3"> But the LORD said to Moses, “Do not fear him, for I have delivered him into your hand, along with all his people and his land. Do to him as you did to Sihon king of the Amorites, who lived in Heshbon.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Deuteronomy 29:7–8; Joshua 13:12; Deuteronomy 3:3–17"> So they struck down Og, along with his sons and his whole army, until no remnant was left. And they took possession of his land.</span>
