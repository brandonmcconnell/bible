<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Isaiah 5:7; Exodus 22:25–27; 1 Corinthians 6:6–8"> About that time there was a great outcry from the people and their wives against their fellow Jews.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Malachi 2:2; Psalm 128:2–4; Psalm 127:3–5"> Some were saying, “We and our sons and daughters are numerous. We must get grain in order to eat and stay alive.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Leviticus 25:35–39; Malachi 3:8–11; Genesis 47:15–25"> Others were saying, “We are mortgaging our fields, our vineyards, and our homes to get grain during the famine.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Ezra 4:13; Ezra 4:20; Deuteronomy 28:47–48"> Still others were saying, “We have borrowed money to pay the king’s tax on our fields and vineyards.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Kings 4:1; Leviticus 25:39–43; Isaiah 58:7"> We and our children are just like our countrymen and their children, yet we are subjecting our sons and daughters to slavery. Some of our daughters are already enslaved, but we are powerless to redeem them because our fields and vineyards belong to others.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Exodus 11:8; Nehemiah 13:25; Ephesians 4:26"> When I heard their outcry and these complaints, I became extremely angry,</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Deuteronomy 23:19–20; Leviticus 25:36; Exodus 22:25"> and after serious thought I rebuked the nobles and officials, saying, “You are exacting usury from your own brothers!” So I called a large assembly against them</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Leviticus 25:47–49; 1 Corinthians 8:11; Matthew 25:29"> and said, “We have done our best to buy back our Jewish brothers who were sold to foreigners, but now you are selling your own brothers, that they may be sold back to us!” But they remained silent, for they could find nothing to say.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Leviticus 25:36; 2 Samuel 12:14; Romans 2:24"> So I continued, “What you are doing is not right. Shouldn’t you walk in the fear of our God to avoid the reproach of our foreign enemies?</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Corinthians 6:1; Exodus 22:25–27; Ezekiel 18:13"> I, as well as my brothers and my servants, have been lending the people money and grain. Please, let us stop this usury.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Isaiah 58:6; Leviticus 6:4–5; Luke 3:8"> Please restore to them immediately their fields, vineyards, olive groves, and houses, along with the percentage of the money, grain, new wine, and oil that you have been assessing them.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Ezra 10:5; Jeremiah 34:8–10; Matthew 19:21–22"> “We will restore it,” they replied, “and will require nothing more from them. We will do as you say.” So I summoned the priests and required of the nobles and officials an oath that they would do what they had promised.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Acts 18:6; 1 Chronicles 16:36; Matthew 10:14"> I also shook out the folds of my robe and said, “May God likewise shake out of his house and possessions every man who does not keep this promise. May such a man be shaken out and have nothing!” The whole assembly said, “Amen,” and they praised the LORD. And the people did as they had promised.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Nehemiah 13:6; 2 Thessalonians 3:8–9; 1 Corinthians 9:4–15"> Furthermore, from the day King Artaxerxes appointed me to be their governor in the land of Judah, from his twentieth year until his thirty-second year (twelve years total), neither I nor my brothers ate the food allotted to the governor.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Nehemiah 5:9; Isaiah 50:10; Proverbs 16:6"> The governors before me had heavily burdened the people, taking from them bread and wine plus forty shekels of silver. Their servants also oppressed the people. But I did not do this, because of my fear of God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Galatians 6:9; 1 Thessalonians 2:5–6; 1 Corinthians 15:58"> Instead, I devoted myself to the construction of the wall, and all my servants were gathered there for the work; we did not acquire any land.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Kings 18:19; 1 Peter 4:9–10; 2 Samuel 9:7"> There were 150 Jews and officials at my table, besides the guests from the surrounding nations.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Kings 4:22–23; Psalm 37:21; Psalm 37:26"> Each day one ox, six choice sheep, and some fowl were prepared for me, and once every ten days an abundance of all kinds of wine was provided. But I did not demand the food allotted to the governor, because the burden on the people was so heavy.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Nehemiah 13:31; Psalm 106:4; Nehemiah 13:14"> Remember me favorably, O my God, for all that I have done for this people.</span>
