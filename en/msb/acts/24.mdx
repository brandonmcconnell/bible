<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 24:11; Acts 23:2; Acts 25:2"> Five days later the high priest Ananias came down with the elders and a lawyer named Tertullus, who presented to the governor their case against Paul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Proverbs 29:5; Proverbs 26:28; Acts 24:26–27"> When Paul had been called in, Tertullus opened the prosecution: “Because of you, we have enjoyed a lasting peace, and your foresight has brought worthy reforms to this nation.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Acts 23:26; Acts 26:25; Luke 1:3"> In every way and everywhere, most excellent Felix, we acknowledge this with all gratitude.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Hebrews 11:32"> But in order not to delay you any further, I beg your indulgence to hear us briefly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Acts 28:22; Acts 24:14; Acts 26:5"> We have found this man to be a pestilence, stirring up dissension among the Jews all over the world. He is a ringleader of the sect of the Nazarenes,</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 24:12; Acts 21:27–32; John 18:31"> and he even tried to desecrate the temple; so we seized him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7"> </span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Acts 23:35; Acts 25:15–16; Acts 25:5"> By examining him yourself, you will be able to learn the truth about all our charges against him.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 2:16; Micah 7:2–3; Psalm 4:2"> The Jews concurred, asserting that these charges were true.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Peter 3:15; 1 Samuel 2:25; Luke 18:2"> When the governor motioned for Paul to speak, he began his response: “Knowing that you have been a judge over this nation for many years, I gladly make my defense.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 24:1; Acts 21:18; Acts 23:11"> You can verify for yourself that no more than twelve days ago I went up to worship in Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 25:8; Acts 28:17; Acts 24:18"> Yet my accusers did not find me debating with anyone in the temple or riling up a crowd in the synagogues or in the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Acts 25:7; 1 Peter 3:16"> Nor can they prove any of their charges against me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Acts 9:2; Romans 3:21; Acts 3:13"> I do confess to you, however, that I worship the God of our fathers according to the Way, which they call a sect. I believe everything that is written throughout the Law and the Prophets,</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Daniel 12:2; John 5:28–29; 1 Thessalonians 4:14–16"> and I have the same hope in God that they themselves cherish, that there will be a resurrection of the dead—of both the righteous and the wicked.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Acts 23:1; 1 Timothy 1:5; 2 Corinthians 4:2"> In this hope, I strive always to maintain a clear conscience before God and man.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Galatians 2:10; Romans 15:25–28; 1 Corinthians 16:1–4"> After several years, then, I returned to Jerusalem to bring alms to my people and to present offerings.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Acts 26:21; Acts 21:26–30; Acts 24:12"> At the time they found me in the temple, I was ceremonially clean and was not inciting a crowd or an uproar. But there are some Jews from the province of Asia</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Acts 23:30; Acts 25:16"> who ought to appear before you and bring charges, if they have anything against me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> Otherwise, let these men state for themselves any crime they found in me when I stood before the Sanhedrin,</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Acts 23:6; Acts 4:2; Acts 26:6–8"> unless it was this one thing I called out as I stood in their presence: ‘It is concerning the resurrection of the dead that I am being tried by you today.’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 24:6; Acts 24:24; Acts 25:26"> On hearing these things Felix, who was well informed about the Way, adjourned the hearing and said, “When Lysias the commander comes, I will decide your case.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Acts 28:16; Acts 27:3; Proverbs 16:7"> He ordered the centurion to keep Paul under guard, but to allow him some freedom and permit his friends to come and minister to his needs.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 20:21; Galatians 2:16; Mark 6:20"> After several days, Felix returned with his wife Drusilla, who was a Jewess. He sent for Paul and listened to him speak about faith in Christ.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="John 16:8; 1 Peter 3:15; Acts 10:42"> As Paul expounded on righteousness, self-control, and the coming judgment, Felix became frightened and said, “You may go for now. When I find the time, I will call for you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="1 Timothy 6:9–10; Proverbs 17:23; Isaiah 56:11"> At the same time, he was hoping that Paul would offer him a bribe to release him. So he sent for Paul frequently and talked with him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Acts 25:9; Acts 25:14; Acts 12:3"> After two years had passed, Felix was succeeded by Porcius Festus. And wishing to do the Jews a favor, Felix left Paul in prison.</span>
