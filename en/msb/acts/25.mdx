<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 23:34; Acts 25:5; Acts 8:40"> Three days after his arrival in the province, Festus went up from Caesarea to Jerusalem,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Acts 25:15; Proverbs 4:16; Acts 24:1"> where the high priest and Jewish leaders presented their case against Paul. They urged Festus</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Acts 9:24; Acts 23:12–15; 1 Samuel 23:19–21"> to grant them a concession against Paul by summoning him to Jerusalem, because they were preparing an ambush to kill him along the way.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Acts 24:23"> But Festus replied, “Paul is being held in Caesarea, and I myself am going there soon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Acts 25:25; John 18:29–30; Acts 25:18–19"> So if this man has done anything wrong, let some of your leaders come down with me and accuse him there.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 25:17; Acts 25:10; Matthew 27:19"> After spending more than ten days with them, Festus went down to Caesarea. The next day he sat on the judgment seat and ordered that Paul be brought in.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Acts 24:13; Luke 23:10; Acts 25:24"> When Paul arrived, the Jews who had come down from Jerusalem stood around him, laying against Paul many serious charges that they could not prove.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Acts 24:12; Acts 28:17; 2 Corinthians 1:12"> Then Paul made his defense: “I have committed no offense against the law of the Jews or against the temple or against Caesar.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 24:27; Acts 12:3; Acts 25:20"> But Festus, wishing to do the Jews a favor, said to Paul, “Are you willing to go up to Jerusalem to stand trial before me on these charges?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Matthew 27:18; 2 Corinthians 4:2; Acts 28:18"> Paul replied, “I am standing before the judgment seat of Caesar, where I ought to be tried. I have done nothing wrong to the Jews, as you yourself know very well.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 28:19; Acts 26:32; Acts 25:25"> If, however, I am guilty of anything worthy of death, I do not refuse to die. But if there is no truth to their accusations against me, no one has the right to hand me over to them. I appeal to Caesar!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 19:21; Philippians 1:12–14; Philippians 1:20"> Then Festus conferred with his council and replied, “You have appealed to Caesar. To Caesar you will go!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Acts 8:40; Acts 26:1; Acts 26:27–28"> After several days had passed, King Agrippa and Bernice came down to Caesarea to pay their respects to Festus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Acts 24:27"> Since he was staying several days, Festus laid out Paul’s case before the king: “There is a certain man whom Felix left in prison.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Acts 25:1–3; Esther 3:9; Luke 18:3–5"> While I was in Jerusalem, the chief priests and elders of the Jews presented their case and requested a judgment against him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="John 7:51; Deuteronomy 19:17–18; Acts 23:30"> I told them that it is not the Roman custom to hand a man over to die before he has had an opportunity to face his accusers and defend himself against their charges.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Acts 25:6; Acts 25:10"> So when they came here with me, I did not delay. The next day I sat on the judgment seat and ordered that the man be brought in.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> But when his accusers rose to speak, they did not charge him with anything I had expected.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Acts 23:29; Acts 18:15; 1 Corinthians 15:3–4"> They only had some contentions with him regarding their own religion and a certain Jesus who had died, but whom Paul affirmed to be alive.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Acts 25:9"> Since I was at a loss as to how to investigate this matter, I asked if he was willing to go to Jerusalem and be tried there on these charges.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Timothy 4:16; Luke 2:1; Acts 25:10–11"> But when Paul appealed to be held over for the decision of the Emperor, I ordered that he be held until I could send him to Caesar.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 9:15; Matthew 10:18; Isaiah 52:15"> Then Agrippa said to Festus, “I would like to hear this man myself.” “Tomorrow you will hear him,” Festus declared.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Acts 26:30; Acts 25:13; 1 John 2:16"> The next day Agrippa and Bernice came with great pomp and entered the auditorium, along with the commanders and leading men of the city. And Festus ordered that Paul be brought in.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 22:22; Acts 25:7; Acts 25:2–3"> Then Festus said, “King Agrippa and all who are present with us, you see this man. The whole Jewish community has petitioned me about him, both here and in Jerusalem, crying out that he ought not to live any longer.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Acts 23:29; Acts 25:11–12; Luke 23:4"> But I found he had done nothing worthy of death, and since he has now appealed to the Emperor, I decided to send him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 26:2–3"> I have nothing definite to write to our sovereign about him. Therefore I have brought him before all of you, and especially before you, King Agrippa, so that after this inquiry I may have something to write.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="John 7:51; Proverbs 18:13"> For it seems unreasonable to me to send on a prisoner without specifying the charges against him.”</span>
