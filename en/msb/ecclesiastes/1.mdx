<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 12:8–10; Ecclesiastes 1:12; Ecclesiastes 7:27"> These are the words of the Teacher, the son of David, king in Jerusalem:</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Psalm 144:4; Romans 8:20; Ecclesiastes 5:10"> “Futility of futilities,” says the Teacher, “futility of futilities! Everything is futile!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 3:9; Ecclesiastes 5:16; Ecclesiastes 2:22"> What does a man gain from all his labor, at which he toils under the sun?</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Psalm 104:5; Zechariah 1:5; Ecclesiastes 6:12"> Generations come and generations go, but the earth remains forever.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Psalm 19:4–6; Habakkuk 3:11; Psalm 42:1"> The sun rises and the sun sets; it hurries back to where it rises.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="John 3:8; Ecclesiastes 11:5; Psalm 107:25"> The wind blows southward, then turns northward; round and round it swirls, ever returning on its course.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Job 38:10–11; Psalm 104:6–9"> All the rivers flow into the sea, yet the sea is never full; to the place from which the streams come, there again they flow.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Proverbs 27:20; Matthew 11:28; Ecclesiastes 4:8"> All things are wearisome, more than one can describe; the eye is not satisfied with seeing, nor the ear content with hearing.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 3:15; Ecclesiastes 6:10; Ecclesiastes 2:12"> What has been will be again, and what has been done will be done again; there is nothing new under the sun.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Acts 7:51; Luke 17:26–30; Matthew 23:30–32"> Is there a case where one can say, “Look, this is new”? It has already existed in the ages before us.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 2:16; Psalm 9:6; Ecclesiastes 9:5"> There is no remembrance of those who came before, and those yet to come will not be remembered by those who follow after.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 1:1; 1 Kings 4:1–19"> I, the Teacher, was king over Israel in Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 3:10; Genesis 3:19; 1 Timothy 4:15"> And I set my mind to seek and explore by wisdom all that is done under heaven. What a miserable task God has laid upon the sons of men to occupy them!</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 2:11; Ecclesiastes 2:17; Ecclesiastes 2:26"> I have seen all the things that are done under the sun, and have found them all to be futile, a pursuit of the wind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 7:12–13; Isaiah 40:4; Ecclesiastes 3:14"> What is crooked cannot be straightened, and what is lacking cannot be counted.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 2:9; 1 Kings 4:30; 1 Kings 10:7"> I said to myself, “Behold, I have grown and increased in wisdom beyond all those before me who were over Jerusalem, and my mind has observed a wealth of wisdom and knowledge.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 2:3; 1 Thessalonians 5:21; Ecclesiastes 1:13–14"> So I set my mind to know wisdom and madness and folly; I learned that this, too, is a pursuit of the wind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Job 28:28; Ecclesiastes 12:12–13; 1 Corinthians 3:18–20"> For with much wisdom comes much sorrow, and as knowledge grows, grief increases.</span>
