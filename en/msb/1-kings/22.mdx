<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Kings 20:34"> Then three years passed without war between Aram and Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Kings 15:24; 1 Kings 22:1; 1 Kings 22:41"> However, in the third year, Jehoshaphat king of Judah went down to visit the king of Israel,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Deuteronomy 4:43; 1 Kings 4:13; Joshua 21:38"> who said to his servants, “Do you not know that Ramoth-gilead is ours, but we have failed to take it from the hand of the king of Aram?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Kings 3:7; Ephesians 5:11; 2 John 1:11"> So he asked Jehoshaphat, “Will you go with me to fight against Ramoth-gilead?” Jehoshaphat answered the king of Israel, “I am as you are, my people are your people, and my horses are your horses.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Proverbs 3:5–6; 2 Chronicles 18:4–5; Jeremiah 42:2–6"> But Jehoshaphat also said to the king of Israel, “Please inquire first for the word of the LORD.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Kings 18:19; Jeremiah 23:14–17; Ezekiel 13:7–16"> So the king of Israel assembled the prophets, about four hundred men, and asked them, “Should I go to war against Ramoth-gilead, or should I refrain?” “Go up,” they replied, “and the Lord will deliver it into the hand of the king.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Kings 3:11–13; 2 Chronicles 18:6–7"> But Jehoshaphat asked, “Is there not still a prophet of the LORD here of whom we can inquire?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Amos 5:10; John 3:19–21; John 7:7"> The king of Israel answered, “There is still one man through whom we can inquire of the LORD, but I hate him because he never prophesies anything good for me, but only bad. He is Micaiah son of Imlah.” “The king should not say that!” Jehoshaphat replied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Kings 9:32; Isaiah 39:7; 1 Kings 22:26–27"> So the king of Israel called one of his officials and said, “Bring Micaiah son of Imlah at once.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Esther 5:1; Esther 6:8–9; 1 Kings 18:29"> Dressed in royal attire, the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah were sitting on their thrones at the threshing floor by the entrance of the gate of Samaria, with all the prophets prophesying before them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Zechariah 1:18–21; Deuteronomy 33:17; Ezekiel 22:27–28"> Now Zedekiah son of Chenaanah had made for himself iron horns and declared, “This is what the LORD says: ‘With these you shall gore the Arameans until they are finished off.’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Chronicles 35:22; 1 Kings 22:6–15; 1 Kings 22:32–36"> And all the prophets were prophesying the same, saying, “Go up to Ramoth-gilead and triumph, for the LORD will deliver it into the hand of the king.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Micah 2:6–7; Psalm 11:1; Isaiah 30:10–11"> Then the messenger who had gone to call Micaiah instructed him, “Behold now, with one accord the words of the prophets are favorable to the king. So please let your words be like theirs, and speak favorably.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Numbers 24:13; Numbers 22:18; 2 Chronicles 18:12–13"> But Micaiah said, “As surely as the LORD lives, I will speak whatever the LORD tells me.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Kings 18:27; Judges 10:14; Matthew 26:45"> When Micaiah arrived, the king asked him, “Micaiah, should we go to war against Ramoth-gilead, or should we refrain?” “Go up and triumph,” Micaiah replied, “for the LORD will deliver it into the hand of the king.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Acts 19:13; Matthew 26:63; Joshua 6:26"> But the king said to him, “How many times must I make you swear not to tell me anything but the truth in the name of the LORD?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Numbers 27:17; Matthew 9:36; 1 Kings 22:34–36"> So Micaiah declared: “I saw all Israel scattered on the hills like sheep without a shepherd. And the LORD said, ‘These people have no master; let each one return home in peace.’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Kings 22:8; Proverbs 10:24; Luke 11:45"> Then the king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, “Did I not tell you that he never prophesies good for me, but only bad?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Job 2:1; Job 1:6; Daniel 7:9–10"> Micaiah continued, “Therefore hear the word of the LORD: I saw the LORD sitting on His throne, and all the host of heaven standing by Him on His right and on His left.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Ezekiel 14:9; Job 12:16; Jeremiah 4:10"> And the LORD said, ‘Who will entice Ahab to march up and fall at Ramoth-gilead?’ And one suggested this, and another that.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Job 2:1; 1 Kings 22:23; Job 1:6–7"> Then a spirit came forward, stood before the LORD, and said, ‘I will entice him.’ ‘By what means?’ asked the LORD.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Judges 9:23; 1 Samuel 19:9; 1 Samuel 16:14"> And he replied, ‘I will go out and be a lying spirit in the mouths of all his prophets.’ ‘You will surely entice him and prevail,’ said the LORD. ‘Go and do it.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Ezekiel 14:9; Matthew 24:24–25; Isaiah 44:20"> So you see, the LORD has put a lying spirit in the mouths of all these prophets of yours, and the LORD has pronounced disaster against you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 23:2; Lamentations 3:30; Micah 5:1"> Then Zedekiah son of Chenaanah went up, struck Micaiah in the face, and demanded, “Which way did the Spirit of the LORD go when He departed from me to speak with you?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="1 Kings 20:30; 2 Peter 2:1; Revelation 19:20"> Micaiah replied, “You will soon see, on that day when you go and hide in an inner room.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> And the king of Israel declared, “Take Micaiah and return him to Amon the governor of the city and to Joash the king’s son,</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 Chronicles 16:10; 2 Chronicles 18:25–27; Revelation 2:10"> and tell them that this is what the king says: ‘Put this man in prison and feed him only bread and water until I return safely.’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Numbers 16:29; Micah 1:2; Acts 13:10–11"> But Micaiah replied, “If you ever return safely, the LORD has not spoken through me.” Then he added, “Take heed, all you people!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="2 Chronicles 18:28; 1 Kings 22:2–9"> So the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah went up to Ramoth-gilead.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="2 Chronicles 35:22; Jeremiah 23:24; 1 Samuel 28:8"> And the king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, “I will disguise myself and go into battle, but you wear your royal robes.” So the king of Israel disguised himself and went into battle.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="1 Kings 20:24; 2 Chronicles 18:30; Jeremiah 16:6"> Now the king of Aram had ordered his thirty-two chariot commanders, “Do not fight with anyone, small or great, except the king of Israel.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Proverbs 13:20; Jonah 2:1–2; Psalm 130:1–4"> When the chariot commanders saw Jehoshaphat, they said, “Surely this is the king of Israel!” So they turned to fight against him, but Jehoshaphat cried out.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Psalm 76:10; 1 Kings 22:31"> And when the chariot commanders saw that he was not the king of Israel, they turned back from pursuing him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="2 Chronicles 35:23; Micah 6:13; 1 Samuel 17:49"> However, a certain man drew his bow without taking special aim, and he struck the king of Israel between the joints of his armor. So the king said to his charioteer, “Turn around and take me out of the battle, for I am badly wounded!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="1 Kings 22:28; 1 Kings 20:42"> The battle raged throughout that day, and the king was propped up in his chariot facing the Arameans. And the blood from his wound ran out onto the floor of the chariot, and that evening he died.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="2 Kings 14:12; 1 Kings 22:31; Judges 21:24"> As the sun was setting, the cry rang out in the army: “Every man to his own city, and every man to his own land!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37"> So the king died and was brought to Samaria, where they buried him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="1 Kings 21:19; Matthew 24:35; Jeremiah 44:21–23"> And the chariot was washed at the pool of Samaria where the prostitutes bathed, and the dogs licked up Ahab’s blood, according to the word that the LORD had spoken.</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Amos 3:15; Ezekiel 27:6; Psalm 45:8"> As for the rest of the acts of Ahab, along with all his accomplishments and the ivory palace and all the cities he built, are they not written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Israel?</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="2 Samuel 7:12; 2 Kings 1:17; 2 Kings 1:2"> And Ahab rested with his fathers, and his son Ahaziah reigned in his place.</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="2 Chronicles 20:31; 1 Kings 22:2; 2 Chronicles 17:1"> In the fourth year of Ahab’s reign over Israel, Jehoshaphat son of Asa became king of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="2 Kings 1:17; 1 Kings 15:2; 1 Kings 15:10"> Jehoshaphat was thirty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem twenty-five years. His mother’s name was Azubah daughter of Shilhi.</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43" data-xref="1 Kings 15:14; 2 Kings 12:3; Proverbs 4:27"> And Jehoshaphat walked in all the ways of his father Asa; he did not turn away from them, but did what was right in the eyes of the LORD. The high places, however, were not removed; the people still sacrificed and burned incense on the high places.</span>
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44" data-xref="1 Kings 22:2; 2 Chronicles 19:2; 2 Kings 8:18"> Jehoshaphat also made peace with the king of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v45" data-v="45" data-xref="1 Kings 14:29; 1 Kings 11:41; 2 Chronicles 20:34"> As for the rest of the acts of Jehoshaphat, along with the might he exercised and how he waged war, are they not written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah?</span>
<span className="v" id="v46" data-v="46" data-xref="1 Kings 15:12; 1 Kings 14:24; Deuteronomy 23:17"> He banished from the land the male shrine prostitutes who remained from the days of his father Asa.</span>
<span className="v" id="v47" data-v="47" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:14; 2 Kings 3:9; 2 Kings 8:20"> And there was no king in Edom; a deputy served as king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v48" data-v="48" data-xref="1 Kings 9:26; 1 Kings 10:22; 1 Kings 9:28"> Jehoshaphat built ships of Tarshish to go to Ophir for gold, but they never set sail, because they were wrecked at Ezion-geber.</span>
<span className="v" id="v49" data-v="49"> At that time Ahaziah son of Ahab said to Jehoshaphat, “Let my servants sail with your servants,” but Jehoshaphat refused.</span>
<span className="v" id="v50" data-v="50" data-xref="2 Chronicles 21:1; 1 Kings 22:40; 1 Kings 15:24"> And Jehoshaphat rested with his fathers and was buried with them in the city of his father David. And his son Jehoram reigned in his place.</span>
<span className="v" id="v51" data-v="51" data-xref="2 Kings 1:17; 1 Kings 22:40; 1 Kings 15:25"> In the seventeenth year of Jehoshaphat’s reign over Judah, Ahaziah son of Ahab became king of Israel, and he reigned in Samaria two years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v52" data-v="52" data-xref="1 Kings 15:26; 1 Kings 21:25; Revelation 3:20"> And he did evil in the sight of the LORD and walked in the ways of his father and mother and of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who had caused Israel to sin.</span>
<span className="v" id="v53" data-v="53" data-xref="1 Kings 16:30–32; Isaiah 65:3; 2 Kings 3:2"> Ahaziah served and worshiped Baal, provoking the LORD, the God of Israel, to anger, just as his father had done.</span>
