<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Genesis 12:10; Genesis 26:1; Ezekiel 14:13"> In the days when the judges judged, there was a famine in the land. A certain man of Bethlehem Judah went to live in the country of Moab with his wife and his two sons.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Genesis 35:19; Micah 5:2; 1 Samuel 17:21"> The name of the man was Elimelech, and the name of his wife Naomi. The names of his two sons were Mahlon and Chilion, Ephrathites of Bethlehem Judah. They came into the country of Moab and lived there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Hebrews 12:10–11; Psalms 34:19; Hebrews 12:6"> Elimelech, Naomi’s husband, died; and she was left with her two sons.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Matthew 1:5; 1 Kings 11:1–2; Deuteronomy 23:3"> They took for themselves wives of the women of Moab. The name of the one was Orpah, and the name of the other was Ruth. They lived there about ten years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Deuteronomy 32:39; Psalms 89:30–32; Isaiah 49:21"> Mahlon and Chilion both died, and the woman was bereaved of her two children and of her husband.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Psalms 132:15; Exodus 3:16; Psalms 111:5"> Then she arose with her daughters-in-law, that she might return from the country of Moab; for she had heard in the country of Moab how the LORD<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="When rendered in ALL CAPITAL LETTERS, “LORD” or “GOD” is the translation of God’s Proper Name (Hebrew “יהוה”, usually pronounced Yahweh).">a</a></sup>had visited his people in giving them bread.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Kings 8:3; Ruth 1:14; Exodus 18:27"> She went out of the place where she was, and her two daughters-in-law with her. They went on the way to return to the land of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Ruth 2:20; Ruth 1:5; Colossians 3:18"> Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, “Go, return each of you to her mother’s house. May the LORD deal kindly with you, as you have dealt with the dead and with me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Ruth 3:1; Acts 20:37; Genesis 29:11"> May the LORD grant you that you may find rest, each of you in the house of her husband.” Then she kissed them, and they lifted up their voices, and wept.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Zechariah 8:23; Psalms 16:3; Psalms 119:63"> They said to her, “No, but we will return with you to your people.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Deuteronomy 25:5; Genesis 38:11"> Naomi said, “Go back, my daughters. Why do you want to go with me? Do I still have sons in my womb, that they may be your husbands?</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Genesis 17:17; 1 Timothy 5:9"> Go back, my daughters, go your way; for I am too old to have a husband. If I should say, ‘I have hope,’ if I should even have a husband tonight, and should also bear sons,</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Job 19:21; Judges 2:15; Psalms 32:4"> would you then wait until they were grown? Would you then refrain from having husbands? No, my daughters, for it grieves me seriously for your sakes, for the LORD’s hand has gone out against me.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Proverbs 18:24; Matthew 16:24; Matthew 10:37"> They lifted up their voices and wept again; then Orpah kissed her mother-in-law, but Ruth stayed with her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Judges 11:24; Joshua 24:15; Zephaniah 1:6"> She said, “Behold,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Behold”, from “הִנֵּה”, means look at, take notice, observe, see, or gaze at. It is often used as an interjection.">b</a></sup>your sister-in-law has gone back to her people and to her god. Follow your sister-in-law.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Samuel 15:21; Ruth 2:11–12; 2 Kings 2:2–6"> Ruth said, “Don’t urge me to leave you, and to return from following you, for where you go, I will go; and where you stay, I will stay. Your people will be my people, and your God<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="The Hebrew word rendered “God” is “אֱלֹהִ֑ים” (Elohim).">c</a></sup>my God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Samuel 3:17; 1 Kings 2:23; 2 Kings 6:31"> Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May the LORD do so to me, and more also, if anything but death parts you and me.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Acts 21:14; Ephesians 6:10; Acts 2:42"> When Naomi saw that she was determined to go with her, she stopped urging her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Matthew 21:10; Isaiah 23:7; Lamentations 2:15"> So they both went until they came to Bethlehem. When they had come to Bethlehem, all the city was excited about them, and they asked, “Is this Naomi?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Job 6:4; Hebrews 12:11; Psalms 73:14"> She said to them, “Don’t call me Naomi.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Naomi” means “pleasant”.">d</a></sup>Call me Mara,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="“Mara” means “bitter”.">e</a></sup>for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Samuel 2:7–8; Job 1:21; Job 16:8"> I went out full, and the LORD has brought me home again empty. Why do you call me Naomi, since the LORD has testified against me, and the Almighty has afflicted me?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Ruth 2:23; 2 Samuel 21:9; Exodus 9:31–32"> So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabitess, her daughter-in-law, with her, who returned out of the country of Moab. They came to Bethlehem in the beginning of barley harvest.</span>
