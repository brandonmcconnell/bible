## Saul and his 3 sons died

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Chronicles 10:1–12; 1 Samuel 28:4; 2 Samuel 1:21"> After that, the Philistia mob fought with the Israel mob. They fought on the hill called Gilboa. Lots of Israel soldiers died there, and the other soldiers got frightened and ran away.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Chronicles 8:33; 1 Samuel 14:49; 1 Samuel 23:17"> The Philistia mob chased Saul and his sons, and they got closer and closer. Then they killed dead Saul’s sons, Jonathan, Abinadab and Malkishua.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Samuel 1:6; Genesis 49:23; 1 Kings 22:34"> The soldiers that were close to Saul fought hard. The Philistia mob shot Saul lots of times with bows and arrows, and they hurt him very badly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Judges 9:54; 2 Samuel 1:6; 2 Samuel 1:9–10"> Saul talked to one of the soldiers there, the one that carried his shield for him. He said, “Get your long knife and stab me. I don’t want that mob to kill me dead. They don’t know God. They might make fun of me as they kill me.” The soldier was frightened and did not want to stab him. So Saul got the knife and stabbed himself in his belly and killed himself.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Chronicles 10:5"> Then that soldier saw that his boss was dead, so he stabbed himself and died too.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Samuel 12:17; Hosea 13:10–11; 1 Chronicles 10:6"> That’s how Saul died, and his sons, and the man that carried his shield, and his soldiers. They all died on that day.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Deuteronomy 28:33; Leviticus 26:32; Leviticus 26:36"> The people that saw what happened told this bad news to everybody along the Jezreel Valley and across the Jordan River. The Israel mob in all those places heard it, and they ran away frightened. They left their towns empty, and the Philistia mob went and lived in those towns.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Chronicles 20:25; 1 Chronicles 10:8"> The next day, the Philistia mob went back up the hill at Gilboa to get stuff from the dead bodies. They saw the dead bodies of Saul and his 3 sons there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Samuel 1:20; Judges 16:23–24; 1 Samuel 17:54"> They cut off Saul’s head and took off his heavy iron plates. Then they sent messengers to every place in Philistia, to tell the story to the people and in the houses of their gods.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Judges 2:13; Joshua 17:11; 1 Samuel 21:9"> They put Saul’s iron plates in one of those houses, the house of the female god called Astarti. At Beth-Shan they hung Saul’s dead body on the wall that was around the town.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Samuel 11:1–11; 2 Samuel 2:4"> The people from another place called Jabesh, in the Gilead country, they heard the bad news too. They remembered that Saul helped them before. They were very upset about the bad things the Philistia mob did to Saul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Chronicles 16:14; Jeremiah 34:5; 2 Samuel 2:4–7"> So that night lots of brave men from Jabesh walked all night to Beth-Shan. They took down the bodies of Saul and his sons from the wall, and they carried them back to Jabesh. They burned the bodies</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Samuel 21:12–14; Genesis 50:10; 1 Samuel 22:6"> and buried the bones under the big tree there. For the next 7 days they ate no food. They just sat sadly together and felt sorry for Saul.</span>
