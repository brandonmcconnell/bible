<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Kings 15:18; 1 Kings 15:20; 1 Kings 22:31"> Ben Hadad king of Aram gathered all his army together. There were thirty-two lesser kings with him, and horses and chariots. He went up, besieged Samaria and fought against it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Isaiah 36:2–22; 2 Kings 19:9; Isaiah 37:9–10"> He sent messengers into the city to Ahab king of Israel, and said to him, “Ben Hadad says this:</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Exodus 15:9; Isaiah 10:13–14"> 'Your silver and your gold are mine. Also your wives and children, the best ones, are now mine.'”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Judges 15:11–13; Deuteronomy 28:48; 1 Samuel 13:6–7"> The king of Israel answered and said, “It is as you say, my master, king. I and all that I have are yours.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> The messengers came again and said, “Ben Hadad says this, 'I sent word to you saying that you must give me your silver, your gold, your wives, and your children.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Jeremiah 25:34; Joel 3:5; 2 Kings 18:31–32"> But I will send my servants to you tomorrow about this time, and they will search your house and your servants' houses. They will seize with their own hands and take away whatever pleases their eyes.'”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Kings 5:7; Proverbs 6:14; 1 Kings 8:1"> Then the king of Israel called all the elders of the land together and said, “Please take note and see how this man seeks trouble. He has sent word to me in order to take my wives, children, and silver and gold, and I have not refused him.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> All the elders and all the people said to Ahab, “Do not listen to him or consent to his demands.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> So Ahab said to the messengers of Ben Hadad, “Tell my master the king, 'I agree to everything that you sent your servant to do the first time, but I cannot accept this second demand.'” So the messengers left and took this response to Ben Hadad.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Kings 19:2; Exodus 11:8; 2 Kings 19:23–24"> Then Ben Hadad sent his reply to Ahab, and said, “May the gods do so to me and more also, if even the ashes of Samaria will be enough for all the people who follow me to have a handful each.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Proverbs 27:1; Isaiah 10:15–16; 1 Samuel 17:44–47"> The king of Israel answered and said, “Tell Ben Hadad, 'No one who is just putting on his armor, should boast as if he were taking it off.'”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Kings 16:9; 1 Kings 20:16; Proverbs 31:4–5"> Ben Hadad heard this message as he was drinking, he and the kings under him who were in their tents. Ben Hadad commanded his men, “Line yourselves up in position for battle.” So they prepared themselves in position of battle to attack the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Kings 20:28; Ezekiel 20:22; Isaiah 7:1–9"> Then behold, a prophet came to Ahab king of Israel and said, “Yahweh says, 'Have you seen this great army? Look, I will place it into your hand today, and you will know that I am Yahweh.'”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="1 Samuel 17:50; Genesis 14:14–16; 1 Kings 18:44"> Ahab replied, “By whom?” Yahweh replied and said, “By the young officers who serve the governors of the districts.” Then Ahab said, “Who will begin the battle?” Yahweh answered, “You.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Judges 7:16; 1 Samuel 14:2; Judges 7:7"> Then Ahab mustered the young officers who served the governors of the districts. They numbered 232. After them he mustered all the soldiers, all the army of Israel; seven thousand in number.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 10:16–17; Hosea 4:11; 1 Kings 16:9"> They went out at noon. Ben Hadad had been drinking himself drunk in his tent, he and the thirty-two lesser kings who were supporting him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Kings 20:14–15; 1 Kings 20:19"> The young officers who served the governors of the districts went forward first. Then Ben Hadad was informed by scouts that he had sent out, “Men are coming out from Samaria.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="2 Kings 14:8–12; 1 Samuel 14:11–12; Proverbs 18:12"> Ben Hadad said, “Whether they have come out for peace or war, take them alive.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> So the young officers who served the governors of the districts went out of the city and the army followed them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Samuel 30:16–17; 2 Kings 19:36; Ecclesiastes 9:11"> Each man killed his opponent. The Arameans fled and Israel pursued them. Ben Hadad the king of Aram escaped on a horse along with some horsemen.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Kings 3:18; Judges 3:28; 2 Kings 3:24"> Then the king of Israel went out and attacked the horses and chariots, and killed the Arameans in a great slaughter.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Samuel 11:1; 1 Kings 20:26; 1 Kings 20:13"> So the prophet came to the king of Israel and said to him, “Go, strengthen yourself, and understand and plan what you are doing, because at the return of the year the king of Aram will come up against you again.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Kings 14:23; 1 Samuel 4:8; Psalms 50:21–22"> The servants of the king of Aram said to him, “Their god is a god of the hills. That is why they were stronger than we were. But now let us fight against them in the plain, and surely we will be stronger than they are.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Kings 22:31; Proverbs 21:30; 1 Kings 20:16"> So you must do this: Remove all the kings from their positions of authority and replace them with military commanders.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Psalms 10:3"> Raise up an army like the army you lost—horse for horse and chariot for chariot—so we can fight them in the plain. Then surely we will be stronger than they are.” So Ben Hadad listened to their advice and did what they advised.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Kings 13:17; 1 Kings 20:22; 1 Samuel 4:1"> After the beginning of the new year, Ben Hadad mustered the Arameans and went up to Aphek to fight against Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Judges 6:5; 1 Samuel 13:5–8; 1 Samuel 14:2"> The people of Israel were mustered and supplied to fight against them. The people of Israel camped before them like two little flocks of goats, but the Arameans filled the countryside.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="1 Kings 20:13; 1 Kings 17:18; Ezekiel 12:16"> Then a man of God came near and spoke to the king of Israel and said, “Yahweh says: 'Because the Arameans have said that Yahweh is a god of the hills, but he is not a god of the valleys, I will place this great army into your hand, and you will know that I am Yahweh.'”</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="2 Chronicles 13:17; 2 Chronicles 28:6; Psalms 10:16"> So the armies camped opposite each other for seven days. Then on the seventh day the battle started. The people of Israel killed 100,000 Aramean footmen in one day.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="1 Kings 22:25; 2 Chronicles 18:24; 1 Kings 20:26"> The rest fled to Aphek, into the city, and the wall fell on twenty-seven thousand men who were left. Ben Hadad fled and went into the city, into an inner room.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 Samuel 3:31; Genesis 37:34; 1 Kings 20:23"> Ben Hadad's servants said to him, “Look now, we have heard that the kings of the house of Israel are merciful kings. Please let us put sackcloth around our waists and ropes around our heads, and go out to the king of Israel. Perhaps he will spare your life.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="1 Kings 20:3–6; Job 12:17–18; Daniel 5:20–23"> So they put sackcloth on their waists and ropes around their heads and then went to the king of Israel and said, “Your servant Ben Hadad said, 'Please let me live.'” Ahab said, “Is he still alive? He is my brother.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Luke 16:8; 2 Kings 10:15; Proverbs 25:13"> Now the men were listening for any sign from Ahab, so they quickly answered him, “Yes, your brother Ben Hadad is alive.” Then Ahab said, “Go and bring him.” Then Ben Hadad came to him, and Ahab had him come up into his chariot.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="1 Kings 15:20; Isaiah 26:10; 1 Kings 20:42"> Ben Hadad said to Ahab, “I will restore to you the cities that my father took from your father, and you may make markets for yourself in Damascus, as my father did in Samaria.” Ahab replied, “I will let you go with this covenant.” So Ahab made a covenant with him and then let him go.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="2 Kings 2:15; 1 Kings 13:17–18; 2 Kings 4:1"> A certain man, one of the sons of the prophets, said to one of his fellow prophets by the word of Yahweh, “Please hit me.” But the man refused to hit him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="1 Kings 13:21–24; 1 Kings 13:26; 1 Samuel 15:22–23"> Then the prophet said to his fellow prophet, “Because you have not obeyed the voice of Yahweh, as soon as you leave me, a lion will kill you.” As soon as that man had left him, a lion came upon him and killed him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="Exodus 21:12; 1 Kings 20:35"> Then the prophet found another man and said, “Please hit me.” So the man hit him and wounded him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="1 Kings 14:2; Matthew 6:16; 1 Kings 22:30"> Then the prophet left and waited for the king by the road; he had disguised himself with a bandage over his eyes.</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="2 Kings 10:24; Job 36:18; 1 Peter 1:18–19"> As the king passed by, the prophet cried out to the king and said, “Your servant went out into the heat of the battle, and a soldier stopped and brought a man to me and said, 'Watch this man. If by any means he goes missing, your life will be given for his life, or you must pay a talent of silver.'</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="2 Samuel 12:5–7; Matthew 25:24–27; Job 15:6"> But because your servant was busy going here and there, the man escaped.” Then the king of Israel said to him, “This is what your punishment will be—you yourself have decided it.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="Jeremiah 6:26; 2 Samuel 13:19; Job 2:8"> Then the prophet quickly removed the bandage from his eyes, and the king of Israel recognized that he was one of the prophets.</span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="1 Kings 22:31–37; 2 Chronicles 18:33–34; 1 Kings 20:39"> The prophet said to the king, “Yahweh says, 'Because you have let go from your hand the man whom I had sentenced to death, your life will take the place of his life, and your people for his people.'”</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43" data-xref="1 Kings 21:4; Esther 5:13; Esther 6:12–13"> So the king of Israel went to his house resentful and angry, and arrived in Samaria.</span>
