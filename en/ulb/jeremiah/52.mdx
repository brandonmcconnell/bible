<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Kings 24:18; Joshua 10:29; 2 Chronicles 36:11"> Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he began to reign; he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Hamutal; she was the daughter of Jeremiah from Libnah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Kings 14:22; 2 Kings 24:19–20; Ezekiel 17:16–20"> He did what was evil in the sight of Yahweh; he did everything that Jehoiakim had done.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Isaiah 3:4–5; 2 Chronicles 36:13; Isaiah 3:1"> Through Yahweh's anger, all these events happened in Jerusalem and Judah, until he drove them from before himself. Then Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Jeremiah 39:1; Zechariah 8:19; Ezekiel 24:1–2"> It happened that in the ninth year of the reign of King Zedekiah, in the tenth month, and on the tenth day of the month, Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, came with all his army against Jerusalem. They camped opposite it, and they built a siege wall around it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> So the city was besieged until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah's reign.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Jeremiah 39:2; Isaiah 3:1; 2 Kings 25:3"> By the ninth day of the fourth month the famine was so severe in the city that there was no food for the people of the land.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Jeremiah 39:4–7; Jeremiah 51:32; 2 Kings 25:4"> Then the city was broken into, and all the fighting men fled and went out of the city at night by the way of the gate that was between the two walls, by the king's garden, although the Chaldeans were all around the city. So they went in the direction of the Arabah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Jeremiah 34:21; Jeremiah 32:4; Jeremiah 38:23"> But the army of Chaldeans pursued the king and overtook Zedekiah in the plains of the Jordan River valley near Jericho. All his army was scattered away from him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Jeremiah 39:5; Numbers 13:21; 2 Kings 25:6"> They captured the king and brought him up to the king of Babylon at Riblah in the land of Hamath, where he passed sentence on him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Jeremiah 22:30; 2 Kings 25:7; Genesis 21:16"> The king of Babylon slaughtered Zedekiah's sons before his own eyes, and at Riblah he also slaughtered all the leaders of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ezekiel 12:13; Jeremiah 34:3–5"> Then he put out Zedekiah's eyes, bound him in bronze chains, and brought him to Babylon. The king of Babylon put him in prison until the day of his death.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Kings 25:8; Jeremiah 39:9; Jeremiah 52:29"> Now in the fifth month, on the tenth day of the month, which was the nineteenth year of the reign of King Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, Nebuzaradan came to Jerusalem. He was the commander of the king's bodyguards and a servant of the king of Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Psalms 79:1; Micah 3:12; 2 Chronicles 36:19"> He burned the house of Yahweh, the king's palace, and all the houses of Jerusalem; also every important building in the city he burned.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Nehemiah 1:3; 2 Kings 25:10"> As for the walls around Jerusalem, all the army of the Babylonians who were with the commander of the bodyguards destroyed them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Jeremiah 15:1–2; Zechariah 14:2"> As for the poorest people, the rest of the people who were left in the city, those who had deserted to the king of Babylon, and the rest of the craftsmen— Nebuzaradan, the commander of the bodyguards, took some of them away into exile.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Kings 25:12; Jeremiah 39:9–10; Jeremiah 40:5–7"> But Nebuzaradan, the commander of the bodyguards, left some of the poorest of the land to work the vineyards and fields.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Jeremiah 27:19–22; Jeremiah 52:21–23; 2 Chronicles 4:12–15"> As for the bronze pillars that belonged to the house of Yahweh, and the stands, and the large bronze basin called “The Sea” that were in the house of Yahweh, the Chaldeans broke them into pieces and carried all the bronze back to Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Exodus 27:3; Numbers 4:14; 1 Kings 7:45"> The pots, shovels, lamp trimmers, bowls, and all the utensils of bronze with which the priests had served in the temple—the Chaldeans took them all away.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 Kings 7:49–50; 2 Chronicles 4:6–22; Leviticus 26:12"> The basins and the incense burners, the bowls, pots, lampstands, pans, and basins that were made of gold, and those made of silver—the commander of the king's guard took them away as well.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Kings 7:47; 2 Kings 25:16; 2 Chronicles 4:18"> The two pillars, the large bronze basin known as “The Sea,” and the twelve bronze bulls that were under the stands, things that Solomon had made for the house of Yahweh, contained more bronze than could be weighed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Kings 25:17; 1 Kings 7:15–21; 2 Chronicles 3:15–17"> The pillars were eighteen cubits high each, and a line around each one measured twelve cubits. Each was four fingers thick and hollow.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Exodus 28:25; 2 Kings 25:17; 2 Chronicles 3:15"> A capital of bronze was on top of it. The capital was five cubits high, with latticework and pomegranates all around. It was all made of bronze. The other pillar and its pomegranates were the same as the first.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Kings 7:20"> So there were ninety-six pomegranates on the capital's sides, and one hundred pomegranates above the surrounding latticework.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="2 Kings 25:18; Jeremiah 29:25; Jeremiah 35:4"> The commander of the bodyguards took prisoner Seraiah, the high priest, together with Zephaniah, the second priest, and the three gatekeepers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Kings 25:19; Esther 1:14; Matthew 18:10"> From the city he took prisoner an officer who was in charge of soldiers, and seven men of those who advised the king, who were still in the city. He also took prisoner the king's army officer responsible for drafting men into the army, along with sixty important men from the land who were in the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> Then Nebuzaradan, the commander of the bodyguards, took them and brought them to the king of Babylon at Riblah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Ezekiel 33:28; Isaiah 32:13–14; Isaiah 6:11–12"> The king of Babylon put them to death at Riblah in the land of Hamath. In this way, Judah went out of its land into exile.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 Kings 24:12–16; 2 Kings 24:2–3; Daniel 1:1–3"> These were the people who Nebuchadnezzar exiled: In the seventh year, 3,023 Judeans.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:12; Genesis 12:5; 2 Kings 25:11"> In the eighteenth year of Nebuchadnezzar he took 832 people from Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:15; Jeremiah 6:9"> In the twenty-third year of Nebuchadnezzar, Nebuzaradan, the commander of the king's bodyguards, exiled 745 Judean people. All the exiled people totaled 4,600.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 Kings 25:27–30; Psalms 3:3; Genesis 40:13"> It happened later in the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin, king of Judah, in the twelfth month, on the twenty-fifth day of the month, that Awel-Marduk, king of Babylon released Jehoiachin king of Judah from prison. This happened in the year that Awel-Marduk began to reign.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Proverbs 12:25; Daniel 5:18–19; Daniel 2:37"> He spoke kindly to him and gave him a seat more honorable than that of the other kings who were with him in Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="2 Samuel 9:7; 1 Kings 2:7; Genesis 41:14"> Awel-Marduk removed Jehoiachin's prison clothes, and Jehoiachin ate regularly at the king's table for the rest of his life,</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="2 Samuel 9:10; Luke 11:3; Matthew 6:11"> and a regular food allowance was given to him every day for the rest of his life until his death.</span>
