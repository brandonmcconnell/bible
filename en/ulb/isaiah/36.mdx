<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Chronicles 32:1; 2 Kings 18:13; 2 Kings 18:17"> In the fourteenth year of King Hezekiah, Sennacherib, king of Assyria, attacked all the fortified cities of Judah and captured them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Chronicles 32:9–23; Isaiah 7:3; 2 Kings 18:17–37"> Then the king of Assyria sent the chief commander from Lachish to Jerusalem to King Hezekiah with a great army. He approached the conduit of the upper pool, on the highway to the launderers' field, and stood by it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:16–17; Isaiah 22:15–21; 2 Samuel 20:24–25"> The Israelite officials who went out of the city to talk with them were Hilkiah's son Eliakim, the palace administrator, Shebna the king's secretary, and Asaph's son Joah, who wrote down the government decisions.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Kings 18:19–37; Jude 1:16; Proverbs 16:18"> The chief commander said to them, “Tell Hezekiah that the great king, the king of Assyria, says, 'What is the source of your confidence?</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Kings 18:7; Jeremiah 52:3; Proverbs 21:30–31"> You speak only useless words, saying there is counsel and strength for war. Now in whom are you trusting? Who has given you courage to rebel against me?</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Ezekiel 29:6–7; Jeremiah 37:5–8; 2 Kings 18:21"> Look, you are trusting in Egypt, that splintered reed that you use as a walking staff, but if a man leans on it, it will stick into his hand and pierce it. That is what Pharaoh king of Egypt is to anyone who trusts in him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Kings 18:4–5; Psalms 42:10–11; Psalms 42:5"> But if you say to me, “We are trusting in Yahweh our God,” is not he the one whose high places and altars Hezekiah has taken away, and has said to Judah and to Jerusalem, “You must worship before this altar in Jerusalem”?</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Samuel 17:40–43; 1 Kings 20:10; Nehemiah 4:2–5"> Now therefore, I want to make you a good offer from my master the king of Assyria. I will give you two thousand horses, if you are able to find riders for them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Isaiah 10:8; Isaiah 30:16–17; Isaiah 36:6"> How could you resist even one captain of the least of my master's servants? You have put your trust in Egypt for chariots and horsemen!</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Kings 18:25; Isaiah 10:5–7; 1 Kings 13:18"> Now then, have I traveled up here without Yahweh to fight against this land and destroy it? Yahweh said to me, “Attack this land and destroy it.”'”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ezra 4:7; Daniel 2:4; 2 Kings 18:26–27"> Then Eliakim son of Hilkiah, and Shebnah, and Joah said to the chief commander, “Please speak to your servants in the Aramean language, Aramaic, for we understand it. Do not speak with us in the language of Judah in the ears of the people who are on the wall.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Kings 18:27; Leviticus 26:29; Lamentations 4:9–10"> But the chief commander said, “Has my master sent me to your master and to you to speak these words? Has he not sent me to the men who sit on the wall, who will have to eat their own dung and drink their own urine with you?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Chronicles 32:18; Psalms 73:8–9; 2 Kings 18:28–32"> Then the chief commander stood and shouted in a loud voice in the Jews' language, saying, “Listen to the words of the great king, the king of Assyria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Thessalonians 2:4; Revelation 13:5–6; Daniel 6:20"> The king says, 'Do not let Hezekiah deceive you, for he will not be able to rescue you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Isaiah 37:23–24; Matthew 27:43; Psalms 71:9–11"> Do not let Hezekiah make you trust in Yahweh, saying, “Yahweh will surely rescue us; this city will not be given into the hand of the king of Assyria.”'</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Kings 4:25; Zechariah 3:10; Micah 4:4"> Do not listen to Hezekiah, for this is what the king of Assyria says: 'Make peace with me and come out to me. Then every one of you will eat from his own vine and from his own fig tree, and drink from the water in his own cistern.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Kings 18:32; Deuteronomy 8:7–9; Job 20:17"> You will do this until I come and take you away to a land like your own land, a land of grain and new wine, a land of bread and vineyards.'</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Isaiah 37:17–18; Isaiah 36:15; Isaiah 37:10"> Do not let Hezekiah mislead you, saying, 'Yahweh will rescue us.' Has any of the gods of the peoples rescued them from the hand of the king of Assyria?</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Isaiah 10:9–11; Jeremiah 49:23; 2 Samuel 8:9"> Where are the gods of Hamath and Arpad? Where are the gods of Sepharvaim? Have they rescued Samaria from my power?</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Psalms 50:21; 2 Chronicles 32:19; Isaiah 37:23–29"> Among all the gods of these lands, is there any god who has rescued his land from my power, as if Yahweh could save Jerusalem from my power?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Proverbs 26:4; Psalms 38:13–15; Matthew 7:6"> But the people remained silent and did not respond, for the king's order was, “Do not answer him.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Isaiah 36:3; Isaiah 33:7; Matthew 26:65"> Then Eliakim son of Hilkiah, who was over the household, Shebna the scribe, and Joah son of Asaph, the recorder, came to Hezekiah with their clothes torn, and reported to him the words of the chief commander.</span>
