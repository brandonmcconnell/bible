<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Kings 1:5; 2 Samuel 12:11; 1 Samuel 8:11"> It came about after this that Absalom prepared a chariot and horses for himself, with fifty men to run before him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Exodus 18:14; Ruth 4:1; Job 24:14"> Absalom would get up early and stand beside the road leading to the city gate. When any man had a dispute to come to the king for judgment, Absalom called to him and said, “From what city have you come?” Then the man would answer, “Your servant is from one of the tribes of Israel.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:15; Ezekiel 22:7; 1 Peter 2:17"> So Absalom would say to him, “Look, your case is good and right, but there is no one empowered by the king to hear your case.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Judges 9:29; Judges 9:1–5; Proverbs 27:2"> Absalom added, “I wish that I were made judge in the land, so that every man who had any dispute or cause might come to me, and I would bring him justice!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Samuel 14:33; Psalms 55:21; Proverbs 26:25"> So it came about that when any man came to Absalom to honor him, Absalom would put out his hand and take hold of him and kiss him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Romans 16:18; 2 Peter 2:3; Proverbs 11:9"> Absalom acted in this way to all Israel who came to the king for judgment. So Absalom stole the hearts of the men of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Isaiah 58:4; 1 Samuel 16:13; 1 Samuel 16:1–2"> It came about at the end of four years that Absalom said to the king, “Please let me go and pay a vow that I have made to Yahweh in Hebron.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Genesis 28:20–21; 2 Samuel 13:37–38; 1 Samuel 1:11"> For your servant made a vow while I was living at Geshur in Aram, saying,' If Yahweh will indeed bring me again to Jerusalem, then I will worship Yahweh.'”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> So the king said to him, “Go in peace.” So Absalom arose and went to Hebron.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Chronicles 12:38; 2 Samuel 5:5; 2 Samuel 3:2–3"> But then Absalom sent spies throughout all the tribes of Israel, saying, “As soon as you hear the sound of the trumpet, then you must say, 'Absalom is king in Hebron.'”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Samuel 9:13; 1 Samuel 22:15; Romans 16:18–19"> With Absalom went two hundred men from Jerusalem, who were invited. They went in their innocence, not knowing anything that Absalom had planned.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Psalms 41:9; 2 Samuel 15:31; Joshua 15:51"> While Absalom offered sacrfices, he sent for Ahithophel from his hometown of Giloh. He was David's counselor. Absalom's conspiracy was strong, for the people following Absalom were constantly increasing.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Judges 9:3; Matthew 27:22; 2 Samuel 15:6"> A messenger came to David saying, “The hearts of the men of Israel are following after Absalom.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Samuel 19:9; Ezekiel 46:18; Psalms 55:3–11"> So David said to all his servants who were with him at Jerusalem, “Arise and let us flee, or none of us will escape from Absalom. Prepare to leave immediately, or he will quickly overtake us, and he will bring down disaster on us and attack the city with the edge of the sword.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="John 6:66–69; Proverbs 18:24; Luke 22:28–29"> The king's servants said to the king, “Look, your servants are ready to do whatever our master the king decides.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Samuel 16:21–22; 2 Samuel 20:3; Psalms 3:1"> The king left and all his family after him, but the king left ten women, who were concubines, to keep the palace.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Psalms 3:1–2; Ecclesiastes 10:7; Psalms 66:12"> After the king went out and all the people after him, they stopped at the last house.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:18; 1 Samuel 30:14; 2 Samuel 20:7"> All his army marched with him, and before him went all the Kerethites, and all the Pelethites, and all the Gittites—six hundred men who had followed him from Gath.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Samuel 18:2; Ruth 1:11–13"> Then the king said to Ittai the Gittite, “Why will you come with us? Return and stay with the king, for you are a foreigner and an exile. Return to your own place.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Samuel 23:13; 2 Samuel 2:6; John 1:17"> Since you just left yesterday, why should I make you wander all over with us? I do not even know where I am going. So return and take your fellow countrymen back. May loyalty and faithfulness go with you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Ruth 1:16–17; Proverbs 17:17; 2 Kings 4:30"> But Ittai answered the king and said, “As Yahweh lives, and as my master the king lives, surely in whatever place where my master the king goes, there also will your servant go, whether that means living or dying.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> So David said to Ittai, “Go ahead and continue with us.” So Ittai the Gittite marched with the king, along with all his men and all the families who were with him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 Samuel 16:2; 1 Kings 2:37; John 18:1"> All the country wept with a loud voice as all the people passed by over the Kidron Valley, and as the king also himself crossed over. All the people traveled on the road toward the wilderness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Numbers 4:15; 2 Samuel 8:17; 2 Samuel 20:25"> Even Zadok with all the Levites, carrying the ark of the covenant of God, were present. They set the ark of God down, and then Abiathar joined them. They waited until all the people had passed by out of the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Jeremiah 25:30; Psalms 26:8; Psalms 43:3–4"> The king said to Zadok, “Carry the ark of God back into the city. If I find favor in the eyes of Yahweh, he will bring me back here and show me again the ark and the place where he lives.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="1 Samuel 3:18; 2 Samuel 22:20; 1 Kings 10:9"> But if he says, 'I am not pleased with you,' look, here am I, let him do to me whatever seems good to him.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="1 Samuel 9:9; 2 Samuel 17:17; 2 Samuel 24:11"> The king also said to Zadok the priest, “Are you not a seer? Return into the city in peace, and your two sons with you, Ahimaaz your son, and Jonathan son of Abiathar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 Samuel 17:16; 2 Samuel 15:23; 2 Samuel 17:1"> See, I will wait at the fords of the Arabah until word comes from you to inform me.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29"> So Zadok and Abiathar carried the ark of God back into Jerusalem, and they stayed there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Esther 6:12; 2 Samuel 19:4; Luke 19:41"> But David ascended barefoot and weeping up the Mount of Olives, and he had his head covered. Every man of the people who were with him covered his head, and they went up weeping as they walked.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 Samuel 17:14; 2 Samuel 17:23; James 3:15"> Someone told David saying, “Ahithophel is among the conspirators with Absalom.” So David prayed, “O Yahweh, please turn Ahithophel's advice into foolishness.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Joshua 16:2; 2 Samuel 1:2; Psalms 3:3–5"> It came about that when David arrived at the top of the road, where God used to be worshiped, Hushai the Arkite came to meet him with his coat torn and earth on his head.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="2 Samuel 19:35"> David said to him, “If you travel with me, then you will be a burden to me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Joshua 8:2; 2 Samuel 16:16–19; Matthew 10:16"> But if you return to the city and say to Absalom, 'I will be your servant, king, as I have been your father's servant in time past, so will I now be your servant,' then you will confuse Ahithophel's advice for me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="2 Samuel 17:15–16"> Will you not have the priests Zadok and Abiathar with you? So whatever you hear in the king's palace, you must tell it to Zadok and Abiathar the priests.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="2 Samuel 17:17; 2 Samuel 15:27; 2 Samuel 18:19–33"> See that they have there with them their two sons, Ahimaaz, Zadok's son, and Jonathan, Abiathar's son. You must send to me by their hand everything that you hear.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="2 Samuel 16:15–16; 1 Chronicles 27:33"> So Hushai, David's friend, came into the city as Absalom arrived and entered into Jerusalem.</span>
