<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Genesis 35:22; Genesis 29:32; Genesis 48:15–22"> The sons of Reuben the firstborn of Israel—now Reuben was Israel's firstborn, but his birthright was given to the sons of Joseph son of Israel because Reuben had defiled his father's couch. So he is not recorded as being the oldest son.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Matthew 2:6; Micah 5:2; Hebrews 7:14"> Judah was the strongest of his brothers, and the leader would come from him. But the birthright was Joseph's—</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Genesis 46:9; Exodus 6:14; Numbers 26:5–9"> the sons of Reuben, the firstborn of Israel were Hanok, Pallu, Hezron, and Karmi.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> The descendants of Joel were these: Joel's son was Shemaiah. Shemaiah's son was Gog. Gog's son was Shimei.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> Shimei's son was Micah. Micah's son was Reaiah. Reaiah's son was Baal.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Chronicles 5:26; 2 Kings 15:29; 2 Chronicles 28:20"> Baal's son was Beerah, whom Tiglath-Pileser king of Assyria took into exile. Beerah was a leader in the tribe of Reuben.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Chronicles 5:17"> Beerah's relatives according to their clans, listed according to their genealogical records: Jeiel the leader, Zechariah, and</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Chronicles 5:4; Numbers 32:34; Numbers 32:38"> Bela son of Azaz son of Shema son of Joel. They lived in Aroer, as far as Nebo and Baal Meon,</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Joshua 22:8–9"> and eastward to the start of the wilderness that extends to the Euphrates River. This was because they had many cattle in the land of Gilead.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Genesis 21:9; 1 Chronicles 5:18–21; Psalms 83:6"> In the days of Saul, the tribe of Reuben attacked the Hagrites and defeated them. They lived in the Hagrites' tents throughout all the land east of Gilead.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Joshua 13:11; Joshua 13:24–28; Deuteronomy 3:10–17"> The members of the tribe of Gad lived near them, in the land of Bashan as far as Salekah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> Joel was their leader; Shapham was second; and Janai and Shaphat in Bashan.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> Their relatives, by their fathers' families, were Michael, Meshullam, Sheba, Jorai, Jakan, Zia, and Eber—seven in all.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> These persons named above were the descendants of Abihail, and Abihail was the son of Huri. Huri was the son of Jaroah. Jaroah was the son of Gilead. Gilead was the son of Michael. Michael was the son of Jeshishai. Jeshishai was the son of Jahdo. Jahdo was the son of Buz.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> Ahi son of Abdiel son of Guni, was head of their fathers' family.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Chronicles 27:29; Song of Songs 2:1; Isaiah 35:2"> They lived in Gilead, in Bashan, in its towns, and in all the pasturelands of Sharon as far as its borders.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Kings 14:16; 2 Kings 14:28; 2 Kings 15:32"> All these were listed by genealogical records in the days of Jotham king of Judah and of Jeroboam king of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Numbers 1:3; Joshua 4:12–13"> The Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half tribe of Manasseh had 44,760 soldiers trained for war, who carried shield and sword, and who drew the bow.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Genesis 25:15; 1 Chronicles 1:31; 1 Chronicles 5:10"> They attacked the Hagrites, Jetur, Naphish, and Nodab.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Psalms 22:4–5; Psalms 9:10; 2 Chronicles 18:31"> They received divine help against them. In this way, the Hagrites and all who were with them were defeated. This was because the Israelites cried out to God in the battle, and he responded to them, because they put their trust in him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Revelation 18:13; Numbers 31:35"> They captured their animals, including fifty thousand camels, 250,000 sheep, two thousand donkeys, and 100,000 men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Chronicles 32:8; Romans 8:31; Joshua 23:10"> Many fell because the battle was from God. They lived in their land until the captivity.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Deuteronomy 3:8–9; Song of Songs 4:8; Psalms 133:3"> The half tribe of Manasseh lived in the land of Bashan as far as Baal Hermon and Senir (that is, Mount Hermon).</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Chronicles 4:38"> These were the heads of their fathers' houses: Epher, Ishi, Eliel, Azriel, Jeremiah, Hodaviah, and Jahdiel. They were mighty men, famous men, heads of their fathers' houses.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Revelation 17:5; Psalms 106:34–39; Exodus 34:15"> But they were unfaithful to their ancestors' God. Instead, they worshiped the gods of the peoples of the land, whom God had destroyed before them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Kings 15:19; 2 Kings 17:6; 2 Kings 15:29"> The God of Israel stirred up Pul king of Assyria (also called Tiglath-Pileser, king of Assyria). He took into exile the Reubenites, Gadites, and the half tribe of Manasseh. He brought them to Halah, Habor, Hara, and to the river of Gozan, where they remain to this day.</span>
