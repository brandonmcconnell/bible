<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Chronicles 34:29–33; 2 Chronicles 30:2; Deuteronomy 31:28"> So the king sent messengers who gathered to him all the elders of Judah and of Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Kings 22:8; Deuteronomy 31:10–13; Revelation 20:12"> Then the king went up to the house of Yahweh, and all the men of Judah and all the inhabitants of Jerusalem with him, and the priests, prophets, and all the people, from small to great. He then read in their hearing all the words of the book of the Covenant that had been found in the house of Yahweh.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Kings 11:14; 2 Kings 11:17; Deuteronomy 13:4"> The king stood by the pillar and made a covenant before Yahweh, to walk after Yahweh and to keep his commandments, his regulations, and his statutes, with all his heart and all his soul, to confirm the words of this covenant that were written in this book. So all the people agreed to stand by the covenant.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Kings 21:3; 2 Kings 21:7; 2 Chronicles 33:3"> The king commanded Hilkiah the high priest, the priests under him, and the gatekeepers to bring out of the temple of Yahweh all the vessels that were made for Baal and Asherah, and for all the stars of heaven. He burned them outside Jerusalem in the fields in the Kidron Valley and carried their ashes to Bethel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Jeremiah 44:17–19; 2 Kings 21:3–4; Hosea 10:5"> He got rid of the idolatrous priests whom the kings of Judah had chosen to burn incense at the high places in the cities of Judah and in the places around Jerusalem—those who burned incense to Baal, to the sun and the moon, to the planets, and to all the stars of heaven.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Chronicles 34:4; 2 Kings 23:15; 1 Kings 14:23"> He brought out the Asherah pole from the temple of Yahweh, outside Jerusalem to the Kidron Valley and burned it there. He beat it to dust and threw that dust onto the graves of the common people.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Kings 15:12; Ezekiel 16:16; 1 Kings 14:24"> He cleared out the rooms of the cultic prostitutes who were in the temple of Yahweh, where the women wove garments for Asherah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Kings 15:22; Joshua 21:17; 1 Kings 19:3"> Josiah brought all the priests out of the cities of Judah and defiled the high places where the priests had burned incense, from Geba to Beersheba. He destroyed the high places at the gates that were at the entrance to the gate of Joshua (the city governor), on the left side of the city gate.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Ezekiel 44:10–14; 1 Samuel 2:36; Malachi 2:8–9"> Although the priests of those high places were not allowed to serve at the altar of Yahweh in Jerusalem, they ate unleavened bread among their brothers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Isaiah 30:33; Jeremiah 19:6; Leviticus 18:21"> Josiah defiled Topheth, which is in the Valley of Ben Hinnom, so that no one might cause his son or his daughter to pass through the fire as a sacrifice to Molech.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ezekiel 8:16; Deuteronomy 4:19; 2 Chronicles 14:5"> He took away the horses that the kings of Judah had given to the sun. They had been in an area at the entrance to the temple of Yahweh, near the room of Nathan-Melek, the chamberlain. Josiah burned the chariots of the sun.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Kings 21:5; Zephaniah 1:5; Jeremiah 19:13"> Josiah the king destroyed the altars that were on the roof of the upper chamber of Ahaz, which the kings of Judah had made, and the altars that Manasseh had made in the two courts of the temple of Yahweh. Josiah smashed them into pieces and threw them into the Kidron Valley.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Kings 11:7; 1 Kings 11:5; Numbers 21:29"> The king ruined the high places east of Jerusalem, south of the mount of corruption that Solomon the king of Israel had built for Ashtoreth, the detestable idol of the Sidonians; for Chemosh, the detestable idol of Moab; and for Molech, the detestable idol of the people of Ammon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Deuteronomy 7:5; Exodus 23:24; 2 Kings 23:16"> He broke the stone pillars into pieces and cut down the Asherah poles and he filled those places with the bones of human beings.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="2 Kings 23:6; 1 Kings 14:16; 1 Kings 21:22"> Josiah also completely destroyed the altar that was at Bethel and the high place that Jeroboam son of Nebat (the one who made Israel to sin) had constructed. He also burned that altar and the high place and beat it to dust. He also burned the Asherah pole.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Kings 13:1–2; 1 Kings 13:32; John 10:35"> As Josiah looked over the area, he noticed the graves that were on the hillside. He sent men to take the bones from the graves; then he burned them on the altar, which defiled it. This was according to the word of Yahweh which the man of God had spoken, the man who spoke of these things beforehand.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Kings 13:1; 1 Kings 13:30–31"> Then he said, “What monument is that I see?” The men of the city told him, “That is the grave of the man of God who came from Judah and spoke about these things that you have just done against the altar of Bethel.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Kings 13:31; 1 Kings 13:1–22"> So Josiah said, “Let it alone. No one should move his bones.” So they let his bones alone, along with the bones of the prophet who had come from Samaria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Chronicles 34:6–7; 2 Kings 17:9; Ezekiel 8:17–18"> Then Josiah removed all the houses on the high places that were in the cities of Samaria, which the kings of Israel had made, and that provoked Yahweh to anger. He did to them exactly what had been done at Bethel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Kings 11:18; 2 Chronicles 34:5; Exodus 22:20"> He slaughtered all the priests of the high places on the altars and he burned human bones on them. Then he returned to Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Deuteronomy 16:1–8; Numbers 9:2–5; 2 Chronicles 35:1–19"> Then the king commanded all the people, saying, “Keep the Passover to Yahweh your God, as it is written in this Book of the Covenant.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Chronicles 30:1–3; 2 Chronicles 30:13–20; 2 Chronicles 35:3–19"> Such a Passover celebration had never been held from the days of the judges who ruled Israel, nor in all the days of the kings of Israel or Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> But in the eighteenth year of King Josiah this Passover of Yahweh was celebrated in Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Leviticus 19:31; 2 Kings 21:6; Genesis 31:19"> Josiah also banished those who talked with the dead or with spirits. He also banished the fetishes, the idols, and all the disgusting things that were seen in the land of Judah and in Jerusalem, so as to confirm the words of the law which were written in the book that Hilkiah the priest had found in the house of Yahweh.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Kings 18:5; Jeremiah 29:13; Deuteronomy 6:5"> Before Josiah, there had been no king like him, who turned to Yahweh with all his heart, all his soul, and all his might, who followed all the law of Moses. Nor did any king like Josiah arise after him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Kings 21:11–13; 2 Kings 24:4; Jeremiah 3:7–10"> Nevertheless, Yahweh did not turn away from the burning of his fierce anger, which burned against Judah because of all that Manasseh had done to provoke him to anger.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 Kings 18:11; 2 Kings 21:13; 2 Kings 21:4"> So Yahweh said, “I will also remove Judah out of my sight, as I have removed Israel, and I will throw away this city that I have chosen, Jerusalem, and the house of which I said, 'My name will be there.'”</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 Kings 20:20"> As for the other matters concerning Josiah, everything that he did, are they not written in the book of the events of the kings of Judah?</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Jeremiah 46:2; 2 Chronicles 35:20–24; Judges 5:19"> In his days, Pharaoh Necho, king of Egypt, went to fight against the king of Assyria at the Euphrates River. King Josiah went to meet Necho in battle, and Necho killed him at Megiddo.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="2 Kings 9:28; 2 Chronicles 36:1–4; 2 Kings 21:24"> Josiah's servants carried him dead in a chariot from Megiddo, brought him to Jerusalem, and buried him in his own grave. Then the people of the land took Jehoahaz son of Josiah, anointed him, and made him king in his father's place.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="1 Chronicles 3:15; 2 Kings 24:18; Jeremiah 22:11"> Jehoahaz was twenty-three years old when he began to reign, and he reigned three months in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Hamutal; she was the daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="2 Kings 21:2–7; 2 Kings 21:21–22"> Jehoahaz did what was evil in the sight of Yahweh, like everything that his ancestors had done.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="1 Kings 8:65; 2 Kings 25:6; Jeremiah 39:5–6"> Pharaoh Necho put him in chains at Riblah in the land of Hamath, so that he might not reign in Jerusalem. Then Necho fined Judah one hundred talents of silver and one talent of gold.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Ezekiel 19:3–4; 2 Kings 24:17; 1 Chronicles 3:15"> Pharaoh Necho made Eliakim son of Josiah king in the place of Josiah his father, and changed his name to Jehoiakim. But he took Jehoahaz away to Egypt, and Jehoahaz died there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="2 Kings 23:33; 2 Kings 15:19–20"> Jehoiakim paid the silver and gold to Pharaoh. In order to meet the demand of Pharaoh, Jehoikim taxed the land and he forced each man among the people of the land to pay him the silver and gold according to their assessments.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="2 Chronicles 36:5; Jeremiah 26:1; 1 Chronicles 3:15"> Jehoiakim was twenty-five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Zebidah; she was the daughter of Pedaiah of Rumah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="2 Chronicles 33:22–23; Ezekiel 19:5–9; Jeremiah 36:31"> Jehoiakim did what was evil in the sight of Yahweh, just as his ancestors had done.</span>
