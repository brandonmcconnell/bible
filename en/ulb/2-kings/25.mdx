<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:4–11; 1 Chronicles 6:15; Jeremiah 34:1–6"> It happened that in the ninth year of the reign of King Zedekiah, in the tenth month, and on the tenth day of the month, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came with all his army against Jerusalem. He camped opposite it, and they built a siege wall around it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2"> So the city was besieged until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah's reign.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:6; Ezekiel 5:12; Jeremiah 38:2"> On the ninth day of the fourth month of that year, the famine was so severe in the city that there was no food for the people of the land.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Ezekiel 33:21; 2 Kings 25:5; Jeremiah 39:2–7"> Then the city was broken into, and all the fighting men fled at night by the way of the gate between the two walls, by the king's garden, although the Chaldeans were all around the city. The king went in the direction of the Arabah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Isaiah 30:16; Jeremiah 39:5; Amos 2:14–16"> But the army of Chaldeans pursued King Zedekiah and overtook him in the plains of the Jordan River valley near Jericho. All his army was scattered away from him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Kings 23:33; Jeremiah 34:21–22; Jeremiah 21:7"> They captured the king and brought him up to the king of Babylon at Riblah, where they passed sentence on him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Jeremiah 32:4–5; Judges 16:21; Jeremiah 39:6–7"> As for Zedekiah's sons, they slaughtered them before his eyes. Then he put out his eyes, bound him in bronze chains, and brought him to Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:12–16; Jeremiah 40:1–4; Lamentations 4:12"> Now in the fifth month, on the seventh day of the month, which was the nineteenth year of the reign of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, Nebuzaradan, a servant of the king of Babylon and commander of his bodyguards, came to Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Amos 2:5; Psalms 74:3–7; 1 Kings 9:8"> He burned the house of Yahweh, the king's palace, and all the houses of Jerusalem; also every important building in the city he burned.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Nehemiah 1:3; Jeremiah 39:8; Jeremiah 52:14–23"> As for all the walls around Jerusalem, all the army of the Babylonians who were under the commander of the bodyguard destroyed them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ezekiel 5:2; Jeremiah 15:1–2; Jeremiah 39:9"> As for the rest of the people who were left in the city, those who had deserted to the king of Babylon, and the remainder of the population—Nebuzaradan, the commander of the bodyguard, took them away into exile.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Jeremiah 40:7; 2 Kings 24:14; Ezekiel 33:24"> But the commander of the bodyguard did leave some of the poorest of the land to work the vineyards and fields.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Chronicles 36:18; Lamentations 1:10; 2 Kings 20:17"> As for the bronze pillars that were in the house of Yahweh, and the stands and the bronze sea that were in the house of Yahweh, the Chaldeans broke them into pieces and carried the bronze back to Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Exodus 27:3; 1 Kings 7:47–50; Exodus 38:3"> The pots, shovels, lamp trimmers, spoons, and all the utensils of bronze with which the priests had served in the temple—the Chaldeans took them all away.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Daniel 5:2–3; Numbers 7:13–14; 1 Kings 7:48–51"> The pots for removing ashes and the bowls that were made of gold, and those made of silver—the captain of the king's guard took them away as well.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Kings 7:47"> The two pillars, the sea, and the stands that Solomon had made for the house of Yahweh contained more bronze than could be weighed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Kings 7:15–22; Jeremiah 52:21–23"> The height of the first pillar was eighteen cubits, and a capital of bronze was on top of it. The capital was three cubits high, with latticework and pomegranates all around on the capital, all made of bronze. The other pillar and its latticework were the same as the first.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Jeremiah 29:25; Ezra 7:1; Jeremiah 21:1"> The commander of the bodyguard took Seraiah the chief priest, together with Zephaniah, the second priest, and the three gatekeepers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Esther 1:14"> From the city he took prisoner an officer who was in charge of soldiers, and five men of those who advised the king, who were still in the city. He also took prisoner the king's army officer responsible for drafting men into the army, along with sixty important men from the land who were in the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Lamentations 4:16; Jeremiah 52:26–27; 2 Kings 23:33"> Then Nebuzaradan, the commander of the bodyguard, took them and brought them to the king of Babylon at Riblah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Deuteronomy 28:64; 2 Kings 23:27; Deuteronomy 4:26"> The king of Babylon put them to death at Riblah in the land of Hamath. In this way, Judah went out of its land into exile.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Jeremiah 39:14; 2 Kings 22:12; Jeremiah 41:2"> As for the people who remained in the land of Judah, those whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had left, he put Gedaliah son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan, in charge of them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Jeremiah 40:7–9; Jeremiah 40:11–12; Joshua 18:26"> Now when all the commanders of the soldiers, they and their men, heard that the king of Babylon had made Gedaliah governor, they went to Gedaliah at Mizpah. These men were Ishmael son of Nethaniah, Johanan son of Kareah, Seraiah son of Tanhumeth the Netophathite, and Jaazaniah son of the Maakathite—they and their men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Jeremiah 40:9–10; Jeremiah 43:6; 2 Samuel 19:23"> Gedaliah made an oath to them and to their men, and said to them, “Do not be afraid of the Chaldean officials. Live in the land and serve the king of Babylon, and it will go well with you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Zechariah 7:5; Zechariah 8:19; Jeremiah 40:15"> But it happened that in the seventh month Ishmael son of Nethaniah son of Elishama, from the royal family, came with ten men and attacked Gedaliah. Gedaliah died, along with the men of Judah and the Babylonians who were with him at Mizpah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Jeremiah 43:4–7; Jeremiah 41:16–18; Jeremiah 42:14–22"> Then all the people, from the least to the greatest, and the commanders of the soldiers, arose and went to Egypt, because they were afraid of the Babylonians.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:31–34; Genesis 40:20; Genesis 40:13"> It happened later in the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin king of Judah, in the twelfth month, on the twenty-seventh day of the month, that Awel-Marduk king of Babylon released Jehoiachin king of Judah from prison. This happened in the year that Awel-Marduk began to reign.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Daniel 2:37; Jeremiah 27:6–11; Daniel 5:18–19"> He spoke kindly to him and gave him a seat more honorable than that of the other kings who were with him in Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="2 Samuel 9:7; Isaiah 61:3; Genesis 41:42"> Awel-Marduk removed Jehoiachin's prison clothes, and Jehoiachin ate regularly at the king's table for the rest of his life.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Luke 11:3; Matthew 6:11; Nehemiah 11:23"> A regular food allowance was given to him every day for the rest of his life.</span>
