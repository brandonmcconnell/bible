<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Isaiah 1:1; Isaiah 7:1; 2 Kings 15:38"> In the seventeenth year of Pekah son of Remaliah, Ahaz son of Jotham king of Judah, began to reign.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Chronicles 28:1–4; 2 Kings 15:34; 1 Kings 11:4–8"> Ahaz was twenty years old when he began to reign, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. He did not do what was right in the eyes of Yahweh his God, as David his ancestor had done.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Deuteronomy 12:31; Leviticus 18:21; 2 Kings 21:2"> Instead, he walked in the way of the kings of Israel; indeed, he made his son pass through the fire, following the detestable practices of the nations, which Yahweh had driven out before the people of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Deuteronomy 12:2; 1 Kings 14:23; Jeremiah 17:2"> He offered sacrifices and burned incense at the high places, on the hilltops, and under every green tree.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Kings 15:37; Isaiah 9:6–7; Isaiah 8:9–10"> Then Rezin, king of Aram and Pekah son of Remaliah, king of Israel, came up to Jerusalem to attack. They besieged Ahaz, but they could not conquer him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Kings 14:22; Deuteronomy 2:8; 1 Kings 9:26"> At that time, Rezin king of Aram recovered Elath for Aram and drove the men of Judah out of Elath. Then the Arameans came to Elath where they have lived to this day.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Kings 15:29; Lamentations 4:17; Hosea 14:3"> So Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath-Pileser king of Assyria, saying, “I am your servant and your son. Come up and save me from the hand of the king of Aram and from the hand of the king of Israel, who have attacked me.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Kings 12:17–18; 2 Chronicles 28:20–21; 2 Chronicles 16:2"> So Ahaz took the silver and gold that was found in the house of Yahweh and among the treasures of the king's palace and he sent it as a gift to the king of Assyria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Amos 9:7; Isaiah 22:6; Amos 1:3–5"> Then the king of Assyria listened to him, and the king of Assyria went up against Damascus, conquered it and carried off its people as prisoners to Kir. He also killed Rezin the king of Aram.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Romans 12:2; Ezekiel 23:16–17; Isaiah 8:2"> King Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Tiglath-Pileser king of Assyria. At Damascus he saw an altar. He sent to Uriah the priest a model of the altar and its pattern and the design for all the workmanship needed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Hosea 4:6; 2 Chronicles 26:17–18; Hosea 5:11"> So Uriah the priest built an altar to be just like the plans that King Ahaz had sent from Damascus. He finished it before King Ahaz arrived back from Damascus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Chronicles 26:16–19; 2 Chronicles 28:25; 1 Kings 13:1"> When the king came from Damascus he saw the altar; the king approached the altar and made offerings on it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Leviticus 1:1–3"> He made his burnt offering and his grain offering, poured out his drink offering, and sprinkled the blood of his fellowship offerings on the altar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:1; Exodus 40:6; Exodus 40:29"> The bronze altar that was before Yahweh—he brought it from the front of the temple, from between his altar and the temple of Yahweh and put it on the north side of his altar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Exodus 29:39–41; 1 Kings 8:64; 2 Chronicles 29:21–24"> Then King Ahaz commanded Uriah the priest, saying, “On the large altar burn the morning burnt offering and the evening grain offering, and the king's burnt offering and his grain offering, with the burnt offering of all the people of the land, and their grain offering and their drink offerings. Sprinkle on it all the blood of the burnt offering, and all the blood of the sacrifice. But the bronze altar will be for me to consult for guidance.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Acts 4:19; Acts 5:29; Jude 1:11"> Uriah the priest did just what King Ahaz commanded.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:14–15; Jeremiah 52:20; 2 Chronicles 28:24"> Then King Ahaz removed the panels and the basins from the portable stands; he also took down the sea from off the bronze oxen that were under it and put it on a stone pavement.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="2 Kings 11:5; 1 Kings 10:5; Ezekiel 46:2"> He removed the covered walkway for the Sabbath that they had built at the temple, along with the king's entry outside the temple of Yahweh, because of the king of Assyria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Kings 15:36; 2 Chronicles 28:26; 2 Kings 20:20–21"> As for the other matters concerning Ahaz and what he did, are they not written in the book of the events of the kings of Judah?</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Matthew 1:9; Isaiah 1:1; 1 Chronicles 3:13"> Ahaz slept with his ancestors and was buried with his ancestors in the city of David. Hezekiah his son became king in his place.</span>
