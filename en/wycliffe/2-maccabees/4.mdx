<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> Simon forsooth before-said, accuser of riches &#91;or of monies&#93;, and of the country, spake evil of Onias, as if he had stirred Heliodorus to these things, and he had been stirrer of evils;</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2"> and he durst say the purveyor of the city, and defender of his folk, and lover of the law of God, traitor &#91;or enemy&#93; of the realm.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3"> But when enmities came forth in so much, that also by some familiar, &#91;or nigh&#93;, friends of Simon, man-slayings were done,</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> Onias beheld the peril of strife, and that Apollonius was mad, as duke of Coelesyria and Phoenicia, for to increase the malice of Simon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> And Onias gave himself to the king; not as accuser of citizens, but beholding with himself the common profit of all the multitude.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> For he saw, that it was impossible that peace were given to things with-out the king’s purveyance, and that Simon might not cease of his folly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7"> But after the passing out of Seleucus’ life, when Antiochus, that was called noble or Epiphanes, had taken realm, Jason, the brother of Onias, coveted the highest or the high priesthood;</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> and Jason went to the king, and promised to him three hundred talents and sixty of silver, and of other rents fourscore talents;</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> over these things he promised also other talents an hundred and fifty, if it were granted to his power, for to ordain a school, and gathering of young men to him; and for to write them that were in Jerusalem Antioch-enes, or men of Antioch.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> And when the king had granted this, and he wielded the princehood, anon he began to translate to heathen customs, men of his lineage.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> And when these things were done away, which by cause of humanity, either courtesy, were ordained of kings to Jews by John, the father of Eupolemus, which was ordained in lawful message of friendship and fellowship with Romans, he destroyed rights, or laws, of citizens, and made shrewd ordinances;</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> for he was hardy for to ordain a school of heathenness under that high tower, and for to put all the best of fair young men in bordel houses.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> Forsooth this was not beginning, but some increasing and profit of heathen and alien life, for the unleaveful and unheard great trespass of unpiousness, and not priest Jason;</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> so that priests not now were given about offices of the altar, but they despised the temple, and left sacrifices, and they hasted for to be made fellows of wrestling or rassling, and of unjust giving of him, that is, of his giving of worst teaching, and in occupations of a dish, either playing with a leaden discus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> And soothly they had honours of fathers at nought, and deemed Greek glories best.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> For cause of which perilous contention had them, and they followed their ordinances; and by all things they coveted them for to be like them, which &#91;or whom&#93; they had enemies and destroyers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> Forsooth for to do unfaithfully &#91;or unpiously&#93; against God’s laws it befall-eth not without pain, but the time pursuing shall declare these things.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> Soothly when justice, done once in five years, was made solemnly in Tyre, and the king was present,</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> Jason, full of great trespasses, sent from Jerusalem men sinners, bearing three hundred double drachmas of silver into sacrifice of Hercules; which these men that bare out asked, that those &#91;or they&#93; were not given in sacrifices, for it needed not, but that those shall be ordained into other expenses.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> But soothly these were offered of him that sent into the sacrifice of Hercules; soothly for men present, those &#91;or they&#93; were given into making of great ships.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> Forsooth Apollonius, son of Menestheus, was sent into Egypt for primates, or princes, of Ptolemy Philometor, the king; when Antiochus knew him made alien from needs of the realm, he counselled for &#91;or to&#93; his own profits, and went from thence, and came to Joppa, and from thence to Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> And he was received of Jason and the city worshipfully, with lights of brands, and praisings, and went in, and from thence he turned the host into Phoenicia.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> And after the time of three years, Jason sent Menelaus, the brother of Simon above-said, bearing riches &#91;or monies&#93; to the king, and of necessary causes to bearing answers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24"> And he was commended to the king, and, when he had magnified the face of his power, he turned in to himself the highest priesthood, and setted above Jason &#91;or above putting to Jason&#93; three hundred talents of silver.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> And by commandments taken of the king, he came, soothly having nothing worthy to priesthood; but he bare the soul of a cruel tyrant, and wrath of &#91;a&#93; wild beast.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> And soothly that Jason, that took his own brother captive, was deceived, and was outlawed, and put out into the country of Ammonites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27"> But Menelaus forsooth wielded the princehood, but of riches promised to the king, he did nothing, when Sostratus, that was sovereign of the high tower, made exaction, or master-ful asking, &#91;or that was provost of the high rock, made exaction, or unjust asking&#93;,</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> for why raising &#91;or extraction&#93; of tributes pertained to him; for which cause both were called to the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29"> And Menelaus was removed from priesthood, and Lysimachus, his brother, was successor; soothly Sostratus was made sovereign of men of Cyprus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30"> And when these things were done, it befell Tarsus and Mallus for to move debate, for that they were given in gift to the concubine of Antiochus, the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31"> Therefore the king hastily came, for to assuage them, and left one of his earls suffectus or suffect consuls, Andronicus, in dignity, or lieutenant.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32"> Forsooth Menelaus deemed that he had taken covenable time, and stole some golden vessels of the temple, and gave to Andronicus, and he sold to Tyre others, and by nigh cities.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33"> And when Onias had known this thing most certainly, he reproved him, and held himself in a secure place at Antiochia, beside Daphne.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34"> Wherefore Menelaus went to Andronicus, and prayed that he would slay Onias. And when he came to Onias, and had given right hands with an oath, though he was suspect to him, he counselled him for to go forth of asylum, and anon he slew him, and dreaded not rightwiseness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35"> For which cause not only Jews, but and other nations, were wroth, and bare heavily of the unjust death of so great a man.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36"> But Jews at Antioch, and Greeks, together complained of the unjust death of Onias, and went to the king, that turned again from places of Cilicia.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37"> Therefore the king Antiochus was sorry in soul for Onias, and was bowed to mercy, and shedded tears, and bethought on the soberness and mildness of the dead man.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38"> And his heart was kindled, and he commanded that Andronicus, unclothed of purple, be led about by all the city, and that in that place in which he had done unpiously against Onias, the cursed man be deprived of life; for the Lord gave to him even-worthy pain.</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39"> Forsooth when many sacrileges were done of Lysimachus, by counsel of Menelaus, in the temple, and the fame was published, &#91;a&#93; multitude was gathered against Lysimachus; for much gold was then borne out.</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40"> Forsooth when the companies rose, and souls were filled with wrath, Lysimachus began for to use almost three thousand armed wicked hands, by some tyrant leader, eld &#91;or old&#93; in age, and also in madness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41"> But as they understood the endeavouring of Lysimachus, others took stones, others strong staffs, some soothly casted ashes into Lysimachus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42"> And many soothly were wounded, some forsooth were cast down, all forsooth were altogether turned into flight; also they slew him sacrileger, either thief of holy things, beside the treasury.</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43"> Therefore of these things doom began for to be moved against Menelaus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44"> And when the king came to Tyre, three men were sent of the elder men, and brought the cause to him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v45" data-v="45"> And when Menelaus was over-come, he promised for to give many riches &#91;or many monies&#93; to Ptolemy, for to counsel the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v46" data-v="46"> Therefore Ptolemy went to the king, set in some porch, as for cause of refreshing, either colding, &#91;or cooling&#93;, and led away from sentence;</span>
<span className="v" id="v47" data-v="47"> and assoiled or absolved from crimes Menelaus, guilty truly of all the malice. Forsooth he damned or condemned by death these wretches, which should be deemed innocents, yea, if they had led cause with Scythians.</span>
<span className="v" id="v48" data-v="48"> Therefore soon they gave unjust pain to them, that pursued cause for the city, and &#91;for the&#93; people, and holy vessels.</span>
<span className="v" id="v49" data-v="49"> Wherefore and men of Tyre were wroth, and were most liberal with the burying of them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v50" data-v="50"> Forsooth for covetousness of them that were in power, Menelaus dwelt in power, waxing in malice, and to deceits of citizens.</span>
