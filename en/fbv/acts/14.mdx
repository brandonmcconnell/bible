<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 18:4; Acts 13:51; Acts 13:46"> In Iconium the same thing happened. Paul and Barnabas went to the Jewish synagogue and spoke so convincingly that many of both the Jewish and Greek-speaking worshipers trusted in Jesus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Acts 13:50; Acts 21:27–30; Acts 14:19"> But the Jews that refused to believe in Jesus stirred up the feelings of the foreigners,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="In other words, the non-Jewish population.">a</a></sup>and poisoned them against the believers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Hebrews 2:4; Ephesians 6:18–20; Acts 20:32"> Paul and Barnabas stayed there a long time, speaking to them boldly in the Lord, who confirmed their message of grace through the miraculous signs that they were enabled to perform.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Acts 28:24; Acts 14:14; Luke 11:21–23"> The inhabitants of the town were divided, with some supporting the Jews and some the apostles.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Acts 14:19; 2 Timothy 3:11; Acts 17:5"> But then the foreigners and the Jews, together with their leaders, decided to attack and stone Paul and Barnabas.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 14:20–21; Matthew 10:23; 2 Timothy 3:11"> However, they found out about it and fled to the region of Lycaonia, to the towns of Lystra and Derbe,</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Acts 14:21; Acts 16:10; 1 Thessalonians 2:2"> where they continued to share the good news.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Acts 3:2; John 9:1–2; John 5:3–5"> In the town of Lystra there was a disabled man who was lame in both feet. He had been crippled from birth and had never been able to walk.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 3:4; Matthew 15:28; Matthew 9:28–29"> He sat there listening to Paul speaking. When Paul looked directly at him, and realized that the man was trusting in God to heal him,</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Isaiah 35:6; John 5:8–9; John 14:12"> Paul said in a loud voice, “Stand up on your feet!” The man jumped to his feet and started walking.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 28:6; Acts 8:10; Acts 12:22"> When the crowds saw what Paul had done, they shouted out in the language of Lycaonia, “The gods have come down to us looking like men!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 19:35"> They identified Barnabas as the Greek god Zeus, and Paul as the god Hermes because he was one who did most of the talking.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Daniel 2:46; Acts 10:25"> The priest of the temple of Zeus that lay just outside the town, brought oxen and wreaths<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Wreaths—these were put on animals just before they were sacrificed.">b</a></sup>to the town gates. He planned to carry out a sacrifice in front of the crowds.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Matthew 26:65; 1 Corinthians 9:5–6; 2 Kings 5:7"> But when the apostles Barnabas and Paul learned what was happening, they tore their clothes,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="In ancient cultures a sign of great distress.">c</a></sup>and rushed into the crowds, shouting out,</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Acts 10:26; 1 Corinthians 8:4; James 5:17"> “People, what are you doing? We are human beings with the same kind of nature as you. We came to bring you good news, so you could turn from these pointless things to a God who is truly alive. He is the one who made heaven, earth, and sea, and everything in them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Acts 17:30; PSALMS 81:12; Romans 1:28"> In past times he allowed all the nations to follow their own ways.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Romans 1:19–20; Joel 2:23; Job 5:10"> Even so he still provided evidence of himself by doing good, sending you rain from heaven and crops in their seasons, providing all the food you need, and filling you with happiness.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Jeremiah 44:16–17; Genesis 19:9; Genesis 11:6"> With these words they barely managed to stop the crowds from offering sacrifices to them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Corinthians 11:25; 2 Timothy 3:11; Acts 13:50–51"> But then some Jews from Antioch and Iconium arrived and won over the crowds. They stoned Paul, and dragged him outside the town, thinking he was dead.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Corinthians 1:9–10; 2 Corinthians 6:9; Acts 14:6"> But when the believers gathered around him, he got up, and went back into the city. The next day he and Barnabas left for Derbe.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Acts 14:19; Acts 14:1; Acts 13:51"> After sharing the good news with the people in that town, and after many had become believers, they went back to Lystra, Iconium, and Antioch.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="1 Peter 5:10; 2 Timothy 3:12; John 16:33"> They encouraged the believers to remain firm and to continue to trust in Jesus. “We have to go through many trials to enter God's kingdom,” they said.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Titus 1:5; Acts 11:30; James 5:14"> After they had appointed elders for every church, and had prayed and fasted with them, Paul and Barnabas left them in the Lord's care, the one that they trusted in.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 13:13–14; Acts 15:38"> They passed through Pisidia, and arrived in Pamphylia.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> They spoke God's word in Perga, and then went on to Attalia.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 15:40; Acts 11:19; 2 Corinthians 1:12"> From there they sailed back to Antioch<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Antioch in Syria, where they had begun their journey (see 13:1).">d</a></sup>where they had started out, having been dedicated there in God's grace to the work they had now accomplished.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="1 Corinthians 16:9; Acts 15:12; Colossians 4:3"> When they arrived, they called the church together. They reported everything God had done through them, and how he had opened a door for the foreigners to trust in him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Acts 11:26; Acts 15:35"> They stayed there with the believers for a long time.</span>
</div>
