<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Matthew 24:9; John 15:20; Matthew 10:17–18"> Around this time King Herod began to persecute some members of the church.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Hebrews 11:37; Matthew 20:23; Matthew 4:21–22"> He had James, John's brother, executed by the sword.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Exodus 23:15; John 12:43; Acts 24:27"> When he saw that the Jews were pleased by this, he had Peter arrested too. (This was during the Feast of Unleavened Bread.)</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Proverbs 19:21; Luke 22:33; Luke 21:12"> After having Peter arrested, he threw him in prison, with four squads of four soldiers each to guard him. He planned to have Peter brought out for a public trial after the Passover.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Corinthians 12:26; 1 Thessalonians 5:17; James 5:16"> While Peter was kept in prison the church prayed earnestly to God for him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 21:33; Hebrews 13:6; Philippians 4:6–7"> The night before Herod was to have him put on trial, Peter was sleeping between two soldiers, chained to each of them, and with guards at the door keeping watch.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Acts 5:19; Acts 16:26; PSALMS 146:7"> Suddenly an angel of the Lord appeared, and a light shone in the cell. The angel shook Peter awake, saying “Quick! Get up!” The chains fell from his wrists,</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> and the angel told him, “Get dressed, and put on your sandals.” So he did. Then the angel told him, “Put on your coat and follow me.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="PSALMS 126:1; Hebrews 11:8; Genesis 45:26"> So Peter followed the angel out. He didn't realize that what the angel was doing was actually happening—he thought he was seeing a vision.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Acts 5:19; Acts 16:26; Isaiah 45:1–2"> They passed the first and second sets of guards, and came to the iron gate that led into the city. This opened for them by itself. They went out and down the street, when suddenly the angel left him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Daniel 6:22; Daniel 3:28; 2 Peter 2:9"> When Peter came to his senses, he said, “Now I realize this really happened! The Lord sent an angel to rescue me from Herod's power, and from everything that the Jewish people had planned.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 John 5:14–15; Acts 12:5; Matthew 18:19–20"> Now that he was conscious of what had happened, Peter went to the house of Mary, the mother of John Mark. Many believers had gathered there, and were praying.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Acts 12:16; Luke 13:25; John 18:16–17"> When he knocked on the gateway door, a servant girl called Rhoda came to open up.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Luke 24:41; Matthew 28:8"> But recognizing Peter's voice, in her excitement she didn't open the door. Instead she ran back inside shouting, “Peter's at the door!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Matthew 18:10; Luke 24:11; Mark 16:14"> “You're mad!” they told her. But she kept on insisting it was true. So they said, “It must be his angel.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“His angel.” Some at the time believed people had a spiritual equivalent that existed whether the individual was alive or dead. Perhaps today the expression would be, “It's his ghost!”">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> Peter continued knocking. When they did eventually open the door, they saw it was him, and were totally shocked.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Acts 21:40; Acts 13:16; Acts 19:33"> Peter held up his hand for them to be quiet, and then explained to them how the Lord had led him out of the prison. “Let James and the brothers know about this,” he told them, and then left to go somewhere else.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Acts 5:22–25; Acts 16:27; Acts 19:23"> When daylight came the soldiers were totally confused as to what had happened to Peter.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Acts 16:27; PSALMS 37:32–33; John 12:10–11"> Herod had a thorough search made for him, but he couldn't be found. After interrogating the guards, Herod ordered that they should be executed.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="The Greek actually says, “that they be led away.” However, most commentators understand this to mean “led away to their deaths,” since the punishment for allowing prisoners to escape was execution.">b</a></sup>Then Herod left Judea and went to stay in Caesarea.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Ezra 3:7; Matthew 11:21–22; Ezekiel 27:17"> Now Herod had become furious with the people of Tyre and Sidon. They sent a joint delegation to see him and managed to win Blastus, the king's personal assistant, over to their side. They pleaded for peace with Herod because they were dependent on the king's territory for food.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> When the day came for their appointment with the king, Herod put on his royal robes, sat on his throne, and gave a speech to them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 14:10–13; Revelation 13:4; Jude 1:16"> The audience shouted in response, “This is the voice of a god, not that of a man!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="PSALMS 115:1; Acts 10:25–26; 2 Thessalonians 2:4"> Immediately the angel of the Lord struck him down, because he did not give God the glory. He was consumed by worms and died.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 6:7; Acts 19:20; 2 Thessalonians 3:1"> But the word of God spread, and more and more people believed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Acts 12:12; 1 Peter 5:13; Acts 15:37"> Barnabas and Saul returned from Jerusalem once they had finished their mission, taking John Mark with them.</span>
