<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 1:1; Philippians 4:16; Acts 20:4"> After Paul and Silas had passed through Amphipolis and Apollonia they arrived at Thessalonica, where there was a Jewish synagogue.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Acts 9:20; Acts 17:17; Acts 18:4"> As usual, Paul went into the synagogue and over the course of three Sabbaths he debated with them using the Scriptures.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Acts 18:28; Acts 9:22; Luke 24:32"> He explained what the Scriptures meant, proving that the Messiah had to die and rise from the dead. “This Jesus I'm telling you about—he is the Messiah,” he told them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Acts 17:12; Acts 13:43; Acts 14:4"> Some of them were convinced and joined Paul and Silas, along with many Greek-speaking worshipers<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Greek-speaking worshipers: the term is usually applied to “heathen” who had come to accept the belief in the one God of Judaism but had not become Jews by circumcision.">a</a></sup>and some leading women of the town.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Galatians 5:26; Acts 17:13; Romans 16:21"> But the Jews became jealous and with some rabble-rousers they gathered from the marketplace<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Literally, “evil men from the market.”">b</a></sup>they formed a mob. They rioted in the town, and attacked Jason's house. They tried to find Paul and Silas so they could bring them before the people.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Acts 24:5; Acts 16:19–21; Acts 28:22"> When they couldn't find them they dragged Jason and some of the other believers before the town leaders, shouting, “These people are famous for causing trouble, turning the world upside down. Now they've come here,</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Luke 23:2; Acts 16:21; John 19:12"> and Jason has made them welcome in his house. They all defy Caesar's decrees, committing treason by saying there is another king called Jesus.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Matthew 2:3; John 11:48"> The people and the leaders of the town were very disturbed when they heard this.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 17:5"> So they made Jason and the others post bail before they let them go.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Acts 17:2; 1 Thessalonians 2:2; Acts 17:13–14"> The believers had Paul and Silas leave for Berea that very night. When they arrived in Berea they went to the Jewish synagogue.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Isaiah 34:16; Proverbs 2:1–5; John 5:39"> The people there had a better attitude than those in Thessalonica in that they were very quick to accept the word, and every day they examined the Scriptures to make sure what they were told was right.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Ephesians 5:14; Acts 14:1; Acts 13:50"> As a result many of them became believers, along with some highly-placed Greek women and men.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 2:14–16; Proverbs 28:25; Acts 6:12"> But when the Jews in Thessalonica heard that Paul was also spreading the word of God in Berea, they went there and caused the same kind of trouble, stirring up the crowds.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Acts 17:10; Matthew 10:23; Acts 9:25"> Immediately the believers sent Paul to the coast, while Silas and Timothy remained behind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Acts 18:5; 1 Thessalonians 3:1; Acts 18:1"> Those escorting Paul took him as far as Athens, and then returned with instructions from Paul to Silas and Timothy that they should join him there as soon as possible.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="PSALMS 119:158; 2 Peter 2:7; PSALMS 119:136"> While Paul was waiting for them in Athens he was very troubled to see all the idolatry in the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Acts 13:16; Acts 14:1–4; Acts 17:2–4"> He debated in the synagogue with the Jews and those who worshiped God,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Presumably the same “class” of believers mentioned in 17:4: foreigners who had accepted the God of Israel but had not become Jews.">c</a></sup>as well as in the marketplace with those he happened to meet from day to day.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:20–21; Colossians 2:8; Luke 11:53"> Some Epicurean and Stoic philosophers also argued with him. “What is he going on about?”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Literally, “What is this seed-collector trying to say?” “Seed-collector” referred to chattering birds picking up seeds in the marketplace; otherwise translated “babbler.”">d</a></sup>they wondered. Others concluded, “He seems to be teaching about some foreign gods,” because he was speaking about Jesus and the resurrection.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Mark 1:27; Acts 17:22; Acts 24:24"> So they took him to the Areopagus<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="A kind of discussion forum of philosophers.">e</a></sup>, and asked him, “Please tell us about this new teaching that you're promoting.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:23; Hosea 8:12; 1 Corinthians 2:14"> We're hearing from you things that sound odd to us, so we'd like to know what they mean.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Timothy 2:16–17; Colossians 4:5; Ephesians 5:16"> (All the Athenians, including foreigners who lived there, spent their whole time doing nothing except explaining or listening to something new.)</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 25:19; Acts 19:35; Acts 17:16"> Paul stood up right in the middle of the Areopagus and said, “People of Athens, I notice you are very religious about everything.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="John 4:22; Galatians 4:8–9; John 17:3"> As I was walking along, looking at your shrines, I found an altar that had the inscription, ‘To an Unknown God.’ This unknown God whom you worship is the one I'm describing to you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Kings 8:27; Deuteronomy 10:14; Acts 7:48"> The God who created the world and everything in it, the Lord of heaven and earth, doesn't live in temples we make.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="PSALMS 50:8–13; Genesis 2:7; Isaiah 42:5"> He doesn't need to be served by us as if he needed anything, since he is the source of all life for every living being.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Deuteronomy 32:7–8; Genesis 3:20; Romans 5:12–19"> From one man he made all the peoples who live on the earth, and decided beforehand when and where they should live.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Jeremiah 23:23–24; Deuteronomy 4:7; Acts 14:17"> God's purpose was that they should seek him, hoping they would reach out for him and find him—though he isn't far from any one of us.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Job 12:10; Colossians 1:17; Hebrews 1:3"> In him we live, move, and exist. Just as one of your own poets wrote, ‘We are his family.’”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Isaiah 40:25; Exodus 20:4; PSALMS 115:4–8"> “Since we are his family we shouldn't think that God is like gold, or silver, or stone, shaped by human artistry and thinking.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Acts 14:16; Titus 2:11–12; Luke 24:47"> God disregarded people's ignorance in the past, but now he commands everyone everywhere to repent.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Matthew 25:31–46; 2 Peter 3:7; Romans 2:16"> For he has set a time when he will rightly judge the world by the man he has appointed, and he proved to everyone that he is the one by raising him from the dead.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Acts 17:18; Acts 26:8; Acts 25:19"> Some of them laughed when they heard about the resurrection of the dead, while others said, “Please come back so we can hear more about this later.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33"> So Paul left them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Acts 17:19; Romans 11:5–6; Acts 13:48"> A few men joined him and trusted in God, including Dionysius, a member of the Areopagus, as well as a woman called Damaris, and some others.</span>
</div>
</div>
