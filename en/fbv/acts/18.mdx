<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:2; 2 Timothy 4:20; 2 Corinthians 1:1"> Paul then left Athens and went to Corinth</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Romans 16:3–4; 1 Corinthians 16:19; 2 Timothy 4:19"> where he met a Jew named Aquila. Aquila was originally from Pontus, and had just arrived from Italy with his wife Priscilla because Claudius<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="The Roman Emperor.">a</a></sup>had ordered all Jews expelled from Rome. Paul went to see them,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Corinthians 4:12; 1 Thessalonians 2:9; 2 Corinthians 11:9"> and because they were in the same business of tent-making, he stayed with them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Corinthians 5:11; Acts 17:17; Acts 28:23"> He debated in the synagogue every Sabbath, convincing both Jews and Greeks.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Corinthians 5:14; Acts 18:28; Acts 20:21"> When Silas and Timothy arrived from Macedonia, Paul felt he had to become more direct in what he said, and told the Jews that Jesus was the Messiah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Ezekiel 33:4; 2 Samuel 1:16; Ezekiel 33:8–9"> When they opposed him and cursed him, he shook out his clothes<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="A symbolic act declaring innocence.">b</a></sup>and told them, “Your blood is on your own heads! I am innocent of any guilt, and from now on I will go to the foreigners.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Acts 16:14; Colossians 4:11; Acts 10:2"> He left and went to stay with Titius Justus, who worshiped God and whose house was next door to the synagogue.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:13–17; Romans 10:14–17; Mark 16:15–16"> Crispus, leader of the synagogue, believed in the Lord together with his whole household. Many of the people of Corinth who heard the message became believers and were baptized.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 23:11; Ephesians 6:19–20; Acts 27:23–25"> The Lord told Paul in a vision at night: “Don't be afraid. Speak up, don't keep quiet—</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Corinthians 12:9; Exodus 4:12; Isaiah 41:10"> because I am with you, and no one will attack you, for many people in this city are mine.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 14:3; Acts 19:10; Acts 20:31"> Paul stayed there for eighteen months, teaching the people the word of God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 13:50; Acts 18:27; Acts 13:7"> However, during the time when Gallio<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Gallio was the brother of Seneca, the Roman Stoic philosopher.">c</a></sup>was the governor of Achaia, the Jews united in an attack against Paul and brought him before the court.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Literally, “judgment seat,” or “judge's bench.” Also in 18:16-17.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Acts 25:8; Acts 24:5–6; Acts 6:13"> “This man is persuading people to worship God illegally,” they declared.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="1 Peter 3:14–15; Acts 25:11; Mark 9:19"> But just as Paul was about to defend himself, Gallio told the Jews, “If you Jews were bringing criminal charges or some serious legal offense, there would be a reason for me to listen to your case.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Acts 23:29; Acts 25:19; 1 Timothy 6:4"> But since you're only arguing over words and names and your own law, then you deal with it yourselves. I won't rule on such matters.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Revelation 12:16; PSALMS 76:10; Romans 13:3–4"> Then Gallio had them ejected from the court.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:1; Amos 6:6; Acts 17:32"> Then the crowd turned on Sosthenes, the leader of the synagogue, and beat him right outside the court, but Gallio wasn't concerned about this at all.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Acts 21:24; Numbers 6:18; Numbers 6:5–9"> Paul stayed on for a while. Then he left the believers and sailed for Syria, taking Priscilla and Aquila along with him. He had his head shaved while in Cenchrae, because he had taken a vow.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Vow: probably a Nazirite vow (see Numbers 6).">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Ephesians 1:1; 1 Timothy 1:3; 2 Timothy 1:18"> They arrived in Ephesus, where Paul left the others behind. He went to the synagogue to reason with the Jews.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Mark 1:37–38; 1 Corinthians 16:12; Acts 20:16"> They asked him to stay longer, but he refused.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Corinthians 4:19; James 4:15; Romans 15:32"> He said his goodbyes, and set sail from Ephesus, telling them, “I'll come back and see you if it's God's will.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 8:40; Acts 15:4; Acts 23:23"> After landing at Caesarea he went to greet the church members,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Possibly the church members in Jerusalem.">f</a></sup>and then carried on to Antioch.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Acts 14:22; Acts 16:6; Acts 15:41"> He spent some time there and then went from town to town through the region of Galatia and Phrygia, encouraging all the believers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Corinthians 4:6; Titus 3:13; 1 Corinthians 3:5–6"> In the meantime a Jew named Apollos, originally from Alexandria, arrived in Ephesus. He was a gifted speaker who knew the Scriptures well.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Acts 19:3; Romans 12:11; Hosea 14:9"> He had been taught the way of the Lord. He was spiritually passionate, and in his speaking and teaching he presented Jesus accurately, but he only knew about John's baptism.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Acts 18:25; Acts 14:3; 1 Corinthians 12:21"> He started speaking openly in the synagogue. So when Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they invited him to join them and explained the way of God to him more fully.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 Thessalonians 2:13–14; James 1:16–18; 1 Peter 1:2–3"> When he decided to go to Achaia, the brothers encouraged him, and wrote to the disciples there telling them to welcome him. When he arrived he was very helpful to those who through grace trusted God,</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Acts 18:5; 1 Corinthians 15:3–4; Acts 9:22"> because he was able to strongly refute the Jews in public debate, demonstrating from the Scriptures that Jesus was the Messiah.</span>
</div>
