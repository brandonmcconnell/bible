<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Exodus 3:18; Exodus 2:14; Jeremiah 1:6"> “But what if they don't believe me or listen to what I say?” Moses asked. “They could say, ‘The Lord didn't appear to you.’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Exodus 4:17; Exodus 4:20; Isaiah 11:4"> The Lord asked him, “What are you holding in your hand?” “A walking stick,” Moses replied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Exodus 7:10–15; Amos 5:19; Exodus 4:17"> “Throw it on the ground,” he told Moses. So Moses did. It turned into a snake and Moses ran away from it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Acts 28:3–6; Genesis 22:1–2; PSALMS 91:13"> “Reach out and grab hold of it by its tail,” the Lord told Moses. Moses did so and it turned back into a walking stick in his hand.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Exodus 19:9; Genesis 48:3; Exodus 3:15"> “You are to do this<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“You are to do this”: supplied for clarity.">a</a></sup>so that they will believe that I the Lord did appear to you, the God of their fathers, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Kings 5:27; Numbers 12:10"> Then the Lord told him, “Put your hand inside your clothes close to your chest.” So Moses did as he was told. When he took his hand out, it was white like snow with a skin disease.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Kings 5:14; Deuteronomy 32:39; Matthew 8:3"> “Now put your hand back inside your clothes,” the Lord said. Moses did so. When he took it out again, his hand had returned to normal.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Normal”: literally, “like his flesh.”">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Isaiah 28:10; Deuteronomy 32:39; Exodus 4:30–31"> “If they don't believe you and they're not convinced by the first sign, they'll believe because of the second sign,” the Lord explained.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Exodus 7:19–25; Exodus 1:22; Matthew 7:2"> “But if they still don't believe you or listen to you because of these two signs, then you are to take some water from the Nile and pour it out on the ground. The Nile water will turn into blood on the ground.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Jeremiah 1:6; Exodus 6:12; Acts 7:22"> Then Moses said to the Lord, “Please excuse me, but I'm not someone who is good with words—not in the past, and not from the time you have been speaking with me, your servant. My speech is slow and I don't say things well.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“My speech is slow and I don't say things well”: literally, “I am heavy of mouth and heavy of tongue.”">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Amos 3:6; PSALMS 146:8; Ezekiel 3:26–27"> “Who gave people mouths?” the Lord asked him. “Who makes people deaf or dumb, able to see or blind? It's I, the Lord, who does that.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Jeremiah 1:9; Luke 12:11–12; Matthew 10:19–20"> Now go, and I myself will be your mouth, and I will tell you what you are to say.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Jonah 1:3; Exodus 4:1; Matthew 13:41"> “Please, Lord, send someone else!” Moses responded.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Mark 14:13–15; Exodus 4:27; 1 Chronicles 21:7"> The Lord got angry with Moses, and told him, “There's your brother Aaron, the Levite. I know he speaks well. He's on his way to meet you, and he'll be very happy to see you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Isaiah 51:16; Deuteronomy 18:18; Luke 21:15"> Speak to him and tell him what to say. I will be your mouth and his mouth, and I will tell you what you are to do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Exodus 7:1–2; PSALMS 82:6; John 10:34–35"> Aaron will speak on your behalf to the people, as if he was your mouth, and you will be in the place of God to him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Exodus 4:2; 1 Corinthians 1:27; Exodus 7:9–20"> Make sure to take your walking stick with you so you can use it to do the signs.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Exodus 3:1; 1 Samuel 1:17; 1 Timothy 6:1"> Then Moses returned to Jethro his father-in-law and told him, “Please allow me to go back to my own people in Egypt so I can see if any of them are still alive.” “Go with my blessing,” Jethro replied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Matthew 2:20; Exodus 2:23; Exodus 2:15"> While Moses was in Midian, the Lord told him, “Go back to Egypt because all those who wanted to kill you have died.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Exodus 17:9; Numbers 20:8–9; Exodus 18:3–4"> Moses put his wife and sons on a donkey and went back to Egypt, carrying the walking stick that God had used to perform miracles.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“The walking stick that God had used to perform miracles:” literally, “the walking stick of God.” This interpretation refers back to the miracles recorded in verses 3 and 4.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Romans 9:18; Isaiah 63:17; Exodus 14:8"> The Lord told Moses, “When you get back to Egypt, make sure to go to Pharaoh and carry out the miracles I've given you to do. I will make him stubborn<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="“Stubborn”: literally, “harden his heart,” similarly translated throughout the book. The same experience is described as being God's action, Pharaoh's own action, and also in the passive with no agent identified.">e</a></sup>and he will not let the people go.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Hosea 11:1; Romans 9:4; Jeremiah 31:9"> This is what you are to tell Pharaoh, ‘This is what the Lord says. Israel is my firstborn son.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Exodus 11:5; Exodus 12:29; PSALMS 135:8"> I ordered you to let my son go so he can worship me. But you refused to release him, so I will now kill your firstborn son.’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Genesis 17:14; Numbers 22:22–23; Hosea 13:8"> But while they were on their way the Lord came to the place where they were staying, wanting to kill Moses.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Joshua 5:2–3; Genesis 17:14; 2 Samuel 16:7"> However, Zipporah used a flint knife to cut off her son's foreskin. She touched his feet with it, and said to him, “You are a blood-husband to me.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> (Calling him a blood-husband referred to circumcision.)<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="The term used here is not well understood. It may mean something like, “Through this blood I have spilled you are now related through marriage to me.” Some interpreters believe the word means “someone who is circumcised.”">f</a></sup>After this the Lord left Moses alone.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Exodus 3:1; Exodus 4:14–16; 1 Kings 19:8"> The Lord had told Aaron, “Go and meet Moses in the desert.” So Aaron went and met Moses at the mountain of God and greeted him with a kiss.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Exodus 4:8–9; Exodus 4:15–16; Matthew 21:29"> Then Moses explained to Aaron everything the Lord had sent him to say and all the miracles he had ordered him to do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Exodus 3:16; Exodus 24:11; Exodus 24:1"> Moses and Aaron traveled to Egypt. There they had all the Israelite elders gather together.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Exodus 4:16"> Aaron shared with them everything the Lord had told Moses, and Moses carried out the miracles so they could see them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Exodus 3:18; Exodus 2:25; PSALMS 106:12–13"> The Israelites were convinced. When they heard that the Lord had come to them, and that he had been touched by their suffering, they bowed their heads and worshiped.</span>
