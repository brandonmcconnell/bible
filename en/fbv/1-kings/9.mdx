<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Chronicles 7:11–6; 1 Kings 9:19; 1 Kings 7:51"> After Solomon had finished the Lord's Temple and the royal palace, having accomplished everything he'd wanted to do,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Kings 3:5; 2 Chronicles 7:12; 1 Kings 11:9"> the Lord appeared to him a second time, as he had appeared to him at Gibeon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="PSALMS 10:17; 1 John 5:14; 2 Kings 20:5"> The Lord told him, “I have heard your prayer and your request to me. I have dedicated this Temple you have built by placing my name on it forever; I will always watch over it and take care of it.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Kings 15:5; 1 Kings 3:14; 1 Kings 11:6"> As for you, if you follow my ways as your father David did, doing everything I've told you to do, and if you keep my laws and regulations,</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Kings 2:4; 1 Kings 6:12; 2 Samuel 7:16"> then I will make your throne secure forever. I made this agreement with your father David, telling him, ‘You will always have a descendant to rule over Israel.’</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Samuel 7:14–16; 1 Chronicles 28:9; 2 Chronicles 15:2"> But if you or your descendants turn away and do not keep the laws and the commandments I have given you, and if you go and serve and worship other gods,</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="PSALMS 44:14; Jeremiah 24:9; Deuteronomy 4:26"> then I will cut Israel off from the land I have given them. I will banish from my presence this Temple I have dedicated to my honor, and I will make it an object lesson of ridicule among the nations.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Deuteronomy 29:24–26; Jeremiah 22:8–9; 2 Chronicles 7:21"> This Temple will become a pile of rubble. All who pass by it will be appalled and will hiss, saying, ‘Why has the Lord acted in such a way to this land and this Temple?’</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Jeremiah 2:19; Jeremiah 5:19; Deuteronomy 29:25–28"> The answer will come, ‘Because they abandoned the Lord their God, who brought their forefathers out of Egypt, and have embraced other gods, worshiping them and serving them. That's why the Lord has brought all this trouble upon them.’”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Kings 6:37–1; 2 Chronicles 8:1–18; 1 Kings 9:1"> It took twenty years for Solomon to construct the two buildings—the Temple of the Lord and his own palace. After this,</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Chronicles 8:2; 2 Chronicles 2:16; 2 Chronicles 2:8–10"> King Solomon gave twenty towns in Galilee to Hiram king of Tyre, because Hiram had provided him with all the cedar and juniper and gold he wanted.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Numbers 22:34; Judges 14:3"> But when Hiram went from Tyre to see the cities that Solomon had given him, he was not happy with them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Joshua 19:27; Amos 1:9; 1 Kings 5:1–2"> “What are these towns you have given me, my brother?” asked Hiram. He called them the land of Cabul,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“The land of Cabul:” suggesting these towns were of no value.">a</a></sup>the name they are known by to this day.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="1 Kings 9:11; 1 Kings 10:10; 1 Kings 9:28"> Even so, Hiram sent the king 120 talents of gold in payment.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Joshua 17:11; 1 Kings 9:24; 2 Samuel 5:9"> Here is the account of the forced labor that King Solomon imposed to build the Lord's Temple, his own palace, the terraces, and the wall of Jerusalem, as well as Hazor, Megiddo, and Gezer.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Kings 3:1; 1 Kings 9:24; 1 Kings 7:8"> Pharaoh, king of Egypt, had attacked and captured Gezer. He had set it on fire, and killed the Canaanites living in the town. He had then given it as a wedding dowry to his daughter, Solomon's wife.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Joshua 16:3; Joshua 21:22; 2 Chronicles 8:4–18"> Solomon rebuilt Gezer and lower Beth-horon,</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Joshua 19:44; 2 Chronicles 8:4"> Baalath and Tamar in the wilderness, in the land of Judah,</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 Kings 9:1; Ecclesiastes 6:9; Exodus 1:11"> and all of Solomon's towns for storage, and the towns for his chariots and for his horsemen, plus whatever Solomon wanted to build in Jerusalem, in Lebanon, and throughout his entire kingdom.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Exodus 23:23; Genesis 15:19–21; Deuteronomy 7:1–3"> The descendants of the Amorites, Hittites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites (people who were not Israelites)</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Joshua 17:12; Judges 1:21; Joshua 15:63"> who remained in the land—those whom the Israelites were unable to destroy completely—were conscripted by Solomon to work as forced laborers, as they continue to do to this day.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Leviticus 25:39; 1 Kings 4:1–27; 1 Samuel 8:11–12"> But Solomon did not enslave any of the Israelites. They were his soldiers, officials, commanders, captains, chariot commanders, and horsemen.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Kings 5:16; 2 Chronicles 8:10; 2 Chronicles 2:18"> They were also the chief officers in charge of Solomon's programs: 550 in command of the people who carried out the work.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Kings 7:8; 2 Samuel 5:9; 2 Chronicles 32:5"> Once Pharaoh's daughter had moved from the City of David to the palace that Solomon had built for her, he built the city terraces.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Chronicles 8:12–13; 2 Chronicles 8:16; Exodus 23:14–17"> Three times each year Solomon sacrificed burnt offerings and friendship offerings on the altar he had built for the Lord, burning incense before the Lord with them, and so fulfilled what was required at the Temple.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“So fulfilled what was required at the Temple.” Some read this as “so he completed building the Temple,” however this has already been stated, and this verse begins by stating that Solomon sacrificed three times a year, clearly regular acts well after the completion of the Temple.">b</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="1 Kings 22:48; Numbers 33:35; Deuteronomy 2:8"> King Solomon built a fleet of ships at Ezion-geber, which is near Eloth on the shore of the Red Sea, in the land of Edom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="1 Kings 10:11; 1 Kings 5:6; 1 Kings 5:9"> Hiram sent his sailors who knew the sea to serve in the fleet with Solomon's men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 Chronicles 8:18; 1 Chronicles 29:4; 1 Kings 10:11"> They sailed to Ophir and brought back 420 talents of gold from there and delivered it to Solomon.</span>
</div>
</div>
