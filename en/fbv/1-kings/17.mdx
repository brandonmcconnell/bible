<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Luke 4:25–26; James 5:17; Luke 1:17"> Elijah the Tishbite (from Tishbe in Gilead), told Ahab, “As the Lord, the God of Israel, lives, the one I serve, in the years to come there will not be any dew or rain unless I say so!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Jeremiah 18:1; Jeremiah 7:1; Hosea 1:1–2"> Then the Lord told Elijah,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Revelation 12:6; Jeremiah 36:26; PSALMS 83:3"> “Leave here and go east. Hide in the valley of the Cherith brook where it meets the Jordan.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Kings 17:9; PSALMS 147:9; Job 38:41"> You can drink from the brook, and I have ordered ravens to bring you food you there.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Kings 19:9; Matthew 16:24; John 15:14"> So Elijah did what the Lord told him. He went to the valley of the Cherith brook, where it meets the Jordan, and stayed there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="PSALMS 78:23–24; Matthew 6:31–33; Jeremiah 37:21"> Ravens brought him bread and meat both in the morning and in the evening, and he drank from the brook.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Isaiah 54:10; Isaiah 40:30–31"> A while later the brook dried up because there had been no rain in the land.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Genesis 22:14; 1 Kings 17:2; Hebrews 13:6"> Then the Lord told Elijah,</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Luke 4:26; Obadiah 1:20; 2 Corinthians 4:7"> “Leave here and go to Zarephath near Sidon and stay there. I have given instructions to a widow there to provide you with food.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="John 4:7; Genesis 24:17; Genesis 21:15"> So he left for Zarephath. When he arrived at the entrance to the town, he saw a woman, a widow, gathering sticks. He called her over and asked her, “Could you bring me a little water in a cup so I can have a drink?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Matthew 25:35–40; 1 Kings 18:4; Hebrews 13:2"> As she was going to get it, he called after her and said, “Oh, and please bring me a piece of bread.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Matthew 15:33–34; 1 Kings 17:1; Genesis 21:16"> She replied, “As the Lord your God lives, I have no bread, only a handful of flour is left in a jar and a little bit of olive oil in a jug. Right now I am gathering a few sticks so I can go and cook what's left for myself and my son so we can eat it, and then we'll die.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Matthew 6:33; Malachi 3:10; Genesis 22:1–2"> Elijah said to her, “Don't be afraid. Go home and do what you said. But first make me a small loaf of bread from what you have and bring it to me. Then make something for yourself and your son.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Matthew 15:36–38; Matthew 14:17–20; 2 Kings 4:42–44"> For this is what the Lord, the God of Israel, says: ‘The jar of flour will not become empty and the jug of olive oil will not run out until the day the Lord sends rain to water the ground.’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Hebrews 11:17; Mark 12:43; Matthew 15:28"> She went and did as Elijah had told her, and Elijah, the widow, and her household were able to eat for many days.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Matthew 9:28–30; John 4:50–51; Luke 1:37"> The jar of flour did not become empty and the jug of olive oil did not run out, just as the Lord had said through Elijah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Peter 1:7; Daniel 5:23; John 11:14"> Later on the woman's son fell sick. (She was the one who owned the house.) He went from bad to worse, and finally he died.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Luke 5:8; John 2:4; 2 Kings 3:13"> “What are you doing to me,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="The literal phrase in the Hebrew is “What for me and for you?” It is sometimes translated “What do I have to do you with you?” but here it is clearly being used as a question regarding the death of the widow's son.">a</a></sup>man of God?” the woman asked Elijah. “Have you come to remind me about my sins and cause my son to die?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Kings 4:32; 2 Kings 4:10; Acts 9:37"> “Give me your son,” Elijah replied. He picked him up from her arms, carried him upstairs to the room where he was staying, and laid him on his bed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="James 5:15–18; Matthew 21:22; Joshua 7:8–9"> Then he cried out to the Lord, saying, “Lord my God, why have you allowed this to happen to this widow who has opened her home to me, this terrible tragedy of causing her son to die?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Acts 20:10; Acts 9:40; 2 Kings 4:33–35"> He stretched himself out on the boy three times, and cried out to the Lord, “Lord my God, please let this boy's life return to him!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Deuteronomy 32:39; Revelation 11:11; Hebrews 11:35"> The Lord responded to Elijah's cry. The boy's life returned to him, and he lived!</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 Kings 4:36–37; Acts 9:41; Luke 7:15"> Elijah took the boy and brought him down from the room into the house, and gave him to his mother. “See, your son is alive,” Elijah told her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="John 16:30; John 2:11; John 3:2"> “Now I'm convinced that you are a man of God, and that what the Lord speaks through you is the truth,” the woman replied.</span>
