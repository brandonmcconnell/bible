<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Kings 9:10; 2 Chronicles 8:1; Ecclesiastes 2:4–5"> However, it took Solomon thirteen years to finish building the whole of his palace.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Kings 10:17; 2 Chronicles 9:16; 1 Kings 10:21"> He built the House of the Forest of Lebanon— a hundred cubits long, fifty cubits wide, and thirty cubits high. There were four rows of cedar pillars that supported cedar beams.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Kings 6:5"> The cedar roof of the house was on top of the beams that rested on the pillars. There were forty-five beams, fifteen in each row.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Ezekiel 40:22; Ezekiel 40:16; 1 Kings 6:4"> The windows were placed high up, in three rows facing each other.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> All the doorways and door casings had rectangular frames, the openings facing each other in sets of three.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Kings 7:12; Ezekiel 41:25–26"> He also had the Hall of Columns made—forty cubits long and thirty cubits wide. It had a porch in front, its canopy also supported by columns.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Proverbs 20:8; PSALMS 122:5; Isaiah 9:7"> The throne room where he sat as judge was called the Hall of Justice, lined with cedar panels from floor to ceiling.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Chronicles 8:11; 1 Kings 3:1; 1 Kings 9:24"> Solomon's own palace where he lived was in a courtyard behind the porch, made in a similar way to the Temple.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“To the Temple”: implied.">a</a></sup>He also had a palace made for Pharaoh's daughter, whom he'd married.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Kings 7:10–11; 1 Kings 5:17"> All these buildings were built using stone blocks that were expensive to produce. They were cut to size and trimmed with saws on the inside and outside. These stones were used from the foundation to the eaves, from the outside of the building all the way to the great courtyard.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Revelation 21:19–20; 1 Corinthians 3:10–11; Isaiah 54:11"> The foundations were laid with very large top-quality stones, between eight and ten cubits long.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ephesians 2:20–22; 1 Peter 2:5"> On these were placed top-quality stones, cut to size, along with cedar timber.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Kings 6:36; 1 Kings 7:6; John 10:23"> Around the great courtyard, the inner courtyard, and the porch of the Lord's Temple were three courses of dressed stone and a course of cedar beams.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:11; 2 Chronicles 2:13–14; 1 Kings 7:40"> King Solomon sent for Hiram<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Hiram,” or “Huram.” Not the King of Tyre who bore the same name.">b</a></sup>from Tyre.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Chronicles 2:14; 2 Chronicles 4:16; Exodus 31:2–6"> He was the son of a widow from the tribe of Naphtali, and his father was from Tyre, a craftsman who worked in bronze. Hiram had great expertise, understanding and being familiar with all kinds of bronze work. He came to King Solomon and carried out all that the king required.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:21; 2 Chronicles 3:15–17; 1 Kings 7:21"> He cast two columns in bronze. They were both eighteen cubits high and twelve cubits in circumference.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Exodus 38:19; 2 Chronicles 4:12–13; Exodus 38:17"> He also cast two capitals in bronze to place on top of the columns. Each capital was five cubits high.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Exodus 28:14; Exodus 28:24–25; Exodus 28:22"> He made a network of lattice using interlinked chains for both capitals, seven for each one.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Exodus 28:14; Exodus 39:15–18; Exodus 28:22"> Around the lattice network he made two rows of ornamental pomegranates to cover the capitals on the top of both the columns.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 Kings 7:22; 1 Kings 6:32–35; 1 Kings 6:18"> The capitals placed on top of columns in the porch were in the shape of lilies, four cubits high.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Chronicles 3:16; 2 Chronicles 4:13; Jeremiah 52:22–23"> On the capitals of both columns were the two hundred pomegranates in rows that encircled them, just above the rounded part that was next to the chain network.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Chronicles 3:17; 1 Kings 6:3; 1 Kings 7:12"> He erected the columns at the entrance porch of the Temple. The southern column he named Jachin, and the northern column he named Boaz.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> The capitals on the columns were in the shape of lilies. And so the work on the columns was finished.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:2; 2 Kings 25:13; Jeremiah 52:17"> Then he made the Sea of cast metal.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="The “Sea” was a very large metal bowl that contained water.">c</a></sup>Its shape was circular, and measured ten cubits from edge to edge, five cubits in height, and thirty cubits in circumference.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Kings 6:18; 2 Chronicles 4:3; Exodus 37:17–22"> Below the edge it was decorated with ornamental gourds that encircled it, ten per cubit all the way around. They were in two rows cast as one piece with the Sea.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:20; 2 Chronicles 4:4–5; Matthew 28:19"> The Sea stood on twelve metal bulls. Three faced to the north, three to the west, three to the south, and three to the east. The Sea was placed on them, with their rears toward the center.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:5; 1 Kings 7:38; 1 Kings 6:18"> It was as thick as the width of a hand, and its edge was like the flared edge of a cup or a lily flower. It held two thousand baths.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:14; 2 Kings 25:13; Jeremiah 52:17"> He also made ten carts to carry basins. The carts measured four cubits long, four cubits wide, and three cubits high.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> This is how they were put together: side panels were attached to uprights.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Hosea 5:14; Ezekiel 10:14; Exodus 37:7"> Both the side panels and the uprights were decorated with lions, bulls, and cherubim. Above and below the lions and the bulls were decorative wreaths.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Ezekiel 10:10–13; Ezekiel 3:13; Ezekiel 1:15–21"> Each cart had four bronze wheels with bronze axles. A basin rested on four supports that had decorative wreaths on each side.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31"> At the top of each cart was a round opening like a pedestal to hold the basin.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“To hold the basin”: implied.">d</a></sup>The opening was one cubit deep, and one and a half cubits wide. The opening had carvings around it. The panels of the cart were square, not round.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32"> The four wheels were under the panels, and the axles of the wheels were attached to the cart. Each wheel measured one and a half cubits in diameter.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Ezekiel 1:18; Ezekiel 1:16"> The wheels were made in the same way as chariot wheels; their axles, rims, spokes, and hubs were all made by casting.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34"> Each cart had four handles, one on each corner, made as part of the stand.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35"> There was a ring on the top of the cart a half cubit wide. The supports and panels were cast as one piece with the top of the cart.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="1 Kings 7:29; 1 Kings 6:29; Ezekiel 41:18–20"> He had designs of cherubim, lions, and palm trees engraved on the panels, supports, and frame, wherever there was space, with decorative wreaths all around.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37"> This is how he made the ten carts, with the same casts, size, and shape.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="Exodus 40:11–12; Zechariah 13:1; 1 John 1:7"> Then he made ten bronze basins. Each one held forty baths and measured four cubits across, one basin for each of the ten carts.</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:10; 2 Chronicles 4:6"> He placed five carts on the south side of the Temple and five on the north side. He placed the Sea on the south side, by the southeast corner of the Temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:11–16; Exodus 24:6; Exodus 39:32–43"> He also made the pots, shovels, and bowls.<br />So Hiram finished making everything required by King Solomon for the Temple of the Lord:</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="1 Kings 7:15–22; 2 Chronicles 4:12"> the two columns; the two capitals shaped like bowls on top the columns; the two chain networks that covered the capitals shaped like bowls on top of the columns;</span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="1 Kings 7:20"> the four hundred ornamental pomegranates for the chain networks (in two rows for the chain networks that covered the capitals on top of the columns);</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43"> the ten carts; the ten basins on the carts;</span>
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44" data-xref="1 Kings 7:23; 1 Kings 7:25"> the Sea; the twelve bulls under the Sea;</span>
<span className="v" id="v45" data-v="45" data-xref="Exodus 27:3; Exodus 38:3; 2 Chronicles 4:16"> and the pots, shovels, and bowls. Everything that Hiram made for King Solomon in the Temple of the Lord was made of polished bronze.</span>
<span className="v" id="v46" data-v="46" data-xref="Joshua 3:16; 2 Chronicles 4:17; Genesis 33:17"> The king had them cast in molds made of clay in the Jordan valley between Succoth and Zarethan.</span>
<span className="v" id="v47" data-v="47" data-xref="1 Chronicles 22:14; 1 Chronicles 22:3; 2 Chronicles 4:18"> Solomon did not weigh anything that had been made because there was just so much—the weight of bronze used could not be measured.</span>
<span className="v" id="v48" data-v="48" data-xref="Exodus 37:10–16; 2 Chronicles 4:8; Exodus 39:36"> Solomon also had made all the items for the Temple of the Lord: the golden altar; the golden table where the Bread of the Presence was placed;</span>
<span className="v" id="v49" data-v="49" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:7; 2 Chronicles 4:20; Zechariah 4:11–14"> the lampstands made of pure gold that stood in front of the inner sanctuary, five on the right and five on the left; the flowers, lamps, and tongs that were all made of pure gold;</span>
<span className="v" id="v50" data-v="50" data-xref="Exodus 25:29; 2 Chronicles 4:21–22; Exodus 27:3"> the basins, wick trimmers, bowls, ladles, and censers that again were all made of pure gold; and the gold hinges for the doors of the inner sanctuary, the Most Holy Place, in addition to the doors of the main hall of the Temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v51" data-v="51" data-xref="2 Chronicles 5:1; 2 Samuel 8:7–11; 1 Chronicles 28:11–18"> In this way all King Solomon's work for the Temple of the Lord was completed. Then Solomon brought in the items his father David had dedicated, the special objects made of silver, the gold, and the Temple furnishings, and he placed them in the treasuries of the Temple of the Lord.</span>
</div>
