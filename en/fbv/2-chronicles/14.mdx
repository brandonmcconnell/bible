<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Chronicles 9:31; 1 Kings 2:10; 1 Kings 14:31"> Abijah died and was buried in the City of David. His son Asa took over as king. For ten years of his reign the country was at peace.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Kings 15:11; 2 Chronicles 31:20; 1 Kings 15:14"> Asa did what was good and right in the Lord's sight.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Deuteronomy 7:5; Exodus 34:13; 2 Kings 23:14"> He took down the foreign altars and high places, smashed their sacred pillars, and cut down the Asherah poles.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Images dedicated to the Canaanite fertility goddess Asherah. Whether “pole” should be added is debated.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Chronicles 30:19; PSALMS 119:10; PSALMS 101:2–8"> He ordered Judah to worship the Lord, the God of their forefathers, and to observe the law and the commandments.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Chronicles 34:4; 2 Chronicles 34:7"> He also took down the high places and the incense altars from all the towns of Judah. Under his rule the kingdom was at peace.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Chronicles 15:15; 1 Chronicles 22:9; Job 34:29"> Because the country was at peace he was able to rebuild the fortified towns of Judah. There were no wars during these years because the Lord had granted him peace.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Peter 3:12; 2 Chronicles 14:6; Jeremiah 29:12–14"> Asa told the people of Judah, “Let us build up these towns and surround them with walls and towers and barred gates. The land is still ours, because we continue to worship the Lord our God. We worship him, and he has given us peace from all our enemies.” So they began the building projects, and completed them successfully.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Chronicles 13:3; 2 Chronicles 17:14–19; 2 Chronicles 11:1"> Asa had an army made up of three hundred thousand men from Judah who carried large shields and spears, and two hundred eighty thousand men from Benjamin who carried regular shields and bows. All of them were brave warriors.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Chronicles 16:8; 2 Chronicles 12:2–3; 2 Chronicles 11:8"> Zerah the Ethiopian attacked them with an army of a thousand times a thousand<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Thousand times a thousand”: though this equates to one million, it may simply mean a very large number.">b</a></sup>men and three hundred chariots, advancing as far as Mareshah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Judges 1:17; Joshua 19:4"> Asa went out to confront him, lining up for battle in the Valley of Zephathah at Mareshah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Jeremiah 1:19; 2 Corinthians 12:9–10; PSALMS 22:5"> Asa called out for help to the Lord his God: “Lord, there is no one apart from you who can help the powerless against the powerful. Please help us, Lord our God, for we trust in you. We have come against this horde because we stand for you,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“Stand for you”: literally, “in your name.” In other words, Asa is saying that the battle is the Lord's, not theirs.">c</a></sup>Lord. You are our God. Do not let a mere human being beat<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Beat”: the word here means to “restrain, hold back.”">d</a></sup>you.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Chronicles 13:15; PSALMS 136:17–18; Deuteronomy 28:7"> The Lord struck the Ethiopians in front of Asa and Judah, and the Ethiopians ran away.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Genesis 10:19; Genesis 26:1; Genesis 20:1"> Asa and his army chased them as far as Gerar. The Ethiopians were killed—there were none who survived, for they were caught between the Lord and his army. The men of Judah carried off a large amount of plunder.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Chronicles 17:10; Genesis 35:5; 2 Chronicles 20:29"> They also attacked all the towns around Gerar, because the inhabitants were terrified of the Lord. The men of Judah took a large amount of plunder from all the towns.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Numbers 31:30–47; Numbers 31:9; 1 Samuel 30:20"> Then they attacked the camps of the herdsmen and took many sheep and camels. Then they went back to Jerusalem.</span>
</div>
</div>
