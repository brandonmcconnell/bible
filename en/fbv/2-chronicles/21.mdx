<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Kings 22:50; 2 Kings 8:16–17; 2 Chronicles 12:16"> Jehoshaphat died and was buried with his forefathers in the City of David, and his son Jehoram took over as king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Chronicles 12:6; 2 Chronicles 23:2; 2 Chronicles 24:5"> His brothers, the sons of Jehoshaphat, were Azariah, Jehiel, Zechariah, Azariah, Michael, and Shephatiah. All were sons of Jehoshaphat, king of Judah.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“Judah,” following some Hebrew manuscripts, the Septuagint, and the Vulgate. Most Hebrew manuscripts have “Israel,” perhaps reflecting the Chronicler's intent that Judah should be seen as being the real inheritor of the title “kingdom of Israel.” Similarly verse 4.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Chronicles 11:23; Deuteronomy 21:15–17; 2 Chronicles 11:5"> Their father had given them many gifts of silver and gold and valuable items, as well as the fortified towns in Judah; but he gave the kingdom to Jehoram because he was the firstborn.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Judges 9:5; 2 Chronicles 21:17; Genesis 4:8"> But once Jehoram had secured the kingdom, he made sure of his position by killing all his brothers, along with some of the princes of Judah.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Kings 8:17–22"> Jehoram was thirty-two when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem for eight years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Chronicles 18:1; 1 Kings 12:28–30; 2 Kings 8:18"> He followed the evil ways of the kings of Israel, and was as bad as Ahab, for he had married one of Ahab's daughters. He did evil in the Lord's sight.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Kings 11:36; PSALMS 132:17–18; 2 Samuel 7:12–17"> However, the Lord did not want to destroy David's line because of the agreement he had made with David, and he had promised that David's descendants would rule forever like an ever-burning lamp.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Chronicles 21:10; 2 Chronicles 20:22–23; Genesis 27:40"> During Jehoram's reign, Edom rebelled against Judah's rule and chose their own king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> So Jehoram crossed into Edom with his officers and all his chariot army. The Edomites surrounded him and his chariot commanders, but he broke through during the night.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="The Hebrew is unclear as to whether this was a night attack, or merely that Jehoram escaped. In any event, the following verses reveal that the rebellion was not put down by Jehoram.">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Chronicles 15:2; Jeremiah 2:13; 2 Chronicles 13:10"> From this time on Edom was in rebellion against Judah's rule, and remains so to this day. At the same time Libnah also rebelled against his rule, because he had abandoned the Lord, the God of his forefathers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Leviticus 20:5; 1 Kings 11:7; Leviticus 17:7"> He also built high places on the mountains of Judah; he made the people of Jerusalem unfaithful to God and led Judah away from him.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Chronicles 14:2–5; 2 Chronicles 17:3–4; 2 Kings 2:1"> Jehoram received a letter from Elijah the prophet that said, “This is what the Lord, the God of David your forefather says, ‘You have not followed the ways of your father Jehoshaphat, or Asa, king of Judah,</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Chronicles 21:4; 2 Chronicles 21:11; 1 Kings 16:30–33"> but instead you have followed the ways of the kings of Israel, and have made the people of Jerusalem unfaithful, and the people of Jerusalem as unfaithful as the family of Ahab. You have even killed your brothers, your father's family, who were better than you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Leviticus 26:21; Exodus 20:5; Hosea 5:11"> Watch out, for the Lord is going to hit your people hard—your sons, your wives, and all that you own.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="2 Chronicles 21:18–19; Acts 1:18; Deuteronomy 28:67"> You yourself will be struck with a terrible illness—a disease of the bowels which will get worse day by day until they come out.’”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Chronicles 17:11; 2 Chronicles 33:11; 1 Kings 11:14"> The Lord stirred up the hostility of the Philistines and of the Arabians (who live near the Ethiopians) against Jehoram.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Chronicles 22:1; 2 Chronicles 22:6; 2 Chronicles 25:23"> They came and invaded Judah, and took away everything they found in the king's palace, along with his sons and his wives, so that only the youngest son Jehoahaz<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“Jehoahaz”: also called Azariah.">c</a></sup>was left.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="2 Chronicles 21:15; Acts 12:23; 2 Kings 9:29"> After all this, the Lord struck Jehoram with a disease of the bowels for which there was no cure.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Chronicles 16:14; Jeremiah 34:5"> Day after day it grew worse, until after two full years passed his bowels came out because of his disease, and he died in agony. His people did not make a fire to honor him as they had done for his forefathers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Jeremiah 22:18; 2 Chronicles 28:27; Jeremiah 22:28"> Jehoram was thirty-two when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem for eight years. When he died, no one mourned him. He was buried in the City of David, but not in the royal tombs.</span>
</div>
