<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Matthew 12:42; Luke 11:31; 1 Kings 10:1–13"> The queen of Sheba heard how famous Solomon was, so she came to Jerusalem to test him with tough questions. She brought with her a very large entourage, with camels loaded with spices, large amounts of gold, and precious gemstones. She came to Solomon and asked him about everything she had on her mind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="John 15:15; 1 Kings 3:12; Mark 4:34"> Solomon answered all her questions. There was nothing he couldn't explain to her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Chronicles 3:1–4; 1 Kings 10:3; 1 Kings 6:1–7"> When the queen of Sheba saw Solomon's wisdom, and the palace he had built,</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Luke 12:37; 1 Kings 4:22–23; PSALMS 143:7"> the food on the table, how his officials lived, how his servants operated and how they were dressed, the clothes of the waiters, and the burnt offerings he presented at the Lord's Temple, she was so astonished<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“She was so astonished”: implied by the phrase (literally) “there was no longer breath in her.”">a</a></sup>she could hardly breathe.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Kings 10:6"> She told the king, “It's true what I heard in my own country about your proverbs<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Proverbs”: literally, “words.”">b</a></sup>and your wisdom!</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Kings 10:7; 2 Chronicles 9:5; 1 Kings 4:34"> But I didn't believe what they told me until I came and saw with my own eyes. In fact, I wasn't told the half of it—the extent of your wisdom far exceeds what I heard!</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="PSALMS 27:4; Proverbs 3:14; Proverbs 10:21"> How happy your people must be! How happy those who work for you, who stand here every day listening to your wisdom!</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Chronicles 2:11; 1 Chronicles 29:23; Isaiah 32:1–2"> Praise the Lord your God who is so pleased with you, who placed you on his throne as king to rule on his behalf. Because of the love of your God for Israel he has made them secure forever, and he has made you king over them to do what is fair and right.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Chronicles 9:1; PSALMS 72:10; PSALMS 72:15"> She presented the king with one hundred and twenty talents of gold, huge amounts of spices and precious stones. Never before had there been spices like those the queen of Sheba gave to King Solomon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Chronicles 8:18; 1 Kings 10:11; 1 Kings 10:22"> (Hiram and Solomon's men, who brought gold from Ophir, also brought algum wood and precious stones.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Kings 10:12; 1 Chronicles 23:5; PSALMS 92:1–3"> The king used the algum wood to make steps for the Temple and for the royal palace, and into lyres and harps for the musicians. Nothing like them had ever been seen before in the land of Judah.)</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Kings 10:13; Ephesians 3:20; PSALMS 20:4"> King Solomon gave the queen of Sheba all she wanted, whatever she asked for. This was far more than she had brought the king. Then she and her attendants returned home to her own country.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="PSALMS 72:15; PSALMS 68:29; 1 Kings 10:14–28"> The weight of gold that Solomon received each year was 666 talents,</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="PSALMS 68:29; PSALMS 72:10"> not including that received from traders and merchants. All the kings of Arabia and governors of the land also brought gold and silver to Solomon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Kings 10:16–17; 2 Chronicles 12:9–10"> King Solomon made two hundred shields of hammered gold. Each shield required six hundred shekels of hammered gold.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Kings 7:2"> He also made three hundred small shields of hammered gold. Each of these shields required three hundred gold coins. The king placed them in the Palace of the Forest of Lebanon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Kings 10:18–20; PSALMS 45:8; Revelation 20:11"> The king also made a great throne of ivory, and covered it with pure gold.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Numbers 24:9; Revelation 5:5; Numbers 23:24"> The throne had six steps, with a golden footstool attached. There were armrests on both sides of the seat, with lions standing beside the armrests.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Matthew 19:28; Revelation 21:12"> Twelve lions stood on the six steps, one on opposite ends of each step. Nothing like this had ever been made for any kingdom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Kings 10:21; Esther 1:7; 2 Chronicles 9:27"> All of King Solomon's drinking cups were gold, and all the utensils of the Palace of the Forest of Lebanon were pure gold. No silver was used, because it was not valued in the days of Solomon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Kings 22:48; Job 39:13; 1 Kings 10:22"> The king had a fleet of ships from Tarshish crewed by Hiram's sailors. Once every three years the ships of Tarshish would arrive with a cargo of gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Chronicles 1:12; PSALMS 89:27; 1 Kings 4:30–31"> King Solomon was greater than any other king on earth in wealth and wisdom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="James 1:5; James 3:17; Proverbs 2:6"> All the kings of the earth wanted to meet Solomon to hear the wisdom that God had placed in his mind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Kings 10:25; Job 42:11; PSALMS 72:10"> Year after year, every visitor would bring gifts—articles of silver and gold, clothes, weapons, spices, horses, and mules.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="1 Kings 4:26; 1 Kings 10:26; 2 Chronicles 1:14"> Solomon had four thousand stalls for horses and chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“Horsemen”: or, “horses,” (the word in Hebrew is the same). However, since the number of stalls for the horses has already been given, it is more likely that this refers to horsemen.">c</a></sup>He kept them in the chariot towns, and also with him in Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="1 Kings 4:21; Genesis 15:18; 1 Kings 4:24"> He ruled over all the kings from the Euphrates River to the land of the Philistines, and to the border with Egypt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 Chronicles 1:15–17; Isaiah 9:10; 1 Kings 10:27–29"> The king made silver as plentiful in Jerusalem as stones, and cedar wood as plentiful as sycamore-figs in the foothills.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="See 1:15.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 Chronicles 1:16; 1 Kings 10:28; Isaiah 31:1"> Solomon's horses were imported from Egypt and from many other lands.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="1 Chronicles 29:29; 1 Kings 11:41–43; 1 Kings 11:29"> The rest of the acts of Solomon, from start to finish, are written in the Records of Nathan the Prophet, in the Prophecy of Ahijah the Shilonite, and in the Visions of Iddo the Seer about Jeroboam, son of Nebat.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="1 Kings 11:42–43"> Solomon ruled in Jerusalem over all of Israel for forty years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="1 Kings 2:10; 2 Samuel 7:12; 1 Kings 1:21"> Then Solomon died and was buried in the city of his father David. His son Rehoboam took over as king.</span>
