<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Corinthians 5:7–8; 2 Chronicles 11:13; Hosea 5:4"> Then Hezekiah sent an announcement to everyone in Israel and Judah, and also sent letters to Ephraim and Manasseh,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“Ephraim and Manasseh”: by this time the northern kingdom of Israel no longer existed, having been destroyed and its people taken away as prisoners by the Assyrian king Shalmanesar. By his invitation Hezekiah is appealing for those who are left in the north to “return home.”">a</a></sup>inviting them to come to the Lord's Temple in Jerusalem to celebrate the Passover of the Lord, the God of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Numbers 9:10–11; 1 Chronicles 13:1–3; 2 Chronicles 30:13"> The king and his officials and the whole assembly in Jerusalem had decided to observe the Passover in the second month,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Deferring the Passover in special circumstances was permitted, see Numbers 9:6-11.">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Chronicles 29:34; Exodus 12:6; Exodus 12:18"> because they hadn't been able to observe it at the usual time since not enough priests had purified themselves and the people hadn't had time to get to Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Chronicles 13:4"> The plan seemed right to both the king and the whole assembly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Judges 20:1; Leviticus 23:2; Esther 8:8–10"> So they decided to send an announcement to everyone in Israel, from Beersheba to Dan, inviting people to come and keep the Passover to the Lord, the God of Israel, in Jerusalem, for many had not done as the Law required.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Jeremiah 51:31; Esther 8:14; 2 Kings 15:19"> So messengers went to all of Israel and Judah carrying letters from the king and his officials and with the king's authorization. They said, “Children of Israel, return to the Lord, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, so that he may return to you who are left, who have escaped the oppression of the kings of Assyria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Chronicles 29:8; Ezekiel 20:13–18; Zechariah 1:3–4"> Don't be like your fathers and those of you who sinned against the Lord, the God of your forefathers He made them into something horrifying, as you can see.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Exodus 32:9; 2 Chronicles 29:10; Deuteronomy 10:16"> So don't be proud and obstinate like your fathers, but give yourselves to the Lord and come to his sanctuary, which he has made holy forever, and serve the Lord your God, that his fierce anger may no longer fall on you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Isaiah 55:7; Micah 7:18; PSALMS 86:5"> If you come back to the Lord, your relatives and children will receive mercy from their captors and will return to this land. For the Lord your God is gracious and merciful. He will not reject you if you come back to him.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Chronicles 36:16; 2 Chronicles 30:6; Nehemiah 2:19"> The messengers went from town to town all over the land of Ephraim and Manasseh as far as Zebulun; but the people laughed at them and mocked them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Chronicles 30:18; 2 Chronicles 30:21; 2 Chronicles 30:25"> Only some men from Asher, Manasseh, and Zebulun weren't too proud to go to Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Jeremiah 32:39; Jeremiah 24:7; Philippians 2:13"> At this time the power of God was helping the people in Judah to all have the same desire to follow the orders of the king and his officials, as indicated by the word of the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Chronicles 30:2; PSALMS 84:7"> Many people gathered in Jerusalem to celebrate the Feast of Unleavened Bread in the second month—a really large crowd.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Chronicles 28:24; 2 Samuel 15:23; 2 Chronicles 15:16"> They went and removed the pagan altars in Jerusalem as well as the incense altars and threw them into the Kidron Valley.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="2 Chronicles 29:34; 2 Chronicles 5:11; Exodus 19:22"> On the fourteenth day of the second month they killed the Passover lamb. The priests and Levites were ashamed,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“Ashamed”: perhaps because there had not been enough of them at the previous celebration, and that they had not take seriously the responsibility to purify themselves. Alternatively the devotion shown by those who were attending the Feast of Unleavened Bread may have prompted the priests and Levites to action.">c</a></sup>and they purified themselves and brought burnt offerings to the Lord's Temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Deuteronomy 33:1; 2 Chronicles 35:15; 2 Kings 11:14"> They stood at their assigned positions, according to the law of Moses, the man of God. The priests sprinkled the blood of the sacrifices, which the Levites gave to them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Chronicles 29:34; 2 Chronicles 35:3–6; Exodus 12:6"> Since many people in the assembly had not purified themselves, the Levites had to kill the Passover lambs on behalf of every unclean person to dedicate the lambs to the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Corinthians 11:28; James 5:15–16; Daniel 9:19"> Most of the people, many of those from Ephraim, Manasseh, Issachar, and Zebulun, had not purified themselves. Yet they ate the Passover meal even though this was not what the Law required, for Hezekiah had prayed for them, saying, “May the good Lord forgive everyone</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Chronicles 19:3; Proverbs 23:26; Job 11:13"> who sincerely wants to follow the Lord God, the God of their forefathers, even though they're not clean according to the sanctuary requirements.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Exodus 15:26; James 5:15–16; PSALMS 103:3"> The Lord accepted Hezekiah's prayer and permitted them this violation.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Permitted them this violation”: the word here is “healed,” but this is in a metaphorical sense, since they were not “sick.” It is in response to Hezekiah's request that they would not be held guilty for breaking the ceremonial Law.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Exodus 12:15; Exodus 13:6; Deuteronomy 12:12"> The people of Israel who were there in Jerusalem celebrated the Feast of Unleavened Bread for seven days with great enthusiasm, and every day the Levites and priests praised the Lord, accompanied by loud instruments.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Chronicles 32:6; Nehemiah 9:3; Isaiah 40:1–2"> Hezekiah spoke positively to all the Levites who showed a good understanding of the Lord. For seven days they ate the food that was assigned to them, presented friendship offerings, and gave thanks to the Lord, the God of their forefathers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Kings 8:65; 2 Chronicles 7:9"> Everyone then agreed to continue to celebrate the festival for seven more days. So for another seven days they celebrated, full of joy.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="2 Chronicles 29:34; 2 Chronicles 35:7–8; Ephesians 4:8"> Hezekiah, king of Judah, gave a thousand bulls and seven thousand sheep as offerings on behalf of the assembly. The officials in turn gave a thousand bulls and ten thousand sheep as offerings on behalf of the assembly. A large number of priests purified themselves.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Chronicles 30:11; 2 Chronicles 30:18; 1 Chronicles 16:10–11"> The whole assembly of Judah celebrated, together with the priests and Levites, and also with the whole assembly that had come from Israel, including the foreigners from Israel and those living in Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Chronicles 7:8–10"> There was such tremendous happiness in Jerusalem, for since the time of Solomon, son of David, king of Israel, nothing like this had happened in the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Deuteronomy 26:15; PSALMS 68:5; Numbers 6:23–26"> The priests and the Levites stood up to bless the people, and God heard them—their prayer ascended to where he lived in heaven.</span>
