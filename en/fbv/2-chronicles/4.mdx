<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Kings 8:64; Ezekiel 43:13–17; 1 Kings 9:25"> Solomon made a bronze altar twenty cubits long, twenty cubits wide, and ten cubits high.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Titus 3:5; Zechariah 13:1; Exodus 30:18–21"> He made a “Sea” from cast metal,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="This was a large basin filled with water. The metal used was probably bronze, but is not specifically identified as such in the text.">a</a></sup>ten cubits in diameter, five cubits in height, and thirty cubits in circumference.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Ezekiel 1:10; Ezekiel 10:14; 1 Corinthians 9:9–10"> Below it were ornamental bulls<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="See 1 Kings 7:24which reads “gourds.”">b</a></sup>all around it, ten per cubit. They were in two rows when it was all cast.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Matthew 28:19–20; Revelation 21:14; Mark 16:15"> The Sea was supported by twelve statues of bulls three facing north, three facing west, three facing south, and three facing east. The Sea was placed on them, with their rears toward the center.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Kings 7:26"> It was as thick as the width of a hand, and its edge was like the flared edge of a cup or a lily flower. It held three thousand baths.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="See 1 Kings 7:26which lists the capacity as two thousand baths.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Kings 7:38; Exodus 30:18–21; 1 Kings 7:40"> He also made ten basins on carts for washing. He placed five on the south side, and five on the north. They were used for cleaning what was used in burnt offerings, but the Sea was used by the priests for washing.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Exodus 25:31–40; 1 Kings 7:49; John 8:12"> He made ten gold lampstands as had been specified,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="See 1 Chronicles 28:15.">d</a></sup>and placed them in the Temple, five on the south side and five on the north.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Kings 7:48; Malachi 1:12; Exodus 25:23–30"> In addition he made ten tables and placed them in the Temple, five on the south side and five on the north. He also made a hundred gold basins.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Kings 6:36; 2 Kings 21:5; 1 Kings 7:12"> Solomon also built a courtyard of the priests, and the large courtyard and doors for the courtyard, and he covered the doors with bronze.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Kings 7:39"> He placed the Sea on the south side, by the southeast corner.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Kings 7:40–51; 1 Kings 7:14"> Hiram also made the pots, shovels, and basins. Hiram completed the work that he had been doing for King Solomon on the Temple of God:</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Kings 7:41; 2 Chronicles 3:15–17"> the two columns; the two bowl-shaped capitals on top of the columns; the two sets of network<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Presumably a network of chains, already mentioned.">e</a></sup>that covered both bowls of the capitals on top of the columns;</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Kings 7:20; 1 Kings 7:42; Jeremiah 52:23"> the four hundred ornamental pomegranates for the two sets of network—two rows of pomegranates for each network covering both the bowl-shaped capitals on top of the columns;</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="1 Kings 7:27–43"> the water carts and the basins on the water carts;</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> the Sea and the twelve bull statues that supported it; the pots, shovels, forks, and everything else.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Chronicles 2:13; 1 Kings 7:13–14; 2 Chronicles 4:11"> All the metalwork Hiram made for King Solomon for the house of the Lord was of polished bronze.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Kings 7:46"> The king cast them in clay molds in the plain of the Jordan between Succoth and Zeredah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Kings 7:47; Jeremiah 52:20; 1 Chronicles 22:14"> Solomon made so many of these things that the weight of the bronze used could not be measured.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Revelation 9:13; Daniel 5:2–3; Exodus 25:23–30"> Solomon also made everything used in the Temple of God: the golden altar; the tables where the Bread of the Presence was displayed;</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:7; Exodus 25:31–37; 1 Kings 6:5"> the lampstands of pure gold and their lamps that were to burn in front of the Most Holy Place as specified;</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Kings 6:29; Exodus 37:20; 1 Kings 6:35"> the decorative flowers, lamps, and tongs—all made of solid gold;</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Kings 12:13; 2 Kings 25:14; Jeremiah 52:18"> the wick trimmers, basins, dishes and censers—all made of gold; and the doors of the Temple: the inner doors to the Most Holy Place, and the doors of the main hall—all covered with gold.</span>
