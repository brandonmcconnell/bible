<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Jeremiah 22:6–7; Jeremiah 22:23; Luke 21:23–24"> Open your doors, Lebanon, so that fire can burn up your cedars!</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Isaiah 2:12–17; Ezekiel 31:17; Isaiah 10:33–34"> Weep, juniper, because the cedar has fallen, the majestic trees are ruined! Weep, oaks of Bashan, for the thick forest has been cut down!</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Jeremiah 50:44; Jeremiah 25:34–36; Zechariah 11:8"> Listen to the howls of the shepherds, for their pastureland<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally, “glory.” The parallel with the second line of the verse indicates something in the natural world.">a</a></sup>is destroyed. Listen to the roars of the young lions, for their Jordan habitat<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Jordan habitat”: literally, “majesty of Jordan.”">b</a></sup>is ruined.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Zechariah 11:7; Matthew 23:37; Romans 15:8"> This is what the Lord my God says: Become a shepherd of the flock marked for slaughter.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Jeremiah 50:7; Hosea 12:8; 1 Timothy 6:5–10"> Those who buy them kill them and don't feel guilty; those who sell them say, “Praise the Lord! Now I'm rich!” Even their shepherds don't care about them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Jeremiah 13:14; Matthew 22:7; Isaiah 9:19–21"> For I will no longer care about the people of the Land, declares the Lord. I am going to make them victims of each other, and of the king. They will devastate the earth and I won't help anyone get away from them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Zechariah 11:14; Zechariah 11:4; Isaiah 11:4"> So I became a shepherd of the flock marked for slaughter by the sheep merchants.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Reading the text as “sheep merchants” rather than “oppressed sheep.”">c</a></sup>Then I took two staffs, one named Grace, the other named Union, and I shepherded the flock.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Isaiah 49:7; Matthew 23:34–36; John 15:18"> In one month I dismissed three shepherds. I became impatient with them<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Scholars are divided as to who “them” refers to. Some take it to mean the three shepherds, others the sheep, and still others the sheep merchants.">d</a></sup>, and they also hated me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Jeremiah 43:11; Matthew 13:10–11; Matthew 21:19"> Then I said, “I will not be your shepherd.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Clearly Zechariah is now speaking to the sheep merchants for whom he was working as a shepherd (11:7)">e</a></sup>If the sheep die, they die. Let those that are to perish, perish. Let those who are left eat each other!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Zechariah 11:7; PSALMS 89:39; Hebrews 8:8–13"> Then I took my staff called Grace and broke it, breaking the agreement I had made with all the peoples.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Since there is no record of an agreement, or “covenant,” with any other nation, it is presumed that “peoples” here refers to the Israelites.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Zephaniah 3:12; Luke 2:38; Deuteronomy 31:21"> It was broken on that day, and the sheep merchants who were watching me knew that it was a message from the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Matthew 26:15; Exodus 21:32; Luke 22:3–6"> I told them, “If you want to pay me my wages, then do so. If not, then don't.” So they paid me my wages—thirty pieces of silver.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Matthew 27:3–10; Acts 1:18–19; Isaiah 53:2–3"> And the Lord said to me, “Throw the money to the treasury,”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Or “to the potter.” However, since “of the Lord's Temple” is mentioned later in the verse, this seems a more likely scenario.">g</a></sup>this measly sum they thought I was worth! So I took the thirty pieces of silver and threw it into the treasury of the Lord's Temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Isaiah 9:21; Isaiah 11:13; Acts 23:7–10"> Then I broke my second staff called Union, breaking the family union between Judah and Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Isaiah 6:10–12; Luke 11:40; Jeremiah 2:26–27"> The Lord told me, Get the things you use as a shepherd, a useless shepherd.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Ezekiel 34:2–6; Jeremiah 23:2; Matthew 23:2–4"> For I am placing a shepherd in charge of the land who won't care for those who are dying, or look for the lost,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Or “the young.”">h</a></sup>or heal the injured, or feed the healthy. Instead he will eat the meat from the fattest sheep. He even tears their hooves off.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Perhaps illustrating the extent of cruelty to the animals.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Jeremiah 23:1; Ezekiel 13:3; 1 Corinthians 8:4"> What disaster is coming to this useless shepherd who abandons the flock! The sword will strike his arm and his right eye. His arm will wither away and his right eye will become completely blind.</span>
