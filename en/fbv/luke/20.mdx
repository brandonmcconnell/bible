<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Matthew 21:23–27; Luke 19:47–48; Acts 6:12"> Once when Jesus was teaching the people in the Temple, telling them the good news, some of the chief priests and religious teachers came with the elders.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Exodus 2:14; Mark 11:28–33; John 2:18"> They asked him, “Tell us: by whose authority are you doing what you do? Who gave you the right to do this?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Matthew 15:2–3; Luke 22:68; Colossians 4:6"> “Let me ask you a question too,” Jesus replied. “Tell me:</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Luke 15:18; Matthew 11:7–19; Matthew 17:11–12"> the baptism of John—was it from heaven, or was it just human?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="John 1:34; John 1:15–18; John 1:30"> They talked about it among themselves: “If we say it was from heaven, he'll ask, ‘Then why didn't you believe him?’</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Matthew 21:26; Matthew 11:9; John 10:41"> And if we say it was just human, everybody will stone us for they're sure John was a prophet.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Isaiah 29:14; 2 Peter 3:3; Isaiah 41:28"> So they answered, “We don't know where it came from.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Proverbs 26:4–5; Matthew 21:27; Mark 11:33"> Jesus replied, “Then I won't tell you by whose authority I do what I do.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Matthew 21:33–46; Mark 12:1–12; Isaiah 5:1–7"> Then he began to tell the people a story: “Once there was a man who planted a vineyard, leased it to some farmers, and went to live in another country for a long while.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Chronicles 36:15–16; Nehemiah 9:26; Matthew 21:34–36"> At harvest-time he sent a servant to the tenant farmers to collect his share of the crop, but the farmers beat the servant and sent him away with nothing.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Hosea 10:1; Hebrews 11:36–37; 1 Thessalonians 2:2"> So the owner sent another servant, but they beat him too and treated him shamefully, and sent him away with nothing.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> So he sent a third servant, but they wounded him and threw him out.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Matthew 3:17; John 3:16–17; Jeremiah 36:7"> The owner of the vineyard asked himself, ‘What shall I do? I know, I'll send my son whom I love. Perhaps they will respect him.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Romans 8:17; Hebrews 1:2; Acts 2:23"> But when they saw him coming, the farmers said to themselves, ‘This is the owner's heir. Let's kill him! That way we can take his inheritance.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Hebrews 13:12; Mark 12:6–9; Matthew 21:37–40"> They threw him out of the vineyard and killed him. Now what will the owner of the vineyard do to them?</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Luke 19:27; Acts 13:46; Matthew 21:41"> He will come and kill these farmers and let others have the vineyard.” When they heard the story, they said, “May this never happen!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="PSALMS 118:22; Isaiah 28:16; Acts 4:11"> But Jesus looked at them and said, “Then why is it written in the Scriptures, ‘The stone the builders rejected has now become the chief cornerstone’?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Quoting Psalms 118:22.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Isaiah 8:14–15; Zechariah 12:3; Daniel 2:44–45"> Anyone who falls on that stone will be broken in pieces; anyone it falls upon will be crushed.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Luke 19:47–48; Luke 20:14; Matthew 21:45–46"> Immediately the religious teachers and the chief priests wanted to arrest him because they realized that the story Jesus told was aimed at them, but they were afraid of what the people would do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Luke 11:54; Matthew 22:15–22; Matthew 27:2"> Watching for an opportunity they sent spies who pretended to be sincere. They tried to catch Jesus out in something he said so they could hand him over to the power and authority of the governor.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 2:4–5; Matthew 26:49–50; Matthew 22:16"> They said, “Teacher, we know that you speak and teach what is good and right, and that you're not swayed by the opinions of others. You truly teach the way of God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Nehemiah 9:37; Mark 12:14–17; Acts 5:37"> So should we pay taxes to Caesar or not?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Matthew 22:18; 1 Corinthians 3:19; Luke 5:22"> But Jesus saw through their trickery, and said to them,</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Matthew 18:28; Acts 11:28; Acts 26:32"> “Show me a coin—a denarius.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Jesus specifically asks for a denarius, a Roman coin.">b</a></sup>Whose image and inscription is on it?” “Caesar's,” they answered.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Romans 13:6–7; Mark 12:17; 1 Peter 2:13–17"> “Then give back to Caesar what belongs to Caesar, and give back to God what belongs to God,” he told them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Luke 20:20; Proverbs 26:4–5; Titus 1:10"> They weren't able to trap him by what he told the people. They were stunned at his reply, and fell silent.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Acts 4:1–2; 1 Corinthians 15:12; Mark 12:18–27"> Then some of the Sadducees, who deny the resurrection, came to Jesus with this question:</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Deuteronomy 25:5–10; Genesis 38:8; Genesis 38:11"> “Teacher,” they began, “Moses gave us a law that if a married man dies leaving a wife without children, then his brother should marry the widow and have children for his dead brother.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Quoting Deuteronomy 25:5.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Leviticus 20:20; Jeremiah 22:30"> Once there were seven brothers. The first had a wife, and died without having children.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30"> The second</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31"> and then the third brother also married her. In the end all seven brothers married her, and then died without having children.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 1:4; Ecclesiastes 9:5; Hebrews 9:27"> Finally the wife died too.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Matthew 22:24–28; Mark 12:19–23"> Now whose wife will she be in the resurrection, since all seven brothers had married her?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Luke 16:8; Luke 17:27; Hebrews 13:4"> “Here in this age people marry and are given in marriage,” Jesus explained.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="2 Thessalonians 1:5; John 5:29; Daniel 12:2–3"> “But those who are considered worthy to share in the age to come and the resurrection from the dead don't marry or are given in marriage.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="Revelation 21:4; Philippians 3:21; Matthew 22:30"> They can't die any longer; they're like the angels and are children of God since they're children of the resurrection.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="Exodus 3:2–6; Mark 12:26–27; Acts 7:30–32"> But on the question of whether the dead are raised, even Moses proved this when he wrote about the burning<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Burning bush”—the Greek simply says “bush.”">d</a></sup>bush, when he called the Lord, ‘the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.’<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Quoting Exodus 3:6.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="John 11:25–26; Colossians 3:3–4; Romans 6:10–11"> He is not the God of the dead, but of the living, for to him they all are alive.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Acts 23:9; Mark 12:28–34; Matthew 22:34–40"> Some of the religious teachers responded, “That was a good answer, Teacher.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="Matthew 22:46; Mark 12:34; Proverbs 26:5"> After this no one dared to ask him any more questions.</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="Mark 12:35–37; Matthew 1:1; Matthew 22:41–45"> Then Jesus asked them, “Why is it said that Christ is the son of David?</span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="PSALMS 110:1; Acts 2:34–35; Hebrews 1:13"> For David himself says in the book of Psalms: The Lord said to my Lord, ‘Sit at my right hand</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43" data-xref="Luke 19:27; PSALMS 110:1; Revelation 19:14–21"> until I make all your enemies a footstool for your feet.’<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Quoting Psalms 110:1.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44" data-xref="Revelation 22:16; Luke 1:31–35; Matthew 1:23"> David calls him ‘Lord,’ so how can he be David's son?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v45" data-v="45" data-xref="Matthew 15:10; Mark 12:38–39; Mark 8:34"> While everyone was paying attention, he said to his disciples,</span>
<span className="v" id="v46" data-v="46" data-xref="Luke 11:43; Mark 12:38–39; Luke 14:7"> “Watch out for religious leaders who like to go around in long robes, and love to be greeted with respect in the markets, and to have the best seats in the synagogues and places of honor at banquets.</span>
<span className="v" id="v47" data-v="47" data-xref="Mark 12:40; James 3:1; Titus 1:16"> They cheat widows out of what they own,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Literally, “they devour widow's houses.”">g</a></sup>and cover up the kind of people they really are with long-winded prayers. They will receive severe condemnation in the judgment.”</span>
