<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Jeremiah 13:11; Hebrews 1:1; Jeremiah 27:2"> This is what the Lord told me to do: Go and buy yourself a linen loincloth and put it on, but don't wash it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Ezekiel 2:8; John 15:14; Proverbs 3:5"> So I went and bought a loincloth as the Lord had instructed me, and I put it on.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Jeremiah 13:8"> Then the Lord gave me another message:</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Micah 4:10; PSALMS 137:1; Jeremiah 51:63–64"> Take the loincloth that you bought and put on, and go immediately to the River Perath<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="The River Perath in Hebrew is usually translated as the Euphrates. However, this would mean Jeremiah going on two round trips of some 700 miles each. Some have suggested that the river in question was one with a similar name located near to Anathoth. Insofar as this is an acted parable it seems likely that others were supposed to see what happened and understand what it meant, so the actual Euphrates seems an unlikely location. However, the symbolic aspect must be included since the invaders would be coming from Babylon on the Euphrates.">a</a></sup>and hide it there in a hole among the rocks.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Exodus 40:16; Exodus 39:42–43; 2 Timothy 2:3"> So I went and hid it at the River Perath, as the Lord had told me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> A long time later the Lord told me, Go to Perath, and get the loincloth that I ordered you to hide there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Romans 3:12; Isaiah 64:6; Jeremiah 13:10"> I went to Perath and dug up the loincloth, and removed it from where I'd hidden it. Obviously it was ruined—completely useless.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> Then a message from the Lord came to me:</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Leviticus 26:19; Jeremiah 13:15–17; Zephaniah 3:11"> This is what the Lord says: I will ruin the arrogance of Judah and the great arrogance of Jerusalem in exactly the same way.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Jeremiah 16:12; Jeremiah 9:14; 2 Chronicles 36:15–16"> These evil people refuse to listen to what I tell them. They follow their own stubborn and evil thinking and run off to worship other gods—they will be like this loincloth, completely useless.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Exodus 19:5–6; Jeremiah 33:9; PSALMS 81:11"> In the same way that a loincloth holds tightly to the body, so I made all the people of Israel and Judah hold tightly to me, declares the Lord. Then they could have been my people, representing me, giving me honor and praise. But they refused to listen.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Ezekiel 24:19"> So tell them this is what the Lord, the God of Israel, says: Every wine jar shall be filled with wine. When they reply, “Don't we know that already? Of course every wine jar should be filled with wine!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Isaiah 63:6; PSALMS 75:8; PSALMS 60:3"> then tell them that this is what the Lord says: I'm going to make everyone who lives in this land drunk—the kings sitting on David's throne, the priests, the prophets, and all the people of Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Deuteronomy 29:20; PSALMS 2:9; Jeremiah 19:9–11"> I'm going to smash them against each other like wine jars,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Like wine jars”: supplied for clarity.">b</a></sup>both parents and children, declares the Lord. I won't let any mercy or pity or compassion stop me from destroying them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Isaiah 28:14–22; Revelation 2:29; Acts 4:19–20"> Listen and pay attention. Don't be arrogant, for the Lord has spoken.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Isaiah 59:9; Isaiah 8:22; Isaiah 5:30"> Honor the Lord your God before he brings the darkness, before you trip and fall in the twilight on the mountains. You long for light to come, but he sends only gloom and complete darkness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Malachi 2:2; Jeremiah 9:1; Jeremiah 14:17"> But if you refuse to listen, I will weep secretly inside because of your pride. My tears pour down because the Lord's flock has been captured.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Jeremiah 22:26; 2 Kings 24:12; 2 Kings 24:15"> Tell the king and the queen mother: Get down from your thrones, because your splendid crowns have fallen from your heads.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:27; Jeremiah 39:9; Deuteronomy 28:52"> The towns in the Negev are surrounded; no one can get through to them. The whole of Judah has been taken away into exile, everyone has been exiled.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Jeremiah 6:22; Jeremiah 1:14; Habakkuk 1:6"> Look up and you'll see the invaders coming from the north. Where is the flock that was given to you to look after? Where are the sheep you were so proud of?</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Isaiah 13:8; Jeremiah 4:31; Jeremiah 5:31"> What are you going to say when he puts your enemies in charge of you, people you once counted as your friends? Won't you suffer pains like a woman in labor?</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Nahum 3:5; Jeremiah 16:10–11; Jeremiah 5:19"> If you say to yourself, Why has this happened to me? it's because you have been so wicked. That's why your skirts were stripped off and you were raped.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Jeremiah 2:22; Jeremiah 9:5; Matthew 19:24–28"> Can Ethiopians change the color of their skin? Can a leopard change its spots? In the same way you can't change and do good because you're so used to doing evil.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="PSALMS 1:4; Leviticus 26:33; Ezekiel 5:12"> I'm going to scatter you like chaff blown away by the desert wind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Job 20:29; Jeremiah 2:32; PSALMS 9:17"> This is what's going to happen to you; this is what I have decided to do to you, declares the Lord, because you have forgotten me and believed in lies.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Lamentations 1:8; Hosea 2:10; Ezekiel 23:29"> I will pull your skirts up over your face, so you will be seen naked and ashamed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Hosea 8:5; Ezekiel 6:13; Ezekiel 24:13"> I watched your acts of adultery and lust, how you prostituted yourselves shamelessly, worshiping idols on the hills and in the fields. Yes, I saw the disgusting things you did. Disaster is coming to you, Jerusalem! How long are you going to remain unclean?</span>

## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">13:4</span> The River Perath in Hebrew is usually translated as the Euphrates. However, this would mean Jeremiah going on two round trips of some 700 miles each. Some have suggested that the river in question was one with a similar name located near to Anathoth. Insofar as this is an acted parable it seems likely that others were supposed to see what happened and understand what it meant, so the actual Euphrates seems an unlikely location. However, the symbolic aspect must be included since the invaders would be coming from Babylon on the Euphrates.</li>
