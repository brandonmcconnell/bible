<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Ezra 2:37–38; 1 Chronicles 24:14; 2 Kings 25:18"> Pashhur, son of Immer, was a priest and the official<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Probably in charge of the Temple guards, and clearly not the high priest.">a</a></sup>in charge of the Lord's Temple. When he heard Jeremiah prophesying these things,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Jeremiah 37:13; Zechariah 14:10; Jeremiah 1:19"> he beat Jeremiah the prophet and had him put in the stocks at the Upper Benjamin Gate, near to the Lord's Temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Jeremiah 20:10; Jeremiah 46:5; Lamentations 2:22"> The following day, when Pashhur had Jeremiah released from the stocks, Jeremiah told him, “The Lord doesn't call you Pashhur (tear in pieces), but Magor-missabib (terror is everywhere).</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Jeremiah 29:21; Jeremiah 25:9; Jeremiah 21:4–10"> For this is what the Lord says: I'm going to make you terrified—you and all those you love. Enemies will kill them as you watch. I will hand Judah over to the king of Babylon. He will kill some, and the rest he will take away into exile in Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Jeremiah 15:13; Ezekiel 22:25; Lamentations 1:10"> I'm going to give it all away. All the wealth of this city, all the results of hard work, all the valuables, all the crown jewels of Judah's kings—I'm going to hand it over to their enemies, who will take it as plunder and carry it off to Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Jeremiah 14:14–15; Lamentations 2:14; Ezekiel 22:28"> You, Pashhur, and everyone who lives with you, will go into captivity. You will go to Babylon. You will die there and be buried—you and all those you love, those to whom you prophesied lies.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Hosea 9:7; Jeremiah 20:9; Lamentations 3:14"> You fooled me, Lord, and I let myself be fooled!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Jeremiah is expressing his fear that his work on behalf of the Lord has been a failure.">b</a></sup>You're stronger than me—you've won! I've become a joke people laugh at all day long. Everyone is mocking me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Chronicles 36:16; Jeremiah 6:10; Jeremiah 5:15–17"> This is because every time I open my mouth I have to shout out warnings of violence and destruction. The Lord's message has become the reason people criticize and ridicule me the whole time.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="PSALMS 39:3; Job 32:18–20; Acts 4:20"> If I tell myself, “I won't talk about him anymore, I won't even mention his name,” then his message is like a fire trapped within me, burning me from the inside out. I'm getting tired of holding it in. I just can't win.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="PSALMS 41:9; PSALMS 31:13; Isaiah 29:21"> I've heard many people gossiping: “He's the one who says, ‘Terror is everywhere!’ He has to be reported! Report what he's doing!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="The idea is that Jeremiah is creating panic and should be reported to the country's leaders.">c</a></sup>All my good friends are waiting for me to slip up. “Maybe he'll make a mistake so we can defeat him and get our revenge on him,” they say.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Jeremiah 1:19; Jeremiah 1:8; Jeremiah 15:20"> But the Lord stands beside me like a powerful warrior. So those who are attacking me will fall. They won't win! Because they're not successful they will be totally disgraced. Their shame won't ever be forgotten.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="PSALMS 62:8; Jeremiah 11:20; PSALMS 54:7"> Lord Almighty, you know without question who is living right. You examine people's thoughts and feelings. So let me see your punishment fall on them, because I've trusted you to judge my case.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Isaiah 25:4; PSALMS 35:9–11; PSALMS 69:33"> Sing to the Lord! Praise the Lord! For he saves the poor from the power of the wicked.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Jeremiah 15:10; Job 3:3–16"> May the day I was born be cursed! May the day my mother gave birth to me never be blessed!</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Genesis 21:5–6; Luke 1:14; Jeremiah 1:5"> May the man be cursed who brought my father the news that made him really happy, saying, “You have a son.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Jeremiah 18:22; Jeremiah 48:3–4; Deuteronomy 29:23"> May that man be like the towns that the Lord destroyed without mercy. May he hear shouts of alarm in the morning and war cries at noon,</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Job 10:18–19; Job 3:10–11; Ecclesiastes 6:3"> because he should have killed me in the womb so that my mother would have been my grave, remaining pregnant forever.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Job 3:20; Lamentations 3:1; 1 Peter 4:14–16"> Why was I born only to see trouble and sadness, and to end my life in shame?</span>
</div>
