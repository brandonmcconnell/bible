<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Chronicles 3:11; 2 Kings 11:21; 2 Kings 11:1–4"> Joash<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Here and elsewhere spelled “Jehoash.”">a</a></sup>became king in the seventh year of the reign of Jehu, and he reigned in Jerusalem for forty years. His mother's name was Zibiah of Beersheba.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Chronicles 24:17–22; 2 Chronicles 26:4; 2 Chronicles 25:2"> Joash did what was right in the Lord's sight during the years that Jehoiada the priest advised him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Kings 14:4; 1 Kings 22:43; 1 Kings 15:14"> Even so, the high places were not removed—the people went on sacrificing and presenting burnt offerings at these places.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Kings 22:4; Exodus 35:5; 1 Chronicles 29:3–9"> Joash told the priests, “Collect together all the money that is brought as holy offerings to the Lord's Temple, whether the census money, the money from individual vows, or the money brought as a voluntary donation to the Lord's Temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Isaiah 58:12; 1 Kings 11:27; 2 Kings 22:5–6"> Let each priest receive the money from those who give, and use it to repair whatever damage is discovered in the Temple.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Chronicles 29:34; 1 Peter 5:2; Isaiah 56:10–12"> But by the twenty-third year of the reign of Joash, the priests still had not repaired the damage to the Temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Chronicles 24:16; 2 Chronicles 24:5–14; 2 Kings 11:4"> So King Joash called together Jehoiada and the other priests and asked, “Why haven't you repaired the damage in the Temple? Don't use any more money you're given for yourselves, instead hand it over to others to repair the Temple.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> The priests agreed not to receive any more money from the people, and that they wouldn't carry out the repairs to the Temple themselves.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Mark 12:41; PSALMS 84:10; Jeremiah 35:4"> Jehoiada the priest took a large wooden box, cut a hole in its lid, and placed it on the right side of the altar next to the entrance to the Lord's Temple. There the priests who guarded the doorway put all the money brought into the Lord's Temple into the collection box.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:17; 2 Kings 22:12; 2 Kings 19:2"> Whenever they saw there was a lot of money in the box, the king's secretary and the high priest would come, count the money brought into the Lord's Temple, and put it into bags.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Kings 22:5–6; 2 Chronicles 24:11–12; 2 Chronicles 34:9–11"> Then they weighed out the money and gave it to the supervisors of the work on the Lord's Temple. They paid the ones doing the work—the carpenters, builders,</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Kings 22:5–6; 1 Kings 5:17–18; Ezra 3:7"> masons, and stonecutters. They also bought the timber and blocks of cut stone needed for the repair of the Lord's Temple, and paid all the other costs of restoring the Temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Chronicles 24:14; 1 Kings 7:48–50; Ezra 1:9–11"> However, the money collected for the Lord's Temple was not used for making silver basins, lamp trimmers, bowls, trumpets, or any items of gold or silver for the Lord's Temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> It was used to pay the workers doing the repairs to the Lord's Temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="2 Kings 22:7; 3 John 1:5; 1 Corinthians 4:2–3"> No accounts were demanded from the men who received the money to pay the workers because they did everything honestly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Leviticus 7:7; Leviticus 5:15–18; Leviticus 4:24"> The money from the guilt offerings and sin offerings was not collected for the Lord's Temple because it belonged to the priests.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Chronicles 24:23–24; Luke 9:53; Jeremiah 42:15"> Around this time Hazael, king of Aram, went and attacked Gath, and captured it. Then he marched to attack Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Kings 15:18; 2 Kings 18:15–16; 2 Kings 12:4"> So King Joash of Judah took all the holy objects dedicated by his forefathers Jehoshaphat, Jehoram, and Ahaziah, the kings of Judah, along with all the items he had dedicated himself, and all the gold found in the treasuries of the Lord's Temple and the royal palace, and he sent everything to Hazael, king of Aram. So Hazael retreated from Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 Kings 14:29; 1 Kings 14:19; 1 Kings 11:41"> The rest of what happened in Joash's reign and all that he did are recorded in the Book of Chronicles of the Kings of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Chronicles 24:24–27; 2 Kings 14:5; 2 Samuel 5:9"> His officials plotted against him and murdered him at Beth Millo, on the road that goes down to Silla.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Kings 14:1; 2 Chronicles 24:26–27"> The officials who attacked and killed him were Jozacar, son of Shimeath, and Jehozabad, son of Shomer. They buried him with his forefathers in the City of David. His son Amaziah succeeded him as king.</span>
