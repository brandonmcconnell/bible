<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Luke 4:27; Romans 15:18; Proverbs 21:31"> Naaman, the king of Aram's army commander was considered a great man by his master and highly respected, for through him the Lord had made the Arameans victorious. He was a mighty warrior, but he was a leper.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Kings 6:23; 2 Kings 13:20; 1 Samuel 13:17–18"> Some Arameans had gone on a raid and had captured a young girl from the land of Israel. She had been made a servant to Naaman's wife.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Acts 26:29; Luke 17:12–14; Numbers 11:29"> She told her mistress, “If only my master would go and see the prophet who lives in Samaria. I'm sure he could cure him of his leprosy.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:26–27; John 4:28–29; John 1:42–46"> Naaman went to his master and explained what the Israelite girl had said.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Judges 14:12; 2 Kings 8:8–9; Numbers 22:17–18"> “You can go,” said the king of Aram, “and I will send a letter with you to the king of Israel.” So Naaman left. He took with him ten talents of silver, six thousand shekels of gold, and ten sets of clothing.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> The letter he took to the king of Israel read: “This letter accompanies my servant Naaman, sent to you so you can heal him of his leprosy.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Samuel 2:6; 1 Kings 20:7; Genesis 30:2"> When the king of Israel read the letter, he ripped his clothes in panic and said, “Does this man think I'm God, having power over life and death, sending me a leper to heal? Obviously he's just trying to invent an excuse to attack me, as anyone can see!”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Kings 18:36–37; Hosea 12:13; Ezekiel 2:5"> But when Elisha, the man of God, heard that the king of Israel had ripped his clothes in panic, he sent a message to the king, saying: “What did you rip your clothes for? Please send the man to me, so he will be convinced there is a prophet in Israel.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 16:37–39; 2 Kings 6:32; Isaiah 60:14"> So Naaman arrived with his horses and chariots and stood waiting at the door of Elisha's house.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="John 9:7; 1 Corinthians 6:11; Leviticus 14:7"> Elisha sent a messenger to him, saying, “Go and wash yourself seven times in the Jordan. Then your body will be healed, and you will be clean.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“Clean”: from an Israelite perspective, anyone with leprosy was unclean.">a</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Matthew 15:27; Luke 14:11; Matthew 8:8"> But Naaman got angry and left, saying, “I expected he would at least come out, stand there and invoke the name of the Lord his God, and wave his hand over where my leprosy is and heal it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Mark 1:9; 2 Kings 5:17; 2 Kings 2:14"> Aren't the rivers of Damascus, Abanah and Pharpar, better than any of these streams of Israel? Couldn't I have washed in them and been healed?” So he turned around and went off in a rage.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Kings 6:21; 2 Kings 13:14; 2 Kings 2:12"> But Naaman's officials went to him and said, “Sir, if the prophet had told you that you had to do something extraordinary, wouldn't you have done it? How much easier is it to do what he says, ‘Wash and you'll be healed’?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Job 33:25; Luke 4:27; Luke 5:13"> So Naaman went down and plunged himself underwater in the Jordan seven times, as the man of God had told him. His body was healed, his skin became like a baby's, and he was clean.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Daniel 2:47; Daniel 6:26–27; Joshua 2:9–11"> Then Naaman and his whole entourage went back to the man of God, stood before him, and Naaman announced, “Now I'm convinced that there is no God in all the world except in Israel. Please accept a gift from me, your servant.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Kings 5:26; 2 Kings 5:20; 2 Kings 3:14"> But Elisha replied, “As the Lord lives, the one I serve, I will not accept anything.” Even though Naaman tried to persuade him to take the gift, he refused.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Acts 26:18; 2 Kings 5:12; Romans 14:1"> So Naaman said, “If you won't, please let me, your servant, take back with me two mule loads of earth, for I will never again bring a burnt offering or make a sacrifice to any other god but the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="2 Kings 7:2; 2 Kings 7:17; 2 Kings 17:35"> In addition, may the Lord forgive me for doing this: When my master goes into the temple of Rimmon to worship there, and I assist him, and I bow down in the temple of Rimmon, may the Lord forgive me for doing so.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 Samuel 1:17; Mark 5:34; Exodus 4:18"> “Go in peace,” said Elisha, and Naaman left. But he had only gone a short way</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Kings 4:12; 2 Kings 4:31; 2 Kings 6:31"> when Gehazi, the servant of Elisha the man of God, said to himself, “Just look how my master has let this Naaman the Syrian off the hook by not accepting the gifts he brought! As the Lord lives, I'll run after him and get something from him.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Luke 7:6–7; 2 Kings 9:17–22; 2 Kings 4:26"> So Gehazi chased after Naaman. When Naaman saw him running after him, he jumped down from the chariot to meet him and asked, “Is everything all right?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Kings 5:5; Isaiah 59:3; Acts 5:3–4"> “Everything's fine,” Gehazi replied. “My master sent me to tell you, ‘I've just found out that two young men have arrived to see me from the sons of the prophets who live in the hill country of Ephraim. Please let them have a talent of silver and two sets of clothing.’”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 Kings 12:10; 1 Kings 20:7; 2 Kings 2:17"> But Naaman replied, “Please, take two talents.” He insisted that Gehazi accept them. Then he tied up two talents of silver in two bags as well as two sets of clothing. He gave them to two of his servants, who carried them for Gehazi.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Kings 21:16; Joshua 7:11–12; Joshua 7:1"> When Gehazi arrived at the hill fortress, he took the gifts from the servants and put them in the house. He told the men they could go, and they left.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Kings 5:22; Genesis 16:8; Proverbs 30:20"> When Gehazi got back and attended his master, Elisha asked him, “Where have you been, Gehazi?”<br />“Your servant hasn't been anywhere,” he replied.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Colossians 2:5; 1 Corinthians 5:3; 2 Kings 6:12"> But Elisha told him, “Didn't I see you in my mind's eye when the man got down from his chariot to meet you? Is this the time to take money and clothing, olive groves and vineyards, sheep and oxen, male and female servants?</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Exodus 4:6; Numbers 12:10; 2 Kings 15:5"> Consequently Naaman's leprosy will stick to you and your descendants forever!” As Gehazi left him, he had leprosy—he looked as white as snow.</span>
</div>
</div>
