<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Kings 11:21; 2 Kings 8:26; 2 Kings 11:4"> Jehoahaz, son of Jehu, became king of Israel in the twenty-third year of the reign of Joash, son of Ahaziah, king of Judah. He reigned in Samaria for seventeen years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Kings 12:26–33; 1 Kings 14:16; 2 Kings 10:29"> He did what was evil in the Lord's sight, and followed the sins that Jeroboam son of Nebat, had made Israel commit; he did not end them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Kings 19:17; 2 Kings 13:24–25; Judges 2:14"> So the Lord was angry with Israel, and he repeatedly allowed them to be defeated by Hazael, king of Aram, and his son Ben-hadad.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Kings 14:26; Exodus 3:7; PSALMS 78:34"> Jehoahaz asked the Lord for help, and the Lord responded to his request because he saw how badly the king of Aram was treating Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Nehemiah 9:27; 2 Kings 14:25; 2 Kings 14:27"> The Lord gave Israel someone who would save them so they no longer were under Aramean rule. Then the Israelites were able to go back to living in safety as before.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Kings 16:33; 2 Kings 13:2; Deuteronomy 7:5"> Even so they did not end the sins that the house of Jeroboam had made Israel commit—they continued to follow them. The Asherah idol still stood in Samaria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Amos 1:3; 2 Kings 10:32; 1 Kings 20:15"> All that was left of Jehoahaz's army were fifty horsemen, ten chariots, and ten thousand soldiers, for the king of Aram had destroyed the rest, turning them into dust like that when grain is threshed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Kings 11:4; 1 Kings 14:29; 1 Kings 14:31"> The rest of what happened in Jehoahaz's reign, all he did, and his great achievements are recorded in the Book of Chronicles of the Kings of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Kings 10:35; 2 Kings 13:13; 2 Kings 14:8"> Jehoahaz died and was buried in Samaria. His son Jehoash<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“Jehoash”: here spelled “Joash,” a variant spelling, but in this translation kept as Jehoash to differentiate from Joash, king of Judah.">a</a></sup>succeeded him as king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> Jehoash, son of Jehoahaz, became king of Israel in Samaria in the thirty-seventh year of the reign of King Joash of Judah, and he reigned for sixteen years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Kings 13:2; 2 Kings 10:29; 2 Kings 3:3"> He did what was evil in the Lord's sight and did not end all the sins that Jeroboam, son of Nebat, had made Israel commit—he continued to follow them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Kings 13:14–25; 2 Kings 14:8–16; 2 Chronicles 25:17–24"> The rest of what happened in Jehoash's reign, all he did, and his great achievements such as his war against Amaziah, king of Judah, are recorded in the Book of Chronicles of the Kings of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Kings 1:21; 2 Kings 14:28–29; 1 Kings 2:10"> Jehoash died, and Jeroboam sat on his throne.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Some believe this change in the formula describing royal succession suggests that Jeroboam was already reigning as co-regent with his father.">b</a></sup>Jehoash was buried in Samaria with the kings of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Kings 2:12; Philippians 2:26; Genesis 48:1"> Elisha had become ill with a disease that would eventually kill him. Jehoash, king of Israel, went to visit him, and wept over him, saying, “My father, my father, the chariots and the horsemen of Israel!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> Elisha told him, “Find a bow and some arrows.” So Jehoash found a bow and some arrows.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Kings 4:34; Genesis 49:24; PSALMS 144:1"> Then Elisha said to the king of Israel, “Pick up the bow.” So the king picked up the bow. Elisha placed his hands on the king's hands.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Kings 20:26; 2 Kings 5:10–14; 1 Corinthians 1:18"> “Open the east window,” he told him. So the king opened it and Elisha said, “Shoot!” So he fired an arrow. Then Elisha explained, “This is the Lord's victory arrow representing the arrow of victory over the Arameans. You will attack the Arameans in Aphek and finish them off.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="2 Kings 4:6; Exodus 17:11; Isaiah 20:2–4"> Then Elisha said, “Pick up the arrows!” So he picked them up. Elisha told the king of Israel, “Hit the ground with them!” He hit the ground three times, and then stopped.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Kings 13:25; Numbers 16:15; 2 Kings 4:40"> The man of God got angry with him, telling him, “You should have hit the ground five or six times. Then you would have attacked the Arameans until you had completely destroyed them. But now you will only attack the Arameans three times.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Kings 24:2; 2 Kings 3:7; 2 Kings 6:23"> Elisha died and was buried. Raiders from the country of Moab used to invade Israel every spring.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Ezekiel 37:1–10; Acts 19:12; John 5:28–29"> One time some Israelites were burying a man when suddenly they saw some raiders coming, so they quickly threw the man into Elisha's tomb. As soon as he touched Elisha's bones, the man came back to life and stood up.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Kings 8:12; 2 Kings 13:3–7; PSALMS 106:40–42"> Hazael, king of Aram, caused problems for Israel through all Jehoahaz's reign.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="PSALMS 105:8; 2 Kings 14:27; Micah 7:18–20"> But the Lord graciously helped them and was kind to them. He looked after them because of his agreement with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Even to this day he's been unwilling to destroy them or to throw them out of his presence.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="PSALMS 125:3; Luke 18:7"> When Hazael, king of Aram, died, his son Ben-hadad succeeded him as king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Kings 13:18–19; 2 Kings 10:32; 2 Kings 14:25"> Then Jehoash, son of Jehoahaz, recovered from Ben-hadad son of Hazael, the towns that Hazael had captured from his father Jehoahaz. Jehoash defeated Ben-hadad three times, and so recaptured the Israelite towns.</span>
