<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Isaiah 1:1; Isaiah 7:1; 2 Kings 15:38"> Ahaz, son of Jotham, became king of Judah in the seventeenth year of the reign of Pekah, son of Remaliah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Chronicles 28:1–4; 2 Kings 15:34; 1 Kings 11:4–8"> Ahaz was twenty when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem for sixteen years. But unlike David his forefather, he did not do what was right in the sight of the Lord his God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Deuteronomy 12:31; Leviticus 18:21; 2 Kings 21:2"> He followed the ways of the kings of Israel, and he even sacrificed his son in the fire, participating in the disgusting practices of the nations the Lord had driven out before the Israelites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Deuteronomy 12:2; 1 Kings 14:23; Jeremiah 17:2"> He sacrificed and presented burnt offerings on the high places and on the hills and under every green tree.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Kings 15:37; Isaiah 9:6–7; Isaiah 8:9–10"> Rezin, king of Aram, and Pekah, son of Remaliah, king of Israel, came and attacked Jerusalem. They besieged Ahaz but couldn't defeat him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Kings 14:22; Deuteronomy 2:8; 1 Kings 9:26"> This was when Rezin, king of Aram, recovered Elath for Edom.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Aram/Edom. These two words are similar in Hebrew. It seems unlikely that the Arameans would have wished to hold a town so far from their own territory in the land of the Edomites. Some versions replace “Rezin, king of Aram” with “the king of Edom,” but he is not previously mentioned. The conclusion is that it is unsure as to whether Elath was conquered by Arameans or Edomites; however it is sure that the town was lost to the people of Judah and was occupied by Edomites.">a</a></sup>He expelled the people of Judah, and sent Edomites to Elath, where they still live to this day.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Kings 15:29; Lamentations 4:17; Hosea 14:3"> Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath-pileser, king of Assyria, saying, “I'm your servant, and your son. Please come and rescue me from the kings of Aram and Israel who are attacking me.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Kings 12:17–18; 2 Chronicles 28:20–21; 2 Chronicles 16:2"> Ahaz took the silver and gold from the Lord's Temple and from the treasuries of the king's palace, and he sent it to the king of Assyria as a gift.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Amos 9:7; Isaiah 22:6; Amos 1:3–5"> The king of Assyria responded positively to him. He went and attacked Damascus, and captured it. He deported its inhabitants to Kir and executed Rezin.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Romans 12:2; Ezekiel 23:16–17; Isaiah 8:2"> King Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Tiglath-Pileser, king of Assyria. During his visit he saw an altar<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Clearly a pagan altar, probably Assyrian. It is likely that Tiglath-pileser required subservient kings to prove their loyalty to him, and this action of Ahaz would have demonstrated this.">b</a></sup>in Damascus, and he sent Uriah the priest a drawing of the altar, along with instructions how to build it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Hosea 4:6; 2 Chronicles 26:17–18; Hosea 5:11"> So Uriah the priest built an altar following all the instructions King Ahaz had sent from Damascus, completing it before King Ahaz returned.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Chronicles 26:16–19; 2 Chronicles 28:25; 1 Kings 13:1"> When the king came back from Damascus he saw the altar. He went over to it and made offerings on it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Leviticus 1:1–3"> He presented his burnt offering and his grain offering, he poured out his drink offering, and sprinkled the blood of his friendship offerings on it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:1; Exodus 40:6; Exodus 40:29"> He also moved the bronze altar that stood before the Lord from the front of the Temple, between the new altar and the Lord's Temple, and he placed it to the north of the new altar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Exodus 29:39–41; 1 Kings 8:64; 2 Chronicles 29:21–24"> Then King Ahaz ordered Uriah the priest: “Use this new important altar to offer the morning burnt offering, the evening grain offering, the king's burnt offering and grain offering, and the burnt offering of all the people, and their grain offerings and their drink offerings. Sprinkle on this altar the blood of all the burnt offerings and sacrifices. The old bronze altar I'll use for divination.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Acts 4:19; Acts 5:29; Jude 1:11"> Uriah the priest followed King Ahaz's orders.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:14–15; Jeremiah 52:20; 2 Chronicles 28:24"> King Ahaz also removed the frames of the movable carts, and also took out the bronze basin from each of them. He removed the Sea from the bronze bulls it rested on and placed it on a stone pedestal.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="2 Kings 11:5; 1 Kings 10:5; Ezekiel 46:2"> He took down the Sabbath canopy they had built in the Temple, as well as the king's outer entrance to the Lord's Temple. He did this to please the king of Assyria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Kings 15:36; 2 Chronicles 28:26; 2 Kings 20:20–21"> The rest of what happened in Ahaz's reign and all he did are recorded in the Book of Chronicles of the Kings of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Matthew 1:9; Isaiah 1:1; 1 Chronicles 3:13"> Ahaz died and was buried with his forefathers in the City of David. His son Hezekiah succeeded him as king.</span>

## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">16:6</span> Aram/Edom. These two words are similar in Hebrew. It seems unlikely that the Arameans would have wished to hold a town so far from their own territory in the land of the Edomites. Some versions replace “Rezin, king of Aram” with “the king of Edom,” but he is not previously mentioned. The conclusion is that it is unsure as to whether Elath was conquered by Arameans or Edomites; however it is sure that the town was lost to the people of Judah and was occupied by Edomites.</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">16:10</span> Clearly a pagan altar, probably Assyrian. It is likely that Tiglath-pileser required subservient kings to prove their loyalty to him, and this action of Ahaz would have demonstrated this.</li>
