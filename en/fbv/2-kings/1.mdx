<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:2; Numbers 24:7; 2 Kings 3:4–5"> After Ahab died, Moab rebelled against Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Kings 1:16; Matthew 10:25; Mark 3:22"> Ahaziah<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Ahab's son who had succeeded him as king of Israel.">a</a></sup>had fallen through the lattice-work<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Lattice-work: perhaps the shade used to cover a window.">b</a></sup>of his upper room in Samaria and had hurt himself badly. So he sent out messengers, telling them: “Go and ask Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron, whether I will get better from this injury.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Kings 17:1; Jonah 2:8; Acts 12:7–11"> But the angel of the Lord told Elijah the Tishbite, “Go and meet the messengers of the king of Samaria and ask them, ‘Is it because there's no God in Israel that you're going to ask advice from Baal-Zebub, the god of Ekron?’</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Genesis 3:4; Genesis 2:17; Ezekiel 18:4"> So this is the Lord's answer: ‘You will not leave the bed on which you are lying. You're definitely going to die.’” And Elijah left.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> The messengers returned to the king, and he asked them, “Why have you come back?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="PSALMS 16:4; 1 Chronicles 10:13–14; Isaiah 41:22–23"> “A man came and met us,” they replied. “He told us, ‘Go back to the king who sent you, and tell him, This is what the Lord says: Is it because there is no God in Israel that you are sending to ask advice from Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron? As a result you will not leave the bed on which you are lying. You're definitely going to die.’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Samuel 28:14; Judges 8:18"> “What was he like, this man who met you and told you all this?” the king asked.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Matthew 3:4; Zechariah 13:4; Mark 1:6"> “He was a hairy man wearing a leather belt around his waist,” they replied. “It's Elijah the Tishbite,” said the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Kings 6:13–14; Luke 6:11–12; Hebrews 11:36"> Then the king sent an army captain with fifty men to Elijah. The captain went up to Elijah, who was sitting on the top of a hill, and told him, “Man of God, the king orders you, ‘Come down!’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Luke 9:54; Daniel 3:25; 1 Kings 18:36–38"> Elijah replied to the captain, “If I am a man of God, then let fire fall from heaven and burn up you and your fifty men.” Fire fell from heaven and burned up the captain and his men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Matthew 2:16; Luke 22:63–64; Jeremiah 5:3"> So the king sent another captain with his fifty men to Elijah. The captain said to Elijah, “Man of God, the king orders you, ‘Come down immediately!’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Kings 1:9–10"> Elijah replied to the captain, “If I am a man of God, then let fire fall from heaven and burn up you and your fifty men.” Fire fell from heaven and burned up the captain and his men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Isaiah 1:5; 1 Samuel 26:21; Ecclesiastes 9:3"> So the king sent a third captain with his fifty men. The third captain went up, kneeled before Elijah, and pleaded with him, “Man of God, please value my life and the lives of these fifty men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Acts 20:24; PSALMS 49:8; Proverbs 6:26"> Yes, fire has fallen from heaven and burned up the first two captains of fifty, along with all their men. But now please value my life!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Isaiah 51:12; Ezekiel 2:6; Jeremiah 1:17"> Then the angel of the Lord told Elijah, “Go down with him. You don't have to be afraid of him.” So Elijah got up and went down with him to the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Kings 1:2–4; 1 Kings 22:28; Exodus 4:22–23"> Elijah told the king, “This is what the Lord says: ‘Is it because there's no God in Israel for you to consult that you have sent messengers to ask advice of Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron? As a result you will not leave the bed on which you are lying. You're definitely going to die.’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Kings 3:1; 1 Kings 22:51; 2 Kings 8:16–17"> Ahaziah died just as the Lord had said through Elijah. Because he had no son, Joram<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“Joram,” spelled here Jehoram. For purposes of clarity the king of Israel is called Joram in this translation, while the king of Judah is called Jehoram, though the names are basically the same and are used interchangeably in the Hebrew text.">c</a></sup>succeeded him as king in the second year of the reign of Jehoram, son of Jehoshaphat, king of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Kings 22:39; 1 Kings 14:19"> The rest of what happened in Ahaziah's reign and what he did are recorded in the Book of Chronicles of the Kings of Israel.</span>
</div>
