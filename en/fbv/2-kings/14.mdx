<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Kings 13:10; 2 Chronicles 25:1–4; 2 Kings 14:15"> Amaziah, son of Joash, became king of Judah in the second year of the reign of Jehoash, son of Jehoahaz, king of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2"> He was twenty-five when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem for twenty-nine years. His mother's name was Jehoaddan of Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Chronicles 25:2–3; Zechariah 1:4–6; 1 Kings 11:4"> He did what was right in the Lord's sight, but not in the same way as his forefather David had. He did everything just as his father Joash had done.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Kings 12:3; 2 Kings 16:4; 2 Kings 15:35"> But the high places were not removed. The people still were sacrificing and presenting burnt offerings in those places.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Chronicles 25:3–4; 2 Kings 12:20–21; Numbers 35:33"> Once he was secure on the throne he executed the officials who had murdered his father the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Deuteronomy 24:16; Ezekiel 18:20; Ezekiel 18:4"> But he did not execute the murderers' children, following the Lord's command in the law of Moses that “Fathers must not be put to death for their children's sins, and children must not be put to death for their father's sins. Everyone is to die for their own sin.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:13; 1 Chronicles 18:12; Joshua 15:38"> Amaziah killed ten thousand Edomites in the Valley of Salt. He attacked and captured Sela and renamed it Joktheel, which is what it is called to this very day.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Chronicles 25:17–24; 2 Samuel 2:14–17; 2 Kings 14:11"> Amaziah sent messengers to the king of Israel, Jehoash, son of Jehoahaz, son of Jehu, telling him, “Let's fight,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“Fight”: literally “meet,” but the context makes it clear that Amaziah was trying to provoke armed conflict.">a</a></sup>face to face!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Judges 9:8–15; 1 Kings 4:33; 2 Chronicles 33:11"> Jehoash, king of Israel, replied to Amaziah, king of Judah: “In Lebanon a thistle sent a message to a cedar, saying, ‘Give your daughter as a wife to my son.’ But a wild animal of Lebanon came by and trampled down the thistle.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Chronicles 26:16; Deuteronomy 8:14; 2 Chronicles 32:25"> You may indeed have defeated Edom. Now you've become arrogant. Stay home and enjoy your victory! Why provoke trouble that will bring you down, and Judah with you?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Joshua 19:38; 2 Chronicles 25:16; Joshua 15:10"> But Amaziah refused to listen, so Jehoash, king of Israel, came to attack him. He and Amaziah, king of Judah, met face to face at Beth Shemesh in Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Samuel 18:17; 1 Samuel 4:10; 1 Kings 22:36"> The army of Judah was defeated by Israel, and ran away home.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Nehemiah 8:16; Nehemiah 12:39; Zechariah 14:10"> Jehoash, king of Israel, captured Amaziah, king of Judah, the son of Joash, son of Ahaziah, at Beth Shemesh. Then Jehoash attacked Jerusalem and knocked down the city wall from the Ephraim Gate to the Corner Gate, about four hundred cubits in length.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="1 Kings 14:26; 1 Kings 7:51; 1 Kings 15:18"> He removed all the gold and silver, and all the items found in the Lord's Temple and in the treasuries of the royal palace, and also some hostages. Then he went back to Samaria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="2 Kings 13:12–13; 1 Kings 14:19–20; 2 Kings 10:34–35"> The rest of what happened in Jehoash's reign, all he did, and his great achievements and his war with Amaziah, king of Judah, are recorded in the Book of Chronicles of the Kings of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Kings 13:9; 2 Kings 13:13; 1 Kings 1:21"> Jehoash died and was buried in Samaria with the kings of Israel. His son Jeroboam succeeded him as king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Chronicles 25:25–28; 2 Kings 13:10; 2 Kings 14:23"> Amaziah, son of Joash, king of Judah, lived for fifteen more years after the death of Joash, son of Jehoahaz, king of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Kings 11:41; 1 Kings 14:29; 2 Kings 13:8"> The rest of the events that happened in Amaziah's reign are recorded in the Book of Chronicles of the Kings of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Joshua 10:31; 2 Kings 15:25; 2 Kings 12:20–21"> A conspiracy against Amaziah took place in Jerusalem, and he ran away to Lachish. But men were sent after him and they murdered him there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Kings 9:28; 2 Kings 12:21; 2 Chronicles 33:20"> They brought him back on horses and buried him in Jerusalem with his forefathers in the City of David.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Kings 21:24; Matthew 1:8–9; 2 Kings 15:13"> Then all the people of Judah made Amaziah's son Azariah king to replace his father. Azariah was sixteen years old.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Kings 16:6; 1 Kings 9:26; Deuteronomy 2:8"> Azariah recaptured Elath for Judah and rebuilt it after his father's death.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Hosea 1:1; Amos 7:9–11; Amos 1:1"> Jeroboam, son of Jehoash, became king of Israel in the fifteenth year of the reign of Amaziah, son of Joash, king of Judah. He reigned in Samaria for forty-one years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="2 Kings 13:6; 2 Kings 13:11; Genesis 38:7"> He did what was evil in the Lord's sight and did not end all the sins that Jeroboam, son of Nebat, had made Israel commit.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Deuteronomy 3:17; Matthew 12:39–40; Joshua 19:13"> He restored the border of Israel to where it had been, from Lebo-hamath as far as the Sea of the Arabah,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Sea of the Arabah”: the Arabah is the Jordan Valley, so this would refer to the Dead Sea.">b</a></sup>as the Lord, the God of Israel, had said through his servant Jonah, son of Amittai, the prophet, who came from Gath-hepher.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Deuteronomy 32:36; Exodus 3:7; 2 Kings 13:4"> The Lord had seen how badly the Israelites were suffering, both slave and free. No one was there to help Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 Kings 13:23; 2 Kings 13:5; Romans 11:2–12"> However, since the Lord had said that he would not wipe out Israel, he saved them through Jeroboam, son of Jehoash.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="1 Kings 11:24; 1 Chronicles 18:5–6; 2 Samuel 8:6"> The rest of what happened in Jeroboam's reign, all he did, his great achievements and the battles he fought, and how he recovered for Israel both Damascus and Hamath, are recorded in the Book of Chronicles of the Kings of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="2 Kings 15:8"> Jeroboam died and was buried with the kings of Israel. His son Zechariah succeeded him as king.</span>
