<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Chronicles 34:29–33; 2 Chronicles 30:2; Deuteronomy 31:28"> Then the king summoned all the elders of Judah and Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Kings 22:8; Deuteronomy 31:10–13; Revelation 20:12"> He went to the Lord's Temple with all the people of Judah and Jerusalem, together with the priests and the Levites, all the people from the least to the greatest, and he read to them the whole Book of the Agreement that had been discovered in the Lord's Temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Kings 11:14; 2 Kings 11:17; Deuteronomy 13:4"> The king stood by the pillar and made a solemn agreement before the Lord to follow him and to keep his commandments, laws, and regulations with total dedication, and to observe the requirements of the agreement as written in the book. All the people entered into the agreement.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Kings 21:3; 2 Kings 21:7; 2 Chronicles 33:3"> Then the king ordered Hilkiah the high priest, the priests of second rank, and the doorkeepers to remove from the Lord's Temple everything made for Baal, Asherah, and the worship of sun, moon, and stars. He burned them outside Jerusalem in the Kidron fields and took their ashes to Bethel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Jeremiah 44:17–19; 2 Kings 21:3–4; Hosea 10:5"> He also dismissed the priests appointed by the kings of Judah to present burnt offerings on the high places of the towns of Judah and in the places all around Jerusalem, those who had sacrificed to Baal, to the sun and moon, to the constellations, and to all the powers of heaven.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Chronicles 34:4; 2 Kings 23:15; 1 Kings 14:23"> He removed the Asherah pole from the Lord's Temple and took it to the Kidron Valley outside Jerusalem. He burned it there, ground it into dust, and threw its dust over the graves of the ordinary people.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Kings 15:12; Ezekiel 16:16; 1 Kings 14:24"> He also demolished the quarters of the cult prostitutes<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Referring to both males and females.">a</a></sup>that were in the Lord's Temple, where the women used to weave tapestries for the Asherah pole.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Kings 15:22; Joshua 21:17; 1 Kings 19:3"> Josiah brought to Jerusalem<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“To Jerusalem”: implied.">b</a></sup>all the priests from the towns of Judah and defiled the high places, from Geba to Beersheba, where the priests had sacrificed burnt offerings. He demolished the high places of the gates, near to the entrance of the gate of Joshua, the governor of the city, which was left of the town gate.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Ezekiel 44:10–14; 1 Samuel 2:36; Malachi 2:8–9"> Though the priests of the high places did not serve at the altar of the Lord in Jerusalem, they did eat unleavened bread with their brother priests.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Isaiah 30:33; Jeremiah 19:6; Leviticus 18:21"> He defiled the Topheth altar in the Valley of Ben-hinnom so that no one could sacrifice his son or daughter in the fire to Molech.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ezekiel 8:16; Deuteronomy 4:19; 2 Chronicles 14:5"> He removed the horses that the kings of Judah had dedicated to the sun from the entrance to the Lord's Temple. They were in the courtyard near the room of a eunuch named Nathan-melech. Josiah also burned the chariots dedicated to the sun.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Kings 21:5; Zephaniah 1:5; Jeremiah 19:13"> He demolished the altars that the kings of Judah had set up on the roof near the upper chamber of Ahaz, and the altars Manasseh had placed in the two courtyards of the Lord's Temple. The king smashed them to pieces and scattered them in the Kidron Valley.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Kings 11:7; 1 Kings 11:5; Numbers 21:29"> The king also defiled the high places to the east of Jerusalem, to the south of the Mount of Corruption, the places which King Solomon of Israel had built for Ashtoreth, the vile goddess of the Sidonians, for Chemosh, the vile god of the Moabites, and for Molech, the vile god of the Ammonites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Deuteronomy 7:5; Exodus 23:24; 2 Kings 23:16"> He smashed the sacred stone pillars to pieces, chopped down the Asherah poles, and covered the places with human bones.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="2 Kings 23:6; 1 Kings 14:16; 1 Kings 21:22"> He also demolished the altar at Bethel, the high place set up by Jeroboam, son of Nebat, who had made Israel sin. Then he burned the high place, ground it to dust, and burned the Asherah pole.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Kings 13:1–2; 1 Kings 13:32; John 10:35"> As Josiah looked around he saw some tombs there on the hill. He had the bones taken from the tombs, and he burned them on the altar to defile it, just as the Lord had said through the man of God who had prophesied these things.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Kings 13:1; 1 Kings 13:30–31"> Then he asked, “Whose gravestone is this that I see?”<br />“It's the tomb of the man of God who came from Judah and proclaimed exactly what you have done to the altar of Bethel,”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="See 1 Kings 13:2.">c</a></sup>the townspeople replied.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Kings 13:31; 1 Kings 13:1–22"> “Let him rest in peace,” said Josiah. “Don't anyone touch his bones.” So they left his bones undisturbed, together with the bones of the prophet who came from Samaria.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Chronicles 34:6–7; 2 Kings 17:9; Ezekiel 8:17–18"> Josiah destroyed, just as he did at Bethel, all the shrines of the high places in the towns of Samaria that the kings of Israel had built that had angered the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Kings 11:18; 2 Chronicles 34:5; Exodus 22:20"> Josiah slaughtered all the priests who were there at the high places on the altars, and burned human bones on them. Then he returned to Jerusalem.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Deuteronomy 16:1–8; Numbers 9:2–5; 2 Chronicles 35:1–19"> The king sent out an order to all the people, “Observe the Passover of the Lord your God, as it is written in this Book of the Agreement.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Chronicles 30:1–3; 2 Chronicles 30:13–20; 2 Chronicles 35:3–19"> Such a Passover as this had not been observed from the days of the judges who ruled Israel on through all the days of the kings of Israel and Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> But in the eighteenth year of King Josiah, this Passover was observed to honor the Lord in Jerusalem.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Leviticus 19:31; 2 Kings 21:6; Genesis 31:19"> In addition, Josiah got rid of the mediums and spiritists, the household gods and idols, and all the disgusting practices that were present in the land of Judah and in Jerusalem. He did this to fulfill the words of the law written in the book that Hilkiah the priest had found in the Lord's Temple.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Kings 18:5; Jeremiah 29:13; Deuteronomy 6:5"> Never before was there a king like him who committed himself to the Lord in all his thoughts and attitudes, and with all his strength, keeping all the Law of Moses. There was no king like him afterwards either.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Kings 21:11–13; 2 Kings 24:4; Jeremiah 3:7–10"> However, the Lord had not given up his furious hostility, which burned against Judah because of all that Manasseh had done to anger him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 Kings 18:11; 2 Kings 21:13; 2 Kings 21:4"> So the Lord announced, “I'm also going to banish Judah from my presence, just as I banished Israel. I will abandon this city that I have chosen, Jerusalem, and the Temple regarding which I said, My name will be there.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 Kings 20:20"> The rest of what happened in Josiah's reign, and all he did, are recorded in the Book of Chronicles of the Kings of Judah.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Jeremiah 46:2; 2 Chronicles 35:20–24; Judges 5:19"> While Josiah was still king, Pharaoh Neco, king of Egypt, led his army to help the king of Assyria at the Euphrates River. King Josiah took his army to fight him at Megiddo, but when Neco saw Josiah he killed him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="2 Kings 9:28; 2 Chronicles 36:1–4; 2 Kings 21:24"> His servants put his body in a chariot, brought him back from Megiddo to Jerusalem, and buried him in his own tomb. Then the people of the land chose Jehoahaz, son of Josiah, anointed him, and made him king in succession to his father.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="1 Chronicles 3:15; 2 Kings 24:18; Jeremiah 22:11"> Jehoahaz was twenty-three when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem for three months. His mother's name was Hamutal, daughter of Jeremiah. She came from Libnah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="2 Kings 21:2–7; 2 Kings 21:21–22"> He did what was evil in the Lord's sight, just as all his forefathers had done.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="1 Kings 8:65; 2 Kings 25:6; Jeremiah 39:5–6"> Pharaoh Neco imprisoned Jehoahaz at Riblah in the land of Hamath to stop him ruling in Jerusalem. He also imposed on Judah a tribute of one hundred talents of silver and a talent of gold.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Ezekiel 19:3–4; 2 Kings 24:17; 1 Chronicles 3:15"> Pharaoh Neco made Eliakim, son of Josiah, king in succession to his father Josiah, and he changed Eliakim's name to Jehoiakim. Neco took Jehoahaz to Egypt, where he died.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="2 Kings 23:33; 2 Kings 15:19–20"> Jehoiakim paid the silver and gold to Pharaoh Neco, but in order to meet Pharaoh's demand he taxed the land and required payment of the silver and the gold from the people, each in proportion to their wealth.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="2 Chronicles 36:5; Jeremiah 26:1; 1 Chronicles 3:15"> Jehoiakim was twenty-five when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem for eleven years. His mother's name was Zebidah, daughter of Pedaiah She came from Rumah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="2 Chronicles 33:22–23; Ezekiel 19:5–9; Jeremiah 36:31"> He did evil in the Lord's sight, just as his forefathers had done.</span>
</div>
</div>
