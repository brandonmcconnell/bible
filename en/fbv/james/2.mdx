<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Proverbs 24:23; James 2:9; Leviticus 19:15"> My friends, as trusting believers in our glorious Lord Jesus Christ, you must not show favoritism.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Luke 15:22; Esther 3:10; Esther 8:2"> Imagine that a man comes into your synagogue wearing gold rings and fine clothes, and then a poor man comes in dressed in rags.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Corinthians 8:9; James 2:6; Luke 7:44–46"> If you pay special attention to the well-dressed man, and say, “Please sit here in a seat of honor,” while you say to the poor man, “Stand over there, or sit on the floor by my feet,”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="John 7:24; Job 34:19; Matthew 7:1–5"> haven't you discriminated and judged with bad motives?</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Matthew 5:3; Luke 6:20; Revelation 2:9"> Listen, my dear friends: Didn't God choose those who the world considers poor to be rich in their trust in him, and to inherit the kingdom he promised to those who love him?</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Proverbs 17:5; 1 Corinthians 11:22; Proverbs 14:31"> But you've treated the poor shamefully. Isn't it the rich who oppress you and drag you before the courts?</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Philippians 2:9–11; Revelation 13:5–6; 1 Timothy 1:13"> Don't they insult the honorable name<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Usually understood to be the name of Jesus.">a</a></sup>of the one who called you and to whom you belong?</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Leviticus 19:18; Galatians 5:14; Matthew 22:39"> If you really observe the royal law of Scripture: “You shall love your neighbor as yourself,”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Quoting Leviticus 19:18.">b</a></sup>then you do well.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Leviticus 19:15; James 2:1–4; 1 John 3:4"> But if you show favoritism, you're sinning. The law convicts you as guilty of breaking it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Galatians 3:10; Matthew 5:18–19; Deuteronomy 27:26"> Someone who observes everything in the law but fails in just one part is guilty of breaking it all.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Exodus 20:13–14; Matthew 5:21–28; Deuteronomy 5:17–18"> God told you not to commit adultery, and he also told you not to kill. So if you don't commit adultery, but you do kill, you've become a law-breaker.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Quoting Exodus 20:13-14or Deuteronomy 5:17-18.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="James 1:25; James 2:8; 2 Peter 1:4–8"> You should speak and act as people who will be judged by the law of freedom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Matthew 5:7; Matthew 6:15; Proverbs 21:13"> Anyone who doesn't show mercy will be judged without mercy. Yet mercy wins out over judgment!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="The exact meaning of this verse is debated, but the fundamental point is to emphasize the merciful character of God.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Matthew 7:26–27; Matthew 7:21–23; James 1:22–25"> My friends, what's the good of someone saying they trust in God when they don't do what's good and right? Can such “trust” save them?</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Luke 3:11; Matthew 25:35–40; Isaiah 58:7"> If a brother or sister doesn't have clothes, or food for the day,</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Proverbs 3:27–28; 1 John 3:16–18; Matthew 25:42–45"> and you say to them, “Blessings on you! Stay warm and have a good meal!” and you don't provide what they need to survive, what's the good of that?</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="James 2:26; 2 Peter 1:5–9; James 2:19–20"> By itself even your trust-based faith in God is dead and worthless if you don't actually do what's good and right.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="James 3:13; James 2:14; Galatians 5:6"> Someone may argue, “You have your trust in God; I have my good deeds.” Well, show me your trust in God without good deeds, and I will show you my trust in God by my good deeds!</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Luke 4:34; Mark 1:24; Matthew 8:29"> You believe that God is one God? That's great—but demons believe in God too, and they're scared of him!</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="James 2:17; James 2:26; Proverbs 12:11"> You foolish people! Don't you know that trust in God without doing what's right is worthless?</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Genesis 22:16–18; Genesis 22:9–12; John 8:39"> Wasn't our father Abraham made right<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Or “shown to be right.”">e</a></sup>by what he did—by offering his son Isaac on the altar?</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 1:3; Hebrews 11:17–19; Galatians 5:6"> You notice that his trust in God worked together with what he did, and through what he did his trust in God was made complete.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Genesis 15:6; Isaiah 41:8; 2 Chronicles 20:7"> In this way scripture was fulfilled: “Abraham trusted God, and this was considered as him doing right,”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Quoting Genesis 15:6.">f</a></sup>and he was called the friend of God.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="See Isaiah 41:8.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="James 2:21–22; James 2:15–18; PSALMS 60:12"> You see that people are made right by what they do, and not just by trusting God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Hebrews 11:31; Joshua 6:22–25; Joshua 2:19–21"> In the same way, wasn't Rahab the prostitute made right by what she did when she looked after the messengers and then sent them away by a different road?</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="James 2:20; James 2:17; James 2:14"> Just as the body is dead without the spirit, trust in God is dead if you don't do what's right.</span>
</div>
