<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Isaiah 59:7–8; PSALMS 109:2–4; PSALMS 3:3–5"> Ahithophel said to Absalom, “Let me choose twelve thousand men and set off in pursuit of David tonight.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Samuel 16:14; 1 Kings 22:31; Zechariah 13:7"> I'll attack him while he is tired and weak. I'll catch him by surprise and all his men will run away. I'll only kill the king</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 5:3; Isaiah 48:22; Isaiah 57:21"> and bring everybody else back to you. When everybody returns apart from the one man you're after, the whole country will be at peace.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Samuel 18:20–21; Romans 1:32; 2 Chronicles 30:4"> This plan looked good to Absalom and to all the elders of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Samuel 15:32–37; 2 Samuel 16:16–19"> But then Absalom said, “Call in Hushai the Archite too, and let's hear what he's got to say as well.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> When Hushai came in, Absalom asked him, “Ahithophel has recommended this plan. Should we go ahead with it? If not, what's your suggestion?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Proverbs 31:8"> “For once Ahithophel's advice isn't good,” Hushai replied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Hosea 13:8; 1 Samuel 16:18; Hebrews 11:32–34"> “You know what your father and his men are like. They're great fighters, and now they're as furious as a she-bear robbed of her cubs. In any case, your father is experienced in military tactics, and he won't spend the night with his men.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Samuel 24:3; 1 Samuel 14:14–15; Judges 20:32–33"> Right now he's holed up in a cave or some place like that. If he attacks first and some of your men are killed, people who hear about it will say, ‘Absalom's men are being slaughtered.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Joshua 2:9–11; Exodus 15:15; Genesis 49:9"> Then even the bravest soldier who has the heart of a lion will be scared to death, because everyone in Israel knows that your father is a powerful man who has brave men with him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Genesis 22:17; Judges 20:1; PSALMS 9:16"> My recommendation is that you call up the entire Israelite army from Dan to Beersheba—an army as numerous as the sand on the seashore! Once they've assembled, then you yourself lead them into battle!</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Samuel 23:23; Obadiah 1:3; 1 Kings 20:10"> Then we'll attack David wherever he is, and we'll fall on him as dew falls on the ground. Neither he nor a single one of all the men with him will be left alive!</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Matthew 24:2; Micah 1:6"> If he tries to find protection in a town, all of Israel will bring ropes to that town, and we will pull it down into the valley so that not even a stone will be left.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Samuel 15:34; 1 Corinthians 1:19–20; Proverbs 19:21"> Absalom and all the Israelite leaders said, “Hushai the Archite's advice is better than Ahithophel's.” For the Lord had decided to block Ahithophel's good advice in order that he might bring disaster on Absalom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="2 Samuel 15:35–36"> Hushai spoke to Zadok and Abiathar, the priests, and told them, “Ahithophel has advised Absalom and the Israelite leaders to act in one way, but I have advised them to act in this different way.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Samuel 15:28; 1 Corinthians 15:54; 2 Corinthians 5:4"> So send a message quickly to David and tell him, ‘Don't wait and spend the night at the fords of the wilderness, but cross over immediately or the king and everybody with him will be destroyed.’”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“Destroyed”: literally, “swallowed up.”">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Samuel 15:36; Joshua 18:16; 2 Samuel 15:27"> Jonathan and Ahimaaz were staying at En-rogel because they couldn't be seen entering the city. A servant girl would come and tell them what was happening. Then they would go and let King David know.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="2 Samuel 3:16; 2 Samuel 16:5; 2 Samuel 19:16"> But a boy did see them and he told Absalom. So the two left immediately and went to the house of a man in the town of Bahurim. He had a well in his courtyard, and they climbed into it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Exodus 1:19; Joshua 2:4–24"> His wife took a cloth to cover the well and spread it out over the opening and then scattered grain over it. No one knew the men were there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Exodus 1:19; Joshua 2:22–23; 1 Samuel 19:12–17"> When Absalom's officers arrived they asked the woman, “Where are Ahimaaz and Jonathan?” “They crossed over the stream,” she replied. The men searched for them but didn't find them, so they went back to Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Samuel 17:15–16"> After Absalom's officers left, the two men climbed out of the well and rushed off to give the king their message. “Have everybody get up and cross the river right away, for Ahithophel's advice is to attack you immediately.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Matthew 10:16; John 18:9; Proverbs 27:12"> David and everybody with him got up and crossed the Jordan. By the time it got light there wasn't anybody who hadn't crossed over.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Matthew 27:5; 2 Kings 20:1; 2 Samuel 15:12"> When Ahithophel realized that his advice had been ignored, he saddled up his donkey and left for his home in the town where he lived. He put his affairs in order and then he hanged himself. He died and was buried in his father's tomb.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Genesis 32:2; 2 Samuel 2:8; Joshua 13:26"> David went on to Mahanaim, and Absalom crossed over the Jordan with the entire Israelite army.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="1 Chronicles 2:13; 2 Samuel 19:13; 2 Samuel 20:9–12"> Absalom had put Amasa in charge of the army to replace Joab. Amasa was the son of a man named Ithra, the Ishmaelite<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Ishmaelite”: following 1 Chronicles 2:17. The Hebrew here has “Israelite.”">b</a></sup>who lived with Abigail, the daughter of Nahash and sister of Zeruiah, Joab's mother.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Joshua 17:1; Deuteronomy 3:15; Numbers 32:1–42"> The Israelites under Absalom set up camp in the land of Gilead.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="1 Kings 2:7; 2 Samuel 10:1–2; 2 Samuel 9:4"> When David arrived at Mahanaim, he was welcomed by Shobi, son of Nahash, from Rabbah of the Ammonites, Machir, son of Ammiel, from Lo-debar, and Barzillai the Gileadite from Rogelim.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="1 Samuel 25:18; Obadiah 1:3; 2 Samuel 16:1–2"> They brought bedding, bowls, and clay jars, as well as wheat, barley, flour, roasted grain, beans, lentils,</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="2 Samuel 16:2; Isaiah 21:14; 2 Samuel 16:14"> honey, curds, sheep, and cheese made from cow's milk for David and the people with him to eat. For they said, “The people are hungry, tired, and thirsty from their time in the wilderness.”</span>
