<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Chronicles 18:1–17; 2 Samuel 7:9; 2 Samuel 21:15–22"> Sometime after this, David attacked and subdued the Philistines, taking Metheg-ammah<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="The meaning of this term is not known. It may be a place name. The parallel passage in Chronicles identifies “Gath and its nearby towns.” 1 Chronicles 18:1.">a</a></sup>from the them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Numbers 24:17; 1 Samuel 10:27; 2 Samuel 8:6"> David also defeated the Moabites. He made them lie down on the ground, and he measured them with a length of cord. He measured two lengths for those to be killed, and one cord length for those to be allowed to live. So he made them subject to him and required them to pay taxes.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Samuel 14:47; 2 Samuel 10:16; 2 Samuel 10:19"> David also defeated Hadadezer, son of Rehob, king of Zobah, as he tried to enforce his control along the Euphrates River.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Joshua 11:6; Joshua 11:9; 1 Chronicles 18:4"> David captured from him 1,000 chariots, 7,000 charioteers, and 20,000 foot soldiers. David hamstrung all the chariot horses—except he saved enough for 100 chariots.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Kings 11:23–25; Isaiah 7:8; Job 9:13"> When the Arameans of Damascus came to help King Hadadezer of Zobah, David killed twenty-two thousand of them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:14; 2 Samuel 7:9; PSALMS 18:34–46"> He placed garrisons in the Aramean kingdom with its capital in Damascus, and made the Arameans subject to him and required them to pay taxes. The Lord gave David victories wherever he went.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Chronicles 9:15–16; 1 Kings 14:26–27; 1 Kings 10:16–17"> David took the shields of gold that belonged to Hadadezer's officers and brought them to Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Chronicles 18:8; 1 Chronicles 22:16; Ezekiel 47:16"> King David also took a large quantity of bronze from Betah and Berothai, towns that had belonged to Hadadezer.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Kings 8:65; 2 Chronicles 8:4; 1 Chronicles 18:9"> When Tou, king of Hamath, learned that David had destroyed the entire army of Hadadezer, king of Zobah,</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Samuel 13:10; 1 Chronicles 18:10; PSALMS 129:8"> he sent his son Joram to David to make friends with him and to congratulate him on his victory in battle over Hadadezer. Tou and Hadadezer had often been at war. Joram brought all kinds of gifts of gold, silver, and bronze.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Kings 7:51; 1 Chronicles 29:2; 1 Chronicles 22:14–16"> King David dedicated these gifts to the Lord, along with the silver and gold he had taken from all the nations he had subdued:</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Samuel 10:14; 2 Samuel 8:2; 1 Chronicles 18:11"> Edom, Moab, the Ammonites, the Philistines, and Amalekites; as well as the plunder taken from Hadadezer, son of Rehob, king of Zobah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Kings 14:7; 2 Samuel 7:9; 1 Chronicles 18:12"> David also made a name for himself when he came back after defeating eighteen thousand Edomites<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="The Hebrew text actually refers to Arameans, but in context this must be a scribal error.">b</a></sup>in the Valley of Salt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Genesis 27:29; Genesis 25:23; 2 Samuel 8:6"> He placed garrisons all through Edom, and all the Edomites became subject to David. The Lord gave David victories wherever he went.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Amos 5:15; PSALMS 89:14; PSALMS 72:2"> David ruled over all Israel. He did what was fair and right for all his people.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Kings 4:3; 1 Chronicles 11:6; 2 Samuel 19:13"> Joab, son of Zeruiah, was the army commander, and Jehoshaphat, son of Ahilud, kept the official records.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Chronicles 6:8; 1 Chronicles 18:16; 1 Chronicles 24:3–4"> Zadok, son of Ahitub, and Ahimelech, son of Abiathar, were priests, while Seraiah was the secretary.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Samuel 30:14; 2 Samuel 20:7; 2 Samuel 20:23"> Benaiah, son of Jehoiada was in charge of the Cherethites and Pelethites;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“The Cherethites and Pelethites”: the king's bodyguard.">c</a></sup>and David's sons were priests.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“David's sons were priests”: clearly not being Levites, David's sons would not be priests in the sense of officiating in religious ceremonies. Some have suggested that as used here, the word means “administrators.” See the parallel passage in 1 Chronicles 18:17.">d</a></sup></span>
