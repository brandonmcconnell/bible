<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Kings 12:16; 2 Chronicles 10:16; Deuteronomy 13:13"> A rabble-rouser called Sheba, son of Bichri, from the tribe of Benjamin, happened to be there. He blew the ram's horn and shouted: “We have no interest in David, no commitment to Jesse's son. Israelites, let's all go home!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="John 6:66–68; Acts 11:23; 2 Samuel 19:15"> So all the men of Israel abandoned David to follow Sheba, son of Bichri. But the men of Judah accompanied their king all the way from the Jordan to Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Samuel 16:21–22; 2 Samuel 15:16; Genesis 40:3"> When David returned to his palace in Jerusalem, he took the ten concubines he had left to look after the palace<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="See 15:16.">a</a></sup>and put them in a house under guard. He took care of their needs but he didn't sleep with them. They were imprisoned until they died, living like widows.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Samuel 19:13; 2 Samuel 17:25; 1 Chronicles 2:17"> Then the king ordered Amasa, “Call up the army of Judah. Have them come to me within three days, and you come too.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Samuel 19:13; 1 Samuel 13:8"> Amasa called up the army of Judah, but he took longer than the time he was given.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Samuel 21:17; 2 Samuel 11:11; 1 Kings 1:33"> David then spoke to Abishai, saying, “Now Sheba the son of Bichri is going to cause us more trouble than Absalom did. Take the king's men and chase him down, or he will take over fortified towns and get away from us.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:18; 1 Kings 1:38; 2 Samuel 20:23"> So Joab's men, along with the Cherethites, the Pelethites,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“The Cherethites, the Pelethites”: David's personal bodyguard.">b</a></sup>and all the experienced fighters, marched out of Jerusalem to chase down Sheba, son of Bichri.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Samuel 3:30; 2 Samuel 2:13; 2 Samuel 20:4–5"> While they were at the large rock in Gibeon, Amasa caught up with them. Joab was dressed for battle. Over his clothes was a belt around his waist with a dagger in its sheath. As he moved forward, it fell out.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="The details of what is happening here are not clear. Some think Joab had a concealed dagger which fell out, perhaps just into his tunic. Others think he intentionally dropped his sword so he would appear to be unarmed, but that he had another weapon, a dagger, still in his belt.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Luke 22:47–48; Matthew 26:48–49; Micah 7:2"> “How are you doing, my brother?” Joab asked Amasa. Joab held Amasa by the beard with his right hand to kiss him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Samuel 2:23; 2 Samuel 3:27; Judges 3:21"> Amasa wasn't prepared for the dagger in Joab's left hand. Joab stabbed him in the belly and his intestines poured out onto the ground. Joab didn't need to stab him twice, because Amasa was already dead. Then Joab and his brother Abishai set off in pursuit of Sheba.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Samuel 20:13; 2 Samuel 20:21; 2 Kings 9:32"> One of Joab's men stood beside Amasa and called out, “If you're on Joab's side, and if you are on David's side, then follow Joab!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Proverbs 24:21–22; PSALMS 9:16; 2 Samuel 17:25"> But Amasa was there, lying in his blood in the middle of the main road. When the man saw that everybody was stopping to look, he pulled the body off the road into a field and threw a cloth over it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Samuel 6:12; Isaiah 7:3; 2 Kings 18:17"> Once Amasa's body was off the road, all the men followed Joab in pursuit of Sheba.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Kings 15:29; 1 Kings 15:20; 2 Chronicles 16:4"> In the meantime Sheba had gone around all the tribes of Israel<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Presumably seeking support for his rebellion.">d</a></sup>and eventually ended up in the town of Abel-beth-maacah. All the Bichrites<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Members of his own family group.">e</a></sup>gathered for battle and followed him into the town.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="2 Kings 19:32; Jeremiah 32:24; 1 Kings 15:20"> Joab's army came and besieged Sheba in Abel-beth-maacah. They built a siege ramp against the town's outer wall. While all of Joab's army was battering the wall to knock it down,</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Samuel 14:2; Ecclesiastes 9:14–18; 1 Samuel 25:32–33"> a wise woman from the town called out, “Listen! Please listen! Tell Joab, ‘Come over here so I can speak to you.’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Samuel 14:12; 1 Samuel 25:24"> He went over to her, and the woman asked, “Are you Joab?” “Yes, that's me,” he replied. “Please listen to what I, your servant, have to say,” she said. “I'm listening,” he replied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Deuteronomy 20:10–11"> Then the woman said, “In times gone by people used to say, ‘If you want advice, go to Abel,’ and that's how arguments were settled.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 Samuel 26:19; 2 Samuel 21:3; 2 Samuel 17:16"> I am one of the peaceful and faithful people of Israel. You're trying to destroy a town that's like a mother in Israel. Why do you want to tear down the Lord's possession?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Luke 10:29; Proverbs 28:13; Jeremiah 17:9"> “Certainly not!” Joab answered. “It's not what I want—to destroy or tear down this town!</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Samuel 26:9; Judges 2:9; 2 Samuel 17:2–3"> That's not the intention. But a man called Sheba, son of Bichri, from the hill country of Ephraim, has rebelled against the king, against David. Just hand over this one man and I will withdraw from the town.” “Fine,” the woman replied, “his head will be thrown over the wall to you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Samuel 20:1; Ecclesiastes 7:19; 2 Samuel 11:6–21"> The woman went and talked with everyone about her wise plan. So they cut off the head of Sheba and threw it to Joab. Then Joab blew the ram's horn to sound the retreat, and all his men left the town and went home. Joab returned to the king in Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:16–18; 1 Chronicles 18:15–17; 1 Kings 4:3–6"> Joab commanded the whole army of Israel. Benaiah, son of Jehoiada, was in charge of the Cherethites and Pelethites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Kings 4:3; 1 Kings 12:18; 1 Kings 4:6"> Adoniram was in charge of the labor force. Jehoshaphat, son of Ahilud, kept the official records.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="1 Kings 4:4; 2 Samuel 8:17; 1 Chronicles 18:16"> Sheva was the secretary. Zadok and Abiathar were the priests,</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Samuel 23:38; Exodus 2:14; Genesis 41:45"> and Ira the Jairite was David's priest.</span>
