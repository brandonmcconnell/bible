<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Samuel 11:1–3; Judges 11:12–28; Judges 10:7–9"> Sometime after this, Nahash, the Ammonite king died and his son Hanun succeeded him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Samuel 11:1; Deuteronomy 23:3–6; Nehemiah 13:1–3"> David said, “I will be kind to Hanun, son of Nahash, just as his father was kind to me.” So David sent representatives to take his condolences to Hanun regarding his father. But when they arrived in the country of the Ammonites,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Genesis 42:16; Genesis 42:9; 1 Corinthians 13:5"> the Ammonite military leaders said to Hanun their king, “Do you really believe David sent condolences to you out of respect for your father? Isn't it more likely that David sent his representatives to scout out the city, spy on it, and then conquer it?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Isaiah 20:4; Jeremiah 41:5; 1 Chronicles 19:3–4"> So Hanun had David's representatives detained, shaved off half of each man's beard, cut off their clothes at the buttocks, and then sent them back home.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Chronicles 19:5; 1 Kings 16:34; Joshua 6:24–26"> When David was told about this, he sent messengers to meet them, because they were very embarrassed. The king instructed them, “Stay in Jericho until your beards have re-grown, and then you can return.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:3; 2 Samuel 8:5; Genesis 34:30"> When the Ammonites realized they had become like a bad smell to David, they sent a request to the Arameans and hired twenty thousand of their foot soldiers from Beth Rehob and Zobah, as well as one thousand men from the king of Maakah, and also twelve thousand men from Tob.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Samuel 23:8–39; 1 Chronicles 19:8–19"> When David learned of this, he sent Joab and the entire army to confront them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Samuel 10:6; Joshua 19:28; Numbers 13:21"> The Ammonites set up their battle lines near the entrance to their town gate, while the Arameans of Zobah and Rehob and the men of Tob and Maacah took up positions by themselves in the open fields.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Judges 20:42–43; Joshua 8:21–22"> Joab realized he would have to fight both in front of him and behind him, so he chose some of Israel's best troops and he took charge of them to lead the attack against the Arameans.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> He put the rest of the army under the command of Abishai, his brother. They were to attack the Ammonites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Galatians 6:2; Nehemiah 4:20; Philippians 1:27–28"> Joab told him, “If the Arameans are stronger than me, you come and help me. If the Ammonites are stronger than you, I'll come and help you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Corinthians 16:13; Deuteronomy 31:6; 1 Samuel 3:18"> Be brave, and fight your best for our people and the towns of our God. May the Lord do what he sees as good!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Kings 20:13–21; 1 Kings 20:28–30; 1 Chronicles 19:14–15"> Joab attacked the Arameans with his forces and they ran away from him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> When the Ammonites saw that the Arameans had run away, they also ran away from Abishai, and retreated into the town. So Joab went back to Jerusalem after fighting the Ammonites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Zechariah 14:2–3; Micah 4:11–12; Isaiah 8:9–10"> As soon as the Arameans saw they had been defeated by the Israelites they reassembled their forces.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Chronicles 19:16; 2 Samuel 8:3–8; 1 Chronicles 18:3"> Hadadezer sent for more Arameans to be brought from beyond the Euphrates River. They arrived in Helam under the leadership of Shobach, commander of Hadadezer's army.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Chronicles 19:17"> When this was reported to David, he assembled all Israel together. He crossed the Jordan and advanced on Helam. The Arameans positioned themselves in battle line against David and fought him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Chronicles 19:18; 2 Samuel 8:4; PSALMS 46:11"> But the Aramean army ran away from the Israelites, and David killed 700 charioteers and 40,000 infantry. He also attacked Shobach, their army commander, and he died there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:6; Revelation 18:10; PSALMS 48:4–5"> When all the kings allied with Hadadezer realized that they had been defeated by Israel, they made peace with David and became subject to him. As a result, the Arameans were afraid to help the Ammonites any more.</span>
