<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Kings 1:5; 2 Samuel 12:11; 1 Samuel 8:11"> Sometime later, Absalom got himself a chariot with horses, and fifty men as bodyguards to run ahead of him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Exodus 18:14; Ruth 4:1; Job 24:14"> He used to get up early and stand by the main road that led to the city gate. When people brought a case to the king for his decision, Absalom would call out and ask them, “What town are you from?” If they replied, “Your servant is from this particular tribe of Israel,”</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:15; Ezekiel 22:7; 1 Peter 2:17"> Absalom would tell them, “Look, you're in the right and you've got a good case. It's such a shame there's no one from the king to hear you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Judges 9:29; Judges 9:1–5; Proverbs 27:2"> Then he would say, “If only there was someone to appoint me as judge for the country. Then everyone could come to me with their case or complaint, and I would give them justice.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Samuel 14:33; PSALMS 55:21; Proverbs 26:25"> When anyone came to bow down before him, Absalom would stop them by reaching out his hand, taking hold of him, and kissing him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Romans 16:18; 2 Peter 2:3; Proverbs 11:9"> This is how Absalom treated all the Israelites who came to the king for his judgment. So he captured the loyalty of the men of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Isaiah 58:4; 1 Samuel 16:13; 1 Samuel 16:1–2"> Four<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Septuagint and Syriac reading. The Hebrew has “forty.”">a</a></sup>years later Absalom asked the king, “Please let me go to Hebron to fulfill a promise I made to the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Genesis 28:20–21; 2 Samuel 13:37–38; 1 Samuel 1:11"> For I, your servant, made this promise while living at Geshur in Aram, saying: ‘If the Lord does bring me back to Jerusalem, I will worship the Lord in Hebron.’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> “Go in peace,” said the king. So Absalom went to Hebron.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Chronicles 12:38; 2 Samuel 5:5; 2 Samuel 3:2–3"> Then Absalom sent his accomplices among all the tribes of Israel, saying, “When you hear the sound of the ram's horn, shout, ‘Absalom is king at Hebron!’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Samuel 9:13; 1 Samuel 22:15; Romans 16:18–19"> Two hundred men from Jerusalem went with Absalom. They had been invited and went in all innocence, because they didn't know anything about what was planned.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="PSALMS 41:9; 2 Samuel 15:31; Joshua 15:51"> While Absalom was offering sacrifices, he sent for Ahithophel the Gilonite, David's advisor, asking him to come from Giloh, the town where he lived. The conspiracy grew stronger, and Absalom's followers went on increasing.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Judges 9:3; Matthew 27:22; 2 Samuel 15:6"> A messenger came to tell David, “Absalom has the loyalty of the men of Israel.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Samuel 19:9; Ezekiel 46:18; PSALMS 55:3–11"> David said to all the officials with him in Jerusalem, “Quick! Let's go! Otherwise we won't be able to get away from Absalom! We must leave immediately, or he will soon catch up with us, attack us, and kill the people here in the city.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="John 6:66–69; Proverbs 18:24; Luke 22:28–29"> “Whatever Your Majesty decides, we'll do what you want,” the king's servants replied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Samuel 16:21–22; 2 Samuel 20:3; PSALMS 3:1"> The king set off with his whole household following him, but he left behind ten concubines to look after the palace.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="PSALMS 3:1–2; Ecclesiastes 10:7; PSALMS 66:12"> The king left with all his soldiers following him. He stopped at the last house,</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:18; 1 Samuel 30:14; 2 Samuel 20:7"> and all his men marched past him, including all the Cherethites and Pelethites, and six hundred Gittites who had come with him from Gath.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Samuel 18:2; Ruth 1:11–13"> The king said to Ittai the Gittite, “Why are you coming with us too? Go back and stay with the new king, because you are a foreigner and an exile a long way from home.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Samuel 23:13; 2 Samuel 2:6; John 1:17"> You only just got here, so why should I make you wander around with us now when I don't even know where I am going? Go back and take your men with you. May the Lord show you kindness and faithfulness.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Ruth 1:16–17; Proverbs 17:17; 2 Kings 4:30"> But Ittai answered the king, “As the Lord lives, and as Your Majesty lives, wherever Your Majesty may be, whether dead or alive, that's where your servant will be!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> “Go ahead, march on!” David replied. Ittai the Gittite marched past with all his men and all the families that were with him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 Samuel 16:2; 1 Kings 2:37; John 18:1"> All the people in the countryside were crying aloud as everyone with David passed by. They crossed the Kidron Valley with the king on the way toward the wilderness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Numbers 4:15; 2 Samuel 8:17; 2 Samuel 20:25"> Zadok was there too, and all the Levites were with him, carrying the Ark of God's Agreement. They set down the Ark of God, and Abiathar offered sacrifices until everyone had left the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Jeremiah 25:30; PSALMS 26:8; PSALMS 43:3–4"> Then the king told Zadok, “Take the Ark of God back to the city. If I find the Lord approves of me, he will bring me back and let me see both the Ark and his Tent again.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="1 Samuel 3:18; 2 Samuel 22:20; 1 Kings 10:9"> But if he says, ‘I'm not happy with you,’ then here I stand. Let him do to me whatever he thinks best.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="1 Samuel 9:9; 2 Samuel 17:17; 2 Samuel 24:11"> The king also told Zadok the priest, “You understand the situation, don't you?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“You understand the situation, don't you?” This could be translated as “You see?” or “Aren't you a seer?” The implication is that David is trusting Zadok to let him know what is happening in Jerusalem.">b</a></sup>Go back to the city safely with your son Ahimaaz, and also Jonathan, son of Abiathar. You and Abiathar take both of your sons back with you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 Samuel 17:16; 2 Samuel 15:23; 2 Samuel 17:1"> I'll wait at the fords of the wilderness until I hear from you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29"> Zadok and Abiathar took the Ark of God back to Jerusalem and remained there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Esther 6:12; 2 Samuel 19:4; Luke 19:41"> David went on his way up the Mount of Olives, weeping as he did so. He had his head covered, and walked barefoot. All the people with him covered their heads, weeping as they went along.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 Samuel 17:14; 2 Samuel 17:23; James 3:15"> David was told, “Ahithophel<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Ahithophel, David's advisor, was Eliam's father according to 23:34, who in turn was the father of Bathsheba (11:3). This would surely have been a factor in Ahithophel joining Absalom's rebellion.">c</a></sup>is one of the people conspiring with Absalom.” So David prayed, “Lord, please make Ahithophel's advice worthless.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Joshua 16:2; 2 Samuel 1:2; PSALMS 3:3–5"> When David arrived at the top of the Mount of Olives, where people worshiped God, there to meet him was Hushai the Archite, with his robe torn and with dust on his head.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="2 Samuel 19:35"> David told him, “If you come with me, you'll only be a burden to me,</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Joshua 8:2; 2 Samuel 16:16–19; Matthew 10:16"> but if you go back to the city and tell Absalom, ‘I will be your servant, Your Majesty! Formerly I worked for your father, but now I'll work for you,’ then you can block Ahithophel's advice for me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="2 Samuel 17:15–16"> Zadok and Abiathar, the priests, will be there too. Tell them everything you hear in the king's palace.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="2 Samuel 17:17; 2 Samuel 15:27; 2 Samuel 18:19–33"> Their two sons, Ahimaaz and Jonathan, are there with them. Send them to me so they can tell me everything you hear.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="2 Samuel 16:15–16; 1 Chronicles 27:33"> David's friend Hushai arrived back in Jerusalem at the same time Absalom was entering the city.</span>
