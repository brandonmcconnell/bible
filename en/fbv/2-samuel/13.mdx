<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Chronicles 3:9; 2 Samuel 3:2–3; 2 Samuel 13:15"> David's son Absalom had a beautiful sister named Tamar, and another of David's sons, Amnon, fell in love with her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Corinthians 7:10; 1 Kings 21:4; Song of Songs 5:8"> Amnon became so infatuated with his sister Tamar that he felt sick. She was a virgin, and Amnon saw it was impossible for him to have her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Samuel 16:9; 1 Corinthians 3:19; James 3:15"> However, Amnon had a friend named Jonadab, and he was the son of David's brother Shimeah. Jonadab was a very cunning man.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Leviticus 20:17; Leviticus 18:9; Esther 5:13–14"> He asked Amnon, “Why are you, the king's son, so down every morning? Why don't you tell me what's wrong?” “I'm in love with Tamar, my brother Absalom's sister,” Amnon replied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Mark 6:24–25; Proverbs 19:27; Acts 23:15"> “Lie down on your bed and pretend you're sick,” Jonadab told him. “When your father comes to see you, tell him, ‘Please have my sister Tamar come and give me some food to eat. She can make it as I watch and she can hand it to me.’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Genesis 18:6; Matthew 13:33"> So Amnon lay down and pretended to be sick. When the king came to see him, Amnon asked him, “Please have my sister Tamar come and make a couple of pancakes as I watch, and she can hand them to me to eat.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7"> Then David sent a message to Tamar at the palace: “Please be so kind as to go to the house of your brother Amnon and make him some food.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> So Tamar went to the house of her brother Amnon to where he was lying down. She took some dough, kneaded it, and cooked the pancakes as he watched.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Genesis 45:1; John 3:20; Judges 3:19"> Then she took the pan and emptied it out before him, but he refused to eat. “Everybody leave me!” Amnon shouted. Everyone left.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> Then Amnon said to Tamar, “Bring the food here into my bedroom so you can hand it to me to eat.” So Tamar took the pancakes she had made to her brother Amnon in his bedroom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Genesis 39:11–12"> But as she handed him the food, he grabbed hold of her, and said, “Come to bed with me, my sister!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Judges 19:23; Leviticus 20:17; Judges 20:6"> “No, you're my brother!” she exclaimed. “Don't rape me! That's not what we do in Israel! Don't do something so shameful!</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Judges 19:24; Genesis 20:12; Genesis 19:8"> Stop and think about me! How could I bear such a disgrace? Think about yourself too! You'd be treated with contempt, regarded as a complete fool in Israel! Please talk with the king, for he won't stop you marrying me.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Samuel 12:11; Esther 7:8; Judges 20:5"> But Amnon wouldn't listen to her, and because he was stronger than she was, he raped her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Ezekiel 23:17"> Then Amnon hated Tamar with immense hatred. His hatred was so strong that it was greater than the love he'd had before. “Get up! Get lost!” he told her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> “No! Don't do this!” she answered. “Sending me away in disgrace would be an even greater evil than what you've already done to me.” But he wouldn't listen to her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> He called for his servant and said, “Get rid of this woman and lock the door behind her!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Judges 5:30; Genesis 37:3; PSALMS 45:13–14"> So his servant threw her out and locked the door behind her. Tamar was wearing the long robe of a princess, which is what the king's virgin daughters wore.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Joshua 7:6; Jeremiah 2:37; 2 Samuel 1:11"> Tamar put ashes on her head and tore her long robe. She put her hands on her head, and she went away crying loudly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Romans 12:19; Genesis 46:15; Genesis 34:2"> Her brother Absalom found her and asked, “Has brother Amnon been with you? Keep quiet for the moment, my sister. He's your brother. Don't be so upset about it.” So Tamar lived as a ruined and abandoned woman in her brother Absalom's home.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Genesis 34:7; 2 Samuel 3:28–29; 1 Samuel 2:29"> When King David heard about it, he was very angry.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Leviticus 19:17–18; Genesis 31:24; Genesis 24:50"> Absalom didn't talk to Amnon at all because he hated Amnon for raping his sister Tamar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Genesis 38:12–13; 1 Samuel 25:4; 2 Chronicles 26:10"> Some two years later, when his sheepshearers were at Baal-hazor near Ephraim, Absalom invited all the king's sons to join the celebrations.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“To join the celebrations.” Added for clarity, the yearly sheepshearing was also a time for feasting.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="2 Samuel 11:8–15; Jeremiah 41:6–7; PSALMS 55:21"> He went to the king and said, “I, your servant, have hired shearers. Would the king and his servants please join me?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Luke 14:23; Judges 19:7–10; 2 Samuel 14:22"> “No, my son,” the king replied, “we can't all go. We would be a burden to you.” Even though Absalom went on asking, he was not willing to go, but he did give Absalom his blessing.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Samuel 20:9; 2 Samuel 3:27; 2 Samuel 11:13–15"> “Well then, at least let my brother Amnon join us,” Absalom responded. “Why do you want him to go?” the king asked.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Proverbs 26:24–26"> But Absalom kept on asking, so the king sent Amnon and his other sons.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Judges 19:6; Judges 19:22; Judges 19:9"> Absalom gave orders to his men, saying, “Pay attention! When Amnon is feeling happy from drinking wine and I tell you, ‘Attack Amnon!’ then kill him. Don't be afraid. I myself am giving you this order. Be strong and be brave.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="2 Samuel 18:9; 1 Kings 1:33; 2 Kings 1:9–12"> So Absalom's men did what Absalom had ordered and killed Amnon. Then all the rest of the king's sons jumped up, got on their mules, and ran away.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30"> While they were on their way back, David received a message, “Absalom has killed all the king's sons—there's not a single one left!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 Samuel 1:11; 2 Samuel 12:16; Job 1:20"> The king stood up, tore his clothes, and lay down on the ground. All his officials stood beside him with their clothes torn.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="1 Samuel 16:9; 2 Samuel 13:3; Genesis 27:41"> But Jonadab, son of David's brother Shimeah, told them: “Your Majesty must not think they have killed all the king's sons—only Amnon is dead. Absalom has been planning ever since the day Amnon raped his sister Tamar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="2 Samuel 19:19"> So, Your Majesty, please don't believe the report that all the king's sons are dead. Only Amnon is dead.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Genesis 4:8–14; Proverbs 28:17; Amos 5:19"> In the meantime, Absalom had run away. When the watchman in Jerusalem<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“In Jerusalem”: added for clarity.">b</a></sup>looked out, he saw a large crowd coming along the road west of him, down the side of the hill.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="The Septuagint adds here: “The watchman went and told the king, ‘I see men coming from the direction of Beth-horon, down the side of the hill.’”">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35"> Jonadab told the king, “Can you see? The king's sons are arriving! It's exactly as your servant said.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="2 Samuel 18:33; 2 Samuel 12:21; 2 Samuel 13:15"> As he finished speaking, the king's sons came in, crying and wailing. Then the king and all his officials also cried loudly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="2 Samuel 3:3; 2 Samuel 14:32; 2 Samuel 13:34"> Absalom ran away to Talmai, son of Ammihud, the king of Geshur. Every day David mourned for his son Amnon.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Amnon”: the name is not explicitly given in the Hebrew text.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="2 Samuel 15:8; 2 Samuel 14:23; 2 Samuel 14:32"> After Absalom had run away to Geshur, he remained there for three years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Genesis 24:67; Genesis 38:12; Genesis 37:35"> King David longed to go and see Absalom, for he had finished grieving over the death of Amnon.</span>
</div>
