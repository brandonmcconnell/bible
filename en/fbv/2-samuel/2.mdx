<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Samuel 30:31; 1 Samuel 23:4; Genesis 32:2"> Sometime after this, David asked the Lord, “Should I go to one of the towns of Judah?”<br />“Yes, do it,” the Lord replied.<br />“Which one should I go to?” David asked.<br />“Go to Hebron,” said the Lord.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Samuel 25:42–43; 1 Samuel 30:5; Luke 22:28–29"> So David moved there with his two wives, Ahinoam from Jezreel and Abigail, Nabal's widow from Carmel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Samuel 30:1; 1 Chronicles 12:1–7; 1 Samuel 27:2–3"> He also brought the men who were with him, along with their families, and they settled in the villages near Hebron.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Samuel 5:5; 1 Samuel 31:11–13; 2 Samuel 5:3"> Then the men of Judah came to Hebron, and there they anointed David king of the people of Judah. When David found out that it was the men from Jabesh-gilead who had buried Saul,</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Samuel 23:21; PSALMS 115:15; Ruth 2:20"> he sent messengers to them, saying, “May the Lord bless you, because you demonstrated your loyal love to Saul your master, and you buried him properly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Proverbs 14:22; Exodus 34:6; Matthew 5:44"> Now may the Lord show you loyal love and trustworthiness, and I will also be good to you because of what you did for Saul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Ephesians 6:10; 2 Samuel 10:12; Genesis 15:1"> So be strong and be brave, for even though Saul your master is dead, the people of Judah have anointed me as their king.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Samuel 14:50; Genesis 32:2; 2 Samuel 3:7–8"> However, Abner, son of Ner, commander of Saul's army, had taken Ishbosheth,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Ishbosheth. It is very unlikely that he was called this to his face. He is identified as “Eshbaal” in 1 Chronicles 8:33and 1 Chronicles 9:39, which means “man of Baal.” However, the writer of this account here found it intolerable that the name of the king should include reference to the pagan god “Baal,” and so altered the name to Ishbosheth, meaning “man of shame.”">a</a></sup>son of Saul, to Mahanaim.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Joshua 19:18; Joshua 13:8–11; Numbers 1:40"> There he set up Ishbosheth as king over Gilead, Asher, Jezreel, Ephraim, and Benjamin, in fact over all Israel.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> Ishbosheth, son of Saul, was forty when he became king over Israel, and he reigned for two years. However, the people of Judah were on David's side.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Kings 2:11; 2 Samuel 5:4–5; 1 Chronicles 3:4"> David ruled in Hebron as king over the people of Judah for seven years and six months.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Joshua 18:25; Joshua 10:2; Joshua 9:3"> One day Abner and Ishbosheth's men left Mahanaim and went to the town of Gibeon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:16; 1 Chronicles 2:16; 2 Samuel 20:23"> Joab, son of Zeruiah, and David's men set off and met them at the pool of Gibeon, where they all sat down, facing each other across the pool.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Samuel 2:17; Proverbs 10:23; Proverbs 17:14"> Abner said to Joab, “Let's have some of the men fight in hand-to-hand combat before us.”<br />“Fine,” Joab agreed.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> So twelve men came forward from each side—twelve for Benjamin and Ishbosheth, and twelve for David.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16"> Each man grabbed his opponent's head and drove his sword into his opponent's side so that they all fell down dead together. That's why this place in Gibeon is called the Field of Sword-edges.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Samuel 3:1"> The battle that followed was hard-fought, but eventually Abner and his men were defeated by David's men.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Chronicles 12:8; Habakkuk 3:19; Song of Songs 8:14"> The three sons of Zeruiah were there: Joab, Abishai, and Asahel. Asahel was a fast runner, like a gazelle racing across the open countryside.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Samuel 2:21; Joshua 1:7; Proverbs 4:27"> He chased after Abner with single-minded determination.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Single-minded determination”: literally, “not turning to the right or to the left.”">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> Abner looked back and asked, “Is that you, Asahel?”<br />“Yes, it's me,” Asahel replied.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Judges 14:19"> Abner told him, “Leave me alone! Go and fight somebody else and take his weapons for yourself!” But Asahel refused to stop chasing him.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Samuel 3:27; Proverbs 29:1; 2 Kings 14:10–12"> Abner warned Asahel again. “Stop chasing me!” he shouted. “Why do you want me to kill you? How could I ever face your brother Joab?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="2 Samuel 3:27; 2 Samuel 4:6; 2 Samuel 5:6"> But Asahel wouldn't stop chasing him, so Abner drove the handle<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="The handle was often sharpened to a point so that it could be stuck into the ground.">c</a></sup>of his spear into his belly. It came out the back, and he fell down dead right there. Everyone who passed by stopped at the place where Asahel had fallen and died.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24"> But Joab and Abishai<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Joab and Abishai were Asahel's brothers.">d</a></sup>set off to chase after Abner. By the time the sun went down they had got as far as the hill of Ammah near Giah, on the way to the wilderness of Gibeon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> Abner's men from the tribe of Benjamin rallied to him there, forming a tight group around him standing at the top of the hill.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Jeremiah 46:14; Jeremiah 46:10; Acts 7:26"> Abner shouted to Joab: “Do we have to keep killing each other forever? Don't you realize that if we go on it'll only get worse? How long are you going to wait before you order your men to stop chasing their brothers?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Proverbs 17:14; 2 Samuel 2:14; Proverbs 15:1"> “As God lives,” Joab replied, “if you had not said anything, my men would have continued chasing their brothers until the morning.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> Joab blew the horn so all the men stopped—they didn't continue chasing or fighting the Israelites.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="2 Samuel 2:8; Song of Songs 2:17"> All through the night Abner and his men marched through the Jordan Valley. They crossed the Jordan River, and continued all morning until they arrived back at Mahanaim.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30"> When Joab got back from chasing Abner, he gathered all the men together. Nineteen of David's men were missing in addition to Asahel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 Samuel 3:1; 1 Kings 20:11"> However, they had killed three hundred and sixty of Abner's men from the tribe of Benjamin.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="1 Chronicles 2:13–16; 1 Samuel 17:58; 2 Chronicles 21:1"> They took Asahel's body and buried him in his father's tomb in Bethlehem. Then they marched all through the night and reached Hebron at dawn.</span>
</div>
