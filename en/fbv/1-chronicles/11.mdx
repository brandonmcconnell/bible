<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Samuel 16:12–13; 1 Samuel 16:1; 2 Samuel 5:3"> All the elders of Israel came to the king at Hebron, and David made a solemn agreement<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“Solemn agreement”: or, “covenant.”">c</a></sup>with them before the Lord. <span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Judges 1:21; Genesis 15:21; Joshua 15:8"> Then David and all the Israelites went to Jerusalem (formerly known as Jebus) where the Jebusites lived.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Chronicles 5:2; 1 Kings 8:1; PSALMS 87:2"> The Jebusites told David, “You will not enter here!” But David did capture the fortress of Zion, now known as the City of David.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:16; 2 Samuel 20:23; 2 Samuel 3:27"> David had said, “Whoever is first to attack the Jebusites will be my commander-in-chief.” Since Joab, son of Zeruiah, was the first, he became commander-in-chief.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Chronicles 11:5; 2 Samuel 5:7; PSALMS 2:6"> David decided to live in the fortress, which is why they named it after him the City of David.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Judges 9:6; 1 Kings 11:27; 2 Kings 12:20"> He built up the city all around it, from the Millo<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="The meaning of this word is uncertain.">d</a></sup>in a circuit all around, while Joab repaired the rest of the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Samuel 3:1; Isaiah 41:10; 1 Chronicles 9:20"> David became more and more powerful,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="“More and more powerful”: literally, “increased and increased.”">e</a></sup>for the Lord Almighty was with him.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Chronicles 11:3; 1 Samuel 16:1; 1 Chronicles 12:38"> These were the leaders of David's powerful warriors who, along with all the Israelites, gave him strong support in becoming king, just as the Lord had promised would happen to Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Samuel 23:8; 1 Chronicles 27:2; 1 Chronicles 12:18"> This is the list of the leading warriors who supported David: Jashobeam, son of Hachmoni, leader of the Three. Using his spear, he once killed 300 men in a single battle.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Chronicles 27:4; 2 Samuel 23:17–19; 2 Samuel 23:23"> After him came Eleazar, son of Dodo the Ahohite, one of the Three leading warriors.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Samuel 23:11–12; 1 Samuel 17:1"> He was with David at Pas-dammin when the Philistines gathered for battle that took place in a field full of barley. The Israelite army ran away when the Philistines attacked,</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="PSALMS 144:10; 2 Kings 5:1; 1 Samuel 14:23"> but David and Eleazar took a stand in the middle of the field, defending their ground and killing the Philistines. The Lord saved them by giving them a great victory.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Chronicles 14:9; 2 Samuel 5:18; Isaiah 17:5"> Another time,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Implied.">f</a></sup>the Three, who were part of the Thirty leading warriors, went down to meet David when he was at the cave of Adullam. The Philistine army was camped in the valley of Rephaim.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Samuel 10:5; 1 Samuel 13:4; 1 Samuel 13:23"> At the time David was in the stronghold, and the Philistine garrison was in Bethlehem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="John 4:14; Isaiah 12:3; 2 Samuel 23:15–16"> David was feeling really thirsty, and he said, “If only someone could bring me a drink of water from the well beside the entrance gate to Bethlehem!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Acts 21:13; 1 Samuel 19:5; Acts 20:24"> The Three broke through the Philistine defenses, took some water from the well at Bethelehem's gate, and brought it back to David. But David refused to drink it, and poured it out as an offering to the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Samuel 23:17; PSALMS 72:14; Judges 9:17"> “God forbid that I should do this!” he said. “It would be like drinking the blood of these men who risked their lives! They risked their lives to bring me a drink.” So he did not drink it. This is just some of the things the Three leading warriors did.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">

<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Corinthians 15:41; Matthew 13:8"> He was the most highly regarded of the Three and was their commander, though he was not one of the first Three.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Identifying a first and second Three seems to be the simplest solution to what are confusing verses.">h</a></sup></span>

<div className="bp">

<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Samuel 17:7; 1 Samuel 17:4; Deuteronomy 3:11"> Another time he killed an Egyptian—a huge man who stood seven feet six inches tall.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Literally “five cubits.”">j</a></sup>The Egyptian had a spear whose shaft was as thick as a weaver's rod. Benaiah attacked him with just a club, but he was able to grab the spear from the Egyptian's hand, and killed him with his own spear.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24"> These were the kind of things Benaiah did that made him as famous as the Three leading warriors.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Samuel 20:23"> He was the most highly regarded of the Thirty, though he was not one of the Three. David put him in charge of his personal bodyguard.</span>
</div>
