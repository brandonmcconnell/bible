(Though he was the firstborn, his birthright was given to the sons of Joseph son of Israel because he had defiled his father's bed.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Reuben had slept with Bilhah, Jacob's concubine. <span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Matthew 2:6; Micah 5:2; Hebrews 7:14"> and even though Judah became the strongest of his brothers and a ruler came from him, the birthright belonged to Joseph.)</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Genesis 46:9; Exodus 6:14; Numbers 26:5–9"> The sons of Reuben the firstborn of Israel: Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> The sons of Joel: Shemaiah his son, Gog his son, Shimei his son,</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> Micah his son, Reaiah his son, Baal his son,</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Chronicles 5:26; 2 Kings 15:29; 2 Chronicles 28:20"> and Beerah his son, the one whom Tiglath-Pileser the king of Assyria took into exile. He (Beerah) was a leader of the Reubenites.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Chronicles 5:17"> Beerah's relatives are, listed in their genealogical records by family: Jeiel (chief), Zechariah,</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Chronicles 5:4; Numbers 32:34; Numbers 32:38"> and Bela of Azaz, son of Shema, son of Joel. They lived from Aroer to Nebo and Baal Meon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Joshua 22:8–9"> On the eastern side they expanded into the land right up to the edge of the desert that continues to the Euphrates River, because their flocks had grown so big in Gilead.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Genesis 21:9; 1 Chronicles 5:18–21; PSALMS 83:6"> In the time of Saul they went to war against the Hagrites, defeating them. They took over the places where the Hagrites had lived in all the regions east of Gilead.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Joshua 13:11; Joshua 13:24–28; Deuteronomy 3:10–17"> Next to them the descendants of Gad lived in Basha, all the way to Salecah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> Joel (chief), Shapham (second), and Janai and Shaphat, in Bashan.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> Their relatives, according to family, were: Michael, Meshullam, Sheba, Jorai, Jacan, Zia, and Eber—a total of seven.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> These were the sons of Abihail, son of Huri, son of Jaroah, son of Gilead, son of Michael, son of Jeshishai, son of Jahdo, son of Buz.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> Ahi son of Abdiel, son of Guni, was their family chief.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Chronicles 27:29; Song of Songs 2:1; Isaiah 35:2"> They lived in Gilead, in Bashan and its towns, and throughout the pasturelands of Sharon all the way to their borders.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Kings 14:16; 2 Kings 14:28; 2 Kings 15:32"> They were all recorded in the genealogy during the time of Jotham, king of Judah, and Jeroboam, king of Israel.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Numbers 1:3; Joshua 4:12–13"> The tribe of Reuben, the tribe of Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh had 44,760 battle-ready strong warriors capable of using shields and swords and bows.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Genesis 25:15; 1 Chronicles 1:31; 1 Chronicles 5:10"> They went to war against the Hagrites, Jetur, Naphish, and Nodab.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="PSALMS 22:4–5; PSALMS 9:10; 2 Chronicles 18:31"> They received help in fighting these enemies because they called out to God during the battles. In this way they were able to defeat the Hagrites and all who were with them. God answered their prayers because they trusted in him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Revelation 18:13; Numbers 31:35"> They captured their enemies' livestock—fifty thousand camels, two hundred fifty thousand sheep, and two thousand donkeys. They also captured one hundred thousand people,</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="2 Chronicles 32:8; Romans 8:31; Joshua 23:10"> and many others were killed because the battle belonged to God. They took over the land and lived there until the exile.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Deuteronomy 3:8–9; Song of Songs 4:8; PSALMS 133:3"> The half-tribe of Manasseh had grown very large. They lived in the land from Bashan to Baal Hermon (otherwise known as Senir and Mount Hermon).</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Chronicles 4:38"> These were the family heads: Epher, Ishi, Eliel, Azriel, Jeremiah, Hodaviah, and Jahdiel. They were strong warriors, famous men, heads of their families.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Revelation 17:5; PSALMS 106:34–39; Exodus 34:15"> But they were unfaithful to the God of their forefathers. They prostituted themselves by following the gods of the peoples of the land, those that God had destroyed before them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Kings 15:19; 2 Kings 17:6; 2 Kings 15:29"> So the God of Israel encouraged Pul, king of Assyria (otherwise known as Tiglath-Pileser king of Assyria), to invade the land. He took into exile the Reubenites, the Gadites and the half-tribe of Manasseh. He brought them to Halah, Habor, Hara, and the river of Gozan, where they remain to this very day.</span>
</div>
