<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Jeremiah 1:2; 2 Chronicles 34:1–33; 2 Peter 1:19"> This is the message that the Lord gave to Zephaniah. He was the son of Cushti, son of Gedaliah, son of Amariah, son of Hezekiah.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Probably king Hezekiah, one of the previous kings of Judah.">a</a></sup>This happened when Josiah, son of Amon, was king of Judah.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Micah 7:13; 2 Kings 22:16–17; Ezekiel 33:27–29"> I will completely sweep away everything from the face of the earth, declares the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Hosea 4:3; Ezekiel 7:19; Jeremiah 12:4"> I will sweep away people and animals, I will sweep away the birds of the air and the fish of the sea. I will overthrow<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Literally, “stumbling-blocks,” which makes the meaning unclear.">b</a></sup>the wicked; I will destroy human beings from the face of the earth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Hosea 10:5; Micah 5:13; 2 Kings 23:4–5"> I will strike<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Literally, “stretch out my hand against.”">c</a></sup>Judah and everyone who lives in Jerusalem. I will destroy all that remains of their Baal worship along with their pagan priests so that even their names will be forgotten.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Implied.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Jeremiah 19:13; 1 Kings 11:33; 2 Kings 17:33"> I will destroy those who go up to the rooftops to bow down before the sun, moon, and stars. They also bow down and swear allegiance to the Lord, but then they do the same to Milcom.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Or “Molech,” a pagan god.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Isaiah 1:4; Jeremiah 2:13; Jeremiah 15:6"> I will destroy those who once worshiped the Lord but don't anymore. They don't seek the Lord or ask for my help.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Habakkuk 2:20; Zechariah 2:13; Zephaniah 1:14"> Be silent before the Lord God! For the day of the Lord is near: the Lord has prepared a sacrifice and consecrated his guests.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="In the context, Israel is the sacrifice, and the Babylonians are the “guests.”">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Isaiah 24:21; Isaiah 3:18–24; Jeremiah 22:24–30"> Then on the day of the Lord's sacrifice I will punish the officials and the king's sons, and those who follow pagan ways<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Literally, “those who dress in foreign clothes.”">g</a></sup>.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Samuel 5:5; Amos 3:10; 1 Samuel 2:15–16"> I will also punish those who jump over the threshold.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="The meaning of this is debated. Some think it was a pagan custom (see for example 1 Samuel 5:4-5). Others tie it to the following verse and see it as eagerness to rob the poor.">h</a></sup>On that day I will punish those who fill up their masters' houses using violence and deception.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Chronicles 33:14; Amos 8:3; 2 Kings 22:14"> On that day, declares the Lord, a cry of grief will come from the Fish Gate, a wailing from the Second Quarter, and a loud crash from the hills.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="James 5:1; Jeremiah 25:34; Hosea 12:7–8"> Those who live in the Market District<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Literally, “the Mortar.”">i</a></sup>will wail in sorrow, for all the merchants<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Literally, “people of Canaan.”">j</a></sup>are destroyed, along with those who trade in silver.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Amos 6:1; Jeremiah 48:11; Ezekiel 8:12"> At that time I will search through Jerusalem with lamps and I will punish those self-satisfied people, who are like wine left on its dregs, who say to themselves, “The Lord will not do good, nor will he do evil.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="In other words, they dismiss the Lord because they don't think he cares about them.">k</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Amos 5:11; Micah 6:15; Deuteronomy 28:30"> Their possessions will be looted; their houses will be demolished. They will build houses, but not live in them; they will plant vineyards, but not drink the wine.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Malachi 4:5; Ezekiel 30:3; Zephaniah 1:7"> The great day of the Lord is near and approaching rapidly. It will be a bitter day—even warriors will cry out loud.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Joel 2:2; Isaiah 22:5; Revelation 6:17"> It will be a day of anger,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="The expression of God being angry or full of wrath is a frequent image in the prophetic writings, but should not be understood in the same way as human anger. God's anger is not emotional like some kind of unthinking “red mist,” but a principled opposition to all that is evil. God uses this anger to try to convince those who are wrong to do what is right for their own sake, not because he “gets mad” and lashes out. Human anger is self-centered; divine anger is other-centered.">l</a></sup>a day of trouble and distress, a day of disaster and ruin, a day of darkness and gloom, a day of clouds and blackness,</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Hosea 8:1; Amos 3:6; Isaiah 32:14"> a day of trumpet calls and battle cries against fortified cities and watchtowers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Revelation 3:17; PSALMS 83:10; Jeremiah 2:17"> I will bring trouble on humanity, making them walk like blind people because they have sinned against the Lord. Their blood will be spilled like so much dust; their intestines like dung.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Zephaniah 3:8; Ezekiel 7:19; Proverbs 11:4"> Their silver and their gold won't help to save them on the day of the Lord's anger. The whole earth will be burned up by the fire of his jealous<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="“Jealous” when applied to God is not the same as human jealousy. It means God's strong desire that people follow only him, for he alone can save. He wants an exclusive relationship for he knows that anything else leads to disaster.">m</a></sup>anger. He will make sure the end of the people of the world is sudden and complete.</span>
</div>
</div>
