<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Chronicles 36:5–7; 2 Kings 24:1–2; 2 Kings 24:13"> During the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim, king of Judah, Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, attacked Jerusalem and surrounded it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Chronicles 36:7; Zechariah 5:11; Jeremiah 27:19–20"> The Lord allowed him to defeat King Jehoiakim,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“The Lord allowed him to defeat King Jehoiakim”: literally, “The Lord gave King Jehoiakim into his hand.”">a</a></sup>and also to remove some of the objects used in the Temple of God. He took them back to Babylon,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Literally, “the land of Shinar.”">b</a></sup>to the house of his god,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Or “gods.”">c</a></sup>placing them in the treasury of his god.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Isaiah 39:7; 2 Kings 20:17–18; 2 Kings 24:15"> Then the king ordered Ashpenaz, his chief eunuch,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="In other words, his chief of staff. Eunuchs were often given such positions in these times, and the term also came to mean the one in charge of the court, without necessarily meaning he had been castrated. The emphasis is on this man's position of authority.">d</a></sup>to take charge of some of the captured Israelites from the royal and noble families.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Daniel 5:11; Acts 7:22; Proverbs 22:29"> “They are to be young men without any physical defect who are good-looking,” he said. “They must be well-educated, quick to learn, have good insight, and be well able to serve in the king's palace and be taught the literature and language of Babylon<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Literally, “the Chaldeans.”">e</a></sup>.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Daniel 1:19; 1 Kings 10:8; Daniel 1:8"> The king also provided them with a daily allowance of the same kind of rich food and wine he was served. At the end of their three years of education they would enter the king's service.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="“Enter the king's service”: literally, “they would stand before the king.” This is taken to mean entering service (see Deuteronomy 10:8).">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Ezekiel 14:14; Matthew 24:15; Daniel 2:17"> Among those chosen were Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, from the tribe of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Daniel 5:12; Daniel 4:8; Daniel 2:49"> The chief eunuch gave them new names: Daniel he called Belteshazzar, Hananiah he called Shadrach, Mishael he called Meshach, and Azariah he called Abednego.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="PSALMS 141:4; Ezekiel 4:13–14; Leviticus 11:45–47"> However, Daniel made up his mind not to make himself impure<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="“Make himself impure”: or “unclean.” An observant Jew would have had several problems in consuming such a diet—the use of unclean meats, the animals not killed in accordance with Levitical law, portions of the meat and also the wine offered to pagan gods, the rich food and wine would not be a healthy diet etc.">g</a></sup>by eating the king's rich food and wine. He asked the chief eunuch to allow him not to make himself impure.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Genesis 39:21; Proverbs 16:7; PSALMS 106:46"> God had helped Daniel to be viewed with kindness and sympathy by the chief eunuch.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Matthew 6:16–18; John 12:42–43; Proverbs 29:25"> But the chief eunuch told Daniel, “I'm afraid of what my lord the king would do to me. He's the one who decided what you should eat and drink. What if he were to see you looking pale and sickly compared to the other young men of your age. Because of you the king would have my head!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> Daniel then spoke with the guard that the chief eunuch had put in charge of Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Romans 14:2; Daniel 1:16; Genesis 1:29–30"> “Please put us, your servants, to the test and just give us vegetables<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="The word means “from plants,” so would include cereals, beans, green plants, etc.">h</a></sup>to eat and water to drink for ten days,” Daniel told him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> “After that, compare us with those young men who ate the king's rich food. Then decide on the basis of what you see.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> The guard agreed to the proposal they made and tested them for ten days.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Exodus 23:25; Proverbs 10:22; 2 Kings 4:42–44"> When the ten days were up they looked healthier and better fed than all the young men who had eaten the king's rich food.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Daniel 1:12"> After that the guard didn't give them the rich food and wine, just vegetables.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Kings 3:12; James 1:5; Daniel 2:21"> God gave these four young men the ability to learn and understand in all areas of literature and knowledge, while Daniel was also given the gift of interpreting all kinds of visions and dreams.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Daniel 1:5"> When their time of education ordered by the king was over, the chief eunuch brought all the young men before King Nebuchadnezzar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Proverbs 22:29; Genesis 41:46; Daniel 1:5"> The king talked with them and none could compare with Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah. So they entered the king's service.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Exodus 8:19; Isaiah 47:12–14; 1 Kings 4:29–34"> Whatever subject the king asked them about, everything that required wisdom of understanding,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="The Hebrew does not say “wisdom and understanding” as most versions translate it. Some argue that “wisdom of understanding” indicates a superlative, in that the highest form of wisdom is indicated. Others suggest that “understanding” qualifies the term “wisdom,” indicating that this wisdom did not include so-called Babylonian “wisdom” of astrology and divination etc.">i</a></sup>he found them ten times better than all the magicians and enchanters in his whole kingdom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Daniel 6:28; Daniel 10:1"> Daniel remained in this position until the first year of King Cyrus' reign.</span>
</div>

## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">1:2</span> “The Lord allowed him to defeat King Jehoiakim”: literally, “The Lord gave King Jehoiakim into his hand.”</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">1:2</span> Literally, “the land of Shinar.”</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">1:2</span> Or “gods.”</li>
<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">1:3</span> In other words, his chief of staff. Eunuchs were often given such positions in these times, and the term also came to mean the one in charge of the court, without necessarily meaning he had been castrated. The emphasis is on this man's position of authority.</li>
<li id="fn-e"><a href="#fnref-e" className="fnb">e</a> <span className="fnr">1:4</span> Literally, “the Chaldeans.”</li>
<li id="fn-f"><a href="#fnref-f" className="fnb">f</a> <span className="fnr">1:5</span> “Enter the king's service”: literally, “they would stand before the king.” This is taken to mean entering service (see Deuteronomy 10:8).</li>
<li id="fn-g"><a href="#fnref-g" className="fnb">g</a> <span className="fnr">1:8</span> “Make himself impure”: or “unclean.” An observant Jew would have had several problems in consuming such a diet—the use of unclean meats, the animals not killed in accordance with Levitical law, portions of the meat and also the wine offered to pagan gods, the rich food and wine would not be a healthy diet etc.</li>
<li id="fn-h"><a href="#fnref-h" className="fnb">h</a> <span className="fnr">1:12</span> The word means “from plants,” so would include cereals, beans, green plants, etc.</li>
<li id="fn-i"><a href="#fnref-i" className="fnb">i</a> <span className="fnr">1:20</span> The Hebrew does not say “wisdom and understanding” as most versions translate it. Some argue that “wisdom of understanding” indicates a superlative, in that the highest form of wisdom is indicated. Others suggest that “understanding” qualifies the term “wisdom,” indicating that this wisdom did not include so-called Babylonian “wisdom” of astrology and divination etc.</li>
</ol>
