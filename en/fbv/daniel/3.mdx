<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Habakkuk 2:19; Jeremiah 16:20; Isaiah 46:6"> King Nebuchadnezzar had a gold statue made that was sixty cubits tall and six cubits wide.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="This corresponds to around 90 feet high by 9 feet wide; however, the numbers in cubits are significant, especially in the Babylonian context.">a</a></sup>He had it set up on the plain of Dura in the province of Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Daniel 3:27; Proverbs 29:12; 1 Kings 12:32"> Then he summoned the provincial governors,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Provincial governors”: literally, “satraps.” Also verse 27 and 6:1.">b</a></sup>prefects, local governors, counselors, treasurers, judges, magistrates, and all the officials of the provinces to come to the dedication of the statue he had set up.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Revelation 17:17; Revelation 17:13; Revelation 13:13–16"> They all<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="The complete group as identified in verse 2 is repeated in the text.">c</a></sup>came to the dedication of the statue Nebuchadnezzar had set up and stood in front of it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Daniel 4:1; Daniel 6:25; Micah 6:16"> Then a herald announced in a loud voice, “People of all nations and languages, pay attention to the king's command!</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Daniel 3:15; Daniel 3:10; Daniel 3:7"> As soon as you hear the sound of the horn, flute, zither, trigon, harp, pipes, and all kinds of musical instruments, you must fall to the ground and worship the gold statue that King Nebuchadnezzar has set up.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Matthew 13:50; Matthew 13:42; Jeremiah 29:22"> Anyone who doesn't immediately fall down and worship will be thrown into a furnace of blazing fire.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Revelation 17:8; Revelation 13:3; Revelation 13:8"> So when all the people heard the sound of the musical instruments<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="The names of five of the six instruments mentioned in verse 5 are repeated again here.">d</a></sup>they all fell down—the people of all nations and languages worshiped the gold statue that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Ezra 4:12–16; Daniel 6:12–13; Esther 3:8–9"> Right then some of the astrologers came forward and made accusations against the Jews.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="“Made accusations against the Jews”: literally, “ate pieces of the Jews.”">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Daniel 5:10; Daniel 6:6; Daniel 6:21"> They said to King Nebuchadnezzar, “May Your Majesty the king live forever!</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Daniel 3:4–7; Daniel 6:12; Esther 3:12–14"> Your Majesty has decreed that everyone who hears the sound of the musical instruments<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="The musical instruments listed in verse 5 are repeated again here. Also verse 15.">f</a></sup>shall fall down and worship the gold statue,</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> and that anyone who does not shall be thrown into a furnace of blazing fire.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Daniel 6:13; Daniel 2:49; Acts 17:7"> But there are some Jews that you placed in charge of the province of Babylon—Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego—who pay no attention to Your Majesty's decree. They do not serve your gods and will not worship the gold statue you set up.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Daniel 3:19; Daniel 2:12; Esther 3:5–6"> This made Nebuchadnezzar absolutely furious. “Bring me Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego!” he demanded. So they were brought before the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Jeremiah 50:2; Daniel 3:1; Isaiah 46:1"> “Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, are you deliberately<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="“Deliberately”: often translated as “true,” but this is an Aramaic word more to do with intent and purpose.">g</a></sup>refusing to serve my gods and worship the gold statue I set up?” asked the king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Acts 5:29; 2 Chronicles 32:15–17; Exodus 5:2"> “Are you ready now to fall down and worship the statue I had made when you hear the sound of the musical instruments? If you don't, you will be immediately thrown into the furnace of blazing fire, and there's no god who can save you from my power!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Daniel 1:7; Daniel 3:12"> “King Nebuchadnezzar, we don't need to defend ourselves before you over this,” Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego replied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Romans 8:31; Luke 1:37; Hebrews 7:25"> “If our God whom we serve so wishes, he is able to rescue us from the furnace of blazing fire. He will save us from your power, Your Majesty.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Acts 5:29–32; Matthew 10:39; Revelation 2:10–11"> But even if he does not, Your Majesty needs to know that we would never serve your gods or worship the gold statue you have set up.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Daniel 3:13; Leviticus 26:24; Proverbs 21:24"> This made Nebuchadnezzar so angry with Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego that his face twisted in rage. “Make the furnace seven times hotter than normal!” he ordered.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Acts 16:23; Acts 16:25; Daniel 3:15"> Then he commanded some of his strongest soldiers, “Tie up Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego and throw them into the furnace of blazing fire!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> So they were tied up, fully dressed in their coats, trousers, turbans, and other clothes,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="The meaning of the words used for these items of clothing is debated.">h</a></sup>and thrown into the furnace of blazing fire.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Exodus 12:33; Proverbs 21:18; Proverbs 11:8"> Because the king's command was so harsh in making the furnace so extremely hot, the flames killed the soldiers who threw them in.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Daniel 6:16–17; Jeremiah 38:6; PSALMS 34:19"> Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, still tied up, fell into the furnace of blazing fire.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Daniel 6:7; Daniel 6:22; Daniel 3:9–10"> Then King Nebuchadnezzar suddenly jumped up in amazement. “Didn't we throw three men tied up into the furnace?” he asked his advisors. “Yes, that's right, Your Majesty,” they replied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Isaiah 43:2; Acts 28:5; Mark 16:18"> “Look!” he cried out. “How is it that I can see four men, not tied up, walking around in the fire and not being burned? And the fourth one looks like a god!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="“A god.” This expression on the lips of a pagan king surely referred to his own religious beliefs. The actual term is “son of god,” however in Hebrew “son of” often simply refers to the actual person, not the son (see for example 2:25which in fact refers to the “sons of the captives,” etc.). However, in verse 28 Nebuchadnezzar identifies him as an angel.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Daniel 3:17; Isaiah 52:12; Revelation 19:5"> Nebuchadnezzar went towards the door of the furnace of blazing fire. “Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, servants of the Most High God, come out! Come here!” he shouted. So Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego came out of the fire.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Isaiah 43:2; Hebrews 11:34; Isaiah 26:11"> The provincial governors, prefects, local governors, and the king's advisors gathered around them and saw that the fire had not harmed them. Their hair wasn't singed, their clothes weren't scorched—there wasn't even the smell of smoke!</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Romans 14:7–8; PSALMS 34:7–8; PSALMS 84:11–12"> Then Nebuchadnezzar said, “Praise the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego! He sent his angel and he rescued his servants who trusted in him! They disobeyed my royal command, putting their lives on the line, and refused to worship any other gods except their God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Daniel 2:5; PSALMS 76:10; Deuteronomy 32:31"> Consequently I am issuing a decree that if anyone of any nation or language speaks disrespectfully about the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego they will be torn to pieces and their houses will be destroyed. There is no other God who is able to save like this!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Daniel 2:49; 1 Samuel 2:30; Daniel 3:12"> Then Nebuchadnezzar promoted Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, giving them even greater responsibilities in the province of Babylon.</span>
</div>
