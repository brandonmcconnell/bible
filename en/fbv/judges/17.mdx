<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Nehemiah 13:25; Ruth 3:10; 1 Samuel 23:21"> told his mother, “Those eleven hundred shekels of silver that were stolen from you and that I heard you curse—I've got the silver. I was the one who took it.”<br />Then his mother said, “My son, the Lord bless you!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="This may mean that the mother was attempting to neutralize the curse with a blessing since it affected her son, or that she was pleased that he had owned up to the theft.">a</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Exodus 20:4; Exodus 20:23; Leviticus 19:4"> He gave back to his mother the eleven hundred shekels of silver. His mother announced, “I'm completely dedicating this money to the Lord. I'm handing it over to my son to have an idol carved, an image made with molten silver.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="It is unclear whether this refers to two objects or one. </div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Isaiah 46:6–7; Jeremiah 10:9–10"> After he'd returned the silver to his mother she gave two hundred shekels to a silversmith who made it into a carved idol, an image made with molten silver. They were kept in Micah's house.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Judges 18:14; Judges 8:27; Genesis 31:19"> Micah had built a shrine for the idol. He also made an ephod and some household gods, and ordained one of his sons as his priest.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Deuteronomy 12:8; Proverbs 14:12; Judges 21:25"> At that time Israel didn't have a king— everyone did what they themselves thought was the right thing to do.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="This is exactly the opposite of the usual phrase “did what was right in the Lord's sight.” Instead of a commendation, this must be seen as the “democratization of wickedness.” The same expression is used in 21:25.">c</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Micah 5:2; Matthew 2:1; Ruth 1:1–2"> One young man, a Levite from the tribe of Judah<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="It's not clear how this man could be both from the tribe of Levi and the tribe of Judah, unless his parents were from different tribes.">d</a></sup>had been living in Bethlehem in Judah,</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Nehemiah 13:10–11; Judges 17:11; Joshua 24:33"> left Bethlehem to look for a different place to live. As he traveled through the hill country of Ephraim, he came to Micah's house.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> “Where are you from?” Micah asked him.<br />“I'm a Levite from Bethlehem in Judah,” the man replied. “I'm looking for a place to live.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Ezekiel 13:19; Judges 18:19–20; 1 Timothy 6:10"> “Come and stay here with me. You can be my ‘father’ and priest, and I'll give you ten shekels of silver a year, plus your clothes and food.” So the Levite went inside</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> and agreed to stay with him. The young man became like a son to him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Judges 18:30; Numbers 16:8–10; Numbers 16:5"> Micah ordained the Levite as his own priest and he lived in Micah's house.</span>
