<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Judges 18:1; Judges 17:8; Malachi 2:15"> At that time Israel didn't have a king. A Levite who was living in a remote area in the hill country of Ephraim married a concubine-wife<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="In other words, a “second-class” wife, not one viewed as having the social status of a true wife.">a</a></sup>from Bethlehem in Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Ezekiel 16:28; Deuteronomy 22:21; Leviticus 21:9"> But she was unfaithful<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Unfaithful”: literally, “acted as a prostitute.” However, some ancient versions have “was angry with him.”">b</a></sup>to him and left him to return to her father's house in Bethlehem. She was there for four months.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Genesis 34:3; Genesis 50:21; Leviticus 19:17"> Then her husband went after her, to talk kindly with her and bring her back home. With him went his servant and two donkeys. She took him to her father's house and when her father met him, he gladly welcomed him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4"> Her father pressed him to stay with them, so he remained for three days, eating, drinking, and sleeping there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Judges 19:8; Genesis 18:5; PSALMS 104:15"> On the fourth day he and his concubine got up early in the morning and prepared to leave, but her father said to his son-in-law, “You'll feel better if you have something to eat before you go.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Judges 19:9; Judges 16:25; Ruth 3:7"> So the two men sat down to eat and drink together. The father said to his son-in law, “Please agree to spend another night here, and you can enjoy yourself!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7"> The man got up to leave, but his father-in-law pressed him to stay, so in the end he spent the night there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8"> On the fifth day he got up early in the morning to leave. But his father-in-law said, “Eat before you go, then leave later this afternoon.” So they had a meal together.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Proverbs 27:1; Luke 24:29; James 4:13–14"> When he got up to leave with his concubine and his servant, his father-in-law told him, “Look it's late—it's already evening. Spend the night here. The day's almost over. Stay here the night and enjoy yourself, then tomorrow you can get up early and be on your way home.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Joshua 15:63; Joshua 15:8; Judges 1:8"> But the man didn't want to spend another night, so he got up and left. He headed towards the town of Jebus (now called Jerusalem) with his two saddled donkeys and his concubine.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Samuel 5:6; Judges 1:21; Judges 19:10"> As they approached Jebus the day was over, and the servant said to his master, “Sir, why don't we stop here at this Jebusite town for the night?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> But his master replied, “No, we're not going to stop in this town where only foreigners live and no Israelites. We'll continue on to Gibeah.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Samuel 10:26; Hosea 5:8; Isaiah 10:29"> Then he told his servant, “Come on, let's try and get to Gibeah or Ramah and spend the night somewhere there.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> So they carried on and reached Gibeah in the territory of Benjamin just as the sun was setting.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Judges 19:18; Matthew 25:35; Matthew 25:43"> They stopped in Gibeah to spend the night, and sat down in the town's main square, but no one invited them to come and stay.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Judges 19:1; PSALMS 104:23; Genesis 3:19"> But later that evening an old man came by, returning from working in the fields. He was from the hill country of Ephraim, but was now living in Gibeah in the territory of Benjamin.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Genesis 32:17; Genesis 16:8"> He looked over and noticed the traveler in the square and asked, “Where are you going and where have you come from?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Judges 18:31; John 15:6; 1 Samuel 1:7"> “We've come from Bethlehem in Judah and we're going to a remote area in the hill country of Ephraim,” the man replied. “I'm from there and I went to Bethlehem, and now I'm going to the Lord's Temple.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“To the Lord's Temple”: the Septuagint reads, “to my house.”">c</a></sup>No one here has invited me to stay.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> There's straw and food for our donkeys, and we your servants have bread and wine—enough for me, the woman, and my servant. We have all we need.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Judges 6:23; James 2:15–16; Hebrews 13:2"> “You are welcome to stay with me,” the man replied. “I can let you have everything you need. Just don't spend the night here in the square.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Genesis 43:24; Genesis 18:4; Luke 7:44"> He took him home and fed the donkeys. The travelers washed their feet and then started to eat and drink.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Genesis 19:4–5; 1 Corinthians 6:9; Judges 20:5"> While they were enjoying themselves, some depraved men from the town came and surrounded the house and banged on the door, shouting to the old man who owned the house, “Bring out the man who came to stay in your house so we can have sex with him.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Genesis 34:7; 2 Samuel 13:12; Judges 20:6"> The man who owned the house went outside and told them, “My brothers, don't act in such an evil way! This man is a guest in my house. Don't do something so disgusting!</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Genesis 19:8; Deuteronomy 21:14; Genesis 34:2"> Look, here's my virgin daughter and the man's concubine. Let me bring them out and you can rape them and do whatever you want to them. But don't do something so disgusting to this man.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Hosea 10:9; Hosea 9:9; Hosea 7:4–7"> But the men refused to listen, so the man grabbed his concubine and threw her outside to them. They raped her and abused her all night until the morning, and only discarded her at dawn.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Judges 19:3; Genesis 18:12; 1 Peter 3:6"> As night turned into day she returned to the house where her master was staying and collapsed in front of the door as it got light.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27"> Her master got up in the morning and opened the door of the house. He went out to continue his journey and there was his concubine, stretched out in the doorway of the house, with her hands holding onto the doorstep.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Judges 20:5; 1 Kings 18:29"> “Get up, let's go,” he told her, but there was no answer. Then the man lifted her onto his donkey and went home.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Judges 20:6–7; 1 Samuel 11:7; Romans 10:2"> When he got home he took a knife, and holding onto his concubine, cut her up, limb by limb, into twelve pieces, and sent these pieces of her to every part of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Judges 20:7; Proverbs 13:10; Hosea 9:9"> Everyone who saw her<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Her”: most translations use “it,” but the Hebrew literally says, “All the ones seeing said…” Clearly it was the dismembered body parts of the concubine that are being referred to here, and so the feminine pronoun makes the most sense.">d</a></sup>said, “Nothing like this has ever been seen before, from the time the Israelites left Egypt up until now. You should think about what happened to her! Decide what to do! Speak up!”</span>
