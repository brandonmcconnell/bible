<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Judges 2:21–22; Deuteronomy 7:22; Proverbs 17:3"> The following are the nations the Lord left and used to test all those Israelites who had not known what it was like to be part of any of the wars in Canaan.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Genesis 3:5; Genesis 3:7; 1 Corinthians 9:26–27"> (He did so to teach warfare to the later generations of Israel, particularly to those who had not previously experienced it.)</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Joshua 13:3; Joshua 13:5; Judges 10:7"> They are: the five rulers of the Philistines, all the Canaanites, the Sidonians, and the Hivites living in the mountains of Lebanon from Mount Baal-hermon to Lebo-hamath.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Judges 3:1; Judges 2:22; Exodus 15:25"> They were left there be to a test for the Israelites, to find out whether the Israelites would keep the Lord's commandments which he had given their forefathers through Moses.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="PSALMS 106:34–38; Genesis 10:15–18; Exodus 3:8"> They lived among the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites, and Jebusites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Deuteronomy 7:3–4; Exodus 34:16; 1 Kings 11:1–5"> The Israelites intermarried with them, marrying their daughters, giving their own daughters to their sons, and worshiped their gods.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Exodus 34:13; 2 Kings 23:6; 1 Kings 18:19"> The Israelites did what was evil in the Lord's sight. They ignored the Lord their God and worshiped the images of Baals and Asherahs.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Judges 2:14; Habakkuk 3:7; Judges 4:9"> The Lord became angry with Israel, so he sold them to Cushan-Rishathaim, king of Aram Naharaim. The Israelites were subject to Cushan-Rishathaim for eight years.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Judges 3:15; Judges 1:13; PSALMS 22:5"> But when the Israelites cried out to the Lord to help them, he provided someone to rescue them, Othniel, son of Kenaz, Caleb's younger brother, and he saved them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Judges 13:25; 1 Samuel 11:6; Judges 14:19"> The Spirit of the Lord came on him, and he became Israel's judge. He went to war with Cushan-Rishathaim, king of Aram, and the Lord handed the king over to Othniel, who was victorious.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Judges 5:31; Judges 8:28; Joshua 11:23"> As a result, the country was a peace for forty years until Othniel, son of Kenaz, died.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Samuel 12:9; Judges 2:19; Judges 2:14"> But once again the Israelites did what was evil in the Lord's sight, and because they did this the Lord gave power to Eglon, king of Moab, to conquer Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Judges 1:16; Deuteronomy 34:3; Judges 5:14"> Eglon had the Ammonites and the Amalekite join him, and then attacked and defeated Israel, taking possession of the City of Palms.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“City of Palms”: Jericho.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Deuteronomy 28:47–48; Deuteronomy 28:40; Leviticus 26:23–25"> The Israelites were subject to Eglon, king of Moab, for eighteen years.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Judges 3:9; PSALMS 78:34; Isaiah 36:16"> Again the Israelites cried out to the Lord to help them, and he provided someone to rescue them, Ehud, son of Gera the Benjamite, a left-handed man. The Israelites sent him to pay the tribute to Eglon, king of Moab.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Song of Songs 3:8; Revelation 2:12; PSALMS 149:6"> Ehud had made for himself a cubit long double-edged sword, and he strapped it to his right thigh under his clothes.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Ezekiel 34:20; Job 15:27; Jeremiah 50:11"> He came and presented the tribute to Eglon, king of Moab, who was a very fat man.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> Then after delivering the tribute he sent home those who had helped carry it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Joshua 4:20; Judges 3:20; Acts 23:18–19"> But when he reached the stone idols near Gilgal, he turned back. He went to see Eglon, and told him, “Your Majesty, I have a secret message for you.” The king told his attendants, “Silence!” and they all left.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Jeremiah 10:7; Amos 3:15; PSALMS 29:1"> Ehud then went over to where Eglon was sitting alone in his cool upstairs room, and told him, “I have a message from God for you.” As the king got up from his seat,</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Zechariah 13:3; Job 20:25; 1 Samuel 15:33"> Ehud grabbed his sword with his left hand from his right thigh and drove it into Eglon's belly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> The handle went in with the blade and the fat closed over it. So Ehud didn't pull the sword out, and the king defecated.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> Then Ehud closed and locked the doors, and escaped through the toilet.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Toilet”: The meaning of the word is uncertain, some believe it means “porch,” however it appears that Ehud managed to get out of the room secretly. Descending through an open latrine seems to be the best conclusion.">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="1 Samuel 24:3"> After he had left, the servants came and saw that the doors of the room were locked. “He must be using the toilet,” they concluded.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> So they waited until they couldn't stand it any more, and since he still hadn't opened the doors of the room, they went and found the key and opened the doors. There was their lord, lying dead on the floor.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> While the servants delayed acting, Ehud escaped, passing the stone idols and on to Seirah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Judges 6:34; 1 Samuel 13:3; Judges 5:14"> When he got there, he blew a trumpet in the hill country of Ephraim, and the Israelites joined him. They went down from the hills, with Ehud leading them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Judges 12:5; Judges 7:9; Judges 7:15"> He told them, “Follow me, for the Lord has handed Moab, your enemy, over to you.” So they followed him down and took control of the fords of the Jordan leading to Moab. They didn't let anyone cross.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Deuteronomy 32:15; Judges 3:17; PSALMS 17:10"> Then they attacked the Moabites and killed around 10,000 of their best and strongest fighting men. Not a single one escaped.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Judges 3:11; Judges 5:31"> Moab was conquered that day and made subject to Israel, and the country was at peace for eighty years.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Judges 5:6; 1 Corinthians 1:17; 1 Samuel 17:50"> After Ehud was Shamgar, son of Anath, who killed six hundred Philistines with an oxgoad. He also rescued Israel.</span>
</div>
</div>
