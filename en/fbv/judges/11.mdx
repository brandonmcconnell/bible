<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Hebrews 11:32; Judges 6:12; 2 Kings 5:1"> Jephthah of Gilead was a strong fighter. He was the son of a prostitute, and his father was Gilead.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Genesis 12:10; Proverbs 2:16; Deuteronomy 23:2"> Gilead's wife gave him sons, who when they grew up, drove Jephthah away, telling him, “You won't inherit anything from our father because you are another woman's son.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“Another woman's son”: this is what the Hebrew says, however it probably has the meaning of “the son of a prostitute.” This is certainly how the Septuagint translators understood it.">a</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Judges 9:4; 1 Samuel 22:2; 2 Samuel 10:8"> Jephthah ran away from his brothers and went to live in the land of Tob. A gang of trouble-makers joined him and he led them out on raids.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="The Hebrew simply says, “they went out with him,” however the context indicates they were a band of mercenaries.">b</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Judges 10:9"> Later on, the Ammonites were at war with Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="PSALMS 118:22–23; Acts 7:35–39; 1 Corinthians 1:27–29"> As the Ammonites were attacking Israel, the elders of Gilead came to get Jephthah from the land of Tob.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> “Come and be our army commander,” they asked Jepthah, “so we can fight the Ammonites.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Revelation 3:9; Genesis 45:4–5; Isaiah 60:14"> “Weren't you the ones who hated me and drove me from my father's house?” Jephthah asked them. “Why are you coming to me now you're in trouble?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Judges 10:18; 1 Kings 13:6; Exodus 10:17"> “Yes, that's why we've turned to you now,”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="It's unclear whether the elders are responding to Jephthah's first or second question. Both are possible as responses.">c</a></sup>the elders of Gilead replied. “Come with us and fight the Ammonites, and you will be the leader of all the people of Gilead.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Numbers 32:20–29"> “So if I go back with you and fight the Ammonites, and the Lord makes me victorious, then I'll be your leader?” Jephthah asked the elders of Gilead.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Jeremiah 42:5; Genesis 31:50; Jeremiah 29:23"> “The Lord will be a witness between us,” they replied. “We'll do whatever you say.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Judges 10:17; 1 Samuel 10:17; Judges 20:1"> So Jephthah went with the elders of Gilead, and the people made him their leader and army commander. And Jephthah repeated all his conditions before the Lord at Mizpah.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Deuteronomy 2:26; Matthew 18:15–16; Proverbs 25:8–9"> Then Jephthah sent messengers to the king of the Ammonites to ask him, “What have you got against me that you want to attack my land?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Genesis 32:22; Numbers 21:24–26; Deuteronomy 2:37"> The king of the Ammonites replied to Jephthah's messengers, “Israel seized my land when they came from Egypt. It extended from the Arnon River to the Jabbok River, and across to the Jordan River. So give it back and there'll be no fighting.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="1 Peter 3:11; Romans 12:18; Hebrews 12:14"> Jephthah sent messengers back to the king of the Ammonites</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Deuteronomy 2:19; Deuteronomy 2:9; Numbers 21:27–30"> to tell him, “This is Jephthah's reply: The Israelites did not take any land from Moab or from the Ammonites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Numbers 14:25; Deuteronomy 1:40; Numbers 20:1"> When they left Egypt, the Israelites went through the desert to the Red Sea and arrived at Kadesh.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Numbers 20:14–21; Deuteronomy 2:4–9; Numbers 20:1"> They sent messengers to the king of Edom, saying, ‘Please let us pass through your country,’ but the king of Edom refused to listen. They also sent the same request to the king of Moab, and he refused too. So they remained at Kadesh.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Deuteronomy 2:1–8; Numbers 22:36; Numbers 21:4–13"> Eventually the Israelites traveled through the desert, avoiding the lands of Edom and Moab. They arrived on the east side of the land of Moab and camped on the other side of the Arnon River. But they did not enter Moab territory, for the Arnon River was its border.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Numbers 21:21–35; Deuteronomy 2:26–36; Deuteronomy 3:1–17"> Then the Israelites sent messengers to Sihon, king of the Amorites, who ruled from Heshbon, and asked him, ‘Please let us pass through your land to our own country.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Joshua 13:15–32; Numbers 21:23–25; PSALMS 136:17–21"> But Sihon didn't trust the Israelites to pass through his territory. So he assembled his army, set up camp at Jahaz, and attacked the Israelites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> However, the Lord, the God of Israel, handed over Sihon and all his people to the Israelites, who defeated them. So the Israelites took over all the land inhabited by the Amorites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Deuteronomy 2:36"> They occupied all the territory of the Amorites from the Arnon River to the Jabbok River, and from the desert to the Jordan River.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> It was the Lord, the God of Israel, who drove out the Amorites before his people Israel, so why should you take it over?</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Numbers 21:29; PSALMS 78:55; Micah 4:5"> Why don't you keep whatever your god Chemosh gave you, and we'll keep whatever the Lord our God has given us?</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Micah 6:5; Joshua 24:9–10; Deuteronomy 23:3–4"> Do you think you're so much better than Balak, son of Zippor, king of Moab? Did he ever quarrel with Israel or attack them?</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Deuteronomy 2:36; Joshua 12:5; Judges 10:8"> Israelites have been living in Heshbon, Aroer, their villages, and in all the towns along the banks of the Arnon River for three hundred years. Why didn't you take them back during that time?</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="1 Samuel 24:15; Genesis 16:5; 1 Samuel 24:12"> I have not sinned against you, but you have done me wrong by going to war against me. Let the Lord, the Judge, decide today between the Israelites and the Ammonites.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 Kings 14:11; Proverbs 16:18"> But the king of Ammon didn't pay any attention to what Jephthah had to say.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Numbers 11:25; Judges 3:10; Judges 13:25"> Then the Spirit of the Lord came on Jephthah. He passed through Gilead and Manasseh, then on through Mizpah of Gilead. From there he advanced to attack the Ammonites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Genesis 28:20; 1 Samuel 1:11; Ecclesiastes 5:1–2"> Jephthah made a solemn promise to the Lord, saying, “If you make me victorious over the Ammonites,</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="PSALMS 66:13–14; Leviticus 27:28–29; 1 Samuel 1:28"> I will dedicate to the Lord whatever comes out of the door of my house to meet me on my safe return from the battle. I will present it as a burnt offering.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Judges 1:4; Judges 2:18; Judges 3:10"> Jephthah advanced to attack the Ammonites, and the Lord gave him the victory over them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Ezekiel 27:17; Deuteronomy 2:36"> He soundly defeated them, capturing twenty cities from Aroer to the area around Minnith, up as far as Abel-keramim. This is how the Ammonites were conquered by the Israelites.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Jeremiah 31:4; Exodus 15:20; Judges 10:17"> When Jephthah arrived home in Mizpah, there came his daughter out to meet him, with tambourines and dancing! She was his only child—he had no son or daughter apart from her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Matthew 14:7–9; Genesis 37:29–30; Genesis 42:36–38"> The moment he saw her, he ripped his clothes in agony and cried out, “Oh no, my daughter! You have crushed me completely! You have destroyed me, for I made a solemn promise to the Lord and I can't go back on it.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="2 Samuel 18:19; Philippians 2:30; Romans 16:4"> She replied, “Father, you have made a solemn promise to the Lord. Do to me what you promised, for the Lord gave you victory over your enemies, the Ammonites.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="Luke 1:25; 1 Samuel 1:6"> Then she went on to say to him, “Just let me do this: let me walk through the hills for two months with my friends and grieve the fact that I'll never marry.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38"> “You can go,” he told her. He sent her away for two months, and she and her friends went into the hills and cried because she would never marry.</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Isaiah 66:3; Judges 11:31; Leviticus 27:28–29"> When the two months were over, she returned to her father, and he did to her as he had promised, and she was a virgin. This is the origin of the custom in Israel</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="1 Kings 9:25; Judges 5:11"> that every year the young women of Israel leave for four days to weep in commemoration of the daughter of Jephthah the Gileadite.</span>
</div>
</div>
