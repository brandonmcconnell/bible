<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Timothy 1:9; Philippians 3:14; Hebrews 4:14–15"> So, my brothers and sisters who live for God and who share in this heavenly calling, we need to think carefully about Jesus—the one we say is sent by God,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally, “apostle.” This word taken from the Greek meant one who is sent.">a</a></sup>and is the High Priest.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Numbers 12:7; 1 Timothy 3:15; Ephesians 2:22"> He was faithful to God in the work he was chosen to do, just like Moses was faithful to God in God's house.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="The word “house” here means more than the building: it refers to the members of the house, the household, the family. Here and in verse 5 Moses faithfulness as a servant in God's house is referencing Numbers 12:7.">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Corinthians 3:9; 1 Peter 2:5–7; Zechariah 6:12–13"> But Jesus deserves much greater glory than Moses, in the same way that the builder of a house deserves more credit than the house.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Hebrews 3:3; Hebrews 1:2; Esther 2:10"> Every house has its builder; God is the builder of everything.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Numbers 12:7; Hebrews 3:2; Exodus 14:31"> As a servant, Moses was faithful in God's house. He provided evidence of what would be announced later.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Corinthians 6:16; 1 Corinthians 3:16; Hebrews 10:35"> But Christ is a son, in charge of God's house. And we are God's house as long as we hold on with confidence to the hope we boast we believe in.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="PSALMS 95:7–11; Hebrews 3:15; Isaiah 55:3"> This is why the Holy Spirit says, “If you hear what God is saying to you today,</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Proverbs 28:14; Exodus 17:7; Zechariah 7:11–12"> don't have a hard-hearted attitude<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“Hard-hearted attitude,” or stubborn/obstinate.">c</a></sup>like the time you rebelled against him, when you tested him in the wilderness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 7:36; Deuteronomy 4:9; Exodus 19:4"> Your fathers put me through it, trying my patience, and they saw the evidence I gave them for forty years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="PSALMS 95:10; 2 Thessalonians 2:10–12; PSALMS 67:2"> That generation made me angry<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="As always, God uses human terms. We should not understand God as being angry as we are, especially when it comes to “losing our temper” and acting in unloving or irrational ways. The same comment applies in 3:11.">d</a></sup>and so I said, ‘They're always mistaken in what they think, and they don't know me or what I'm doing.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Deuteronomy 1:34–35; Hebrews 4:3; Numbers 32:10–13"> So in my frustration I vowed, ‘They shall not enter my rest.’”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="“Rest.” This concept is developed more in chapter 4 and is related to the Sabbath, the Promised Land, and God's invitation to come to him. While it is not the easiest of phrases, “entering rest” is perhaps the best translation since it retains the basis which is to be developed later, and includes all the various allusions. The quotation is fromPsalms 95:7-11.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Jeremiah 17:5; Jeremiah 7:24; Jeremiah 17:9"> Brothers and sisters, make sure that none of you has an evil mindset that's given up trusting in the God of life.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 5:11; Hebrews 10:24–25; 2 Timothy 4:2"> Encourage each other every day while you still have “today,” so that none of you will be deceived by sin and become hard-hearted.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Hebrews 3:6; Hebrews 6:11; Hebrews 12:10"> For we are partners with Christ as long as we hold on to our confidence in God from beginning to end.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Hebrews 3:7–8; PSALMS 95:7; Hebrews 10:38"> As Scripture says, “If you hear what God is saying to you today, don't have a hard-hearted attitude like the time you rebelled against him.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Quoting Psalms 95:7-8.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Numbers 14:2; Numbers 14:30; Numbers 26:65"> Who was it that rebelled against God, even though they heard what he said? Wasn't it all those who were led out of Egypt by Moses?</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Numbers 14:29; Numbers 26:64–65; Numbers 14:32–33"> Who was God upset with for forty years? Wasn't it those who sinned, those who were buried in the desert?</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Numbers 14:30; Hebrews 4:6; Deuteronomy 1:34–35"> Who was God speaking of when he vowed they should not enter into his rest? Wasn't it those who disobeyed him?</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Jude 1:5; John 3:36; Mark 16:16"> So we see that they were not able to enter because they didn't trust him.</span>

The quotation is fromPsalms 95:7-11.</li>
