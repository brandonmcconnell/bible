<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Genesis 14:18–20; PSALMS 57:2; Hebrews 6:20"> Melchizedek was king of Salem and priest of the Most High God. He met Abraham who was returning from defeating the kings and blessed him.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="See Genesis 14:18.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Numbers 18:21; PSALMS 85:10–11; 1 Samuel 8:17"> Abraham gave him a tithe of all that he had won. Melchizedek's name means “king of right” while king of Salem means “king of peace.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Hebrews 7:17; Hebrews 7:23–28; Exodus 6:20–27"> We don't have any information about his father or his mother or his family tree. We don't know when he was born or when he died. Like the Son of God he continues as a priest forever.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Genesis 14:20; Acts 2:29; Acts 7:8–9"> Think how great this man was for Abraham the patriarch to give him a tithe of what was won in battle.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Leviticus 27:30–33; 2 Chronicles 31:4–6; Numbers 18:21–32"> Yes, the sons of Levi who are priests were commanded by the law to receive a tithe from the people, their brothers and sisters, who are descended from Abraham.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Romans 4:13; Hebrews 7:3–4; Genesis 13:14–17"> But Melchizedek who doesn't share their ancestry received tithes from Abraham, and blessed the one who had God's promises.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Chronicles 30:27; Genesis 48:15–20; 1 Timothy 3:16"> There's no argument that the lesser person is blessed by the one who is greater.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Hebrews 6:20; Hebrews 5:6; Revelation 1:18"> In the one case tithes are received by men who die, but in the other by one who is said to be living.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Romans 5:12; Genesis 14:20; Hebrews 7:4"> So you could say that Levi, the one who receives tithes, has paid tithes through being a descendant of Abraham,</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Genesis 35:11; Hebrews 7:5; Genesis 46:26"> for he was yet to be born from his father<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Literally, “in his father's loins.”">b</a></sup>when Melchizedek met Abraham.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Hebrews 8:7; Galatians 2:21; Galatians 4:9"> Now if perfection could have been achieved through the priesthood of Levi (for that's how the law was received), what was the need for another priest to come following the order of Melchizedek, and not following the order of Aaron?</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Jeremiah 31:31–34; Isaiah 66:21; Ezekiel 16:61"> If the priesthood is changed, then the law needs to be changed too.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Hebrews 7:11; Hebrews 7:14; Numbers 17:5"> The one we're talking about comes from a different tribe, a tribe that has never provided priests to serve at the altar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Micah 5:2; Isaiah 11:1; Luke 3:33"> It's clear that our Lord is a descendant of Judah, and Moses said nothing about priests coming from this tribe.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="PSALMS 110:4; Hebrews 7:17–21; Hebrews 7:3"> What makes it even clearer is when another priest appears who is similar to Melchizedek,</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Revelation 1:18; Colossians 2:20; Hebrews 7:28"> and who didn't become a priest by virtue of his human ancestry but by the power of a life that cannot be ended.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="PSALMS 110:4; Hebrews 5:6; Hebrews 7:21"> That's why it says, “You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Quoting Psalms 110:4.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Romans 8:3; Acts 13:39; Galatians 4:9"> So the previous rule has been set aside because it was powerless and didn't work,</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Acts 13:39; Hebrews 4:16; Hebrews 9:9"> (for the law didn't make anything perfect). But now it's been replaced by a better hope by which we can come close to God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Numbers 18:7; Hebrews 5:6; PSALMS 110:4"> This<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Referring to the new way of approaching God.">d</a></sup>was not without an oath, even though those who become priests do so without an oath.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="PSALMS 110:4; Hebrews 7:17; Hebrews 6:16–18"> But he became a priest with an oath because God told him, “The Lord has taken a solemn vow and will not change his mind: You are a priest forever.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Quoting Psalms 110:4.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="1 Corinthians 11:25; Genesis 44:32; Hebrews 8:6–12"> This is how Jesus became the guarantee of an agreed relationship with God<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="“An agreed relationship with God.” This translates a single word in Greek that has traditionally been translated as “covenant.” However, the word “covenant” does not normally occur in everyday English and so has become a “theological” word. Much has been written about this concept and the terms used, and “covenant” has often been retained as there does not seem to be an effective way of explaining what is meant here. The covenant concept is extensively developed in chapters 8 and 9. Problems exist with alternative words. The word “contract” can mean the result of bargaining, which is not the case here. Similarly “treaty” or “agreement” when seen in human terms may refer to mutual negotiations. But here the word refers to something that is God's initiative, and certainly is not between equals. Perhaps a better concept is “a promise that is agreed to with corresponding obligations,” but such wording would be even more cumbersome.">f</a></sup>that is so much better.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Nehemiah 12:10–11; 1 Chronicles 6:3–14; Hebrews 7:8"> There have been many priests because death prevented them from being able to continue;</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="John 12:34; Hebrews 7:28; Isaiah 9:6–7"> but since Jesus lives forever, his priesthood is permanent.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Romans 8:34; 1 John 2:1–2; 1 Timothy 2:5"> As a result he is able to save completely those who come to God through him, living always to plead their case on their behalf.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="2 Corinthians 5:21; Hebrews 4:14–15; 1 Peter 2:22"> He is exactly the high priest we need: holy and without fault, pure and separate from sinners, and given a place in the highest heavens.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Hebrews 9:28; Hebrews 9:12; Hebrews 9:14"> Unlike those human high priests, he doesn't need to offer a daily sacrifice for his sins and then the sins of the people. He did this once, and for everyone, when he offered himself.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Hebrews 2:10; Hebrews 7:21; Hebrews 1:2"> The law appoints imperfect men as high priests, but God gave his solemn vow after the law, and appointed his Son, perfect forever.</span>

Much has been written about this concept and the terms used, and “covenant” has often been retained as there does not seem to be an effective way of explaining what is meant here. The covenant concept is extensively developed in chapters 8 and 9. Problems exist with alternative words. The word “contract” can mean the result of bargaining, which is not the case here. Similarly “treaty” or “agreement” when seen in human terms may refer to mutual negotiations. But here the word refers to something that is God's initiative, and certainly is not between equals. Perhaps a better concept is “a promise that is agreed to with corresponding obligations,” but such wording would be even more cumbersome.</li>
