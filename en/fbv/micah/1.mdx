<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Jeremiah 26:18; Isaiah 1:1; Hosea 1:1"> Here is the message that the Lord gave to Micah of Moresheth at the time when Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah were kings of Judah. This is what he saw concerning Samaria and Jerusalem.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Isaiah 1:2; PSALMS 11:4; Revelation 3:13"> Listen, all the nations! Pay attention, everyone on earth! The Lord God testifies against you from the Lord's holy Temple.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Amos 4:13; Isaiah 26:21; Deuteronomy 32:13"> Look! The Lord is coming, leaving his place and coming down, and is walking on the high places of the earth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Nahum 1:5; PSALMS 97:5; Judges 5:4"> The mountains melt beneath him, the valleys burst apart, like wax in front of a fire, like water rushing down a slope. All this is happening because of the rebellion of the descendants of Jacob, the sins of the people of Israel.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Amos 8:14; Jeremiah 2:19; 1 Thessalonians 2:15–16"> What is the rebellion of the descendants of Jacob? Isn't it what is happening in Samaria? Where are the idolatrous high places<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="The high places were where pagan gods were worshiped.">a</a></sup>of Judah? Aren't they right in Jerusalem?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Samaria was the capital of the northern kingdom, and Judah the capital of the southern. This indicates the extent of the apostasy.">b</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Ezekiel 13:14; Micah 3:12; Lamentations 4:1"> Therefore I will make Samaria a pile of rubble in a field, a place for planting vineyards. I will roll its stones down into the valley; I will lay bare its foundations.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Deuteronomy 23:18; Hosea 2:12; Hosea 8:6"> All their carved idols will be smashed to pieces. All that was earned by their temple prostitutes will be burned. All of their idols will be destroyed, for what she collected through the earnings of temple prostitutes will be taken away and used to pay other temple prostitutes.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Isaiah 22:4; Isaiah 20:2–4; Jeremiah 4:19"> Because of this I will weep and wail, I will walk barefoot and naked, and will howl like jackals and mourn like owls.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Jeremiah 30:11–15; Isaiah 8:7–8; 2 Chronicles 32:1–23"> Their<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Referring back to the people of Samaria.">c</a></sup>wound cannot be healed, it has extended to Judah, and reached right to the gates of Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Samuel 1:20; Jeremiah 6:26; Joshua 18:23"> Don't mention it in Gath; don't weep at all.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Gath was a Philistine city. The meaning is that this tragic situation should not be shared with Israel's enemies, so that they do not take advantage or gloat.">d</a></sup>People of Beth-le-aphrah, roll in the dust.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Rolling in the dust was a way of showing extreme grief. In addition, there is a wordplay on the names of the towns mentioned in this section, but some of the meanings have been lost.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Isaiah 20:4; Jeremiah 48:9; Micah 1:8"> Leave,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="This is thought by some to refer to the exile.">f</a></sup>people of Shaphir, naked and ashamed. People of Zanaan don't come out. People of Beth Ezel mourn, for you have lost your support.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Meaning uncertain.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Jeremiah 14:19; Micah 1:9; Amos 3:6"> The people of Maroth wait anxiously for relief, but disaster has come down from the Lord on the gate of Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Kings 18:17; 2 Chronicles 32:9; Joshua 10:3"> Harness the team of horses to the chariot, you people of Lachish, because the sins of the people of Jerusalem<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Literally, “daughter of Zion.”">h</a></sup>began with you, for the sins of Israel were first found in you.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Joshua 15:44; 2 Kings 16:8; 2 Samuel 8:2"> Send your goodbye gifts to Moresheth.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Micah's hometown. Some see these gifts as related to the dowry given to a daughter when she married, before she left. In other words, the people of Israel were saying goodbye to Moresheth—perhaps by enemy conquest since it appears to be close to the border with the Philistines.">i</a></sup>The town of Achzib is a deception to the kings of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Joshua 15:44; 1 Samuel 22:1; Joshua 15:35"> I will bring a conqueror to attack you, people of Moresheth. The leaders of Israel will go to Adullam.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Adullam was the cave where David hid when he was running away from king Saul (1 Samuel 22:1). In other words, the leaders of Israel will run away to hide.">j</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Isaiah 22:12; Job 1:20; Jeremiah 7:29"> Shave your heads, for the children you love will be taken away; make yourselves as bald as a vulture, for they will be exiled far away from you.</span>
</div>
</div>
