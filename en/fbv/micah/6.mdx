<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Exodus 12:51; Deuteronomy 7:8; Amos 2:10"> For I brought you out of the land of Egypt and rescued you from slavery. I sent Moses, Aaron, and Miriam to lead you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Joshua 5:9–10; Numbers 25:1; Judges 5:11"> My people, remember what Balak, king of Moab, planned to do, and what Balaam, son of Beor, told him<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Balak wanted Balaam to curse the Israelites, but Balaam was only able to speak blessings. See Numbers 22to Numbers 24.">a</a></sup>, and what happened from Shittim to Gilgal,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Between Shittim and Gilgal Israelites crossed the Jordan River.">b</a></sup>so you could know all the good things the Lord does.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="PSALMS 51:16–17; Romans 10:2–3; PSALMS 40:6–8"> What should I take with me when I approach the Lord, when I bow down before the God of heaven? Shall I come before him with burnt offerings, with calves a year old?</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Kings 16:3; 1 Samuel 15:22; PSALMS 51:16"> Will the Lord be pleased with thousands of rams, or ten thousand rivers of olive oil? Shall I sacrifice my firstborn child for my rebellion, my own flesh and blood for the sins I have committed?</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Deuteronomy 10:12–13; Proverbs 21:3; Colossians 3:12"> The Lord has told you what is good, and what the Lord asks from you: to do what is right, to love kindness, and to walk in humility<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Or “respectfully.”">c</a></sup>with your God.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Revelation 3:19; Hosea 14:9; Isaiah 30:27"> The voice of the Lord calls to the city—to respect your name is to be wise—pay attention to the rod and who summoned it.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="This is taken to mean the “rod” of punishment through the invasion by foreign armies.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Amos 3:10; Amos 8:5–6; Jeremiah 5:26–27"> Are there still ill-gotten gains in the houses of the wicked? Are they still using dishonest measures in selling grain?</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Hosea 12:7; Leviticus 19:36; Proverbs 16:11"> How can I accept those who use unfair scales and dishonest weights?</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Isaiah 1:23; Jeremiah 9:8; Hosea 7:13"> Your rich people make money by using violence; they tell lies and practice deceit.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Acts 12:23; Deuteronomy 28:21–22; Hosea 13:16"> So I will strike you to make you ill; I will ruin you because of your sins.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Hosea 4:10; Leviticus 26:26; Isaiah 65:13"> You will eat, but never be satisfied; inside you will still feel hungry. Even though you try to save money, what you save won't be worth anything because I will give it to the sword.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="“Give it to the sword.” This could mean either that what is saved will be destroyed, or that it will be taken by the invaders.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Zephaniah 1:13; Amos 5:11; Jeremiah 12:13"> You will sow, but you won't reap; you will press your olives, but not use the oil; you will make your wine, but drink none of it.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Jeremiah 51:51; 1 Kings 16:25–33; Jeremiah 7:24"> You have kept the laws of Omri, and adopted all the practices of the house of Ahab,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="King Omri and his son Ahab were two of the most evil kings of Israel.">f</a></sup>you have followed their ways. So I will make your country desolate and the people who live there an object of scorn; you will bear the shame of my people.</span>
</div>
