<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Esther 5:3; John 16:24; Esther 5:6"> At this second dinner, as they were drinking wine, the king asked Esther again, “What are you really asking for, Queen Esther? It will be given to you. What do you want? You shall have it, as much as half my empire!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Jeremiah 38:26; PSALMS 122:6–9; Esther 7:7"> Queen Esther answered, “If the king looks on me favorably, and if it please Your Majesty to grant me my life, that is my request; and the lives of my people, that is what I ask.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Esther 3:9; Deuteronomy 28:68; Esther 3:13"> For my people and I have been sold<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“Sold”: or, “handed over.”">a</a></sup>to be destroyed, killed, and annihilated. If we had only been sold as slaves, I would have kept quiet, because our suffering would not have justified disturbing the king.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Or “although our suffering could not have compensated for what the king lost.”">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Acts 5:3; Genesis 27:33; Job 9:24"> The king asked Queen Esther, demanding to know, “Who is this? Where is the man who has dared to do this?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Job 18:5–12; 2 Thessalonians 2:8; PSALMS 139:19–22"> “The man, the opponent, the enemy, is this evil Haman!” Esther replied. Haman shook with terror in front of the king and the queen.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Esther 1:12; Proverbs 19:12; Revelation 3:9"> The king was furious. He got up, leaving his wine, and went out into the palace garden. Haman stayed behind to beg for his life from Queen Esther, for he realized the king planned an evil<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="The same word is used here as Esther uses to describe Haman in verse 6.">c</a></sup>end for him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Esther 1:6; Esther 6:12; Job 9:24"> When the king came back in from the palace garden to the dining room, Haman had thrown himself<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Thrown himself”: literally, “had fallen,” but this was not an accidental fall but a deliberate attempt to ask for mercy. However, this only compounded his guilt in the king's eyes.">d</a></sup>on the couch where Queen Esther was. The king shouted out, “Is he even going to rape the queen here in the palace, right in front of me?” As soon as the king said this, the servants covered Haman's face.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="PSALMS 7:15–16; Proverbs 11:5–6; Esther 1:10"> Then Harbonah, one of the eunuchs attending the king, said: “Haman set up a pole beside his house for Mordecai, the one whose report saved the king's life. The pole is fifty cubits high.” “Impale him on it!” the king ordered.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="PSALMS 7:16; Daniel 6:24; Ezekiel 5:13"> So they impaled Haman on the pole that he had set up for Mordecai. Then the anger of the king died down.</span>
