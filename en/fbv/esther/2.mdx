<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Daniel 6:14–18; Esther 7:10; Esther 1:12–21"> Later on, after all this had happened, King Xerxes' anger subsided and he thought about Vashti and what she'd done, and the decree issued against her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Kings 1:2; Genesis 12:14; Esther 6:14"> His advisors suggested to him,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="This suggestion may have been more for themselves, since if the king did bring Vashti back, their lives could have been in danger as the architects of her downfall.">a</a></sup>“Why not order a search to find beautiful young virgins for Your Majesty?</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Esther 2:12–15; Esther 2:8–9; Esther 1:1–2"> Your Majesty should appoint officers in each province of his empire to gather all the beautiful young women and bring them to the king's harem at the fortress of Susa. They should be placed under the supervision of Hegai, the king's eunuch in charge of the women, and they should be given beauty treatments.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Matthew 14:6; Matthew 20:16; Esther 1:21"> The young woman the king finds most attractive can become queen in place of Vashti.” The king thought this was a good idea, so he acted upon it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Samuel 16:5; 1 Samuel 9:1; Esther 10:3"> There was a Jewish man living in the fortress of Susa named Mordecai, son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, a Benjamite</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Kings 24:14–15; 2 Chronicles 36:20; Jeremiah 24:1"> who was among those taken prisoner with King Jehoiachin of Judah and carried into exile from Jerusalem by King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Esther 2:15; Esther 1:11; 2 Corinthians 6:18"> He had brought up Hadassah (or Esther),<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Hadassah was her Hebrew name, Esther her Persian name.">b</a></sup>his uncle's daughter, because she had no father or mother. The young woman had a beautiful figure and was very attractive. After her father and mother died, Mordecai had adopted her as his own daughter.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Esther 2:3; Esther 2:15"> When the king's order and decree had been announced, many young women were brought to the fortress of Susa under the supervision of Hegai. Esther was also taken to the king's palace and placed under the care of Hegai, who was in charge of the women.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Genesis 39:21; Proverbs 16:7; Esther 2:12"> Esther caught his eye and he treated her favorably. He quickly arranged beauty treatments and special food for her. He also provided her with seven specially-chosen maids from the king's palace, and moved her and her maids to the best location in the harem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Esther 2:20; Matthew 10:16; Esther 3:8"> Esther had not let anyone know her nationality or who her family was, because Mordecai had ordered her not to.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Genesis 37:14; 1 Samuel 17:18; Acts 15:36"> Each day Mordecai spent time walking around in front of the courtyard of the harem so he could find out how Esther was doing and what was happening to her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 4:4–5; Proverbs 7:17; Isaiah 57:9"> Before it was the turn of a young woman to go to King Xerxes, she had to complete twelve months of beauty treatments for women that were required: six months with oil of myrrh, and six with perfumed oils and ointments.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> When it was time for the young woman to go to the king, she was given whatever<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Probably referring to clothing and jewelry.">c</a></sup>she asked for to take with her from the harem to the king's palace.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Esther 4:11; Genesis 34:19; Isaiah 62:4–5"> In the evening she would go, and in the morning she would return to a different harem under the supervision of Shaashgaz, who was the king's eunuch in charge of the concubines. She would not go back to be with the king again unless he was particularly attracted to her and called for her by name.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Song of Songs 6:9; Esther 2:3; Esther 9:29"> (Esther was the daughter of Abihail, Mordecai's uncle. Mordecai had adopted her as his own daughter.) When it was Esther's turn to go to the king, she didn't ask for anything to take with her except what Hegai advised. (He was the king's eunuch in charge of the women.) Esther was looked on with admiration by everybody.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Esther 8:9; Esther 2:3; Esther 2:1"> Esther was taken to King Xerxes into his royal palace, in the tenth month, the month of Tebeth, in the seventh year of his reign.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="PSALMS 113:7–8; Luke 1:48–52; Ezekiel 17:24"> The king loved Esther more than all the other women. He treated her more favorably and with greater kindness than all of the other virgins. So he placed the royal crown on her head and made her queen instead of Vashti.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Song of Songs 3:11; Luke 14:8; Judges 14:10–17"> The king gave a great feast for all his officials and administrators—Esther's feast.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="In the Septuagint this is identified as a wedding feast.">d</a></sup>He also declared it a holiday throughout the provinces and distributed generous gifts.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Esther 2:21; Esther 2:3–4; Esther 5:13"> Even though there had been a second gathering of virgins,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Various interpretations have been given to this phrase. However, 2:3records the decree to “gather virgins” and this could be a second phase of this process. Note also that there is no definite article before virgins in this verse, so likely it would not refer to the existing group.">e</a></sup>and Mordecai had been given a position by the king,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="“Given a position by the king”: literally, “sitting at the king's gate.” Also in verse 21 and subsequently.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Ephesians 6:1–3; Esther 2:10; Esther 2:7"> Esther still did not let anyone know about her family or her nationality, as Mordecai had ordered her. She followed Mordecai's instructions just as she did when he brought her up.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Esther 6:2; 1 Kings 16:9; 2 Kings 21:23"> At that time, as Mordecai was doing his work at the palace gate, Bigthan and Teresh, two eunuchs who guarded the entrance to the king's rooms, became furious with King Xerxes and looked for a way to assassinate him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Esther 6:1–2; Romans 11:33; Ecclesiastes 10:20"> Mordecai found out about the plot and reported it to Queen Esther. Esther in turn told the king on Mordecai's behalf.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Genesis 40:22; Genesis 40:19; Esther 10:2"> When the plot was investigated and found to be true, both men were impaled on poles.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Execution by impaling was the usual method, not by hanging with a noose.">g</a></sup>This was recorded in the official Book of Records by order of the king.</span>
