<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Samuel 19:20; 2 Chronicles 15:1; 1 Samuel 19:23"> and as he looked at Israel camped there according to their respective tribes, the Spirit of God came on him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Numbers 22:31; Numbers 24:16; Numbers 23:7"> He gave a declaration, saying:</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Revelation 1:17; Revelation 1:10; Numbers 12:6"> “This is the prophecy of Balaam, son of Beor, the prophecy of a man who sees with eyes that are wide open,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“Wide open”: the word only occurs here and in verse 15. <span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> How well set out your tents are, Jacob; the places where you live, Israel!</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="PSALMS 1:3; Isaiah 58:11; PSALMS 104:16"> They look like wooded valleys, like gardens beside a river, like aloe trees the Lord has planted, like cedars at the water's edge.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Samuel 5:12; 1 Chronicles 14:2; Revelation 17:1"> The Israelites will pour out bucketfuls of water; their descendants will have plenty of water. Their king will be greater than King Agag; their kingdom will be glorious.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="PSALMS 45:5; PSALMS 2:9; Numbers 14:9"> God led them out of Egypt with great power, as strong as an ox, destroying enemy nations, breaking their bones, piercing them with arrows.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Genesis 12:3; Genesis 27:29; Genesis 49:9"> They are like a lion that crouches and lies down. They are like a lioness that nobody dares to disturb. Those who bless you will be blessed; those who curse you will be cursed.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Numbers 23:11; Ezekiel 21:14; Ezekiel 21:17"> Balak got angry with Balaam, and beat his fists together. He told Balaam, “I brought you here to curse my enemies, and now look! You keep on blessing them, doing it three times.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Numbers 22:17; Numbers 22:37; Hebrews 11:24–26"> Leave right now! Go home! I promised to pay you well, but the Lord has made sure you wouldn't receive any payment.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Numbers 22:18; Numbers 22:38"> But Balaam said to Balak, “Didn't I already explain to the messengers you sent</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Numbers 16:28; Numbers 22:18; Numbers 22:20"> that even if you gave me your whole palace full of silver and gold, I couldn't do anything I wanted or disobey the command of the Lord my God in any way? I can only say what the Lord tells me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Micah 6:5; Revelation 2:14; Genesis 49:1"> Listen! I'm going back home now to my own people, but first let me warn you what these Israelites are going to do to your people in the future.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Numbers 24:3–4; Numbers 23:7; Matthew 13:35"> Then Balaam gave a declaration, saying, “This is the prophecy of Balaam, son of Beor, the prophecy of a man whose eyes are wide open</span>

<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Revelation 22:16; Luke 1:32–33; Genesis 49:10"> I see him, but this isn't now. I observe him, but this isn't close at hand. He will crush the heads of the Moabites, and destroy all the people of Seth.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“The people of Seth”: if this was taken literally, such a description would also include the Israelites as descendants of Seth. <span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Amos 9:12; Genesis 27:29; 2 Samuel 8:14"> The country of Edom will be conquered, his enemy Seir<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Seir was the old name for Edom.">c</a></sup>will be conquered, and the Israelites will be victorious.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Genesis 49:10; Micah 5:2; PSALMS 2:1–12"> A ruler from Jacob will come and destroy those left in the city.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Exodus 17:14; Judges 6:3; 1 Chronicles 4:43"> Balaam turned his attention to the Amalekites and gave this declaration about them, saying, “Amalek was first among the nations, but they will end up being destroyed.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Genesis 15:19; Judges 1:16; Job 29:18"> He turned his attention to the Kenites and gave this declaration about them, saying, “Where you live is safe and secure, like a nest on a cliff-face.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="PSALMS 83:8; Genesis 10:22; Ezra 4:2"> But Kain will be burned down when Assyria conquers you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Numbers 23:23; Malachi 3:2; 2 Kings 5:1"> Then Balaam gave another declaration, saying, “It's a tragedy! Who can survive when God does this?</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Genesis 10:4; Numbers 24:20; Daniel 10:20"> Ships will be sent from Cyprus to attack Assyria and Eber, but they too will be permanently destroyed.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Numbers 31:8; Joshua 13:22; Numbers 24:11"> Then Balaam left and returned to his own country, and Balak left too.</span>

## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">24:4</span> “Wide open”: the word only occurs here and in verse 15. It is variously translated as “closed” or “open,” however the meaning is essentially clear in that Balaam is referring to prophetic insight. The Latin Vulgate has “eyes that are blocked” while the Greek Septuagint has “who truly sees.”</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">24:17</span> “The people of Seth”: if this was taken literally, such a description would also include the Israelites as descendants of Seth. In the context of Hebrew parallel poetry here it probably refers specifically to the Moabites. In the parallel passage in Jeremiah 48:45it reads “rebellious people.”</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">24:18</span> Seir was the old name for Edom.</li>
