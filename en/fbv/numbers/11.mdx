<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Numbers 21:5; Numbers 16:35; 2 Kings 1:12"> It wasn't long before the people started to complain about how much they were suffering. When the Lord heard what they were saying, he became angry. Fire from the Lord burned them, destroying some on the edge of the camp.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Numbers 21:7; James 5:16; Numbers 16:45–48"> The people cried to Moses for help. He prayed to the Lord and the fire subsided.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Deuteronomy 9:22"> So that place was named Taberah,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“Taberah” means “burning.”">a</a></sup>because the fire from the Lord burned them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Corinthians 10:6; Exodus 12:38; PSALMS 106:14"> A group of troublemakers<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Generally associated with the “mixed multitude” that left Egypt with the Israelites (see Exodus 12:38)">b</a></sup>among them had such intense food cravings they affected the Israelites who started crying again, asking “Who's going to get us some meat to eat?</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Exodus 16:3; PSALMS 17:14; Philippians 3:19"> We think back to all the fish we ate in Egypt that didn't cost us anything, as well as the cucumbers, melons, leeks, onions, and garlic.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Numbers 21:5; 2 Samuel 13:4"> We're fading away here! The only thing we ever see is this manna!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Exodus 16:31; Genesis 2:12; Exodus 16:14–15"> Manna looked like coriander seeds, light in color like gum resin.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Exodus 16:31; Exodus 16:16–18; Exodus 16:23"> The people would go out and collect it, grind it up in a mill or crush it in a mortar. Then they would boil it in a pot and make it into flatbread. It tasted like pastries made with the best olive oil.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Exodus 16:13–14; PSALMS 78:23–25; Deuteronomy 32:2"> When the dew came down on the camp at night the manna would come down with it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="PSALMS 78:21; Numbers 16:27; Numbers 14:1–2"> Moses heard all the families crying at the entrance to their tents. The Lord became very angry, and Moses was also upset.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Deuteronomy 1:12; Exodus 5:22; Jeremiah 15:10"> He asked the Lord, “Why have you made things so tough for me, your servant? Why are you so unhappy with me that you have placed on me the heavy responsibility for all these people?</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Isaiah 49:23; Isaiah 40:11; 1 Thessalonians 2:7"> Are they my children? Did I give birth to them, so you could tell me, ‘Hold them close to your chest like a nurse carrying a baby’ and take them to the land you promised to give to their forefathers?</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Matthew 15:33; Mark 8:4; John 6:5–9"> Where am I supposed to get meat for all of them? They keep on complaining to me, ‘Get us some meat to eat!’</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Exodus 18:18; Deuteronomy 1:9–12; 2 Corinthians 2:16"> I can't go on carrying all these people by myself—it's just too much.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Philippians 1:20–24; 1 Kings 19:4; Jonah 4:3"> If this is the way you're going to treat me, then please just kill me now so I don't have to face how depressed I've become. Please grant me this one request.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Exodus 24:1; Exodus 24:9; Deuteronomy 16:18"> The Lord told Moses, “Bring before me seventy Israelite elders who you know are respected as leaders by the people. Take them to the Tent of Meeting. They will stand there with you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Numbers 11:25; 1 Samuel 10:6; 1 Corinthians 12:4–11"> I will come down and talk with you there. I will take some of the Spirit you have and give it to them. They will take some of the responsibility for the people so you won't have to bear it alone.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Exodus 19:10; Acts 7:39; Exodus 16:3–7"> Tell the people: Purify yourselves, for tomorrow you'll have meat to eat, because you were complaining and the Lord heard you saying, ‘Who's going to get us some meat to eat? We were better off in Egypt!’ So the Lord is going to provide you with meat to eat.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> You're going to eat it, not for just a day or two, and not for five or ten or twenty days.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="PSALMS 106:15; Numbers 21:5; PSALMS 78:27–30"> You're going to eat it for a whole month until it makes you vomit and it comes out through your nostrils, because you have rejected the Lord who is right here with you, complaining to him by saying, ‘Why on earth did we ever leave Egypt?’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Exodus 12:37; Numbers 1:46; Genesis 12:2"> But Moses replied, “Here I am with 600,000 people and you're telling me, ‘I'm going to give them meat and they'll eat it for a month’?</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Matthew 15:33; 2 Kings 7:2; John 6:9"> Even if all our flocks and herds were slaughtered, would that be enough for them? Even if all the fish in the sea were caught, would that be enough for them?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Isaiah 59:1; Isaiah 50:2; Genesis 18:14"> “Doesn't the Lord have the power to do that?” the Lord responded. “Now you're going to find out whether what I've said will happen or not!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Numbers 11:16; Numbers 11:26"> Moses went and shared with the people what the Lord said. He summoned seventy elders of the people and had them stand around the tent.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Numbers 11:17; Joel 2:28–29; 1 Samuel 10:10"> Then the Lord came down and talked to him. The Lord took some of the Spirit Moses had and gave it to them. They prophesied, but this didn't ever happen again.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Jeremiah 36:5; 1 Samuel 20:26; 1 Samuel 10:22"> However, two men named Eldad and Medad had stayed behind in the camp. The Spirit came on them too. (They had been put on the list of the seventy elders, but they hadn't gone to the tent. But they prophesied where they were in the camp anyway.)</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27"> A young lad ran and told Moses, “Eldad and Medad are prophesying in the camp.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Mark 9:38–40; Luke 9:49–50; John 3:26"> Joshua, son of Nun, who had been Moses' assistant since he was young, reacted, saying, “Moses, my lord, you have to stop them!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:5; Matthew 9:37–38; Luke 10:2"> “Are you jealous for my reputation?” Moses replied. “I wish that every one of the Lord's people were prophets and that the Lord would give his Spirit to all of them!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30"> Then Moses went back to the camp with the elders of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Exodus 16:13; PSALMS 105:40; PSALMS 78:26–29"> The Lord sent a wind that blew quail in from the sea and deposited them near the camp. They covered the ground to a depth of about two cubits and extended for a day's journey in every direction from the camp.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Ezekiel 45:11; Exodus 16:36"> All through that day and night, and all through the next day, the people went on collecting the quail. Everyone collected at least ten homers,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Estimated at a volume of 220 liters.">c</a></sup>and they spread them out to dry all around the camp.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="PSALMS 78:30–31; PSALMS 106:14–15; Numbers 25:9"> But while the people were still biting into the meat, before they even chewed it down, the Lord showed his burning anger against them, killing some of them with a severe disease.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Deuteronomy 9:22; 1 Corinthians 10:6; Numbers 33:16"> They named that place Kibroth-hattaavah,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Meaning “graves of craving.”">d</a></sup>because that was where they buried the people who had these intense food cravings.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Numbers 33:17; Numbers 12:16; Deuteronomy 1:1"> Then they moved on from Kibroth-hattaavah to Hazeroth, where they stayed for some time.</span>
