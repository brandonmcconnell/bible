<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:39; 1 Timothy 4:14; 1 Corinthians 12:1"> Make love your most important objective! But also do your best to gain spiritual gifts, especially the ability to speak God's message.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally, “prophesy,” but in the sense of telling the good news rather than foretelling the future. The terms “speaks God's message,” “God's prophetic message,” or “speaks for God” are used here.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Mark 16:17; Acts 19:6; Acts 10:46"> Those who speak in a tongue<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="This is clearly not the use of normal human language. There is much debate over this phenomenon. Clearly the early church was gifted with the ability to speak and be understood in different human languages as Acts 2 makes clear. However, some kind of “ecstatic utterance” appears to be in view here. Its practice in Corinth was being abused, and so Paul has to counteract the problem here.">b</a></sup>are not talking to people, but to God, because nobody can understand them as they speak mysteries in the Spirit.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:12; 1 Timothy 4:13; Jude 1:20"> However, the words of those who speak for God build people up—they provide encouragement and comfort.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:22; 1 Corinthians 14:26; Mark 16:17"> Those that speak in a tongue only build themselves up, but those who speak God's message build up the church. I would like it if you all spoke in tongues, but I'd prefer if you could speak God's message.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Corinthians 12:28–30; 1 Corinthians 12:10; Mark 16:17"> Those who speak for God are more important than those who speak in tongues, unless they interpret what's been said, so that the church can be built up.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Ephesians 1:17; Romans 6:17; 2 Timothy 3:16"> Brothers and sisters, if I come to you speaking in tongues, what benefit would I be to you unless I bring you some revelation, or knowledge, or prophetic message, or teaching?</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:8; Luke 7:32; 1 Corinthians 13:1"> Even when it comes to something non-living such as musical instruments like a flute or a harp—if they don't produce clear notes, how will you know what tune is being played?</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Numbers 10:9; Ezekiel 33:3–6; Jeremiah 4:19"> Similarly, if the trumpet doesn't give a clear sound, who will get ready for battle?</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Corinthians 9:26"> It's the same situation for you—unless you speak using words that are easy to understand, who will know what you're saying? What you say will be lost on the wind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> There are surely many languages in this world, and there is meaning in every one of them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Acts 28:2; Romans 1:14; Colossians 3:11"> If I don't understand the language, those who speak make no sense to me, and I make no sense to them.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Literally, I am a barbarian to the one who speaks, and the one who speaks is a barbarian to me. The very word “barbarian” comes from the idea that sounds being made make no sense—“baa-baa” etc.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:26; 1 Corinthians 14:3–4; 1 Corinthians 14:1"> It's the same for you—if you are keen to have spiritual gifts, try to have many of those that will build up the church.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Mark 11:24; 1 Corinthians 12:30; John 14:13–14"> Anyone who speaks in a tongue should pray that they're able to translate what they say.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:2; 1 Corinthians 14:15–16; 1 Corinthians 14:19"> For if I pray out loud in a tongue, my spirit is praying, but it does nothing for my understanding!</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Colossians 3:16; John 4:23–24; Ephesians 6:18"> So then, what should I do? I will pray “in the Spirit,” but I will pray with my mind too. I will sing “in the Spirit,” but I will sing with my mind too.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Paul here seems to be using the phraseology of some of those in Corinth who were proud of being “in the Spirit” as if that was superior to anything else. Paul points out that being “in the Spirit” is not helpful unless it produces understanding.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Chronicles 16:36; PSALMS 106:48; Revelation 5:14"> For if you only pray “in the Spirit,” how can ordinary people that don't have understanding say “Amen” after your prayer of thanks, since they haven't a clue what you said?</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:4; 1 Corinthians 14:6"> You might have said a great prayer of thanks, but the other hasn't been helped!</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:4–6; 1 Corinthians 4:7"> I thank God that I can speak in tongues more than all of you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:21–22; 1 Corinthians 14:4"> But in church I would rather speak five understandable words to teach others than ten thousand words in a tongue nobody understands.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Peter 2:2; 1 Corinthians 13:11; Romans 16:19"> Brothers and sisters, don't think like children. Be as innocent as babies in regard to evil, but be grown up in your understanding.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Isaiah 28:11–12; Jeremiah 5:15; Deuteronomy 28:49"> As Scripture records, “‘I will speak to my people through other languages and the lips of foreigners, but even then they won't listen to me,’ says the Lord.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Quoting Isaiah 28:11-12.">e</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Acts 2:6–12; Mark 16:17; 1 Corinthians 14:1"> Speaking in tongues is a sign, not to believers, but those who don't believe. Speaking God's prophetic message is the opposite: it is not for those who don't believe, but for believers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Acts 2:13; Acts 26:24; 1 Corinthians 11:18"> If the whole church is meeting together and you are all speaking in tongues, and some people come in who don't understand, or people who don't believe, won't they say that you are insane?</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 2:37; 1 Corinthians 2:15; Hebrews 4:12–13"> But if everyone is speaking God's message, and someone comes in who isn't a believer, or someone who doesn't understand, they will be convinced and called to account by everyone's words.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Isaiah 45:14; Zechariah 8:23; PSALMS 72:11"> Their secret thoughts will be revealed, so they will fall to their knees<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Literally, “fall on his face.”">f</a></sup>and worship God, affirming that God is truly among you.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Ephesians 5:19; Romans 14:19; 1 Corinthians 12:7–10"> So then, brothers and sisters, what should you do? When you meet together, different people will sing, or teach, or share a special message, or speak in a tongue, or give an interpretation. But everything should be done to build up and encourage the church.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:26"> If anyone wants to speak in a tongue, make it just two, or three at the most, taking turns, and someone should interpret what is said.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> If there's no one there to interpret, those who speak in tongues should keep quiet in church and only speak to themselves, and God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="1 Corinthians 12:10; 1 Thessalonians 5:19–21; 1 John 4:1–3"> Similarly, have two or three of those who give God's prophetic message speak, and let everyone else think about what was said.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 5:19–20; Job 33:31–33; Job 32:11"> However, if a special revelation comes to someone who is sitting down, then the first speaker should give way to them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 5:14; 1 Corinthians 14:3; 1 Thessalonians 5:11"> You can all speak for God, one at a time, so that everyone can learn and be encouraged.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Acts 4:19–20; 1 John 4:1; 1 Samuel 10:10–13"> It is for those who speak for God to control their prophetic inspiration,</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="James 3:17–18; 1 Corinthians 14:40; 2 Thessalonians 3:16"> for God is not a God of disorder but of peace and quiet.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="This statement is to deal with the situation where people are claiming special revelation/inspiration and therefore wanting precedence—with the obvious arguments that would result.">g</a></sup>This is the way it should be in all the churches of God's people.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="1 Timothy 2:11–12; Genesis 3:16; Numbers 30:3–13"> “Women should stay quiet in the churches—they shouldn't speak. They should respect their situation, as the laws states.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Ephesians 5:12; 1 Peter 3:7; Ephesians 5:25–27"> If they want to learn they can do so at home, asking their husbands. It is not proper<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="“Not proper.” By using this term (which also can mean shameful or disgraceful) Paul reveals this is related to the cultural context.">h</a></sup>for women to speak in church.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="The reason for placing 14:34-35 in quote marks is the understanding that this is the position outlined in the letter from Corinth, and Paul is quoting them before refuting their argument. The fact that just 3 chapters earlier in 11:5Paul refers to women praying and prophesying indicates that this statement cannot be read as a blanket ban on women speaking in church. From the context it appears possible that women in the church at Corinth were questioning and arguing, being part of the disorder he mentions, and this suggested solution in the letter from the church at Corinth is what Paul is addressing here.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="Acts 18:1–17; Zechariah 14:8; Acts 17:15"> What? Did the word of God begin with you? Are you the only ones it came to?</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="1 John 4:6; 2 Corinthians 10:7; 2 Peter 3:2"> Anyone who thinks they are a prophet, or that they have some spiritual gift, should be aware that what I'm writing to you is a command of the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="2 Timothy 4:3–4; Hosea 4:17; Revelation 22:11–12"> Those who ignore this will themselves be ignored.</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="1 Corinthians 12:31; 1 Corinthians 14:1; 1 Thessalonians 5:20"> So my brothers and sisters, make it your aim to speak for God. Don't prohibit speaking in tongues.</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="Colossians 2:5; Titus 1:5; 1 Corinthians 14:26–33"> Just make sure everything done is done properly and in an orderly manner.</span>
</div>

## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">14:1</span> Literally, “prophesy,” but in the sense of telling the good news rather than foretelling the future. The terms “speaks God's message,” “God's prophetic message,” or “speaks for God” are used here.</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">14:2</span> This is clearly not the use of normal human language. There is much debate over this phenomenon. Clearly the early church was gifted with the ability to speak and be understood in different human languages as Acts 2 makes clear. However, some kind of “ecstatic utterance” appears to be in view here. Its practice in Corinth was being abused, and so Paul has to counteract the problem here.</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">14:11</span> Literally, I am a barbarian to the one who speaks, and the one who speaks is a barbarian to me. The very word “barbarian” comes from the idea that sounds being made make no sense—“baa-baa” etc.</li>
<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">14:15</span> Paul here seems to be using the phraseology of some of those in Corinth who were proud of being “in the Spirit” as if that was superior to anything else. Paul points out that being “in the Spirit” is not helpful unless it produces understanding.</li>
<li id="fn-e"><a href="#fnref-e" className="fnb">e</a> <span className="fnr">14:21</span> Quoting Isaiah 28:11-12.</li>
<li id="fn-f"><a href="#fnref-f" className="fnb">f</a> <span className="fnr">14:25</span> Literally, “fall on his face.”</li>
<li id="fn-g"><a href="#fnref-g" className="fnb">g</a> <span className="fnr">14:33</span> This statement is to deal with the situation where people are claiming special revelation/inspiration and therefore wanting precedence—with the obvious arguments that would result.</li>
<li id="fn-h"><a href="#fnref-h" className="fnb">h</a> <span className="fnr">14:35</span> “Not proper.” By using this term (which also can mean shameful or disgraceful) Paul reveals this is related to the cultural context.</li>
<li id="fn-i"><a href="#fnref-i" className="fnb">i</a> <span className="fnr">14:35</span> The reason for placing 14:34-35 in quote marks is the understanding that this is the position outlined in the letter from Corinth, and Paul is quoting them before refuting their argument. The fact that just 3 chapters earlier in 11:5Paul refers to women praying and prophesying indicates that this statement cannot be read as a blanket ban on women speaking in church. From the context it appears possible that women in the church at Corinth were questioning and arguing, being part of the disorder he mentions, and this suggested solution in the letter from the church at Corinth is what Paul is addressing here.</li>
</ol>
