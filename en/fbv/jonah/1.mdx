<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Luke 11:32; Luke 11:29–30; 2 Kings 14:25"> The Lord spoke<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally, “the word of the Lord came to...” indicating a specific message delivered to a prophet.">a</a></sup>to Jonah the son of Amittai, telling him,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Genesis 18:20–21; Genesis 10:11; Isaiah 58:1"> “Go immediately to the great city of Nineveh and condemn it because I have seen the wickedness of its people.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="PSALMS 139:7–12; Genesis 4:16; Joshua 19:46"> But Jonah left and ran away to Tarshish<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Probably Tartessus on the southern coast of Spain.">b</a></sup>to get away from the Lord.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Literally, “from before the face of the Lord.” He was trying to remove himself from God's presence, showing that he was rejecting God's call.">c</a></sup>He went to Joppa where he found a ship leaving for Tarshish. He paid the fare and went on board to sail to Tarshish in order to get away from the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="PSALMS 107:23–31; Matthew 8:24–27; Exodus 10:19"> But the Lord sent a terrible wind across the sea, creating a violent storm that threatened to break up the ship.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Jeremiah 2:28; Acts 27:18–19; 1 Kings 18:26"> The sailors were terrified and each of them prayed to their own god to save them. They threw the cargo overboard to make the ship lighter. Meanwhile, Jonah had gone down into the hold where he had laid down and fallen fast asleep.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Jonah 3:9; PSALMS 107:28–29; Ephesians 5:14"> The ship's captain went to Jonah and asked him, “How can you be sleeping? Get up and pray to your God. Maybe he will take notice of what's happening to us and we won't drown.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Proverbs 16:33; Joshua 7:13–18; 1 Samuel 10:20–21"> Then the sailors said to each other, “Let's draw lots<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="A process similar to drawing straws.">d</a></sup>so we can find out who's to blame for this disaster that's fallen on us.” They drew lots and Jonah's name was came up.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Genesis 47:3; 1 Samuel 30:13; Joshua 7:19"> So they asked him, “Tell us who is responsible for this trouble that we're in. What do you do for a living? Where are you from? What is your country? What is your nationality?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="PSALMS 146:5–6; PSALMS 95:5–6; Nehemiah 9:6"> “I am a Hebrew,” Jonah replied, “and I worship<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Literally, “fear.”">e</a></sup>the Lord, the God of the heavens, the sea, and the land.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Jonah 1:3; Job 27:22; Joshua 7:25"> The sailors became even more terrified and said to Jonah, “What have you done?” because Jonah had explained to them that he was running away from the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Samuel 21:1–6; Micah 6:6–7; 1 Samuel 6:2–3"> “What shall we do to you to calm the storm?” they asked him, since the storm was getting worse.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Samuel 24:17; 1 Chronicles 21:17; Joshua 7:12"> “Pick me up and throw me into the sea,” Jonah replied. “Then the sea will become calm, because I know it's because of me that you're in this terrible storm.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Proverbs 21:30; Job 34:29"> Instead the sailors tried to row to get back to the shore, but they couldn't because the sea had become so wild from the worsening storm.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="PSALMS 115:3; PSALMS 135:6; Ephesians 1:11"> Then they called out to the Lord,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="The sailors use the same name for God as Jonah did, Yahweh, showing that they believe him to be responsible.">f</a></sup>“Lord! Please don't kill us for sacrificing this man's life or for spilling innocent blood, for you, Lord, made it happen.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Or “it pleased you Lord to do this.”">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Luke 8:24; PSALMS 93:3–4; PSALMS 107:29"> So they picked Jonah up and threw him into the sea, and it became calm.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="PSALMS 50:14; Genesis 8:20; Daniel 6:26"> The sailors were overcome with fear. They offered a sacrifice to the Lord and made many promises<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Or “vows.”">h</a></sup>to him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Matthew 12:40; Luke 11:30; Matthew 16:4"> The Lord sent a huge fish<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Note that a whale is not mentioned.">i</a></sup>to swallow Jonah. Jonah spent three days and nights in the belly of the fish.</span>
