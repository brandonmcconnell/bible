<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Luke 4:16–30; Matthew 13:54–58; Matthew 2:23"> Jesus left and went home to Nazareth with his disciples.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="John 7:15; John 6:42; Acts 4:13–14"> On Sabbath he began teaching in the synagogue, and many of those who were listening were surprised. “Where does he get these ideas?” they asked. “What's this wisdom he's been given? Where does he get the power to do miracles?</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Matthew 12:46; Matthew 13:55–57; John 6:42"> Isn't this the carpenter, Mary's son—the brother of James, Joseph, Judas and Simon? Don't his sisters live here among us?” They were offended and rejected him.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“And rejected him”—implied in the idea of taking offense.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="John 4:44; Luke 4:24; Matthew 13:57"> “A prophet is treated with respect except in his home town, among his relatives, and within his own family,” Jesus told them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Matthew 13:58; Mark 9:23; Mark 5:23"> The result was that Jesus could not do any miracles there, except to heal a few sick people.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Matthew 9:35; Matthew 8:10; Luke 13:22"> He was amazed at their lack of trust. Jesus traveled around the villages, teaching as he went.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 4:9–10; Luke 10:17–20; Matthew 10:1–5"> He called together the twelve disciples, and began sending them out two by two, giving them authority over evil spirits.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Luke 9:3; Matthew 10:9–10; Luke 10:4"> He told them not to take anything with them except a walking stick—no bread, no bag, and no money in their belts.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 12:8; Ephesians 6:15"> They could wear sandals, but they were not to take an extra shirt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Luke 10:7–8; Luke 9:4; Matthew 10:11–13"> “When you're invited into a home, stay there until you leave,” he told them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Matthew 10:14–15; Acts 18:6; Luke 10:10–15"> “If you're not welcomed and not listened to, then shake the dust from your feet as you leave as a sign you have given up on them.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Literally, “a witness to them.” The act of shaking even the dust of the place from your feet was to indicate you completely abandoned them.">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Matthew 4:17; Luke 13:3; Acts 20:21"> So they went around telling people to repent.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="James 5:14–15; Mark 6:7; Luke 10:17"> They drove out many demons, and healed many who were sick by anointing them with oil.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Luke 9:7–9; Mark 6:14–29; Luke 23:7–12"> King Herod got to hear about Jesus since he had become well-known. Some said, “This is John the Baptist risen from the dead. That's why he has such miraculous powers.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Matthew 16:14; Malachi 4:5; Matthew 21:11"> Others said, “It's Elijah.” Still others said, “He's a prophet, like one of the prophets of the past.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Revelation 11:10–13; Matthew 27:4; Luke 9:9"> But when Herod heard about it, he said, “It's John, the one I beheaded! He's come back from the dead!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Luke 3:19–20; Matthew 11:2; Luke 3:1"> For Herod had given orders to arrest and imprison John because of Herodias, his brother Philip's wife, whom Herod had married.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Leviticus 20:21; Leviticus 18:16; 1 Kings 22:14"> John had been saying to Herod, “It's against the law to marry your brother's wife.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 7:9; Ephesians 4:26–27; Genesis 39:17–20"> So Herodias had a grudge against John and wanted him killed. But she wasn't able to arrange it</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Matthew 21:26; Matthew 14:5; 2 Chronicles 24:15–22"> because Herod knew John was a holy man who did what was right. Herod protected John and though what John said was very disturbing to him, he was still happy to hear what he had to say.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Esther 2:18; Genesis 40:20; 1 Peter 4:3"> Herodias had her opportunity on Herod's birthday. He gave a banquet for the nobles, military officers, and important leaders of Galilee.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Esther 1:10–12; Matthew 14:6; Daniel 5:2"> Herodias' own daughter came in and danced for them. Herod and those eating with him were delighted at her performance, so he told the girl,</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Esther 7:2; Esther 5:3; Esther 5:6"> “Ask me for whatever you like, and I'll give it to you.” He confirmed his promise with an oath, “I'll give you up to half my kingdom.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Job 31:31; Ezekiel 19:2–3; Genesis 27:8–11"> She went out and asked her mother, “What should I ask for?” “The head of John the Baptist,” she replied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Romans 3:15; Numbers 7:19–89; Numbers 7:13"> The girl hurried back in and said to the king, “I want you to give me right now the head of John the Baptist on a plate.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Matthew 27:24–25; Matthew 14:9; Matthew 27:3–5"> The king was very upset, but because of the oaths he'd made in front of his guests, he didn't want to refuse her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Matthew 14:10–11"> So he immediately sent an executioner to bring him John's head. After beheading him in the prison,</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> the executioner brought John's head on a plate and gave it to the girl, and the young girl handed it to her mother.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Matthew 14:12; 2 Chronicles 24:16; Matthew 27:57–60"> When John's disciples heard what had happened they came and took his body and placed it in a tomb.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Luke 9:10; Luke 10:17; Matthew 10:2"> The apostles returned<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="From their tour of the villages, spreading the good news.">c</a></sup>and gathered around Jesus. They told him all they had done and what they had taught.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Mark 3:20; John 6:1; Mark 1:45"> “Come with me, just yourselves. We'll go to a quiet place, and rest for a while,” Jesus told them, because there was so much coming and going that they didn't even have time to eat.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Mark 8:2–9; Mark 6:45; Mark 4:36"> So they went by boat to a quiet place to be alone.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="John 6:2; Matthew 15:29–31; Mark 6:54–55"> But people saw them leaving and recognized them. So people from all the surrounding towns ran ahead and got there before them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Matthew 9:36; Luke 9:11; Numbers 27:17"> When Jesus got off the boat he saw a huge crowd, and he felt pity for them, because they were like sheep without a shepherd. So he began to teach them about many things.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="John 6:5–15; Luke 9:12–17; Matthew 14:15–21"> It was getting late in the day and Jesus' disciples came to him. They told him, “We're miles from anywhere, and it's really late.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="Matthew 15:23; Mark 3:21; Mark 5:31"> You should tell the people to go and buy themselves something to eat in the villages and countryside nearby.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="2 Kings 4:42–44; Matthew 15:32–33; Mark 8:2–3"> But Jesus replied, “You give them something to eat.” “What? We'd need more than six month's pay<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Literally, “200 denarii.”">d</a></sup>to buy bread to feed all these people,” the disciples replied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="Matthew 15:34; Mark 8:5; John 6:9"> “Well, how much bread do you have?” Jesus asked. “Go and see.” They went and checked, and told him, “Five loaves, and a couple of fish.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:40; 1 Corinthians 14:33; Matthew 15:35"> Jesus told everyone to sit down in groups on the green grass.</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="Luke 9:14–15"> They sat in groups of hundreds and fifties.</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="Matthew 14:19; Mark 7:34; Luke 9:16"> Then he took the five loaves and the two fish. Looking up to heaven he blessed the food and broke the bread into pieces. Then he handed the bread to the disciples to give to the people, and he divided the fish up between all of them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="Luke 9:17; Mark 8:8–9; Matthew 15:37–38"> Everyone ate until they were full.</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43" data-xref="Mark 8:19–20"> Then they collected up the leftovers of the bread and fish—twelve basketfuls.</span>
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44"> A total of five thousand men plus their families ate the food.</span>
<span className="v" id="v45" data-v="45" data-xref="Matthew 14:22–33; John 6:15–21; Mark 6:32"> Immediately after this Jesus instructed his disciples to get back into the boat. They were to go on ahead to Bethsaida on the other side of the lake while he sent the people on their way.</span>
<span className="v" id="v46" data-v="46" data-xref="Mark 1:35; Matthew 6:6; Luke 6:12"> Once he'd said goodbye to them he went up into the hills to pray.</span>
<span className="v" id="v47" data-v="47" data-xref="Matthew 14:23; John 6:16–17"> Late that evening the boat was in the middle of the lake while Jesus was alone, still on land.</span>
<span className="v" id="v48" data-v="48" data-xref="Luke 24:28; Luke 12:38; Job 9:8"> He could see them being buffeted about as they rowed hard because the wind was blowing against them. In the early morning hours Jesus came to them, walking on the water. He would have passed them,</span>
<span className="v" id="v49" data-v="49" data-xref="Matthew 14:25–26; Luke 24:37; Job 9:8"> but when they saw him walking on the water they thought he was a ghost. They screamed out</span>
<span className="v" id="v50" data-v="50" data-xref="Matthew 14:27; John 6:19–20; Isaiah 43:2"> because they could all see him and were absolutely terrified. Jesus told them right away: “Don't worry, it's me. Don't be afraid!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v51" data-v="51" data-xref="PSALMS 93:3–4; Mark 4:39; John 6:21"> He went over to them and climbed into the boat, and the wind died down. They were totally shocked,</span>
<span className="v" id="v52" data-v="52" data-xref="Mark 8:17–21; Luke 24:25; Isaiah 63:17"> for they hadn't understood the meaning of the feeding miracle due to their stubborn, hard-hearted attitude.</span>
<span className="v" id="v53" data-v="53" data-xref="Matthew 14:34–36; John 6:24–25; Luke 5:1"> After crossing the Sea they arrived at Gennesaret and moored the boat.</span>
<span className="v" id="v54" data-v="54" data-xref="PSALMS 9:10; Philippians 3:10"> As they climbed out, the people immediately recognized Jesus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v55" data-v="55" data-xref="Matthew 4:24; Mark 2:1–3; Mark 3:7–11"> They ran everywhere around the whole area to bring in all the sick on their mats to where they'd heard Jesus was.</span>
<span className="v" id="v56" data-v="56" data-xref="Matthew 9:20; Luke 6:19; Numbers 15:38–39"> Wherever he went, in the villages, in the towns, or in the countryside, they put the sick in the marketplaces and begged Jesus to let the sick touch just the edge of his clothes. Everyone who touched him was healed.</span>
