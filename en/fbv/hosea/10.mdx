<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Hosea 8:11; Leviticus 26:1; Philippians 2:21"> Israel is like a spreading<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="The word used here usually means “to lay waste.” While many translations use terms such as “luxuriant,” the point made here is that this vine is unpruned and overgrown, and additionally only produces fruit “for itself”—not a good description of a productive vine for the gardener.">a</a></sup>vine that produces fruit for itself. The more fruit they produced, the more altars<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Altars used for pagan worship.">b</a></sup>they built. The more productive the land, the more beautiful they made the sacred pillars.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Again, pagan symbols used for the worship of fertility gods.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Micah 5:13; 1 Kings 18:21; Zephaniah 1:5"> The people have minds that are deceptive, and now they must take responsibility for their guilt. The Lord will break apart their altars and destroy their sacred pillars.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Hosea 10:15; Hosea 10:7; Hosea 13:11"> Then they will say, “We have no king, for we do not fear the Lord, and yet what would a king do for us?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Amos 5:7; Amos 6:12; Hosea 4:2"> They speak empty words, swearing false promises to make a covenant.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="The context is making false promises to God of agreements they do not intend to keep.">d</a></sup>Their “justice” flourishes like poisonous weeds in the furrows of a field.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Hosea 9:11; Hosea 5:8; 2 Kings 23:5"> Those who live in Samaria tremble in awe at the calf of Beth-aven.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="The real name of the place was Bethel, the house of God, but because of the pagan practices celebrated there the later prophets referred to it as Beth-aven, the house of nothingness (idols).">e</a></sup>Its people mourn for it in their pagan rituals, while its idolatrous priests celebrate its glory. But it will certainly be taken away from them.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="The verbs used in this verse describe the pagan worship of Baal, in which his death is mourned with self-mutilation and then his return is celebrated with orgiastic rites. However, the idol will eventually be taken away by the Assyrians when they invade.">f</a></sup>It will be brought to Assyria as tribute for the great king.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Hosea 5:13; Isaiah 30:3; Daniel 11:8"> Ephraim will be disgraced, and Israel will be ashamed at its own decisions.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Decisions—that it trusted in idols, and sought help from heathen nations.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Hosea 10:3; Jude 1:13; 2 Kings 1:3"> Samaria and its king will be destroyed, swept away like a twig on the surface of the water.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Luke 23:30; Revelation 6:16; Isaiah 2:19"> The high places of Aven,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Where the pagan shrines were located.">h</a></sup>where Israel sinned, will be demolished, and thorns and thistles will grow over their altars. Then they will call to the mountains, “Bury us!” and to the hills, “Fall on us!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Hosea 9:9; Judges 20:5; Judges 19:22–30"> Since the days of Gibeah you have been sinning, Israel, and you haven't changed. Do the people of Gibeah think that war will not come to them?</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Ezekiel 5:13; Hosea 4:9; Jeremiah 16:16"> When I choose I will punish the wicked. The nations will be gathered together against them when they are punished for their double crime.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Hosea 4:16; Jeremiah 50:11; Deuteronomy 25:4"> Ephraim is like a trained heifer that loved to thresh the grain, but now I will place a yoke on her strong neck. I will harness Ephraim; Judah will have to pull the plow; and Jacob must break up the ground for himself.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Jeremiah 4:3–4; James 3:18; Isaiah 45:8"> Sow for yourselves what is right and you will reap unfailing love. Break up the unplowed ground. It's time to go to the Lord until he comes and rains down goodness on you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Job 4:8; Galatians 6:7–8; Proverbs 1:31"> But instead you have planted wickedness and reaped an evil harvest. You have eaten the fruit of lies, because you trusted in your own strength and in your many warriors.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Hosea 13:16; 2 Kings 18:9–10; Amos 3:8"> The terrifying noise of battle will rise against your people, and all your fortresses will be destroyed, just as Shalman devastated Beth Arbel in the time of war. Even mothers and children were dashed to pieces.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Hosea 10:5; Hosea 10:7; Romans 7:13"> This is what will happen to you, Bethel, because of your great wickedness. At dawn, the king of Israel will be utterly destroyed.</span>
