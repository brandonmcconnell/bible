<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">11:2</span> Sometimes this is translated: “The more I called them, the more they went from me,” but this requires significant changes to the original text. In other words even at the Exodus Israel was hankering after the things of Egypt and only left under pressure. Many would have preferred to stay, and Hosea traces the apostasy he is dealing with to a reluctant and rebellious spirit of some even at the time of the Exodus. This is confirmed by the second part of the verse.</li>

<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">11:4</span> The image shifts to care for a farm animal. The Hebrew literally says, “I became like those who lift up a yoke that was in their jaws.” The burden is not removed altogether, but is made easier to bear.</li>

<li id="fn-i"><a href="#fnref-i" className="fnb">i</a> <span className="fnr">11:9</span> Meaning God would not totally annihilate them as he did with the cities mentioned above.</li>
