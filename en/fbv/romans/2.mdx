<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Luke 6:37; Romans 2:3; John 8:7–9"> So if you judge others you don't have any excuse, whoever you are! For in whatever way you condemn others, you're judging yourself, because you're doing the same things.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="PSALMS 9:7–8; Zephaniah 3:5; PSALMS 145:17"> We know that God's judgment on those who do such things is based on truth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Proverbs 16:5; Luke 12:14; Romans 1:32–1"> When you judge them do you really think that you however can avoid God's judgment?</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Peter 3:9; Isaiah 30:18; Exodus 34:6"> Or is it that you're treating his wonderful kindness, tolerance, and patience with contempt, not realizing that God in his kindness is trying to lead you to repent?</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 12:14; Romans 1:18; Romans 2:2–3"> Due to your hard-hearted attitude and your refusal to repent, you're making things far worse for yourself on the day of retribution when God's judgment is demonstrated to be absolutely right.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="PSALMS 62:12; Jeremiah 32:19; Matthew 16:27"> God will make sure everyone receives what they deserve according to what they've done.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Quoting Psalms 62:12.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Timothy 4:7–8; Luke 8:15; 2 Timothy 1:10"> Those who have kept on trying to do what is good and right will receive glory and honor, immortality and eternal life.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="John 3:18–21; 2 Thessalonians 1:8; 1 Timothy 6:3–4"> But those who think only of themselves, rejecting the truth and deliberately choosing to do evil, will receive punishment and furious hostility.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Peter 4:17; Ezekiel 18:4; Romans 1:16"> Everyone who does evil will have trouble and suffering—the Jewish people first, and the foreigners too.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Isaiah 32:17; Isaiah 26:12; Numbers 6:26"> But everyone who does good will have glory, honor, and peace—the Jewish people first, and the foreigners too.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Deuteronomy 10:17; Acts 10:34; 2 Chronicles 19:7"> God has no favorites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Corinthians 9:21; Galatians 3:10; Galatians 2:16–19"> Those who sin even though they don't have the written law<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Referring to the law written down by Moses. Those who don't have the written law are the “foreigners,” while those who have the written law are the Jews.">b</a></sup>are still lost, while those who sin that do have the written law will be condemned by that law.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="James 1:22–25; 1 John 3:7; James 2:21–25"> Just listening to what the law says doesn't make you right in God's sight. It's those who do what the law says who are made right.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Romans 2:12; Romans 1:19–20; Romans 2:27"> The foreigners don't have the written law, but when they instinctively do what it says, they are following the law even without having the written law.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="2 Corinthians 5:11; 2 Corinthians 1:12; Romans 1:18–19"> In this way they show how the law works that's written in their minds. As they think about what they're doing, their conscience either accuses them for doing wrong or defends them for doing what is good and right.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 12:14; 1 Corinthians 4:5; Acts 17:31"> The good news I share is that a day is coming when God will judge, through Jesus Christ, everyone's secret thoughts.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Romans 2:23; Micah 3:11; Isaiah 48:1–2"> What about you who call yourself a Jew? You rely on the written law and boast about having a special relationship to God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Thessalonians 5:21; Deuteronomy 4:8; James 4:17"> You know what he wants; you do what's right because you've been taught from the law.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Revelation 3:17–18; Matthew 15:14; Isaiah 49:6"> You're absolutely sure that you can guide the blind, and that you are a light to those in darkness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Timothy 3:5; Titus 1:16; 2 Timothy 1:13"> You think you can set ignorant people straight, a teacher of “children,” because you know from the law all the truth there is to know.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Corinthians 9:27; Matthew 23:3–28; Luke 12:47"> So if you're busy teaching others, why don't you teach yourself? You tell people not to steal, but are you stealing?</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Malachi 3:8; Matthew 12:39; Mark 11:17"> You tell people not to commit adultery, but are you committing adultery? You tell people not to worship idols, but do you profane temples?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Or “rob temples.”">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="James 4:16–17; Romans 2:17; Romans 3:2"> You boast about having the law, but don't you misrepresent God by breaking it?</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Ezekiel 36:20–23; Isaiah 52:5; 2 Peter 2:2"> As Scripture says, “God's character is defamed among the foreigners because of you.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Quoting Isaiah 52:5. Literally, “the name of God,” which has primarily to do with his character.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Jeremiah 4:4; Galatians 5:3–6; Acts 7:51"> Being circumcised<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Circumcision, given by God to Israel in the Old Testament, was a sign that they were his special people.">e</a></sup>has value only if you do what the law says. But if you break the law, your circumcision is as worthless as those who are not circumcised at all.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Colossians 2:11; 1 Corinthians 7:18–19; Romans 8:4"> If a man who is not circumcised<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Not circumcised, meaning a non-Jew or “foreigner.”">f</a></sup>keeps the law, he should be considered as being circumcised even though he's not.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Matthew 12:41–42; Romans 2:29; 2 Corinthians 3:6"> The uncircumcised foreigners who keep the law will condemn you if you break the law, even though you have the written law and circumcision.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Galatians 6:15; Matthew 3:9; Romans 9:6–8"> It's not what's on the outside that makes you a Jew; it's not the physical sign of circumcision.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Deuteronomy 30:6; Philippians 3:3; Deuteronomy 10:16"> What makes you a Jew is on the inside, a “circumcision of the heart” that doesn't follow the letter of the law but the Spirit. Someone like that is looking for praise from God, not from people.</span>
