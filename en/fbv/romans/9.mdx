<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Timothy 2:7; Romans 1:9; Galatians 1:20"> I am in Christ, and what I say is true. I'm not lying! My conscience and the Holy Spirit confirm</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Philippians 3:18; PSALMS 119:136; Jeremiah 13:17"> how terribly sad I am, how I have never-ending pain in my heart,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Exodus 32:32; Esther 8:6; Romans 11:14"> for my own people, my brothers and sisters. I would rather be cursed myself, separated from Christ, if that would help them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Ephesians 2:12; PSALMS 147:19; Romans 9:6"> They are my fellow-Israelites, God's chosen people. God revealed to them his glory and made agreements<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally, “covenants.”">a</a></sup>with them, giving them the law, true worship, and his promises.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="John 10:30; John 1:1–3; Colossians 1:16–19"> They are our forefathers—ancestors of Christ, humanly-speaking, the one who rules over everything, the eternally-blessed God. Amen.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Romans 2:28–29; Galatians 6:16; Romans 11:1–2"> It's not that God's promise has failed. For not every Israelite is a true Israelite,</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Genesis 21:12; Hebrews 11:18; John 8:37–39"> and not all those who are descended from Abraham are his true children. For Scripture says, “Your descendants will be counted through Isaac,”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Quoting Genesis 21:12.">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="John 1:13; Romans 4:11–16; Galatians 3:26–29"> so it's not Abraham's actual children who are counted as God's children, but only those children of God's promise who are considered his true descendants.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Genesis 18:10; Genesis 18:14; Genesis 17:21"> This is what the promise was: “I will return next year and Sarah will have a son.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Quoting Genesis 18:10-14.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Genesis 25:21–23; Luke 16:26; Romans 5:11"> In addition Rebecca's twin sons had the same father, our forefather Isaac.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Timothy 1:9; Ephesians 1:4–5; 2 Peter 1:10"> But even before the children were born, and before they'd done anything right or wrong (so that God's purpose could continue, proving God's calling of people is not based on human performance),</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Genesis 25:22–23; 2 Samuel 8:14; 1 Kings 22:47"> she was told, “The older brother will serve the younger one.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Quoting Genesis 25:23.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Malachi 1:2–3; Deuteronomy 21:15; Genesis 29:33"> As Scripture says, “I chose Jacob, but rejected Esau.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Quoting Malachi 1:2-3.">e</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Chronicles 19:7; PSALMS 145:17; Deuteronomy 32:4"> So what should we conclude? That God was unjust? Certainly not!</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Exodus 33:19; Romans 9:16; Micah 7:18"> As he said to Moses, “I will be merciful to whoever I should show mercy, and I will have compassion on whoever I should show compassion.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Quoting Exodus 33:19.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Ephesians 2:8; Matthew 11:25–26; 1 Peter 2:9–10"> So it does not depend on what we want, or our own efforts, but the merciful nature of God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Exodus 9:16; Proverbs 16:4; Exodus 14:17–18"> Scripture records God saying to Pharaoh: “I put you here for a reason—so that through you I could demonstrate my power, and so that my name could be made known throughout the earth.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Quoting Exodus 9:16.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Joshua 11:20; Exodus 4:21; Romans 9:15–16"> So God is merciful to those he wishes to be, and hardens the attitude of those he wants to.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="In the Old Testament, this expression is used to describe obstinate rejection of God, such as the experience of the Pharaoh of the Exodus. In Exodus 9Pharaoh is variously described as choosing a stubborn attitude, or that God gave him a stubborn attitude, or in the passive that he had a stubborn attitude. So this verse in Romans should not be taken to mean that God deliberately hardens people's attitudes and then punishes them for it. The stubborn attitude is a rejection of divine grace.">h</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Daniel 4:35; 2 Chronicles 20:6; 1 Corinthians 15:12"> Now you'll argue with me and ask, “So why does he still blame us then? Who can resist the will of God?”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Quoting Isaiah 29:16; Isaiah 45:9.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Isaiah 29:16; Isaiah 45:9–11; Isaiah 64:8"> That's no way to speak, for who are you—a mere mortal—to contradict God? Can something that is created say to its creator, “Why did you make me like this?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Isaiah 64:8; Proverbs 16:4; 2 Timothy 2:20–21"> Doesn't a potter have the right to use the same batch of clay to make both a decorative bowl and an everyday pot?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Literally, “pots of value and dishonor.”">j</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Proverbs 16:4; Jude 1:4; Romans 9:21"> It's as if God, wanting to demonstrate his opposition to sin<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Literally, “show anger.”">k</a></sup>and to reveal his power, bears patiently with these “pots destined for destruction,”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Romans 2:4; 1 Peter 1:2–5; Colossians 1:27"> so that he might reveal the greatness of his glory through these “pots of mercy” which he has prepared in advance for glory.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Colossians 3:11; Acts 13:47–48; Ephesians 3:6–8"> This is who we are—people he has called, not just from among the Jews, but from among the foreigners too...</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Hosea 2:23; 1 Peter 2:10; John 16:27"> As God said in the book of Hosea, “Those who are not my people I will call my people, and those who are not loved I will call the ones I love,”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Quoting Hosea 2:23.">l</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Hosea 1:9–10; 1 John 3:1–3; Galatians 3:26"> and, “It will happen that at the place where they were told, ‘You're not my people,’ there they will be called the children of the living God.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-m" id="fnref-m" data-fn="Quoting Hosea 1:10.">m</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Isaiah 10:20–23; Romans 11:4–6; Isaiah 1:9"> Isaiah cries out regarding Israel: “Even if the children of Israel have become as numerous as the sands of the sea, only a small number<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-n" id="fnref-n" data-fn="Literally, “remnant.”">n</a></sup>will be saved.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Isaiah 10:23; Revelation 19:11; Acts 17:31"> For the Lord is going to quickly and completely finish his work of judgment on the earth.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-o" id="fnref-o" data-fn="Quoting Isaiah 10:22-23.">o</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Isaiah 1:9; Isaiah 13:19; Amos 4:11"> As Isaiah previously said, “If the Lord Almighty had not left us some descendants, we would have become just like Sodom and Gomorrah.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-p" id="fnref-p" data-fn="Quoting Isaiah 1:9.">p</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Philippians 3:9; Hebrews 11:7; Galatians 2:16"> What shall we conclude, then? That even though the foreigners were not even looking to do right, they did grasp what is right, and through their trust in God did what was morally right.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Romans 11:7; Romans 3:20; James 2:10–11"> But the people of Israel, who looked to the law to make them right with God, never succeeded.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:23; 1 Peter 2:8; Luke 7:23"> Why not? Because they relied on what they did rather than trusting in God. They tripped on the stumbling-block,</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Isaiah 28:16; Isaiah 8:14–15; Romans 10:11"> just as Scripture predicted: “Look, I'm placing in Zion a stumbling-block, a rock that will offend people. But those who trust in him won't be disappointed.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-q" id="fnref-q" data-fn="Combining Isaiah 28:16and Isaiah 8:14.">q</a></sup></span>
</div>
</div>
