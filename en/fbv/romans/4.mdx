<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Isaiah 51:2; Romans 4:16; 2 Corinthians 11:22"> Let's take Abraham as an example. From a human viewpoint he is the father of our nation. Let's ask, “What was his experience?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:31; 2 Corinthians 11:30; Philippians 3:9"> For if Abraham was set right by what he did, he would have had something to boast about—but not in God's eyes.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Genesis 15:6; James 2:23; Galatians 3:6–8"> However, what does Scripture say? “Abraham trusted God, and so he was considered as being a good person who did right.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Quoting Genesis 15:6.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Romans 11:6; Romans 9:32; Romans 11:35"> Whoever works gets paid—it's not considered as a gift, but because they've earned their wages.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Philippians 3:9; Romans 3:22; Titus 3:3–7"> But God, who makes sinners right, considers them as right not because they've worked for it but because they trust in him. This is why</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Timothy 1:9; Philippians 3:9; 1 Corinthians 1:30"> David speaks of the happiness of those whom God considers as right, and not because they worked for it:</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="PSALMS 32:1–2; PSALMS 130:3–4; Luke 7:47–50"> “How happy are those whose wrongs are forgiven, and whose sins are covered.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="PSALMS 32:2; 1 Peter 2:24; 2 Corinthians 5:19–20"> How happy are those the Lord does not consider sinful.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Quoting Psalms 32:1-2.">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Galatians 3:14; Colossians 3:11; Galatians 3:26–28"> Now is this blessing just for the Jews, or is it for others too? We've just stated that Abraham was accepted as good and right because he trusted God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Galatians 6:15; Galatians 5:6; Genesis 17:10"> But when did this happen? When Abraham was a Jew or before?</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Romans 3:22; Luke 19:9; 2 Corinthians 1:22"> It was actually before he became a Jew by being circumcised, which was a confirmation of his trust in God to make him right. This happened before he was circumcised, so he is the father of everyone who trusts in God and are considered as right by God, even though they may not be circumcised Jews.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Song of Songs 1:8; John 8:39–40; Proverbs 2:20"> He is also the father of circumcised Jews not merely because they're circumcised, but because they follow the example of the trust in God our father Abraham had before he was circumcised.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Galatians 3:29; Genesis 22:17–18; Genesis 28:14"> God's promise to Abraham and his descendants that the world would belong to him was not based on his keeping of the law, but because he was made right through his trust in God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Galatians 3:18–24; Philippians 3:9; Romans 4:16"> For if the promised inheritance is based on keeping the law, then the issue of trusting God is not necessary, and the promise is pointless.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Romans 5:13; Galatians 3:10; Romans 3:19–20"> For the law results in punishment<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Punishment for law-breaking, which of course includes everyone.">c</a></sup>—but if there's no law then it cannot be broken.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Romans 3:24–26; Ephesians 2:8; Galatians 3:22"> So the promise is based on trusting God. It is provided as a free gift, guaranteed to all the children of Abraham—not merely to those who follow the law,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Paul is not saying here that those who obey the Mosaic law are consequently made right with God—he has already dealt with that issue. He is simply pointing out that those who are not following the Mosaic law are not excluded by God.">d</a></sup>but also to those who trust like Abraham, the father of us all.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Corinthians 1:28; Genesis 17:4–5; John 5:21"> As Scripture says, “I've made you the father of many nations.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Quoting Genesis 17:5.">e</a></sup>For in the presence of God, Abraham trusted in the God who makes the dead alive and speaks into existence what didn't previously exist.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Romans 8:24; Genesis 15:5–6; Acts 27:25"> Against all hope Abraham in hope trusted God, so he could become the father of many peoples, just as God had promised him: “This is how many descendants you'll have!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Referring to Genesis 15:5.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Hebrews 11:11–19; Romans 4:20–21; Genesis 17:17"> His trust in God didn't weaken even though he thought his body was practically dead (he was around a hundred years old), and knew that Sarah was too old to have children.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Luke 1:45; 1 Corinthians 16:13; Daniel 10:19"> He held on to God's promise—he didn't doubt it. Instead his trust in God grew stronger, and he gave glory to God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Luke 1:37; Jeremiah 32:27; Genesis 18:14"> He was totally convinced that what God had promised he had the power to deliver.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Romans 4:6; Romans 4:3"> That's why Abraham was considered right by God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Corinthians 10:11; Romans 15:4; 1 Corinthians 10:6"> The words “Abraham was considered right”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Quoting Genesis 15:6.">g</a></sup>weren't just written down for his benefit.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Acts 2:24; 1 Peter 1:21; Acts 13:30"> They were for us too, those of us who will be considered as right, since we trust in God who raised our Lord Jesus from the dead.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="1 John 4:9–10; Isaiah 53:5–6; 2 Corinthians 5:21"> Jesus was handed over to die because of our sins,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="See Isaiah 53:4-5">h</a></sup>and was raised to life to make us right.</span>
</div>
