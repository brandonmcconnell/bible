<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Proverbs 31:1; 2 Peter 1:19–21"> These are the words of Agur, son of Jakeh. An oracle. This is what the man says, God, I'm really tired, I'm worn out.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="This verse is variously translated, mainly depending on whether it refers to named people or what the words actually mean. Since both the Septuagint and the Vulgate do not assume the words to be names, this approach is followed here.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="PSALMS 73:22; 1 Corinthians 3:18; Isaiah 6:5"> I'm so stupid I'm not really a man; I can't even think like a human being.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Revelation 3:7; Ephesians 3:18–19; Romans 11:33"> I have not learned wisdom; I have no knowledge of the Holy One.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="John 3:13; Ephesians 4:8–10; Job 26:8"> Who has gone up to heaven, and come down? Who holds the winds in the palm of his hands? Who has wrapped up the waters in his cloak? Who has set the earth's boundaries? What is his name, and what is his son's name? Are you sure you don't know?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="The point of the questions is to show that no one but the Creator could do all these things.">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="PSALMS 18:30; James 3:17; PSALMS 12:6"> Every word God says has been proved true. He defends everyone who comes to him for protection.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Deuteronomy 12:32; Deuteronomy 4:2; Revelation 22:18–19"> Don't add anything to his words, or he will criticize you and you'll be shown to be a liar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Luke 10:42; 2 Kings 2:9; PSALMS 27:4"> God, I want to request two things from you. Please don't refuse to let me have them before I come to die.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Timothy 6:6–8; PSALMS 62:9–10; PSALMS 119:37"> Keep me from being false, help me not to tell lies. Don't make me poor or rich; just provide me with the food I need.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Hosea 13:6; Deuteronomy 31:20; Ezekiel 16:49–50"> Otherwise if I have plenty of money,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“I have plenty of money”: literally, “I'm full.”">c</a></sup>I may give up on you, saying, “Who is the Lord?” while if I'm poor I may steal and bring the name of my God into disrepute.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Romans 14:4; Ecclesiastes 7:21; Deuteronomy 23:15"> Don't slander a servant to his master, or he will curse you and you'll suffer for it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Proverbs 20:20; Proverbs 30:17; Exodus 21:17"> There are some who curse their fathers and do not bless their mothers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Luke 18:11; Proverbs 16:2; 2 Timothy 3:5"> There are some who see themselves as pure but they're still filthy—they have not been washed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Proverbs 6:17; Isaiah 2:11; PSALMS 101:5"> There are some who think themselves so high and mighty, and who look down on others.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="PSALMS 57:4; PSALMS 3:7; PSALMS 14:4"> There are some who have teeth like swords, incisors like knives, ready to devour the poor from the earth, the needy from society.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Proverbs 30:21; Proverbs 30:29; Proverbs 6:16"> The leech has two daughters who cry out, “Give me! Give me!” There are three things that are never satisfied, four that never say, “Enough!”:</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Proverbs 27:20; Habakkuk 2:5; Genesis 30:1"> The grave, the womb that doesn't become pregnant, the earth thirsty for water, and the fire that never says, “Enough!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Proverbs 23:22; Deuteronomy 21:18–21; Proverbs 30:11"> People who ridicule their fathers and despise obedience to the mother will have their eyes pecked out by wild ravens and eaten by young vultures.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Job 42:3; PSALMS 139:6"> These three things are amazingly hard for me, four things I just can't understand:</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Isaiah 40:31; Job 39:27; Exodus 22:16"> The way an eagle soars in the sky, the way a snake slides over a rock, the way a ship sails across the sea, the way a man and a woman fall in love.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Proverbs 5:6; Proverbs 7:13–23; Numbers 5:11–30"> This is the way of a woman who commits adultery: she eats,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="This is often assumed to refer to the act of adultery.">d</a></sup>she wipes her mouth, and then says, “I haven't done anything wrong!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> Three things make the earth tremble, there are four things it can't support:</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Proverbs 19:10; Ecclesiastes 10:7; 1 Samuel 25:10–11"> a slave becoming a king, a stupid person eating like a pig,</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Proverbs 21:19; Proverbs 27:15; Proverbs 29:21"> an unbearable woman getting married, and a maidservant taking her mistress's place.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Job 12:7"> There are four things on earth that are small, but very wise:</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Proverbs 6:6–8"> Ants—they're not strong, but they work hard all summer storing up food.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="PSALMS 104:18; Leviticus 11:5"> Hyraxes<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Also known as rock badgers or coneys.">e</a></sup>—they don't have much power, but they make their homes in the rock.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Joel 2:25; Joel 1:6–7; Joel 2:7–11"> Locusts—they don't have a king, but they all march in line abreast.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> Lizards—you can catch them in your hands, but they live in the king's palace.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29"> There are three things that are glorious to watch as they walk, four that look dignified as they move:</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Numbers 23:24; Judges 14:18"> The lion, supreme among wild animals, who isn't frightened of anything.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Daniel 3:15–18; Proverbs 20:2; Proverbs 16:14"> The strutting starling,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="“Starling”: as per modern Hebrew and Aramaic, some ancient versions have “rooster.”">f</a></sup>the male goat, and a king with his army.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Job 21:5; Job 40:4; Proverbs 26:12"> If you have been foolishly boasting about yourself, or if you've been planning to do something wrong, stop and put your hand over your mouth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Proverbs 17:14; Proverbs 29:22; Proverbs 28:25"> Just as churning milk produces butter, and twisting someone's nose makes it bleed, so stirring up anger causes arguments.</span>
