<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Matthew 5:13–16; Galatians 2:12–14; Nehemiah 13:26"> Dead flies can make perfumed oil smell bad. Likewise a little foolishness outweighs great wisdom and honor.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Luke 12:18–20; Ecclesiastes 10:14; Ecclesiastes 10:10"> The mind of the wise person chooses the right side, but the mind of the fool goes left!</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Proverbs 18:2; Proverbs 13:16; Proverbs 18:6"> Just the way that fools walk down the road shows they have no sense, making clear to everyone their stupidity.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Proverbs 25:15; Ecclesiastes 8:3; 1 Samuel 25:24–44"> If your superior gets angry with you, don't give up and leave. If you stay calm even bad mistakes can be resolved.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 5:13; Ecclesiastes 4:7; Ecclesiastes 9:3"> I also realized there's another evil here on earth: rulers make a big mistake</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Proverbs 28:12; Proverbs 29:2; Esther 3:1"> when they put fools in high positions, while those who are richly qualified<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“Richly qualified”: literally simply “the rich,” but surely more is in view here than simply accumulated wealth.">a</a></sup>are put in low positions.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Proverbs 19:10; Esther 6:8; Proverbs 30:22"> I've seen slaves riding on horseback, while princes walk on the ground like slaves.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="In that society, it would have been very unlikely that slaves, often captured enemies, would have ever ridden on horseback. In contrast the image of princes being forced to walk is to show a loss of their dignity.">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Proverbs 26:27; PSALMS 7:15–16; Esther 7:10"> If you dig a pit, you could fall in. If you knock down a wall, you could be bitten by a snake.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9"> If you quarry stone, you could be injured. If you split logs,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Or “cut down trees.”">c</a></sup>you could be hurt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="James 1:5; Colossians 4:5; Ecclesiastes 10:15"> If your ax is blunt and you don't sharpen it, you have to use a lot more force. Conclusion:<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Implied.">d</a></sup>being wise brings good results.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Jeremiah 8:17; PSALMS 58:4–5; PSALMS 64:3"> If the snake bites the snake charmer before it's charmed, there's no benefit to the snake charmer!</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Colossians 4:6; Luke 4:22; Proverbs 22:11"> Wise words are beneficial, but fools destroy themselves by what they say.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Samuel 25:10–11; Proverbs 29:9; Acts 6:9–11"> Fools begin by saying foolish things, and end up talking evil nonsense.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Proverbs 15:2; Ecclesiastes 6:12; Ecclesiastes 5:3"> Fools talk on and on, however no one knows what's going to happen, so who can say what the future holds?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="See 8:7.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Jeremiah 50:4–5; Isaiah 57:1; Isaiah 35:8–10"> Work makes fools so worn out they can't achieve anything.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="“They can't achieve anything”: literally, “can't find their way to town,” a colloquial expression meaning that people become confused so they are not successful.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Isaiah 3:12; Isaiah 5:11–12; Isaiah 3:4–5"> You're in trouble if the king of your country is young, and if your leaders are busy feasting from early morning.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Proverbs 31:4–5; Proverbs 28:2–3; Isaiah 5:11"> You're fortunate if your king comes from a noble family, and your leaders feast at the proper time to give themselves energy, and not to get drunk.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Proverbs 14:1; Proverbs 24:30–34; Proverbs 20:4"> Lazy people let their roofs collapse; idle people don't repair their leaky houses.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="PSALMS 104:15; Ecclesiastes 2:1–2; 1 Peter 4:3"> A good meal brings pleasure; wine makes life pleasant; money provides for all needs.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Luke 12:2–3; Acts 23:5; Ecclesiastes 7:21–22"> Don't talk badly about the king, not even in your thoughts. Don't talk badly about leaders,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="“Leaders”: literally, “the rich.”">g</a></sup>even in the privacy of your bedroom. A bird may hear what you say and fly away to tell them.</span>
