<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 3:17; Matthew 16:3; Ecclesiastes 8:5–6"> Everything has its own time—a time for all that happens here:<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="This is the Teachers observation, not his instruction. For this reason the verb participle is used, rather than the infinitive, since the infinitive might suggest that this is what should happen, e.g. “a time of killing” (i.e. when it happens) rather than “a time to kill” (when it ought to happen).">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Hebrews 9:27; Job 14:5; Matthew 15:13"> A time of birth, and a time of death. A time of planting, and a time of harvest.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Hosea 6:1–2; 1 Samuel 2:6; Jeremiah 45:4"> A time of killing, and a time of healing. A time of tearing down, and a time of building up.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Romans 12:15; PSALMS 30:5; James 4:9"> A time of crying, and a time of laughing. A time of mourning, and a time of dancing.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Kings 3:25; Joshua 10:27; Joshua 4:3–9"> A time of throwing away stones, and a time of gathering up stones.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="This mention of stones and its meaning is debated. Jewish tradition indicates it is a euphemism for making love.">b</a></sup>A time of embracing, and a time of avoiding embracing.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Philippians 3:7–8; Luke 9:24–25; Mark 8:35–37"> A time of searching, and a time of giving up searching. A time of keeping, and a time of throwing away.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Amos 5:13; Micah 7:5; Lamentations 3:28"> A time of tearing, and a time of mending. A time of keeping quiet, a time of speaking up.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Ephesians 5:25; Ephesians 5:28–29; Luke 14:26"> A time of loving, and a time of hating. A time of warfare, and a time of peace.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 1:3; Ecclesiastes 5:16; Proverbs 14:23"> So what do you get for all your hard work?</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 1:13–14; Genesis 3:19; Ecclesiastes 2:26"> I have examined what God gives us to do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Romans 11:33; Romans 1:19–20; Job 11:7"> Everything God does is beautifully timed, and even though he has also placed the idea of eternity<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“Eternity”: the word used here has the meaning of ages past and ages future, “continuation to the most distant time.”">c</a></sup>in our minds, we can't fully understand what God does from beginning to end.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="PSALMS 37:3; Ecclesiastes 3:22; 1 Timothy 6:18"> I concluded that there's nothing better than being happy and looking for the good in life.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="PSALMS 128:2; Ecclesiastes 9:7; Ecclesiastes 2:24"> In addition everyone should eat and drink and enjoy their work—this is God's gift to us.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="James 1:17; PSALMS 33:11; Romans 11:36"> I also concluded that everything God does lasts forever: nothing can be added to it or taken away from it. God acts in this way so that people may stand in awe of him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 1:9–10; Ecclesiastes 6:10"> Whatever was, is; and whatever will be, has been, and God examines the whole of time.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Literally, “God seeks what has been chased away.” This phrase has been variously interpreted, but perhaps the best in the context is that time is open to God—things forgotten by human beings (“chased away”) are still accessible to him and are what he examines (“seeks”).">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 4:1; Ecclesiastes 5:8; PSALMS 94:21–22"> I also observed that here on earth there was evil even in the place where there was supposed to be justice; even where things were meant to be right, there was evil.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Corinthians 5:10; Ecclesiastes 12:14; Matthew 16:27"> But then I thought to myself, “Ultimately God will judge both those who do right and those who do wrong, and every deed and action, at the appointed time.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="PSALMS 73:22; PSALMS 49:12; Hebrews 9:27"> I also thought to myself, “Regarding what happens to human beings—God proves to us that we're no better than animals.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="This thought is in reaction to the realization that wickedness takes the place of justice, mentioned in 3:16.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="PSALMS 49:12; PSALMS 49:20; PSALMS 89:47–48"> For what happens to human beings is the same as what happens to animals—in the same way one dies, the other dies too. They all have the breath of life—so regarding any advantage human beings have over animals, there is none. Definitely this is very hard to understand!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="In addition, since this follows a discussion of “breath” (that can also be translated “spirit”), this can be linked to the continued theme of “transience” and “difficult to understand” which as mentioned before comes from the word for vapor or breath.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Genesis 3:19; Job 34:15; Ecclesiastes 12:7"> They all end up in the same place—they all came from dust, and they all return to dust.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 12:7; John 14:3; 2 Corinthians 5:1"> Who really knows whether the breath of life<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="“Breath of life”: or “spirit.”">g</a></sup>of human beings goes up above, and the breath of life of animals goes down below to the earth?</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 2:24; Ecclesiastes 3:11–12; Ecclesiastes 10:14"> So I concluded that there's nothing better than for people to enjoy their work. This is what we are meant to do. For who can bring anyone back from the dead to show them what will happen after they die?</span>

## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">3:1</span> This is the Teachers observation, not his instruction. For this reason the verb participle is used, rather than the infinitive, since the infinitive might suggest that this is what should happen, e.g. “a time of killing” (i.e. when it happens) rather than “a time to kill” (when it ought to happen).</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">3:5</span> This mention of stones and its meaning is debated. Jewish tradition indicates it is a euphemism for making love.</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">3:11</span> “Eternity”: the word used here has the meaning of ages past and ages future, “continuation to the most distant time.”</li>
<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">3:15</span> Literally, “God seeks what has been chased away.” This phrase has been variously interpreted, but perhaps the best in the context is that time is open to God—things forgotten by human beings (“chased away”) are still accessible to him and are what he examines (“seeks”).</li>
<li id="fn-e"><a href="#fnref-e" className="fnb">e</a> <span className="fnr">3:18</span> This thought is in reaction to the realization that wickedness takes the place of justice, mentioned in 3:16.</li>
<li id="fn-f"><a href="#fnref-f" className="fnb">f</a> <span className="fnr">3:19</span> In addition, since this follows a discussion of “breath” (that can also be translated “spirit”), this can be linked to the continued theme of “transience” and “difficult to understand” which as mentioned before comes from the word for vapor or breath.</li>
<li id="fn-g"><a href="#fnref-g" className="fnb">g</a> <span className="fnr">3:21</span> “Breath of life”: or “spirit.”</li>
