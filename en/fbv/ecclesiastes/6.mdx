<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 5:13"> I have observed another evil here on earth, and it has a great impact on humanity.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 5:19; PSALMS 17:14; PSALMS 73:7"> God gives wealth, possessions, and honor to someone. They have everything they want. But God doesn't let them enjoy what they have. Instead somebody else does! This is hard to fathom, and is truly evil.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Job 3:16; Jeremiah 22:19; Ecclesiastes 4:3"> A man could have one hundred children, and grow old, but it wouldn't matter how long his life was if he couldn't enjoy it and at the end receive a decent burial. I would say that a stillborn child would be better off than him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="PSALMS 109:13"> The way a stillborn child comes into the world and then leaves is painfully hard to understand—arriving and departing in darkness—and who he would have been is never known.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally, “In darkness his name will be covered.” However, this is more than simply remaining nameless. Name in Hebrew thought is associated with character and personality, so what is being said here is that the stillborn child will never have the chance to become a person.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="PSALMS 58:8; Job 14:1; Job 3:10–13"> He never saw the light of day or knew what it was like to live. Yet the child finds rest, and not this man.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Hebrews 9:27; Ecclesiastes 3:20; Job 30:23"> Even if this man were to live a thousand years twice over he still wouldn't be happy. Don't we all end up in the same place—the grave?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“The grave”: implied.">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="John 6:27; Proverbs 16:26; 1 Timothy 6:6–8"> Everyone works so they can live<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="The word is literally “mouth,” so generally “to eat” is understood. However, human work is to supply all kinds of needs, so it is suggested that the application is more general than simply eating.">c</a></sup>, but they're never satisfied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Proverbs 19:1; Ecclesiastes 2:14–16; PSALMS 101:2"> So then, what real advantage do wise people have over those who are fools? And do poor people really gain anything in knowing how to behave in front of others?</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 1:14; Job 31:7; Ecclesiastes 1:2"> Be happy with what you have instead of running after what you don't! But this is also hard to do, like running after the wind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Job 9:32; Isaiah 45:9–10; Job 40:2"> Everything that exists has already been described<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Described”: literally, “named.” However, in Hebrew thought “name” is far more than a simple appellation, it is descriptive of the object or person.">d</a></sup>. Everyone knows what people are like, and that you can't win an argument with a superior.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="“A superior”: literally, “stronger”: this could refer to either physical or mental strength. <span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 4:16; Ecclesiastes 5:7; Ecclesiastes 2:3–11"> For the more words you use, the harder it is to make sense. So what's the point?</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="James 4:14; Job 14:2; Ecclesiastes 3:22"> Who knows what's best for us and our lives? During our short lives that pass like shadows we have many unanswered questions. And who can tell us what will happen when we're gone?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="This could mean what will happen on earth once people die, or what will happen to people after death. ## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">6:4</span> Literally, “In darkness his name will be covered.” However, this is more than simply remaining nameless. Name in Hebrew thought is associated with character and personality, so what is being said here is that the stillborn child will never have the chance to become a person.</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">6:6</span> “The grave”: implied.</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">6:7</span> The word is literally “mouth,” so generally “to eat” is understood. However, human work is to supply all kinds of needs, so it is suggested that the application is more general than simply eating.</li>
<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">6:10</span> “Described”: literally, “named.” However, in Hebrew thought “name” is far more than a simple appellation, it is descriptive of the object or person.</li>
<li id="fn-e"><a href="#fnref-e" className="fnb">e</a> <span className="fnr">6:10</span> “A superior”: literally, “stronger”: this could refer to either physical or mental strength. However, some kind of argument is probably in view, but the point is that a superior does not have to “play by the rules” of argument. Some take the “stronger” one to mean God, in which case the gist of the phrase means “you can't argue with God.” In addition, some have seen in this verse an argument for predestination, but the text does not necessarily support this.</li>
<li id="fn-f"><a href="#fnref-f" className="fnb">f</a> <span className="fnr">6:12</span> This could mean what will happen on earth once people die, or what will happen to people after death. Both interpretations are possible from the text.</li>
</ol>
