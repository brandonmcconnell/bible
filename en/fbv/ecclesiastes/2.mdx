<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Luke 12:19; Ecclesiastes 8:15; James 5:5"> So then I thought to myself, “Alright, let me examine pleasure and see how good that is.” But this too turned out to be something temporary that passes.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Proverbs 14:13; Ecclesiastes 7:2–6; 1 Peter 4:2–4"> I conclude that laughing your way through life is stupid, and pleasure—what use is that?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="The Teacher is not saying you should not laugh. He is referring to those people who make a joke of everything, not taking life seriously.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Proverbs 20:1; Ecclesiastes 1:17; Proverbs 31:4–5"> Then I used my mind to examine the attraction<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="The word used means to “draw” or “attract.”">b</a></sup>of wine to my body. My mind still guiding me with wisdom, I took it until I acted like a fool,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Some believe that this means the Teacher became drunk in this “experiment.” The phrase is literally, “I grasped (it) until folly.”">c</a></sup>so that I might see whether this was good for people to do during their time here.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Kings 7:1–12; 2 Chronicles 8:11; 2 Chronicles 26:10"> Then I tried great construction projects. I built houses for myself; I planted vineyards for myself.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Luke 17:27–29; Song of Songs 4:12–1; Jeremiah 39:4"> I made for myself<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="The repetition of the word “myself” may seem redundant, but that the Teacher was primarily thinking of himself is surely significant.">d</a></sup>gardens and parks, planting them with all kinds of fruit trees.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Nehemiah 2:14; PSALMS 1:3; Jeremiah 17:8"> I constructed for myself reservoirs to water all these growing trees.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Kings 3:4; Genesis 15:3; 1 Kings 4:23"> I bought male and female slaves, and their children also belonged to me. I also owned many herds and flocks, more than anyone in Jerusalem before me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Kings 10:10; 1 Kings 9:28; 2 Samuel 19:35"> I collected for myself great quantities of silver and gold, paid to me as tribute by kings and provinces. I brought in for myself male and female singers, and enjoyed many concubines<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="This word does not occur anywhere else in the Bible, so the meaning is assumed from the context.">e</a></sup>—all a man could want!</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Chronicles 29:25; Ecclesiastes 1:16; 1 Kings 10:23"> I became great—greater than anyone in Jerusalem before me. All the while my wisdom stayed with me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 9:9; Ecclesiastes 5:18; Ecclesiastes 3:22"> I didn't stop myself trying anything I wanted. Whatever I felt like enjoying, I did. I even enjoyed everything I had accomplished, a reward for all my work.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 1:14; 1 John 2:16–17; Ecclesiastes 1:3"> But when I thought about what I had worked so hard to achieve, everything I'd done, it was so short-lived—as significant as someone trying to catch the wind. There really is no enduring benefit here on earth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 1:17; Ecclesiastes 7:25; Ecclesiastes 1:9–10"> So I started to think about wisdom—and madness and foolishness. For what can anyone who comes after the king do that hasn't already been done?</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Ephesians 5:8; Ecclesiastes 7:11–12; Matthew 6:23"> I recognized that wisdom is better than foolishness just as light is better than darkness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="PSALMS 49:10; Proverbs 17:24; Ecclesiastes 9:11"> The wise see where they're going,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Literally, “The wise has his eyes in his head.”">f</a></sup>but fools walk in darkness. But I also realized that they all come to the same end.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 6:8; Ecclesiastes 1:14; Ecclesiastes 1:2"> Then I thought to myself, “If I'm going to end up the same as a fool, what's the point of being so wise?” So I thought to myself, “This is also hard to understand!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 1:11; Ecclesiastes 9:5; PSALMS 88:12"> Nobody remembers the wise or the fool for very long—in the future everything will be forgotten. Whether wise or foolish, they both die.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 2:11; Jonah 4:8; Ecclesiastes 4:2"> So I ended up feeling disgusted<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="“Feeling disgusted”: literally “hated.”">g</a></sup>with life because everything that happens here on earth is so distressing.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="“Distressing”: the word used here also means evil, trouble, injury, misery etc.">h</a></sup>It's so incomprehensible,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="“Incomprehensible”: again the Teacher is not saying life is without meaning, but that it is hard to discover the meaning.">i</a></sup>like trying to control the wind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="PSALMS 39:6; PSALMS 49:10; Luke 16:27–28"> I even ended up hating what I had achieved here on earth because I have to hand it over to whoever comes after me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="James 3:17; Ecclesiastes 3:22; Ecclesiastes 9:13"> And who knows whether he will be wise or foolish? Yet he will rule over everything I accomplished through my wisdom here on earth. This is just so frustrating, so hard to understand!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="This is a good example of what the Teacher is trying to say. He is not saying it is “meaningless” to leave a legacy, the problem is that you cannot know how it will be used, and that this hard to take!">j</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Genesis 43:14; Job 17:11–15; 2 Corinthians 1:8–10"> I decided to give up, my mind in despair over the significance of all my life's achievements.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 2:17–18; 2 Chronicles 31:20–21; 2 Chronicles 34:2"> For you can work wisely, knowledgably, and with skill—and who benefits? Someone who hasn't worked for it! This is both frustrating and totally unjust!</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="PSALMS 127:2; Ecclesiastes 1:3; Philippians 4:6"> What do you get here on earth for all your hard work and worry?</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Job 14:1; Job 5:7; PSALMS 6:6–7"> Your working life is full of trouble and strife—even at night your thoughts keep you awake. This is tough to comprehend!</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 3:22; Ecclesiastes 8:15; 1 Timothy 6:17"> So what's the best thing to do? Eat, drink, and enjoy your work, recognizing as I did that these things are given to us by God,</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 2:1–12; 1 Kings 4:21–24"> for who can eat or enjoy life apart from him?</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Proverbs 13:22; Job 27:16–17; Proverbs 28:8"> To those who are good, God gives wisdom, knowledge, and joy. But to the sinner God gives the task of gathering and collecting wealth, only to hand it over to someone who pleases God. This also shows how fleeting life is, and hard to understand—like trying to understand how the wind blows.</span>
