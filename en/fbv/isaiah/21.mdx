<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Isaiah 14:23; Isaiah 13:1; Jeremiah 51:42"> A message about the desert by the sea.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“Desert by the sea,” usually taken to refer to Babylon. Although there is no literal sea nearby, the large Euphrates River can be referred to as a “sea” in the same way the Nile is described in 18:2, 19:5.">a</a></sup>Like storm winds passing through the Negev, something is coming from the desert, from a land of terror—</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Isaiah 33:1; Jeremiah 49:34; PSALMS 60:3"> a horrifying vision that has been explained to me. The betrayer still betrays; the destroyer still destroys. Elamites and Medes, go ahead, attack and lay siege to Babylon,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Babylon”: supplied for clarity.">b</a></sup>for I'm putting a stop to all the pain it has caused.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“All the pain it has caused”: literally, “all its sighing.”">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Isaiah 13:8; PSALMS 48:6; Isaiah 26:17"> As a result my body is filled with agony. I'm overwhelmed with pain, like the pain of a woman giving birth. I am confused by what I hear; I am distressed by what I see.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Luke 21:34–36; 1 Samuel 25:36–38; Daniel 5:1"> My mind falters; I shake in panic. The night I looked forward to has become terrifying.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Jeremiah 51:11; Jeremiah 51:57; Jeremiah 51:39"> They set the table, they spread out the rugs, they eat and drink… “Get up, officers! Prepare your shields for battle!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Ezekiel 33:2–7; Ezekiel 3:17; Habakkuk 2:1–2"> This is what the Lord told me: “Go! Have a lookout keep watch, and make sure he reports what he sees.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Isaiah 21:9; Isaiah 37:24; Hebrews 2:1"> When he sees chariots coming pulled by pairs of horses, riders on donkeys and on camels, he should watch very carefully, paying close attention.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Habakkuk 2:1–2; Isaiah 56:10; 1 Peter 5:8"> Then the lookout<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Dead Sea Scroll reading. Hebrew text, “lion.”">d</a></sup>shouted “Sir, I've stood here on the watchtower day after day; night after night I've remained at my post.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Revelation 18:2; Revelation 14:8; Jeremiah 51:8"> Now look! A man in a chariot with a pair of horses is coming.” Then he said, “Babylon has fallen, has fallen! All the idols of its gods lie smashed on the ground!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Jeremiah 51:33; Micah 4:13; 2 Kings 13:7"> My poor downtrodden people, so badly mistreated, I have told you what I have heard from the Lord Almighty, the God of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Genesis 25:14; Obadiah 1:1–16; Amos 1:6"> A message about Edom.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="The word used here actually means silence, and is a play on words with the actual name of the country of Edom. This is how the Septuagint interprets it.">e</a></sup>A voice is calling to me from Seir, asking, “Watchman, what time of night is it? Watchman, what time of night is it?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Acts 17:30–32; Acts 2:37–38; Ezekiel 18:30–32"> The watchman replies, “Morning is coming, but the night is coming again soon. If you want to ask again, then come back and ask.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Jeremiah 25:23–24; 1 Chronicles 1:32; Ezekiel 27:15"> A message about Arabia. Caravans from Dedan, spend the night in the bushes.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Genesis 25:15; Job 6:19; Romans 12:20"> People of Tema, take water to the thirsty, meet the refugees with food.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Job 6:19–20; Isaiah 13:14"> They're running away from a fierce battle, from swords, drawn swords, from bows and arrows.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="PSALMS 120:5; Isaiah 60:7; Isaiah 16:14"> This is what the Lord told me: “Within one year, just as a contract worker precisely counts years, all the glory of Kedar will be gone.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Zechariah 1:6; Numbers 23:19; Matthew 24:35"> There will only be a few left of the archers, the warriors of Kedar.” The Lord, the God of Israel, has spoken.</span>

## Footnotes

<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">21:2</span> “Babylon”: supplied for clarity.</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">21:2</span> “All the pain it has caused”: literally, “all its sighing.”</li>
<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">21:8</span> Dead Sea Scroll reading. Hebrew text, “lion.”</li>
<li id="fn-e"><a href="#fnref-e" className="fnb">e</a> <span className="fnr">21:11</span> The word used here actually means silence, and is a play on words with the actual name of the country of Edom. This is how the Septuagint interprets it.</li>
</ol>
