<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Kings 15:37; 2 Kings 16:1; Isaiah 1:1"> It was during the reign of Ahaz son of Jotham, son of Uzziah, king of Judah, that Rezin, king of Aram, marched to attack Jerusalem. Pekah, son of Remaliah, king of Israel, joined in the attack, but they couldn't conquer the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Isaiah 8:12; Isaiah 7:13; Isaiah 9:9"> When the royal family of Judah was told,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“The royal family of Judah”: literally, “the house of David.” Also in verse 13.">a</a></sup>“Aram and Ephraim have an alliance,” Ahaz and his people were terrified and they shook like trees in the forest tossed about by the wind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Kings 18:17; Isaiah 36:2; Romans 9:27"> Then the Lord told Isaiah, “Take your son Shear-jashub<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="His name means “a remnant shall return,”">b</a></sup>with you and go and meet Ahaz. He'll be at the end of the aqueduct of the upper pool, by the road to the laundry field.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Isaiah 35:4; Lamentations 3:26; Deuteronomy 20:3"> Tell him, ‘Calm down and keep quiet. Don't be afraid or scared over a couple of smoldering bits of firewood, over the burning anger of Rezin and Aram, and of Remaliah's son.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="PSALMS 83:3–4; Zechariah 1:15; PSALMS 2:2"> Aram has plotted to destroy you together with Ephraim and Remaliah's son saying,</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> Let's go and attack Judah! We'll terrorize it and conquer it for ourselves, and make Tabeel's son its king.’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Isaiah 8:10; Lamentations 3:37; Isaiah 46:10–11"> But this is what the Lord God says, “This plan won't materialize—it just won't happen!</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Isaiah 17:1–3; Genesis 14:15; 2 Samuel 8:6"> For the head of Aram is Damascus, and the head of Damascus is Rezin.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Suggesting that the plan was dependent merely on the ambition of one man. Similarly in following verse regarding Remaliah's son.">c</a></sup>In addition, within sixty-five years Israel<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Israel”: literally, “Ephraim,” the most prominent tribe of Israel. Also in the following verse.">d</a></sup>as a nation will be destroyed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Hebrews 11:6; Romans 11:20; 2 Chronicles 20:20"> The head of Israel is Samaria, and the head of Samaria is Remaliah's son. If you don't trust in me, then you won't survive.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="“If you don't trust in me, then you won't survive.” There is a play on words in Hebrew. “In me”: supplied for clarity.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Isaiah 8:5; Isaiah 1:5; Isaiah 10:20"> Later the Lord sent another message to Ahaz,</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Isaiah 37:30; Isaiah 38:7–8; Isaiah 38:22"> “Ask the Lord your God for a sign, whether as deep as where people are buried or as high as heaven.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Ezekiel 33:31; 2 Chronicles 28:22; Malachi 3:15"> “No, I'm not going to ask,” Ahaz replied. “I refuse to put the Lord to the test.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Isaiah 43:24; Isaiah 63:10; Ezekiel 34:18"> Then Isaiah said, “Listen, royal family of Judah! Isn't it enough for you to wear people out? Do you have to wear my God out too?</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Matthew 1:23; Isaiah 9:6; Luke 1:31"> This is why the Lord himself is going to give you a sign. Look! The virgin<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="“Virgin”: or “young woman.” The word used here is inclusive of both meanings. There is however a Hebrew word that exclusively means virgin but it is not used here, so the focus is not on the issue of viriginity. Matthew's reapplication of this prophecy should not blind us to its immediate application in the time of Ahaz. In this connection the rest of the prophecy should not be disregarded which in its immediate historical fulfillment (Isaiah 8) related to Isaiah's wife giving birth to a son. In her case she was not a virgin, since she had already given birth to a son previously.">f</a></sup>will become pregnant and give birth to a son, and she will call him Immanuel.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Immanuel means “God with us.”">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Isaiah 7:22; Amos 5:15; Matthew 3:4"> He will eat curds and honey until the time he knows to refuse evil and choose the good.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Isaiah 8:4; Deuteronomy 1:39; Isaiah 9:11"> For before the boy knows to refuse evil and choose the good, the land of the two kings<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Referring to king of Aram and the king of Israel.">h</a></sup>you're afraid of will be deserted.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Isaiah 8:7–8; Isaiah 10:5–6; 2 Chronicles 28:19–21"> The Lord will make you, your people, and the royal family experience a time unlike anything since the day Ephraim split from Judah. He will bring the king of Assyria to attack you!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="“To attack you.” Supplied for clarity.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Isaiah 5:26; Joshua 24:12; Deuteronomy 1:44"> At that time the Lord will whistle to call flies from the distant rivers of Egypt and bees from the country of Assyria.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Obviously referring to the kings of Egypt and Assyria. Their armies are referenced in the next verse.">j</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Isaiah 2:19; Jeremiah 16:16; Isaiah 2:21"> They will all come and descend on the steep valleys and rock crevices, on all the thorn bushes and waterholes.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Isaiah 8:7; Ezekiel 29:18; Isaiah 10:15"> At that time the Lord will use a razor hired from beyond the Euphrates River, the king of Assyria, to shave you from head to toe, including your beards.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Isaiah 5:17; Jeremiah 39:10; Isaiah 37:30"> At that time someone who manages to keep a young cow and two sheep alive</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Isaiah 7:15; Matthew 3:4; 2 Samuel 17:29"> will eat curds, because they produce so much milk—for everyone who survives in the land will eat curds and honey.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Isaiah 5:6; Song of Songs 8:11–12; Matthew 21:33"> At that time everywhere that once had a thousand vines worth a thousand shekels will only have brambles and thorns.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Genesis 27:3"> People will go hunting there with bows and arrows because the land will be covered with brambles and thorns.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Isaiah 7:21–22; Isaiah 13:20–22; Isaiah 5:17"> In fact, all the hills that were once tilled by the hoe you won't want to go to because you'll worry about the brambles and thorns there. They'll just be where cattle are let loose and where sheep roam.</span>
