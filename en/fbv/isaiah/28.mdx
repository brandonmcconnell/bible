<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Hosea 7:5; Isaiah 28:7; 2 Kings 14:25–27"> Tragedy is coming to the city of Samaria,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“The city of Samaria”: supplied for clarity.">a</a></sup>the crowning glory of Ephraim's drunks, to the fading flower of wonderful beauty, sitting above a fertile valley, and beloved by those hammered by wine.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Isaiah 30:30; Nahum 1:8; Ezekiel 13:11"> Watch out, for the Lord has someone who is strong and powerful! He is going to smash it to the ground like a hailstorm and a tornado, like a torrential rain and an overwhelming flood.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Isaiah 26:6; Hebrews 10:29; Revelation 11:2"> That crowning glory of Ephraim's drunks will be trampled underfoot.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Nahum 3:12; Micah 7:1; Isaiah 28:1"> That fading flower of wonderful beauty, sitting above a fertile valley, will be like figs ripe before the summer harvest—as soon as people discover them, they grab and eat them.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Peter 5:4; Isaiah 62:3; Isaiah 60:19"> At that time the Lord Almighty will be a beautiful, glorious crown that brings pride to those of his people who are left.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="PSALMS 18:32–34; John 5:30; Joshua 1:9"> He will be an inspiration to the judges to do what's right, and he will encourage those who fight off the attacks on the gate.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Isaiah 56:10–12; Luke 21:34; Isaiah 19:14"> But these people also drink so much wine and beer that they sway from side to side and stumble over. Even priests and prophets stagger along, their minds muddled by beer and wine. Because of the drink they are confused about visions and make mistakes when they give decisions.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Jeremiah 48:26; Proverbs 26:11; Habakkuk 2:15–16"> All their tables are full of vomit—filth is everywhere.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="PSALMS 131:2; Jeremiah 6:10; John 12:38"> “Just who is he trying to teach knowledge to?” they ask.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“They ask.” Supplied for clarity.">b</a></sup>“Who is he explaining his message to? To children just weaned from milk, to babies just removed from the breast?</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Isaiah 28:13; 2 Timothy 3:7; Jeremiah 11:7"> He tells us this blah and that blah, blah upon blah, and again blah and blah, and even more blah and blah!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="There are indeed eight “blahs” in the Hebrew. The word was also used to describe idols, because they are also “blah”!">c</a></sup>It's a bit here and a bit there.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Corinthians 14:21; Deuteronomy 28:49; Isaiah 33:19"> Fine—so now the Lord will talk to this people in foreign languages that sound strange to them!<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Since the people had said that God was talking nonsense to them, he now will reveal his truth through other languages in order that he might communicate with them—even though these languages would sound like “blah” to them! Also verse 13.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Matthew 11:28–29; Isaiah 30:15; Jeremiah 6:16"> He had told them, “You can rest here. Let those who are tired rest. This is the place where you can safely relax.” But they refused to listen.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Matthew 13:14; Isaiah 6:9–10; 2 Peter 3:16"> Therefore the Lord's message to them will become, “This blah and that blah, blah upon blah, and again blah and blah, and even more blah and blah, a bit here and a bit there,” so that they'll fall over backwards, and they'll be wounded, trapped, and captured.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="In other words, since they treat the Lord's message so contemptuously, they will reap the painful consequences of dismissing the Lord's advice.">e</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Isaiah 28:22; Isaiah 29:20; Isaiah 1:10"> So pay attention to the Lord's message, you scornful rulers who lead these people in Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Isaiah 8:7–8; Ezekiel 13:22; Amos 2:4"> You claim, “We've made an agreement with death; we've got a contract with the grave. When the terrible disaster rushes by, it won't affect us, because our lies protect us and we hide in our own deceptions.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="PSALMS 118:22; Acts 4:11–12; 1 Peter 2:6–8"> Consequently, listen to what the Lord God says, Look! I'm laying a foundation stone in Jerusalem, a strong, well-tested stone. It's a valuable cornerstone that provides a firm foundation. Anyone who trusts in it won't be shaken loose.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="“Shaken loose”: the word used normally means “hurry,” but can also have the meaning “disturbed.”">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Kings 21:13; Amos 7:7–9; Revelation 16:21"> I will make justice as straight as a measuring line, and doing what's right the standard rule. Hail will destroy the protection of your lies, and water will flood the place where you're hiding.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Isaiah 7:7; Isaiah 8:10; Isaiah 28:15"> Your agreement with death will be canceled; your contract with the grave will be revoked. When the terrible disaster rushes by, it will trample you underfoot.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Habakkuk 3:16; Isaiah 36:22; 2 Kings 24:2"> It will rush by time after time, dragging you away morning upon morning, day and night, rushing on and on. Once you understand this message you will be absolutely terrified.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Isaiah 64:6; 1 Corinthians 1:18–31; Romans 9:30–32"> The bed is too short so you can't stretch out; the blanket is too narrow so you can't cover yourself.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Chronicles 14:11; Joshua 10:12; 2 Samuel 5:20"> The Lord will come on the attack like he did to the Philistines at Mount Perazim, like he shook them in the Valley of Gibeon,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="“Philistines”: supplied for clarity. See 1 Chronicles 14:8-16.">g</a></sup>coming to do what he has to do, his strange work; coming to act as he must, his unusual action.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Isaiah 10:22–23; Isaiah 24:1–23; Matthew 27:44"> So don't mock, or your imprisonment will be even worse, for the Lord, the Lord Almighty, has explained to me his decision to destroy the whole country.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Isaiah 1:2; Jeremiah 22:29; Revelation 2:11"> Listen to what I'm saying! Listen and pay attention! Hear what I have to say!</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Jeremiah 4:3; Hosea 10:11–12"> Does the farmer spend all his time plowing? Does he spend all his time preparing the soil?</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Ezekiel 4:9; Matthew 23:23; Exodus 9:31–32"> Once he has everything ready doesn't he sow seeds like dill and cumin, doesn't he plant wheat and barley in rows, with spelt grain as a border?</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Exodus 31:3–6; Exodus 36:2; Job 39:17"> His God gives him instructions and teaches him the right thing to do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Isaiah 41:15; Isaiah 27:7–8; Jeremiah 46:28"> You don't use a heavy tool to thresh dill! You don't use the wheel of a cart to thresh cumin! Instead you use a stick to beat out the dill, and a rod to beat out the cumin.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="1 Corinthians 9:9–10; Matthew 3:12; John 12:24"> Grain used for bread is easily damaged so you don't thresh it forever. When you drive your cart wheels over it with your horses, you don't crush it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Romans 11:33; Jeremiah 32:19; Job 5:9"> This is also from the Lord Almighty who is very wise and gives great advice.</span>
</div>
