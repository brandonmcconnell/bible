Once again he will choose Israel and he will bring them back to live once more in their own land. Foreigners will come and join them there, and they will unite with the descendants of Jacob.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Isaiah 61:5; Isaiah 60:14; Jeremiah 30:16"> Nations will go with them and escort them to their own land. The foreigners who stay in the Lord's land will serve the Israelites. In this way the captors become their captives, and they rule over their former oppressors.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Jeremiah 30:10; Isaiah 32:18; Ezra 9:8–9"> At that time the Lord will give you relief from your pain and trouble, and from the hard labor you were forced to do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Isaiah 49:26; Habakkuk 1:2–10; Jeremiah 51:34–35"> You will mock the king of Babylon, saying, “How your oppressive rule has been ended, and your insolence stopped!</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="PSALMS 125:3; Isaiah 9:4; Jeremiah 48:15–17"> The Lord has broken the rod of the wicked, the rulers' scepter.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Daniel 7:19–21; James 2:13; Revelation 17:16–17"> You kept on furiously hitting foreign peoples without stopping, and aggressively ruled nations with unrestrained persecution.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="PSALMS 126:1–3; PSALMS 98:7–9; PSALMS 96:11–13"> Now the whole earth rests peacefully, and everyone starts celebrating!</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Ezekiel 31:16; Isaiah 55:12–13; Zechariah 11:2"> The cypress and cedar trees are glad you're gone. They sing, ‘Since you were cut down no woodcutters are coming to cut us down!’</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Ezekiel 32:21–32; Jeremiah 50:8; Proverbs 15:24"> Those in the grave beneath are keen to meet you when you arrive. It wakes the spirits of the dead to welcome you, those of all the rulers of the earth. All the kings of the nations stand up from their thrones.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="These verses are poetic, and not to be taken literally.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="PSALMS 49:6–14; Ecclesiastes 2:16; PSALMS 82:6–7"> They will all speak up and tell you, ‘So you too are as weak as we are—you've become just like us!</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Daniel 5:25–30; Job 21:11–15; Isaiah 66:24"> Your pride is now buried with you in the grave, along with the harp music you loved. Maggots are the bed you lie on, and worms are your blanket.’</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Ezekiel 28:13–17; Revelation 12:7–10; Luke 10:18"> Morning star,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Literally, “shining one,” referring to the planet Venus whose rise in the sky promised the soon-coming dawn. In the Septuagint this was translated as “dawn bringer” which in Latin became “Lucifer,” (light bringer) which is the basis for the King James translation.">b</a></sup>son of the dawn, how you have fallen from heaven! Destroyer of nations, you have been cut down to the ground!</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Daniel 8:10–12; Ezekiel 28:2; Ezekiel 28:12–16"> You said to yourself: ‘I will ascend to heaven. I will raise my throne above the stars of God. I will sit enthroned on the mountain of meeting, the summit of the northern mountain.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="This fits with Babylonian mythology in which the gods were thought of as meeting on a mountain north of Babylon.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Isaiah 47:8; Genesis 3:5; 2 Thessalonians 2:4"> I will ascend to the high places above the clouds; I will make myself like the Most High.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Matthew 11:23; Ezekiel 32:23; Acts 12:22–23"> But you are dragged down to the grave, into the depths of the pit.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Jeremiah 50:23; PSALMS 58:10–11; Jeremiah 51:20–23"> Those who see you will stare at you, examining you closely, asking, ‘Is this the man who shook the earth, who made kingdoms tremble?</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Joel 2:3; Isaiah 13:19–22; Isaiah 45:13"> Is this the one who turned the world into a desert, destroyed cities, and never let his prisoners return home?’</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="2 Chronicles 24:25; Isaiah 22:16; Ezekiel 32:18–32"> All the other kings of the nations lie in splendor in their great mausoleums.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Jeremiah 41:9; Jeremiah 41:7; 1 Kings 21:24"> But you are thrown out of your grave like a branch nobody wants, buried underneath the bodies of those killed by the sword. You are like a corpse trodden underfoot. You are tossed into a pit full of rocks—</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="PSALMS 21:10; Job 18:19; PSALMS 37:28"> you will not be buried like those other kings<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Like those other kings”: supplied for clarity.">d</a></sup>because you destroyed your own land and killed your own people. The descendants of those who do evil will never survive.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Matthew 23:35; Exodus 20:5; Habakkuk 2:8–12"> Get ready to execute his sons because of their fathers'<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="“Fathers” is plural to suggest that the guilt was not just the immediate father, but his ancestors as well.">e</a></sup>sins. Don't let them take over the earth; don't let them fill the whole world with their cities.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Jeremiah 50:29–35; Proverbs 10:7; 1 Kings 14:10"> I will come and attack them, declares the Lord Almighty. I will destroy everything: their reputation, those who remain, their children, and their descendants, says the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Zephaniah 2:14; Isaiah 13:21–22; 1 Kings 14:10"> I will make Babylon into a place for water birds<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Some suggest a species of owl, others the bittern.">f</a></sup>and into marshland. I will sweep her away with the broom of destruction, declares the Lord Almighty.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Proverbs 19:21; Job 23:13; Isaiah 46:10–11"> The Lord Almighty has sworn an oath: It will be as I have planned. It will happen as I have decided.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Isaiah 9:4; Nahum 1:13; Isaiah 37:36–38"> I will smash the Assyrians when they are in my country Israel; I will trample them underfoot on my mountains. I will remove their yoke from my people, and take away the burdens they place on my people's shoulders.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Isaiah 23:9; Zephaniah 3:6–8; Exodus 15:12"> This is the plan I have made regarding the whole earth; my hand stretches out to control all the nations.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Isaiah 43:13; Job 23:13; 2 Chronicles 20:6"> The Lord Almighty has made his plan, and who will block it? His hand stretches out, and who will push back against it?</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 Kings 16:20; Isaiah 13:1; 2 Chronicles 28:27"> The following message came in the year King Ahaz died.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Isaiah 30:6; Isaiah 11:8; 2 Chronicles 26:6"> All you Philistines, don't celebrate the fact that the rod that was hitting you is broken, because from the root of that snake will grow up a viper, its fruit will be a flying serpent.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Isaiah 7:21–22; Zephaniah 2:4–7; Isaiah 5:17"> The poor will have food, and the needy will live in safety, but you Philistines will die in a famine, and I<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="“I”: Dead Sea Scroll reading. Traditional text, “he.”">g</a></sup>will kill those who survive.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Jeremiah 1:14; Isaiah 20:1; Isaiah 14:29"> Howl, gates! Weep, town! Melt away in fear, all you Philistines! For a cloud of smoke is approaching from the north—an army with no soldier hanging back.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="PSALMS 102:16; PSALMS 87:5; James 2:5"> What will be the answer given to the messengers from that nation? “The Lord was the one who laid the foundations of Zion, and that's where his suffering people will be kept safe.”</span>
</div>

<li id="fn-f"><a href="#fnref-f" className="fnb">f</a> <span className="fnr">14:23</span> Some suggest a species of owl, others the bittern.</li>
