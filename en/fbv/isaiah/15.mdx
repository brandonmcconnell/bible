<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Amos 2:1–3; Jeremiah 48:1–47; Isaiah 11:14"> A message about Moab. The town of Ar in Moab is devastated, destroyed in a single night! The town of Kir in Moab is devastated, destroyed in a single night!</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Jeremiah 48:1; Leviticus 21:5; Isaiah 3:24"> The people of Dibon go up to their temple to weep at its high places. The Moabites weep over the towns of Nebo and Medeba. Every head has been shaved, every beard has been cut off in mourning.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Jonah 3:6–8; Jeremiah 19:13; Deuteronomy 22:8"> They wear sackcloth in their streets. On the rooftops and in the open squares they all weep, falling down crying.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Jeremiah 48:34; 1 Kings 19:4; Jonah 4:8"> The people of Heshbon and Elealeh cry out in sadness, their voices heard as far as Jahaz. That's why even the bravest soldiers of Moab shout—because they're so terrified.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Jeremiah 48:5; Jeremiah 4:20; Luke 19:41–44"> I weep over Moab. Moabite refugees run all the way to Zoar and to Eglath-shelishiyah. Weeping they go up Luhith hill; crying for their destruction they walk along the road to Horonaim.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Joel 1:10–12; Isaiah 19:5–7; Numbers 32:36"> The waters of Nimrim have all dried up. The grass is withered, all the vegetation has disappeared—nothing green is left.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Jeremiah 48:36; Nahum 2:12–13; Isaiah 10:6"> Everything they had gained, all their possessions, they have to carry across Willow River.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Ezekiel 47:10; Isaiah 15:2–5; Jeremiah 48:31–34"> Their cry of grief echoes through the whole country of Moab; their wailing and mourning extends all the way from Eglaim to Beer-elim.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Kings 17:25; Leviticus 26:28; Leviticus 26:24"> River Dimon<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Or “Dibon.”">a</a></sup>is full of blood, but I will bring more upon the town of Dimon—a lion to attack the Moabite refugees and those who are left in the country.</span>
