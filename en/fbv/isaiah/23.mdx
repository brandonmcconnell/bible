<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Joel 3:4–8; Amos 1:9–10; Genesis 10:4"> A message about Tyre. Howl, people on the ships of Tarshish! Tyre has been destroyed—nothing is left of the houses and the harbor. They heard the news from the people of Cyprus.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Isaiah 47:5; Isaiah 41:1; Ezekiel 28:2"> Stay shocked into silence, people of the coastlands, merchants of Sidon, and sailors.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Jeremiah 2:18; 1 Chronicles 13:5; Ezekiel 27:33"> Egyptian grain<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“Egyptian grain”: literally, “the grain of Shihor.”">a</a></sup>came across the wide oceans. The Nile's harvest was what made Tyre money; she was the merchant to the nations.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Revelation 18:23; Genesis 10:15; Jeremiah 47:3–4"> Feel the shame, Sidon! For the fortress of the sea says,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Tyre was called the daughter of Sidon (Isaiah 23:12), so the words that follow could be Sidon mourning the loss of her “daughter.” Alternatively it could be Tyre, mourning the loss of her children (inhabitants).">b</a></sup>“I have no children, having never been in labor or given birth. I have not brought up young men or brought up young women.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Joshua 2:9–11; Exodus 15:14–16; Revelation 18:17–19"> When the news about Tyre reaches Egypt they will be in agony.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Isaiah 23:12; Isaiah 21:15; Isaiah 23:10"> Sail across to Tarshish! Howl, people of the coastlands!</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Isaiah 22:2; Isaiah 32:13; Joshua 19:29"> Is this really your triumphant city, whose beginnings are from the distant past, who has sent out people to colonize faraway places?</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Isaiah 10:8; Jeremiah 50:44–45; Deuteronomy 29:24–28"> Who planned this attack on Tyre? Tyre, who created kingdoms, whose merchants were princes, whose traders were honored around the world!</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="James 4:6; Isaiah 13:11; Job 40:11–12"> The Lord Almighty planned it, to humble its pride in all its glory, and to bring down all who receive worldly honor.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Job 12:21; Isaiah 23:14; PSALMS 18:32"> Work your land, people of Tarshish, as they do beside the Nile, for you don't have a harbor anymore.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="The Hebrew of this verse is unclear. It apparently suggests that the people of Tarshish should turn to agriculture now that there won't be any more trade from the mother city of Tyre.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Zechariah 9:3–4; Exodus 14:21; Jeremiah 47:7"> The Lord held his hand out over the sea and shook kingdoms.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Kingdoms”: presumably the other Phoenician kingdoms along the coast from Tyre.">d</a></sup>He has condemned Phoenicia, giving the order to destroy their fortresses.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Revelation 18:22; Ezekiel 26:13–14; Isaiah 23:1–2"> He said, “Don't celebrate any more, mistreated virgin daughter of Sidon. Go and sail over to Cyprus—however, even there you won't find rest.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Habakkuk 1:6; Isaiah 10:5; 2 Kings 17:24"> Look at the country of the Babylonians, this people that are not as they used to be! The Assyrians have turned it into a place for desert animals. They set up their siege towers, they demolished the fortresses, and ruined the country.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Isaiah 23:1; Isaiah 23:6; Ezekiel 27:25–30"> Howl, people on the ships of Tarshish because your fortress is destroyed!</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Jeremiah 25:22; Jeremiah 25:9–11; Daniel 7:14"> At that time Tyre will be forgotten for seventy years, a king's lifetime, as it were. But at the end of these seventy years, Tyre will be like the song about a prostitute,</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Jeremiah 30:14; Proverbs 7:10–12"> “Take a lyre and walk around the city, forgotten prostitute! Play and sing so people will remember you!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Nahum 3:4; Ezekiel 27:6–36; Revelation 19:2"> After seventy years, the Lord will restore Tyre. But then she will go back to hiring herself out as a prostitute, selling herself to all the kingdoms of the world.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="PSALMS 72:10; Deuteronomy 26:12–14; Philippians 4:17–18"> However, her profits and what she earns will be consecrated to the Lord. They won't be kept or saved up, for her business earnings will go to those who worship the Lord, to provide them with plenty of food and good clothes.</span>

## Footnotes

<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">23:4</span> Tyre was called the daughter of Sidon (Isaiah 23:12), so the words that follow could be Sidon mourning the loss of her “daughter.” Alternatively it could be Tyre, mourning the loss of her children (inhabitants).</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">23:10</span> The Hebrew of this verse is unclear. It apparently suggests that the people of Tarshish should turn to agriculture now that there won't be any more trade from the mother city of Tyre.</li>
<li id="fn-d"><a href="#fnref-d" className="fnb">d</a> <span className="fnr">23:11</span> “Kingdoms”: presumably the other Phoenician kingdoms along the coast from Tyre.</li>
