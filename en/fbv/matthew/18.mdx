<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Mark 9:33–37; Luke 9:46–48; Matthew 20:20–28"> Around that time the disciples came to Jesus, and asked, “Who is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Matthew 19:13–14; Mark 9:36–37; 1 Kings 3:7"> Jesus called over a small child. He had the child stand in front of them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Mark 10:14–15; 1 Peter 2:2; Matthew 19:14"> “I tell you the truth, unless you change the way you think and become like little children, you won't ever enter the kingdom of heaven,” he told them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="James 4:10; Luke 14:11; Mark 10:43"> “But whoever humbles themselves and becomes like this little child is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Matthew 25:40; Mark 9:37; John 13:20"> Whoever accepts a little child like this in my name accepts me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Mark 9:42; Luke 17:1–2; Matthew 18:10"> But anyone who makes one of these little ones who trust in me sin, it would be better for them to have a large millstone<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally, “a donkey millstone,” referring to the massive millstone turned by a donkey, rather than a small millstone turned by hand.">a</a></sup>tied around their neck and be drowned in the depths of the sea.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Luke 17:1; 1 Corinthians 11:19; Matthew 26:24"> What a disaster is coming on the world for all its temptations to sin! Temptations will surely come, but it will be a disaster for the person through whom the temptation comes!</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Matthew 5:29–30; Philippians 3:8–9; Romans 13:12"> If your hand or your foot causes you to sin, cut it off and throw it away. It's better for you to enter eternal life crippled or lame rather than to have two hands or two feet and to be thrown into eternal fire.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 14:22; Matthew 18:8; Matthew 5:29"> If your eye causes you to sin, pull it out and throw it away. It's better for you to enter eternal life with one eye than to have two eyes and to be thrown into the fire of Gehenna.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Gehenna.” See note under 5:22.">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="PSALMS 91:11; PSALMS 34:7; Hebrews 1:14"> Make sure you don't look down on these little ones. I tell you that in heaven their angels are always with<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Literally, “see the face of.”">c</a></sup>my heavenly Father.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> <sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Verse 11 is not in the earliest manuscripts.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Ezekiel 34:12; Luke 15:4–7; Ezekiel 34:16"> What do you think about this? If a man has a hundred sheep, and one of them wanders off, won't he leave the ninety-nine on the hills and go in search of the one that's wandered away?</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Zephaniah 3:17; Luke 15:5–10; James 2:13"> And if he finds it, I tell you he really rejoices over that sheep more than the ninety-nine that didn't wander away.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="John 17:12; John 6:39–40; 2 Peter 3:9"> In the same way my heavenly Father does not want any of these little ones to be lost.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Leviticus 19:17; Luke 17:3–4; Galatians 6:1"> If a brother<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Or “fellow believer.”">e</a></sup>sins against you,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="The words “against you” are not in all the early manuscripts. If they are omitted, then this recommendation becomes a general one dealing with anyone's sins, whether “against you” or not.">f</a></sup>go and point out the wrong to him, just between the two of you. If he listens to you, you've won your brother over.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Deuteronomy 19:15; 2 Corinthians 13:1; John 8:17"> But if he doesn't listen, then take one or two more people with you, so that by two or three witnesses the truth can be confirmed.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="See Deuteronomy 19:15.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Thessalonians 3:6; 1 Corinthians 5:9–7; 2 Thessalonians 3:14–15"> If, however, he refuses to listen to them, then tell the church. If he refuses to listen to the church as well, then treat him as a foreigner<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Literally, a “Gentile,” a non-believer.">h</a></sup>and a tax collector.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Matthew 16:19; 2 Corinthians 2:10; John 20:23"> I tell you the truth, whatever you ban on earth will have been banned in heaven, and whatever you allow on earth will have been allowed in heaven.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Matthew 7:7; 1 John 5:14–16; Mark 11:24"> I also tell you that if two of you agree here on earth about something you're praying for, then my heavenly Father will do it for you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Corinthians 5:4; Revelation 21:3; Matthew 28:20"> For where two or three gather together in my name, I'm there with them.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Luke 17:3–4; Matthew 18:15"> Peter came to Jesus and asked him, “Lord, how many times should I forgive my brother for sinning against me? Seven times?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Colossians 3:13; Mark 11:25; Matthew 6:14–15"> “No, not seven times. I would say seventy times seven!” Jesus told him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Matthew 13:24; Romans 14:12; Matthew 3:2"> “This is why the kingdom of heaven is like a king who wanted to settle accounts with those servants who owed him money.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="PSALMS 38:4; PSALMS 130:3–4; Luke 16:5"> As he began to settle accounts, one servant was brought to him who owed him ten thousand talents.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="An astronomical amount.">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Kings 4:1; Nehemiah 5:5; Luke 7:42"> Since he didn't have the money to pay, his master ordered him to be sold, along with his wife and children and all his possessions, so that the debt could be paid back.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Matthew 18:29; Luke 7:43; Matthew 8:2"> The servant fell down on his knees and said to his master, ‘Please be patient with me! I will pay everything back!’</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="PSALMS 145:8; Nehemiah 9:17; PSALMS 86:15"> The master took pity on the servant, released him, and canceled the debt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Matthew 20:2; Ezekiel 45:9; Deuteronomy 15:2"> But when that same servant went out he found one of his fellow-servants who owed him just a hundred denarii.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="A denarius was a small coin. The contrast is between the huge amount forgiven the first servant and the very small amount owed to him by the second servant.">j</a></sup>He grabbed him by the neck and choked him, saying, ‘Pay me back what you owe me!’</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Matthew 18:26; Matthew 6:12; Philemon 1:18–19"> His fellow-servant threw himself down at the man's feet and begged him, ‘Please be patient with me! I will pay you back!’</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="1 Kings 22:27; 1 Kings 21:27–29"> But the man refused, and went and threw his fellow-servant into prison until he paid back what he owed.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Romans 12:15; Hebrews 13:3; Mark 3:5"> When the other servants saw what took place they were shocked and upset. They went and told their master everything that had happened.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Luke 19:22; Matthew 25:26; Romans 3:19"> Then the master called the man back in and told him, ‘You evil servant! I forgave you all your debt because you begged me to.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Colossians 3:13; Matthew 6:12; Ephesians 4:32–2"> Shouldn't you have been merciful to your fellow-servant as well, just as I was merciful to you?’</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Matthew 5:25–26; James 2:13; Luke 12:58–59"> His master became angry and handed him over to the jailors until he had repaid all the debt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Matthew 6:14–15; James 2:13; Luke 6:37–38"> This is what my heavenly Father will do to every one of you unless you sincerely<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="Literally, “from your heart.”">k</a></sup>forgive your brothers.”</span>
