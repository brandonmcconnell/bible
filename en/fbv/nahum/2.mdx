Spears with their wooden shafts<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="The word for spear here is that of the wood used, and it is debated whether this is pine, cypress, or fir.">d</a></sup>are held up and shaken.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Jeremiah 4:13; Ezekiel 26:10; Nahum 3:2–3"> Chariots race madly through the streets, rushing back and forth across the town squares. Bright as torches, they dash like lightning flashes.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Jeremiah 46:12; Jeremiah 51:27–28; Jeremiah 50:29"> He shouts orders to his officers. They stumble as they rush to attack the wall. The battering ram is set up.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Isaiah 45:1–2; 2 Peter 3:10–11"> The river gates are opened; the palace washes away.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Or “dissolves in fear.”">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Isaiah 38:14; Isaiah 59:11; Luke 23:48"> “Queen” Nineveh<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="The word used here is of uncertain meaning. It does not occur anywhere else in the Old Testament.">f</a></sup>is stripped, and led away into exile, with her servant girls mourning like doves, and beating their breasts.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Jeremiah 51:13; Revelation 17:1; Jeremiah 46:5"> Nineveh is a leaky pool—its population is like water running away. “Stop! Stop” people shout, but nobody turns back.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Ezekiel 26:12; Daniel 11:8; Nahum 2:12–13"> Loot the silver! Loot the gold! There's an endless supply—an abundance of everything you could ever want.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Joel 2:6; PSALMS 22:14; Daniel 5:6"> Deserted, destroyed, devastated! Hearts faint, knees tremble, stomachs ache. Everyone's faces turn pale.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Isaiah 5:29; Jeremiah 2:15; Isaiah 31:4"> Where now is the lion's den? Where is the place where the young lions used to feed? Where is the lion, and the lioness, and the lion cub that were afraid of no one?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="The symbol of the lion was used extensively by the Assyrians, and also reflects their cruel treatment of their victims.">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Jeremiah 51:34; PSALMS 17:12; Isaiah 10:6–14"> The lion tears apart meat for his cubs, and strangles prey for his lionesses. He fills his den with prey, his lair with carcasses.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Jeremiah 21:13; PSALMS 46:9; Nahum 3:5"> Watch out! For I am against you, declares the Lord Almighty. I will set fire to your chariots and they will go up in smoke. Your strong young men<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Literally, “young lions.”">h</a></sup>will be killed by the sword. I will stop you plundering other peoples.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Literally, “cut off your prey from the earth.”">i</a></sup>The demands of your ambassadors<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Emissaries: the Assyrians sent their representatives to other nations to demand submission and tribute.">j</a></sup>will no longer be heard.</span>
</div>
