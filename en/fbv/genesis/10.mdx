<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Genesis 2:4; Genesis 9:7; Genesis 5:1"> The following are the genealogies<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="These genealogies are repeated in 1 Chronicles 1:5-27.">a</a></sup>of the sons of Noah: Shem, Ham, and Japheth. They had sons born to them after the flood.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Ezekiel 38:2; Ezekiel 38:6; Revelation 20:8"> The sons<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Note that “sons” throughout this chapter can also mean “descendants.”">b</a></sup>of Japheth: Gomer, Magog, Madai, Javan, Tubal, Meshech, and Tiras.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Ezekiel 27:14; Jeremiah 51:27"> The sons of Gomer: Ashkenaz, Riphath, and Togarmah.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Daniel 11:30; Isaiah 23:12; Numbers 24:24"> The sons of Javan: Elishah, Tarshish, Kittim, and Dodanim.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“Dodanim”: the Septuagint takes the spelling to be Rodanim, as does the parallel passage in 1 Chronicles 1:7. Note that the last two names at least are probably those of a group of people rather than a personal name.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Zephaniah 2:11; Isaiah 42:4; Jeremiah 2:10"> The descendants of these ancestors spread throughout the coastal areas, each group having their own language, with their families developing into different nations.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Chronicles 1:8–16; Ezekiel 27:10; PSALMS 105:23"> The sons of Ham: Cush, Mizraim, Put, and Canaan.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Ezekiel 27:22; Ezekiel 27:15; Isaiah 21:13"> The sons of Cush: Seba, Havilah, Sabtah, Raamah, and Sabteca.<br />The sons of Raamah: Sheba and Dedan.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Micah 5:6"> Cush was also the father of Nimrod, who set himself up as the first tyrant on earth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Chronicles 28:22; Genesis 6:11; Genesis 25:27"> He was a powerful fighter who defied<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Defied”: The Septuagint reads “against” or “versus.”">d</a></sup>the Lord; which is why there's the expression, “Like Nimrod, a powerful fighter who defied the Lord.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Genesis 11:9; Genesis 11:2; Genesis 14:1"> His kingdom began in the cities of Babel,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="“Babel” or “Babylon.” Nimrod is the first person in Scripture described as having a kingdom, normally associated with an imposed rule using force.">e</a></sup>Erech, Akkad, and Calneh, all located in the land of Shinar.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="“Shinar”: or “Babylonia.”">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Micah 5:6; PSALMS 83:8; Nahum 1:1"> From there he moved into Assyria<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="“Assyria”: in Micah 5:6Assyria is called “the land of Nimrod.”">g</a></sup>and built the cities of Nineveh, Rehoboth-Ir, Calah,</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12"> and Resen, which lies between Nineveh and the great city of Calah.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Chronicles 1:11–12; Jeremiah 46:9; Ezekiel 30:5"> Mizraim was the father of the Ludites, the Anamites, the Lehabites, the Naphtuhites,</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Jeremiah 47:4; 1 Chronicles 1:12; Deuteronomy 2:23"> the Pathrusites, the Casluhites, and the Caphtorites (ancestors of the Philistines).<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="See Jeremiah 47:4and Amos 9:7.">h</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Numbers 34:2–15; Exodus 34:11; Genesis 23:3"> Canaan was the father of Sidon, his firstborn, and of the Hittites,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="“The Hittites”: literally “Heth.”">i</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Samuel 24:18; Judges 1:21; Zechariah 9:7"> the Jebusites, the Amorites, the Girgashites,</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Genesis 34:2"> the Hivites, the Arkites, the Sinites,</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Joshua 18:22; 2 Samuel 8:9; Ezekiel 27:8"> the Arvadites, the Zemarites, and the Hamathites.<br />Later the Canaanite tribes spread out</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Genesis 14:2; Genesis 19:24–25; Joshua 12:7–8"> and the territory of the Canaanites stretched from Sidon towards Gerar and all the way to Gaza, then towards Sodom, Gomorrah, Admah, and Zeboiim, all the way to Lasha.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Genesis 10:6; Genesis 11:1–9"> These were the sons of Ham according to their tribes, languages, lands, and nation.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Numbers 24:24; Genesis 11:10–26"> Shem, whose older brother<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="See note on 5:32.">j</a></sup>was Japheth, also had sons. Shem was the forefather of all the sons of Eber.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Isaiah 66:19; Jeremiah 25:25; Genesis 14:1–9"> The sons of Shem: Elam, Asshur, Arphaxad, Lud, and Aram.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Job 1:1; Jeremiah 25:20"> The sons of Aram: Uz, Hul, Gether, and Mash.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="“Mash”: The Septuagint and 1 Chronicles 1:17read “Meshech.”">k</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Luke 3:35; Genesis 11:12–15"> Arphaxad was the father of Shelah. Shelah was the father of Eber.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="1 Chronicles 1:19; Genesis 10:32; Deuteronomy 32:8"> Eber had two sons. One was named Peleg,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="The word means “divided.”">l</a></sup>because in his time the earth was divided; the name of his brother was Joktan.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="1 Chronicles 1:20–28"> Joktan was the father of Almodad, Sheleph, Hazarmaveth, Jerah,</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="1 Chronicles 1:20–28"> Hadoram, Uzal, Diklah,</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="1 Chronicles 1:20–28; 1 Kings 10:1; Genesis 25:3"> Obal, Abimael, Sheba,</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="1 Kings 9:28; 1 Chronicles 9:13; Genesis 2:11"> Ophir, Havilah, and Jobab. These were all sons of Joktan.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Numbers 23:7"> They lived in the land lying between Mesha to Sephar, in the hill country to the east.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Genesis 10:5; Genesis 10:20; Acts 17:26"> These were the sons of Shem, according to their tribes, languages, lands, and nations.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Genesis 9:19; Acts 17:26; Genesis 10:1"> These were all the tribes descended from Noah's sons, according to their genealogies and national groups. From these ancestors the different nations of the earth spread around the world after the flood.</span>
</div>
