<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Acts 2:6; Zephaniah 3:9; Isaiah 19:18"> At that time the whole world spoke just one language and used words with the same meaning.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Genesis 10:10; Daniel 1:2; Genesis 14:1"> As they moved east they discovered a plain in the land of Shinar so they settled there.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Genesis 14:10; Exodus 1:14; Exodus 2:3"> They said to one another, “Come on, let's make some bricks and bake them with fire.” (They used brick instead of stone, and tar instead of cement).<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="This was because in the Babylonian plain there was no stone to use for buildings.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Deuteronomy 1:28; John 5:44; Luke 1:51"> Then they said, “Now let's build a city for ourselves with a tower whose top reaches the heavens. That way we'll gain a great reputation and we won't end up being scattered all over the world.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Genesis 18:21; PSALMS 33:13–14; John 3:13"> But the Lord came down to take a look at the city and the tower that the people were building.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="PSALMS 2:1–4; Genesis 3:22; Genesis 11:1"> The Lord said, “Look! These people are united and they all speak one language. If this is what they can do now when they're just getting started, nothing will be impossible for them when they all put their minds to it!</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="PSALMS 33:10; PSALMS 55:9; Job 5:12–13"> We need to go down and mix up their language and make it confused so they won't be able to understand what they're saying to one other.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Luke 1:51; Deuteronomy 32:8; Genesis 10:25"> The Lord sent them away from there and scattered them all over the world, and they gave up building the city.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Genesis 10:10; Genesis 10:5; Genesis 10:20"> That's why it was called Babel,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Babel sounds like the Hebrew word for “confuse.”">b</a></sup>because the Lord made the language of the world confused.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Chronicles 1:17–27; Genesis 10:21–22; Luke 3:34–36"> The following is the genealogy of Shem. When Shem was 100, he had Arphaxad. This was two years after the flood.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Genesis 9:7; PSALMS 144:12; Genesis 1:28"> Shem lived another 500 years after Arphaxad was born and had other sons and daughters.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Luke 3:36"> When Arphaxad was 35, he had Shelah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> Arphaxad lived another 403 years after Shelah was born and had other sons and daughters.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> When Shelah was 30, he had Eber.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> Shelah lived another 403 years after Eber was born and had other sons and daughters.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Genesis 10:21; 1 Chronicles 1:19; Numbers 24:24"> When Eber was 34, he had Peleg.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> Eber lived another 430 years after Peleg was born and had other sons and daughters.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Luke 3:35"> When Peleg was 30, he had Reu.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> Peleg lived another 209 years after Reu was born and had other sons and daughters.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Luke 3:35"> When Reu was 32, he had Serug.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> Reu lived another 207 years after Serug was born and had other sons and daughters.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Joshua 24:2"> When Serug was 30, he had Nahor.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> Serug lived another 200 years after Nahor was born and had other sons and daughters.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Luke 3:34; Joshua 24:2"> When Nahor was 29, he had Terah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25"> Nahor lived another 119 years after Terah was born and had other sons and daughters.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Joshua 24:2; 1 Chronicles 1:26–27; Genesis 22:20–24"> When Terah was 70, he had Abram, Nahor, and Haran.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Once again (see note on 5:32) these sons are not listed in birth order. Abram is listed first because of his importance.">c</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Genesis 12:4; Genesis 14:12; Genesis 11:31"> The following is the genealogy of Terah. Terah was the father of Abram, Nahor, and Haran. Haran was the father of Lot.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Genesis 15:7; Nehemiah 9:7; Acts 7:2–4"> However, Haran died while his father, Terah, was still alive, in Ur of the Chaldeans, the land of his birth.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Genesis 17:15; Genesis 22:20; Genesis 24:15"> Abram and Nahor both got married. Abram's wife was named Sarai, and Nahor's wife was named Milcah. (She was the daughter of Haran, who was the father of both Milcah and Iscah).</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Genesis 29:31; Genesis 21:1–2; Luke 1:7"> Sarai wasn't able to become pregnant and so had no children.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Nehemiah 9:7; Acts 7:2–4; Genesis 24:10"> Terah took his son Abram, his grandson Lot (who was the son of Haran), his daughter-in-law Sarai (the wife of his son Abram), and left Ur of the Chaldeans to move to the land of Canaan. They got as far as Haran and settled there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32"> Terah lived for 205 years and died in Haran.</span>
</div>
</div>
