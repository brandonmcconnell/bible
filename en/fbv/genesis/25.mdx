<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 Chronicles 1:32–33; Genesis 23:1–2; Genesis 28:1"> Abraham married another wife; her name was Keturah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Chronicles 1:32–33; Numbers 25:17–18; Job 2:11"> She had the following sons: Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian, Ishbak, and Shuah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Ezekiel 25:13; Jeremiah 49:8; Ezekiel 27:20"> Jokshan was the father of Sheba and Dedan. The descendants of Dedan were the Asshurites, the Letushites, and the Leummites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Isaiah 60:6"> The sons of Midian were Ephah, Epher, Hanoch, Abida, and Eldaah. These were all descendants of Keturah.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Genesis 24:36; Hebrews 1:2; Galatians 3:29"> Abraham left everything he owned to Isaac.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Genesis 21:14; Judges 6:3; Acts 14:17"> But while he was still alive, he gave gifts to the sons of his concubines and sent them to live in the east, well away from Isaac.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Genesis 12:4"> Abraham lived to be 175</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Genesis 35:28–29; Genesis 15:15; 1 Chronicles 29:28"> when he breathed his last and died at a good old age. He had lived a full life, and now he joined his forefathers in death.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Genesis 50:13; Genesis 35:29; Genesis 49:29–30"> His sons Isaac and Ishmael buried him in the cave of Machpelah near Mamre, in the field that had belonged to Ephron, son of Zohar, the Hittite.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Genesis 23:16; Genesis 49:31"> This was the field Abraham had bought from the Hittites. Abraham was buried there with his wife Sarah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Genesis 22:17; Genesis 16:14; Genesis 24:62"> After Abraham's death, God blessed his son Isaac, who was living near Beer-lahai-roi.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="PSALMS 83:6; Genesis 17:20; Genesis 21:13"> This is the genealogy of Abraham's son Ishmael. His mother Hagar was Sarah's Egyptian slave.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Chronicles 1:29–31; Isaiah 60:7; Song of Songs 1:5"> These were the names of the sons of Ishmael according to their family genealogy: Nebaioth (firstborn), Kedar, Adbeel, Mibsam,</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Isaiah 21:11; Isaiah 21:16"> Mishma, Dumah, Massa,</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Chronicles 5:19; Job 2:11"> Hadad, Tema, Jetur, Naphish, and Kedemah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Genesis 17:20; Genesis 17:23"> These were the sons of Ishmael, and these became the names of the places where they lived and camped—the twelve family rulers of their tribes.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Genesis 15:15; Genesis 25:7–8"> Ishmael lived to be 137. Then he breathed his last and died, and joined his forefathers in death.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Genesis 16:12; Genesis 20:1; 2 Kings 23:29"> Ishmael's descendants inhabited the region from Havilah to Shur, near the border of Egypt in the direction of Asshur. They were forever fighting with one other.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="The Hebrew of this verse is unclear. However, see 16:12.">a</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Matthew 1:2; Luke 3:34; Acts 7:8"> The following is the genealogy of Abraham's son Isaac. Abraham was the father of Isaac.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Genesis 24:29; Genesis 24:67; Genesis 22:23"> When Isaac was 40 he married Rebekah, the daughter of Bethuel the Aramean from Paddan-aram and the sister of Laban the Aramean.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Luke 1:13; Isaiah 58:9; Ezra 8:23"> Isaac prayed to the Lord for help on behalf of his wife because she couldn't have children. The Lord answered his prayer and she became pregnant.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="1 Samuel 9:9; 1 Samuel 28:6; 1 Samuel 30:8"> The twin babies inside her struggled with each other. So she asked the Lord, “Why is this happening to me?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Romans 9:10–13; Genesis 27:40; Genesis 27:29"> “You have two nations inside you,” the Lord replied. “You're going to give birth to two peoples who will compete against each other. One will be stronger than the other; the older one will be the servant of the younger one.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24"> When the time came she gave birth to twins.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Genesis 27:11; Genesis 27:16; Genesis 27:23"> The first baby to be born was red, and covered with hair like a coat. So they named him Esau.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Esau sounds like the word for “hair.”">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Genesis 27:36; Hosea 12:3; Genesis 38:28–30"> Then his twin brother was born, holding on to Esau's heel. So he was named Jacob.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Jacob sounds like the words “heel” or “deceiver.”">c</a></sup>Isaac was 60 when they were born.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Hebrews 11:9; Genesis 21:20; Genesis 10:9"> The boys grew up and Esau became a skilled hunter, at home in the countryside. Jacob was quiet and liked to stay at home in the tents.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Genesis 27:19; Genesis 27:31; Genesis 27:4"> Isaac loved Esau because he brought him tasty wild game to eat, while Rebekah loved Jacob.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Proverbs 13:25; Isaiah 40:30–31; 1 Samuel 14:28"> One day Jacob was cooking some stew when Esau got back from the countryside, tired out and starving hungry.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Genesis 36:1; Exodus 15:15; Genesis 36:9"> “Give me some of that red stew,” Esau told Jacob. “I'm absolutely starving!” (That's how Esau got his other name, “Edom,” meaning “red.”)</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31"> “First sell me your rights as the firstborn son,” Jacob replied.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Malachi 3:14; Job 21:15; Job 22:17"> “Look! I'm dying here! What use are the rights of the firstborn to me?” Esau declared.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Hebrews 12:16; Genesis 27:36; Hebrews 6:16"> “First you have to swear to me,” Jacob demanded. So Esau swore an oath selling his rights of the firstborn to Jacob.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Philippians 3:18–19; Hebrews 12:16–17; 1 Corinthians 15:32"> Then Jacob gave Esau some bread and lentil stew. He ate and drank, and then he got up and left. By doing this Esau showed how little he cared for his rights as the firstborn son.</span>
</div>
