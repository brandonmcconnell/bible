</div>
<div className="bp">

<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Isaiah 59:6; Genesis 2:25; Genesis 3:5"> Immediately they saw everything differently and realized they were naked. So they sewed fig leaves together to cover themselves up.</span>

<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Jeremiah 23:24; Proverbs 15:3; Hebrews 4:13"> Later they heard the Lord walking in the garden in the evening when the breeze was blowing. Adam and Eve went and hid out of sight of the Lord God among the trees of the garden.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Genesis 4:9; Genesis 16:8; Genesis 18:20–21"> The Lord God called out to Adam, “Where are you?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Genesis 2:25; PSALMS 119:120; Isaiah 47:3"> “I heard you walking in the garden and I was afraid because I was naked, so I hid,” he replied.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Romans 3:20; Genesis 4:10; PSALMS 50:21"> “Who told you that you were naked?” asked the Lord God. “Did you eat fruit from the tree I ordered you not to?”</span>

<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Proverbs 28:13; James 1:13–15; Job 31:33"> “It was the woman you gave me who gave me the fruit from the tree, and I ate it,” Adam replied.</span>

</div>

</div>
<div className="bp">

<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Isaiah 61:10; 2 Corinthians 5:21; 2 Corinthians 5:2–3"> The Lord God made Adam and Eve clothes from animal skins and dressed them.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Revelation 2:7; Revelation 22:14; Proverbs 3:18"> Then the Lord God observed, “Look, the human beings<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="“The human beings”: literally, “the man,” but this must be understood inclusively since Eve had also fallen.">f</a></sup>have become like one of us, knowing both what is good and what is evil. Now if they take the fruit from the tree of life and eat it, then they'll live forever!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Genesis 4:2; Genesis 3:19; Genesis 4:12"> So the Lord God expelled them from the Garden of Eden. He sent Adam to cultivate the ground from which he'd been made.</span>

## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">3:1</span> “Every”: the word could also be translated “any,” however this would then mean that the serpent was suggesting that God had told Adam and Eve not to eat fruit from any tree in the garden, which seems less likely.</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">3:3</span> “Otherwise you'll die.” The word used for “otherwise,” can indicate a possibility of something happening, rather than absolute certainty. So the phrase could be translated, “otherwise you might die,” a difference from God's clear prohibition, also claiming that God had said the fruit was not to be touched.</li>
