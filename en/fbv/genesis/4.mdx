<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="1 John 3:12; Genesis 3:15; Genesis 4:25"> Adam slept with his wife Eve and she became pregnant. She gave birth to Cain, and said, “With the Lord's help I have made a man.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 John 3:15; 1 John 3:12; Luke 11:51"> Later she gave birth to his brother Abel. Abel became a shepherd, while Cain was a crop farmer.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Numbers 18:12; Leviticus 2:1–11; 1 Kings 17:7"> Sometime later Cain brought some of the produce he'd grown as an offering to the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Hebrews 11:4; Proverbs 3:9; Exodus 13:12"> Abel also brought an offering: the firstborn lambs of his flock, selecting the very best parts to offer. The Lord was pleased with Abel and his offering,</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Hebrews 11:4; Job 5:2; Luke 15:28–30"> but he wasn't pleased with Cain and his offering, which made Cain very angry and he frowned in annoyance.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Isaiah 1:18; Job 5:2; Luke 15:31–32"> The Lord said to Cain, “Why are you so angry? Why do you look so annoyed?</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="James 1:15; Romans 2:6–10; Isaiah 3:10–11"> If you were doing what's right, then you'd be looking happy.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“Looking happy”: literally, “lifted up.” In the previous verse, the literal meaning is that Cain's “face fell.” So the opposite would be for his face to be “lifted up,” in other words, he would look happy.">a</a></sup>But if you don't do what's right, then sin will be like an animal crouching outside your home, ready to pounce on you. It wants to have you, but you must be the one in control.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Matthew 23:35; 1 John 3:12–15; Jude 1:11"> Later, when Cain was talking with his brother Abel<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="The Septuagint and some other ancient versions add here, “Let's go out into the fields.” The way the sentence is structured in the Hebrew does suggest some words are missing.">b</a></sup>they went out into the fields where Cain attacked his brother and killed him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Proverbs 28:13; John 8:44; PSALMS 9:12"> “Where is your brother Abel?” the Lord asked Cain. “How should I know?” he replied. “Am I supposed to be my brother's care-giver?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Hebrews 12:24; PSALMS 72:14; Hebrews 11:4"> “What have you done?” the Lord asked. “Your brother's blood is crying out to me from the ground.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Galatians 3:10; Job 16:18; Genesis 3:14"> Consequently you are more cursed than the ground because you soaked it with your brother's blood.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Hosea 9:17; Leviticus 26:36; Leviticus 26:20"> When you cultivate the ground, it won't produce crops for you. You'll be always on the run, wandering all over the earth.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Job 15:22; Revelation 16:9; Revelation 16:11"> “My punishment is more than I can take,” Cain replied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Numbers 35:19; PSALMS 143:7; Job 15:20–24"> “Look! You're driving me away right now—cursing the ground and banishing me from your presence. I'm going to have to hide and always be on the run, left to wander all over the earth. Anyone who finds me is going to kill me!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Ezekiel 9:4; PSALMS 79:12; Genesis 4:24"> But the Lord replied, “No, Cain. Anyone who kills you will be punished seven times over.” The Lord placed a mark on Cain so that no one who came across him would kill him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Jeremiah 23:39; Job 2:7; Genesis 4:14"> So Cain left the Lord's presence and went to live in a land called Nod, east of Eden.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“Nod” means “wandering.”">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="PSALMS 49:11; Ecclesiastes 2:4–11; Daniel 4:30"> Cain slept with his wife and she became pregnant. She had a son named Enoch. At that time Cain was building a town, so he named it after his son Enoch.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Genesis 36:2; Genesis 5:21"> Enoch had a son named Irad. Irad was the father of Mehujael, Mehujael was the father of Methushael, and Methushael was the father of Lamech.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Genesis 2:24; Matthew 19:8; Genesis 2:18"> Lamech married two women. The first was named Adah, and the second was named Zillah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Genesis 4:2; Jeremiah 35:9–10; John 8:44"> Adah had a son named Jabal. He was the father<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Father” can also mean “ancestor.”">d</a></sup>of those who live in tents and have livestock.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Job 21:12; Isaiah 5:12; Genesis 31:27"> He had a brother named Jubal; he was the father of all those who play stringed and wind instruments.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Deuteronomy 33:25; Deuteronomy 8:9; 2 Chronicles 2:7"> Zillah also had a son. He was named Tubal-cain and he was a blacksmith, making different kinds of tools out of bronze and iron. Tubal-cain's sister was named Naamah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Numbers 23:18; Exodus 20:13; Leviticus 19:18"> At one time Lamech told his wives, “Adah and Zillah, listen to me. You wives of Lamech, pay attention to what I have to say. I killed a man because he wounded me; I killed a young man because he injured me.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Matthew 18:22; Genesis 4:15"> If the sentence for killing Cain was to be punished seven times over, then if someone kills me, Lamech, the punishment should be seventy-seven times.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Genesis 4:8; Genesis 4:10–11; 1 Chronicles 1:1"> Adam slept with his wife again, and she had a son and named him Seth,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="“Seth,” meaning “substitute,” or “given.”">e</a></sup>explaining that, “God has given me another child to replace Abel, the one Cain killed.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="PSALMS 116:17; 1 Kings 18:24; Joel 2:32"> Later Seth had a son named Enosh,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="“Enosh,” meaning “mankind” or “people.”">f</a></sup>because at that time people began to worship the Lord by name.</span>
