<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Corinthians 4:6; John 1:5; Isaiah 45:7"> God said, “Let there be light!” and there was light.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 2:13; Genesis 1:18; Ecclesiastes 11:7"> God saw that the light was good, and he separated the light from the darkness.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="PSALMS 74:16; Isaiah 45:7; 1 Thessalonians 5:5"> God called the light “day,” and he called the darkness “night.” So there was evening, and then the morning, making day one.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="It is significant that the “day” is measured from darkness to light, which is still the Jewish method of calculating days.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Jeremiah 51:15; PSALMS 33:6; Job 37:18"> God said, “Let there be an expanse<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Expanse.” Older translations have often rendered this word as “firmament,” borrowing from the Latin “firmamentum.” That this referred to some ancient belief that the sky was like a dome of hammered metal, and therefore a tangible physical object, has now been shown to be mistaken. <span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Proverbs 8:28–29; PSALMS 148:4; Matthew 8:27"> So God made an expanse to divide the waters that were above from the waters that were below. And that's what happened.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Genesis 1:5; Genesis 1:19; Genesis 1:10"> God called the expanse, “sky.” So there was evening, and then the morning, making day two.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Peter 3:5; Jonah 1:9; Jeremiah 5:22"> God said, “Let the waters below the sky collect together in one place so that the land may appear.” And so it was.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Deuteronomy 32:4; PSALMS 104:31; Genesis 1:4"> God called the land “earth” and the waters “seas.” God saw that it was good.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="PSALMS 104:14–17; Hebrews 6:7; PSALMS 147:8"> God said, “Let the earth produce vegetation—plants that produce seeds and trees that produce seeded fruit—each one according to its own kind.” And that's what happened.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Mark 4:28; Isaiah 55:10–11; Matthew 13:24–26"> The earth produced vegetation—plants that produce seeds and trees that produce seeded fruit—each one according to its own kind. God saw that it was good.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13"> So there was evening, and then the morning, making day three.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="PSALMS 104:19–20; Deuteronomy 4:19; Jeremiah 31:35"> God said, “Let there be lights in the sky to separate day from night, and to provide a way to mark seasons, days, and years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> They shall be lights in the sky to shine on the earth.” And that's what happened.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="PSALMS 136:7–9; Deuteronomy 4:19; PSALMS 8:3"> God created two great lights:<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Hebrew has words for sun and moon, but they are not used here, perhaps to avoid any temptation to worship the sun and the moon.">c</a></sup>the larger one in charge of the day, and the smaller one in charge of the night. He created the stars too.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="PSALMS 8:3; PSALMS 8:1; Job 38:12"> God placed these lights in the sky to shine upon the earth,</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Jeremiah 31:35; PSALMS 19:6"> to be in charge of the day and in charge of the night, and to separate light from darkness. God saw that it was good.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> So there was evening, and then the morning, making day four.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Genesis 2:19; PSALMS 104:24–25; 1 Kings 4:33"> God said, “Let the waters be full of living creatures, and let birds fly above the earth in the sky.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="PSALMS 104:24–26; Genesis 1:25; Genesis 8:19"> So God created huge sea creatures and all the living things that swim and fill the waters, each one according to its own kind; and every bird that flies, each one according to its own kind. God saw that it was good.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Job 40:15; Genesis 8:17; PSALMS 107:38"> God blessed them and said, “Reproduce and increase, and fill the waters of the seas, and let the birds increase on the earth.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23"> So there was evening, and then the morning, making day five.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="PSALMS 50:9–10; Genesis 6:20; Genesis 8:19"> God said, “Let the land produce living creatures, each one according to its own kind—the livestock, the creatures that run along the ground, and the wild animals, each one according to its own kind.” And that's what happened.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Jeremiah 27:5; Job 12:8–10; Genesis 2:19–20"> God made the wild animals, the livestock, and the creatures that run along the ground, all according to their own kind. God saw that it was good.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Colossians 3:10; Ephesians 4:24; PSALMS 8:4–8"> God said, “Let us make human beings in our image who are like us.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="This aspect of being “like” God conveys the idea of being “patterned” after God. <span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Matthew 19:4; Ephesians 2:10; Genesis 5:1–2"> So God created human beings in his own image. He created them in the image of God. He created them male and female.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="The repetition of “created” in this verse is surely significant, which is why this is placed first in each sentence.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Genesis 9:1; Genesis 9:7; Leviticus 26:9"> God blessed them and told them, “Reproduce, increase, and spread throughout the earth and control it; exercise authority over the fish of the sea and the birds of the air and every creature that moves on the earth.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Genesis 9:3; PSALMS 104:14–15; PSALMS 145:15–16"> God said, “Look, I'm giving you as your food every seed-bearing plant anywhere on earth, and every tree that produces fruit with seeds.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="PSALMS 104:14; PSALMS 145:15–16; PSALMS 147:9"> I'm giving all the green plants to all the land animals, to the birds, and to every creature that moves on the earth—to every living thing.” And that's what happened.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="1 Timothy 4:4; PSALMS 104:24; PSALMS 19:1–2"> God saw everything that he had created, and yes, it was very good. So there was evening, and then the morning, making day six.</span>

## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">1:5</span> It is significant that the “day” is measured from darkness to light, which is still the Jewish method of calculating days.</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">1:6</span> “Expanse.” Older translations have often rendered this word as “firmament,” borrowing from the Latin “firmamentum.” That this referred to some ancient belief that the sky was like a dome of hammered metal, and therefore a tangible physical object, has now been shown to be mistaken. In fact, Latin translations from the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries usually use the word “expansionem.”</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">1:16</span> Hebrew has words for sun and moon, but they are not used here, perhaps to avoid any temptation to worship the sun and the moon.</li>

The word is also translated “similitude,” “figure” or “form.” The most essential aspect of this resemblance is surely that of character.</li>
