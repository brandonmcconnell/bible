<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Judges 6:3; Judges 6:33; Hosea 12:12"> Jacob went quickly on his way,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“Went quickly on his way”: literally, “lifted up his feet.”">a</a></sup>and arrived in the land of the eastern people.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="John 4:14; Isaiah 49:10; John 4:6"> As he looked around he saw a well in a field with three flocks of sheep lying down beside it, waiting to be given water. A large stone covered the top of the well.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3"> The usual practice was that<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“The usual practice was that”: supplied for clarity.">b</a></sup>once all the flocks had arrived, the shepherds would roll away the stone from the well and give their sheep water. Then they would put the stone back again.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Genesis 28:10; Genesis 27:43; Acts 7:2"> Jacob asked them, “My brothers, where are you from?” “We're from Haran,” they replied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Genesis 24:24; Genesis 24:29; Genesis 31:53"> “Do you know Laban, Nahor's grandson?” he asked. “Yes, we know him,” they replied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Samuel 20:9; Genesis 37:14; Exodus 18:7"> “How is he?” he asked. “He's well,” they replied. “Look! In fact here's his daughter Rachel coming with the sheep right now.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Ephesians 5:16; Galatians 6:9–10"> “Look, there's still plenty of daylight left,” said Jacob. “It's too early to round up the sheep yet. Why not let them drink so they can go back to grazing?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Mark 16:3; Luke 24:2; Genesis 34:14"> “We can't do that until all the flocks have arrived,” they told him. “Then we roll away the stone from the well and let the sheep drink.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Exodus 2:21; Song of Songs 1:7–8; Exodus 2:15–16"> While he was still talking with them Rachel arrived with the flock she was looking after for her father.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Exodus 2:17"> When Jacob saw Rachel, the daughter of Laban, his mother's brother, he went over and rolled away the stone from the well so Laban's sheep could drink.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Genesis 33:4; Genesis 45:14–15; Genesis 45:2"> Then Jacob kissed Rachel and wept for joy.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Genesis 24:28; Genesis 13:8; Genesis 14:14–16"> (He had told her that he was a relative of her father, Rebekah's son.) She ran and told her father what had happened.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Genesis 24:29; Acts 20:37; Romans 16:16"> As soon as Laban heard the news about Jacob he ran out to meet him. He hugged him and kissed him, and took him home. After Jacob had explained everything to Laban,</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Samuel 19:12–13; Genesis 2:23; Judges 9:2"> Laban told him, “No question about it—you're my own flesh and blood!” Jacob stayed with Laban for a month.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Genesis 30:28; Genesis 31:7"> One day Laban said to him, “You're my relative so you shouldn't be working for me for nothing! Tell me, what should I pay you?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Ruth 4:11; Genesis 49:31; Genesis 31:4"> Laban had two daughters. The older one was Leah, and the younger one was Rachel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Genesis 12:11; Genesis 46:19–22; Genesis 24:16"> Leah had kind<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“Kind”: literally, “soft” or “gentle.”">c</a></sup>eyes, but Rachel had a shapely figure and beautiful looks.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Hosea 12:12; Exodus 22:16–17; Genesis 34:12"> Jacob was in love with Rachel so he promised Laban, “I'll do seven years work<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Seven years work”: in contrast to Abraham's servant Eliezer (chapter 24) Jacob had arrived with no gifts or dowry, so he offers his service as payment in kind.">d</a></sup>for you for Rachel, your younger daughter.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Isaiah 6:11; Isaiah 6:5; PSALMS 12:2"> “Well it's better for me to give her to you than anyone else,” Laban replied. “So stay here and work for me.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Corinthians 13:7; Ephesians 5:2; Hosea 12:12"> Jacob worked for Laban for seven years, but to him they seemed like just a few days because he really loved her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Judges 15:1; Genesis 29:20; Genesis 4:1"> Then Jacob said to Laban, “I've completed the time we agreed. Now give me your daughter to be my wife.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Revelation 19:9; Ruth 4:10–13; Judges 14:10–18"> So Laban organized a wedding banquet<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="“A wedding banquet”: the word actually means “a drinking party,” which is probably the only way the deception could have been successful.">e</a></sup>and invited everyone around to come.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Micah 7:5; Genesis 38:14–15; Genesis 24:65"> But once it was dark Laban brought his daughter Leah to Jacob, and he slept with her.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Genesis 16:1; Genesis 30:9–12; Genesis 46:18"> (Laban also arranged for his servant Zilpah to be Leah's personal maid.)</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Matthew 7:2; Proverbs 11:31; 1 Corinthians 3:13"> When morning came, he saw it was Leah! He went to Laban and asked angrily, “What have you done to me? It was for Rachel that I worked for you! Why have you deceived me?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> “Here we don't give the younger daughter in marriage before the firstborn,” Laban replied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Leviticus 18:18; Judges 14:12; Genesis 2:2–3"> “Finish this week of wedding celebrations and then I'll give you the other daughter as well, as long as you work another seven years for me.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> Jacob agreed. He finished the week of wedding celebrations for Leah, and then Laban gave Jacob his daughter Rachel as his wife as well.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Genesis 35:22; Genesis 29:24; Genesis 30:3–8"> (Laban also arranged for his servant Bilhah to be Rachel's personal maid.)</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Genesis 31:41; Genesis 29:20; Genesis 31:15"> So Jacob slept with Rachel as well, and he loved Rachel more than Leah. He worked for Laban another seven years for Rachel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="1 Samuel 2:21; Matthew 10:37; Deuteronomy 21:15"> When the Lord saw that Leah wasn't loved he helped Leah to have children, but not Rachel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Deuteronomy 26:7; Exodus 4:31; Exodus 3:7"> Leah became pregnant, and had a son she named Reuben,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="“Reuben”: means “Look, a son!” and also sounds like “he saw my suffering.”">f</a></sup>for she said, “The Lord saw how much I was suffering and now my husband will love me!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Genesis 34:25; Genesis 49:5–6; Genesis 34:30"> Then Leah became pregnant again, and had another son. She said, “The Lord has heard that I'm not loved so he gave me this son.” So she named him Simeon.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="“Simeon”: means “he hears.”">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Exodus 32:26–29; Genesis 49:5–7; Genesis 46:11"> Leah became pregnant for the third time, and had another son. She said, “Finally my husband will be attached to me because now I've given him three sons.” That's why he was named Levi.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="“Levi”: means “attached” or “joined.”">h</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Matthew 1:2; 1 Chronicles 5:2; Genesis 49:8–12"> Once again Leah became pregnant and had another son. She named him Judah,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="“Judah”: means “praise.”">i</a></sup>for she said, “Now I can really praise the Lord!” After that she had no more children.</span>
