<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Genesis 20:3; Genesis 29:14; Exodus 4:9"> A full two years later, Pharaoh had a dream that he was standing beside the River Nile.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Isaiah 19:6"> He saw seven cows coming up from the river. They looked well-fed and healthy as they grazed among the reeds.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Genesis 41:20–21; Genesis 41:4"> Then he saw another seven cows that came up behind them. They looked ugly and skinny as they stood beside the other cows on the bank of the Nile.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Kings 3:15"> Then the ugly, skinny cows ate the well-fed, healthy cows. Then Pharaoh woke up.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Deuteronomy 32:14"> Pharaoh fell asleep again and had a second dream. Seven heads of grain were growing on one stalk, ripe and healthy.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Ezekiel 17:10; Hosea 13:15; Ezekiel 19:12"> Then seven heads of grain grew up after them, thin and dried by the east wind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Genesis 37:5; Genesis 20:3"> The seven thin and dried heads of grain swallowed up the ripe and healthy ones. Then Pharaoh woke up and realized he'd been dreaming.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Daniel 4:7; Exodus 7:22; Matthew 2:1"> The next morning Pharaoh was worried by his dreams,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“By his dreams”: supplied for clarity.">a</a></sup>so he sent for all the magicians and wise men in Egypt. Pharaoh told them about his dreams, but no one could interpret their meaning for him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Genesis 40:14; Genesis 40:23; Genesis 40:1–3"> But then the chief cupbearer spoke up. “Today I've just remembered a bad mistake I've made,” he explained.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Genesis 39:20; Genesis 40:2–3; Genesis 37:36"> “Your Majesty was angry with some of your officials and you imprisoned me in the house of the commander of the guard, along with the chief baker.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Genesis 40:5–8"> We each had a dream. They were different dreams, each with its own meaning.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Genesis 40:12–19; Genesis 37:36; Genesis 39:1"> A young Hebrew was there with us, a slave of the commander of the guard. When we told him our dreams, he interpreted for us the meaning of our different dreams.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Ezekiel 43:3; Genesis 40:20–22; Genesis 40:12"> Everything happened just as he said it would—I was given back my job and the baker was hanged.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Daniel 2:25; PSALMS 113:7–8; PSALMS 105:19–22"> Pharaoh summoned Joseph, and they quickly brought him from the prison. After he'd shaved and changed his clothes, he was presented to Pharaoh.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Daniel 5:16; Daniel 5:12; PSALMS 25:14"> Pharaoh said to Joseph, “I had a dream, but no one can interpret its meaning. But I've heard that when someone tells you a dream you know how to interpret it.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Corinthians 3:5; Genesis 40:8; Daniel 2:47"> “It's not me who can do this,” Joseph replied. “But God will explain its meaning to set Your Majesty's mind at rest.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> Pharaoh explained to Joseph, “In my dream I was standing on the bank of the Nile.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Jeremiah 24:8; Jeremiah 24:1–3; Jeremiah 24:5"> I saw seven cows coming up from the river. They looked well-fed and healthy as they grazed among the reeds.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> Then I saw another seven cows that came up behind them. They looked sickly and ugly and skinny—I've never seen such ugly cows in the whole of Egypt!</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> These skinny, ugly cows ate the first seven healthy-looking cows.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Ezekiel 3:3; PSALMS 37:19; Revelation 10:9–10"> But afterwards you couldn't tell they'd eaten them because they looked just as skinny and ugly as before. Then I woke up.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> Then I fell asleep again. In my second dream I saw seven heads of grain growing on one stalk, ripe and healthy.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="PSALMS 129:6–7; Hosea 13:15; 2 Kings 19:26"> Then seven heads of grain grew up after them, withered and thin and dried by the east wind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Daniel 4:7; Genesis 41:8; Exodus 8:19"> The seven thin heads of grain swallowed up the healthy ones. I told all this to the magicians, but none of them could explain its meaning to me.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Daniel 2:45; Revelation 4:1; Ephesians 1:17"> “Pharaoh's dreams mean the same thing,” Joseph responded. “God is telling Pharaoh what he is going to do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Genesis 41:47; Genesis 41:2; Genesis 41:53"> The seven good cows and the seven good heads of grain represent seven good years of harvest.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Of harvest”: supplied for clarity.">b</a></sup>The dreams mean the same thing.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 Kings 8:1; 2 Samuel 24:19"> The seven skinny and ugly cows that came after them and the seven thin heads of grain dried by the east wind represent seven years of famine.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Genesis 41:25; Genesis 41:16"> It's just as I told Your Majesty—God has shown Pharaoh what he is going to do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Genesis 41:26; Genesis 41:46–47; Genesis 41:49"> There are going to be seven years with plenty of food produced throughout the whole country of Egypt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Genesis 47:13; Genesis 41:54; James 5:17"> But after them will come seven years of famine. People will forget the time when there was plenty of food throughout Egypt. Famine will ruin the country.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Isaiah 24:20; 1 Samuel 5:6"> The time of plenty will be completely forgotten because the famine that follows it will be so terrible.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Numbers 23:19; Isaiah 46:10–11; Isaiah 14:24–27"> The fact that the dream was repeated twice means that it has definitely been decided by God, and that God is going to do this soon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Genesis 41:39; Acts 6:3; Deuteronomy 1:13"> So Your Majesty should choose a man with insight and wisdom, and put him in charge of the whole country of Egypt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Proverbs 22:3; Luke 16:5; Proverbs 6:6–8"> Your Majesty should also appoint officials to be in charge of the land, and have them collect one-fifth of the produce of the country during the seven years of plenty.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Genesis 45:6–7; Genesis 41:56; Exodus 4:13"> They should collect all the food during the good years that are soon coming, and store the grain under Pharaoh's authority, keeping it under guard to provide food for the towns.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="Genesis 47:13–25"> This will be a food reserve for the country during the seven years of famine so that the people won't die of starvation.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="Acts 7:10; 1 Kings 21:2; 2 Samuel 3:36"> Pharaoh and all his officials thought Joseph's proposal was a good idea.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="Job 32:8; Daniel 5:14; Numbers 27:18"> So Pharaoh asked them, “Where can we find a man like this who has the spirit of God in him?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Genesis 41:33; Genesis 41:28; Genesis 41:25"> Then Pharaoh spoke to Joseph, telling him, “Since God has revealed to you all this, and there's no one like you with such insight and wisdom,</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="Proverbs 22:29; Acts 7:10; PSALMS 105:21–22"> you will be in charge of all my affairs, and all my people will obey your orders. Only I with my status as king<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“My status as king”: literally “the throne.”">c</a></sup>will be greater than you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="Matthew 28:18; Philippians 2:9–11; Genesis 42:6"> Then Pharaoh said to Joseph, “Look, I'm putting you in charge of the whole country of Egypt.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="Daniel 5:29; Daniel 5:7; Esther 3:10"> Pharaoh took off his signet ring from his finger and put it on Joseph's finger. He dressed him in fine linen clothes and placed a golden chain around his neck.</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43" data-xref="Acts 7:10; Genesis 45:8; Genesis 45:26"> He had Joseph ride in the chariot designated for his second-in-command while his attendants went ahead, shouting, “Bow down!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Bow down!” This Egyptian loan word is variously translated: “Attention!” “Make way!” “Praise!” “Do homage!” All relate to honoring a dignitary.">d</a></sup>This is how Pharaoh gave Joseph authority over all of Egypt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44" data-xref="PSALMS 105:22; Exodus 11:7"> Then Pharaoh told Joseph, “I am Pharaoh, but without your permission nobody will lift a hand or a foot anywhere in the whole country.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v45" data-v="45" data-xref="Genesis 46:20; Ezekiel 30:17; Exodus 2:16"> Pharaoh gave Joseph the name Zaphenath-paneah,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Meaning “The God speaks and he (the subject) lives.”">e</a></sup>and arranged for him to marry Asenath, the daughter of Potiphera, priest of On. This is how Joseph rose to power over the whole of Egypt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v46" data-v="46" data-xref="Daniel 1:19; 1 Kings 12:6; 1 Samuel 16:21"> Joseph was thirty when he started working for Pharaoh, king of Egypt. After he had left Pharaoh, Joseph traveled on an inspection tour<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="“On an inspection tour”: supplied for clarity.">f</a></sup>throughout Egypt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v47" data-v="47" data-xref="Genesis 26:12; PSALMS 72:16"> During the seven years of good harvests, the land produced plenty of food.</span>
<span className="v" id="v48" data-v="48" data-xref="Genesis 41:34–36; Genesis 47:21"> He collected all the food during the seven good years, and he stored the grain produced in the local fields in each town.</span>
<span className="v" id="v49" data-v="49" data-xref="PSALMS 78:27; Judges 6:5; Genesis 22:17"> Joseph piled up so much grain that it was like the sand of the seashore. Eventually he stopped keeping records because there was just so much!</span>
<span className="v" id="v50" data-v="50" data-xref="Genesis 46:20; Genesis 48:5; 2 Samuel 8:18"> It was during this time, before the years of famine came, that Joseph had two sons by Asenath, the daughter of Potiphera, priest of On.</span>
<span className="v" id="v51" data-v="51" data-xref="PSALMS 30:5; Proverbs 31:7; Deuteronomy 33:17"> Joseph named his firstborn Manasseh,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="“Manasseh” means “cause to forget.”">g</a></sup>because he said, “The Lord has made me forget all my troubles and all my father's family.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v52" data-v="52" data-xref="Genesis 49:22; Genesis 50:23; Genesis 17:6"> His second son he named Ephraim,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="“Ephraim” means “fruitful.”">h</a></sup>because he said, “God has made me fruitful in the country of my misery.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v53" data-v="53" data-xref="Genesis 41:29–31; PSALMS 73:20; Luke 16:25"> The seven years of plenty in Egypt came to an end,</span>
<span className="v" id="v54" data-v="54" data-xref="Genesis 45:11; Acts 7:11; PSALMS 105:16"> and the seven years of famine began, just as Joseph had said. There was famine in all the other countries but the whole of Egypt had food.</span>
<span className="v" id="v55" data-v="55" data-xref="Matthew 17:5; Matthew 3:17; Jeremiah 14:1–6"> When all of Egypt was hungry, the people cried out to Pharaoh for food, and he told everyone, “Go and see Joseph and do whatever he tells you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v56" data-v="56" data-xref="Genesis 42:6; Zechariah 5:3; Luke 21:35"> The famine had spread all over the country so Joseph opened all the storehouses and sold grain to the people of Egypt. The famine was very bad in Egypt,</span>
<span className="v" id="v57" data-v="57" data-xref="Genesis 41:54; Genesis 42:5; Genesis 41:56"> in fact the famine was very bad everywhere, so people from other countries all around came to Egypt to buy grain from Joseph.</span>
