<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Numbers 26:29; Genesis 41:51; Genesis 50:23"> This was the allocation to the tribe of Manasseh, Joseph's firstborn son. Machir was Manasseh's firstborn son who was the father of Gilead. Because Machir had been an excellent fighter, Gilead and Bashan had already been allocated to him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Chronicles 7:18; Numbers 26:29–32; Judges 6:11"> The allocation was for the rest of the tribe of Manasseh, to the families of Abiezer, Helek, Asriel, Shechem, Hepher, and Shemida. These were the male descendants of Manasseh, the son of Joseph, by families.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Numbers 27:1; Numbers 26:33; Numbers 36:2–11"> But Zelophehad, son of Hepher, son of Gilead, son of Machir, son of Manasseh, had no sons. He only had daughters, whose names were Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Joshua 14:1; Numbers 27:5–7; Galatians 3:28"> They approached Eleazar the priest, Joshua the son of Nun, and the leaders, and told them, “The Lord ordered Moses to give us an allocation of land along with our brothers.” So Joshua allotted land to them along with their brothers, as the Lord had ordered.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Joshua 17:2–3; Numbers 32:30–42; Joshua 13:29–31"> Consequently Manasseh received ten shares of land beside the land of Gilead and Bashan on the other side of the Jordan,</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6"> because the daughters of the tribe of Manasseh received an allocation along with the sons. (The land of Gilead had been allotted to the rest of the descendants of Manasseh).</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Joshua 21:21; Joshua 20:7; Joshua 24:32"> The boundary of the tribe of Manasseh ran from Asher to Michmethath, near Shechem, then south to the spring of Tappuah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Joshua 16:8; Joshua 15:34; Joshua 12:17"> The land around Tappuah was allocated to Manasseh, but the town of Tappuah, which was on the border of Manasseh's land, was allocated to Ephraim.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Joshua 16:3; Joshua 19:29; Joshua 16:8–9"> From there the boundary ran down to the valley of Kenah. South of the valley some of the towns belonged to Ephraim among the towns of Manasseh. The boundary ran along the north side of the valley and ended at the sea.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10"> To the south the land belonged to Ephraim, and to the north, to Manasseh. The sea is the boundary. The boundary to the north was with Asher, and with Issachar to the east.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Chronicles 7:29; 2 Kings 9:27; Judges 5:19"> The following towns with their associated villages were allocated to Manasseh but lay within the land of Issachar and Asher: Beth-shan, Ibleam, Dor (on the coast), Endor, Taanach, and Megiddo.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="The Hebrew at the end of the verse is difficult to understand. It says literally “three of the heights.” One solution is that it is a reference to the third named town, Dor, which is now specifically identified as “the one on the coast,” or Naphath-dor. See 12:23.">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Judges 1:27–28; Exodus 23:29–33; Romans 6:12–14"> But the descendants of Manasseh could not take possession of these towns because the Canaanites were determined to go on occupying the land.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Joshua 16:10; Philippians 4:13; Judges 1:35"> However, later on, when the Israelites grew strong enough, they made the Canaanites do forced labor—but they did not drive them out.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Numbers 26:34–37; Genesis 48:19; Genesis 48:22"> Then the descendants of Joseph came to Joshua and asked him, “Why have you given us only one allocation—only one share of the land—when there's so many of us because the Lord has blessed us so much?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Genesis 13:7; Genesis 14:5; Luke 12:48"> Joshua told them, “If there's so many of you, if the hill country of Ephraim is too small for you, then go and clear ground for yourselves from the forest in the land of the Perizzites and the Rephaim.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Kings 4:12; Judges 4:3; Judges 1:19"> The descendants of Joseph replied, “The hill country isn't big enough for us, but the Canaanites living in the lowlands have iron chariots, both those in Beth-shan and its villages, and those in the valley of Jezreel.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> Joshua said to the tribes of Ephraim and Manasseh, the descendants of Joseph, “Since you are so many, and you are so strong, you will be given more than just one share.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Hebrews 13:6; Romans 8:31; Numbers 14:6–9"> You will be allocated the hill country in addition. Though it is forest, you will clear it and own it, from one end to the other. You will drive out the Canaanites, even though they have iron chariots, and even though they are strong.”</span>
</div>
