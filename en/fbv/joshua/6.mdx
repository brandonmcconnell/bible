<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Luke 24:49; Matthew 12:19; Isaiah 42:2"> However, Joshua ordered them, “Don't shout, don't even talk. Don't say anything until I tell you all to shout—then shout!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11"> So the Ark of the Lord was carried around the city, circling it once. Then they returned to the camp and spent the night there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Joshua 3:1; Hebrews 11:7–8; Genesis 22:3"> Joshua got up early in the morning, and the priests picked up the Ark of the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Galatians 6:9; Matthew 24:13; 1 Chronicles 15:26"> The seven priests with the seven rams' horns went ahead of the Ark of the Lord, blowing the horns. <span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Joshua 6:15; Joshua 6:11; Joshua 6:3"> So on the second day they marched around the city, circling it once, and returned to the camp. They did this for a total of six days.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="PSALMS 119:147; 2 Peter 1:19; Matthew 28:1"> On the seventh day they got up at dawn and marched around the city in the usual way, except that this day they circled the city seven times.</span>

The men went in right away and captured the city.</span>

<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Hebrews 11:7; Genesis 18:24; Acts 27:24"> So the spies went and brought out Rahab, her father and mother, and all who were with her. They brought out the whole family and took them to a place near the Israelite camp.</span>

<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Hebrews 11:31; Matthew 1:5; Judges 1:24–25"> Joshua spared Rahab the prostitute and her family because she hid the men Joshua had sent to spy out Jericho. And she lives among the Israelites to this day.</span>

## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">6:17</span> The term used means that it is “set apart,” “consecrated,” or “devoted” to the Lord, which in this instance meant that no one was to benefit from anything in Jericho—all had to be destroyed. This is similar to the idea of the “sacred” and the “holy”—dedicated solely to God. In some ways the “setting apart” of Jericho was similar to a ban—it belonged to God alone.</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">6:20</span> The Hebrew text states that the people shouted and the horns blew, and then that when they heard the horns, the people shouted. Since it is considered that there was just one event, the repetition of the horns and the shout seems superfluous.</li>
</ol>
