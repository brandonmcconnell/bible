<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Joshua 6:21; Hebrews 7:1; Joshua 8:2"> Adoni-zedek, king of Jerusalem, learned that Joshua had captured Ai and destroyed the town completely, as he had also done to Jericho, and had killed its king, just as he had the king of Jericho. He also heard that the Gibeonites had made peace with the Israelites and were allied with them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Deuteronomy 11:25; Joshua 2:24; Exodus 15:14–16"> The people of Jerusalem were very frightened by this because Gibeon was a large town—as large as any town ruled by the king, and larger than Ai, and its men were tough fighters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Chronicles 11:9; Micah 1:13; Joshua 10:1"> So Adoni-zedek, king of Jerusalem, sent a message to Hoham, king of Hebron, Piram, king of Jarmuth, Japhia, king of Lachish, and Debir, king of Eglon, saying,</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Joshua 9:15; Joshua 10:1; Isaiah 8:9–10"> “Come and help me attack Gibeon because they have made peace with Joshua and the Israelites.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Isaiah 8:9–10; Genesis 15:16; Joshua 10:6"> So these five Amorite kings (the kings of Jerusalem, Hebron, Jarmuth, Lachish, and Eglon) and their armies gathered and set off. They surrounded Gibeon and then began their attack.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Joshua 9:6; Joshua 5:10; Deuteronomy 1:15"> The Gibeonites sent a message to Joshua at the camp in Gilgal, saying, “Please don't abandon us, your servants! Come quickly and save us! We need your help for all the Amorite kings of the hill country have joined in attacking us.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Joshua 8:1; Isaiah 8:12; Isaiah 8:14"> So Joshua, all his fighting men and best fighters, set off from Gilgal.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Romans 8:31; Deuteronomy 3:2; PSALMS 27:1–2"> The Lord had said to Joshua, “Don't be afraid of them, for you will defeat them. Not a single one will be able to stand against you.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 9:10; 2 Timothy 2:3; Proverbs 24:11–12"> By marching all night from Gilgal, Joshua arrived without warning.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="PSALMS 18:14; Joshua 16:3; Joshua 16:5"> The Lord threw the Amorite armies into a panic when they saw the Israelites. He struck them down with a great blow at Gibeon; he chased them all the way up to Beth-horon, cutting them down on the way to Azekah and Makkedah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Isaiah 28:2; Judges 5:20; PSALMS 18:12–14"> As they ran away from the Israelites down the slope from Beth-horon, the Lord hurled large hailstones down on them from the skies all the way to Azekah. More were killed by the hailstones than were killed by the swords of the Israelites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Habakkuk 3:11; Isaiah 38:8; Isaiah 60:20"> On the day that the Lord handed the Amorites over to the Israelites, Joshua spoke by<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“By”: or “because of.” Though often translated as “to,” the sense here indicates divine approval rather than a conversation.">a</a></sup>the Lord in the presence of the Israelites, saying, “Sun, stand still over Gibeon! Moon, stand still over the Valley of Aijalon!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Habakkuk 3:11; Isaiah 38:8; Joshua 10:14"> The sun stopped moving, and the moon stood still, until the nation of Israel had inflicted defeat on their enemies. (This is recorded in the Book of Jashar<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Book of Jasher”: Or “Book of the Upright.” This book is no longer known. It is also referred to in 2 Samuel 1:18.">b</a></sup>). The sun stopped in the middle of the sky and did not go down for around a full day.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Deuteronomy 1:30; Exodus 14:14; Joshua 10:42"> There wasn't a day like this ever before or since when the Lord listened to a human voice in such a way. It was because the Lord was fighting for Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Joshua 10:43; Joshua 10:6"> Then Joshua and all the army returned to the camp at Gilgal.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Samuel 24:8; Amos 9:2; PSALMS 139:7–10"> The five kings had run away and hid in a cave at Makkedah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> When Joshua was told that the five kings were hiding in a cave at Makkedah,</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Matthew 27:66; Job 21:30; Amos 9:1"> he gave this order, “Roll some large stones to block the entrance to the cave and have some men guard it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="PSALMS 18:37–41; Jeremiah 8:14; Isaiah 9:14–15"> But don't you stay there. Chase the enemy down and attack them from the rear. Don't let them escape to their towns for the Lord has given them to you to defeat.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“To you to defeat”: literally, “into your hand.”">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Joshua 8:24; 2 Samuel 20:6; Joshua 10:10"> So Joshua and the Israelites totally defeated them, striking them down and killing them. Only a few of them survived to escape back to their towns.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Exodus 11:7; Isaiah 54:17; Isaiah 57:4"> The army returned to Joshua at the camp at Makkedah, and no one dared even to threaten the Israelites.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="In other words, the surrounding peoples were so intimidated by this success that they didn't dare even to speak against the Israelites, let alone attack them.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="1 Samuel 15:32"> Then Joshua said, “Open the cave entrance and bring out the five kings to me from the cave.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Joshua 10:1; Joshua 10:5; Joshua 10:3"> So they did, bringing out the five kings from the cave—the kings of Jerusalem, Hebron, Jarmuth, Lachish, and Eglon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Malachi 4:3; Romans 16:20; Revelation 2:26–27"> When they had brought the kings to Joshua, he summoned all the fighting men, and said to the commanders who had gone with him, “Come here, and put your feet on the necks of these kings.” So they came over and put their feet on their necks.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Corinthians 1:10; Joshua 1:9; Ephesians 6:10"> Joshua said to them, “Don't ever be afraid or discouraged! Be strong and be brave! For the Lord is going to do the same thing to all your enemies that you're going to fight!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Joshua 8:29; 2 Samuel 21:9; Matthew 27:25"> Then Joshua killed the kings and hung their bodies on five trees and left them hanging there until the evening.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="See Deuteronomy 21:22-23.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Joshua 8:29; Deuteronomy 21:22–23; Joshua 7:26"> As the sun went down Joshua gave the order to take their bodies down from the trees and throw them into the cave where they had been hiding. Then the Israelites piled up stones over the entrance to the cave, and they are there to this very day.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Joshua 6:21; Deuteronomy 7:16; Deuteronomy 20:16–17"> That day Joshua captured Makkedah, killing all its inhabitants, including the king. He set it apart and completely destroyed it and everyone in it,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="See the explanation at 6:17.">f</a></sup>leaving no survivors. He killed the king of Makkedah just as he had killed the king of Jericho.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Joshua 21:13; Joshua 15:42; Jeremiah 52:1"> Then Joshua and the Israelite army left Makkedah and went to attack Libnah,</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30"> and the Lord gave the town and its king to the Israelites. Joshua had everyone in it killed, leaving no survivors. He killed its king just as he had killed the king of Jericho.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Joshua 10:3; Joshua 12:11; Joshua 10:5"> Then Joshua and the Israelite army moved on from Libnah to Lachish, surrounding the town and attacking it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32"> The Lord gave the town to the Israelites who captured it on the second day. Joshua had everyone in it killed, just as he had done in Libnah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Joshua 16:10; Judges 1:29; Joshua 16:3"> Then Horam, king of Gezer, came with his army to help Lachish, but Joshua and his men killed them, leaving no survivors.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Joshua 10:3; Joshua 12:12; Joshua 15:39"> Joshua and the Israelite army moved on from Lachish to Eglon, surrounding the town and attacking it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Leviticus 26:44; Joshua 10:37; Job 19:10"> They captured it the same day. Joshua had everyone in it killed that very day. He set it apart and completely destroyed it, just as he had done in Lachish.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="Judges 1:10; Joshua 15:13; Joshua 14:13–14"> Joshua and the Israelite army left Eglon and went to attack Hebron.</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37"> They captured the town, as well as the towns nearby. Joshua had all the inhabitants killed, leaving no survivors. Just as he had done in Eglon, he set it apart and completely destroyed it and everyone in it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="Joshua 15:15; Joshua 12:13; Joshua 15:49"> Then Joshua and the Israelite army turned and went to attack Debir.</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Obadiah 1:18; Joshua 11:8; Deuteronomy 3:3"> He captured it and its king and all the towns nearby. Joshua had all the inhabitants killed, leaving no survivors. Just as he had done in Hebron, he set it apart and completely destroyed it and everyone in it. He killed the king of Debir just as he had killed the king of Libnah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="Deuteronomy 7:2–16; Exodus 23:31–33; Joshua 8:27"> So Joshua conquered the whole land—the hill country, the Negev, the foothills, and the slopes—and all their kings. He didn't leave a single survivor. He killed everyone<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Literally, “all that breathed.” This did not refer to the animals, however.">g</a></sup>as the Lord, the God of Israel, had commanded.</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="Joshua 11:16; Joshua 15:51; Deuteronomy 9:23"> Joshua destroyed them from Kadesh-barnea to Gaza and the whole of the land from Goshen<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Not the Goshen in Egypt.">h</a></sup>to Gibeon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="Joshua 10:14; PSALMS 46:7; PSALMS 80:3"> All the kings and their lands were conquered by Joshua in one campaign because the Lord, the God of Israel, was fighting for the Israelites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43" data-xref="Joshua 10:15; Joshua 4:19; 1 Samuel 11:14"> Joshua and the Israelite army then returned to the camp at Gilgal.</span>
</div>
