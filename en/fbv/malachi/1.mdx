<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Habakkuk 1:1; Isaiah 13:1; Haggai 1:1"> A prophecy:<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally, “burden.”">a</a></sup>This message came from the Lord concerning Israel through Malachi.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Isaiah 41:8–9; Jeremiah 31:3; Deuteronomy 10:15"> I have loved<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="The tense of the verb indicates not just a past action but a past action that continues in the present.">b</a></sup>you, says the Lord. But you ask, “How have you loved us?” Wasn't Esau Jacob's brother? the Lord responds. But I loved Jacob</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Obadiah 1:10; Ezekiel 35:3–4; Jeremiah 49:16–18"> and despised Esau. I have made Esau's mountain homeland into a wasteland, and turned his inheritance into a desert for jackals.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Ezekiel 25:14; Ezekiel 35:9; Ezekiel 11:10"> The people of Edom may be saying: “We have been beaten down, but we will rebuild the ruins.” But this is what the Lord Almighty says, They may try and build, but I will tear down. They will be called a land of wickedness, and the people those who make the Lord angry<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Divine anger must not be equated with human anger. Here it has the sense of God's total hostility to evil, rather than an emotional self-centered reaction.">c</a></sup>forever.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Deuteronomy 11:7; PSALMS 58:10–11; Ezekiel 38:16"> You<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="The people of Israel.">d</a></sup>will see this destruction with your own eyes, and you will say, “The Lord is great, even beyond the borders of Israel.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Luke 6:46; Isaiah 1:2; John 13:13–17"> A son honors his father, and a servant respects his master. So if I am your father, where is my honor? If I am your master, where is my respect? says the Lord Almighty to you priests who show contempt for me. But you ask, “How have we shown contempt for you?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Malachi 1:12; 1 Corinthians 11:27–32; Malachi 1:8"> By making defiled<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="“Defiled”: the concept here is that the priests have not followed the Lord's instructions as to how sacrifices were to be offered, showing a careless contempt in their worship of the Lord.">e</a></sup>offerings on my altar. Then you ask, “How have we defiled you?” By saying<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="The priests may not have said so audibly but their actions show what they were saying to themselves.">f</a></sup>the Lord's table doesn't deserve respect.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Deuteronomy 15:21; Leviticus 22:19–25; Hosea 8:13"> When you offer a blind animal as a sacrifice, isn't that wrong? Or when you offer an animal that is crippled or sick, isn't that wrong? Would you give such gifts to your governor? Would he be pleased with you? Would he be kind and show favor to you? asks the Lord Almighty.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Exodus 32:11; Jeremiah 27:18; 1 Peter 1:17"> So why don't you try being kind to God, begging him to be merciful to you?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="This line is usually taken as ironic.">g</a></sup>But when you bring such offerings, why should he show favor to you? asks the Lord Almighty.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Micah 3:11; Amos 5:21–24; Jeremiah 6:20"> I really wish one of you would shut the Temple doors to stop you lighting pointless fires<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Fires were lit on the altar to burn up the sacrifices. God is saying he doesn't want such sacrifices. They are pointless, because they do not represent any repentance on the part of the worshipers.">h</a></sup>on my altar! I am not pleased with you, says the Lord Almighty, and I will not accept offerings from you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Isaiah 45:6; Isaiah 54:5; Isaiah 66:19–20"> I am honored by nations from the farthest east to the distant west; everywhere people make offerings to me of incense and pure sacrifices. I am honored among the nations, says the Lord Almighty.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Malachi 1:6–8; Malachi 2:8; Amos 2:7"> But you dishonor me when you say the Lord's table doesn't deserve respect, and that its food can be treated with contempt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Micah 6:3; Leviticus 22:19–23; Mark 14:37–38"> You say, “All this is too much trouble!” and you sniff scornfully at it, says the Lord Almighty. But when you bring animals that are stolen or crippled or sick and offer them as sacrifices, should I accept what you're giving? asks the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Corinthians 8:12; Acts 5:1–10; Ecclesiastes 5:4–5"> Cursed are those who cheat by vowing to bring a ram as a sacrifice and then offers an imperfect animal to the Lord. For I am a great King, says the Lord Almighty, and I am respected<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="Literally, “feared,” but in this instance is linked to the respect that is missing mentioned in 1:6.">i</a></sup>among the nations!</span>
