<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Genesis 12:10; Genesis 26:1; Ezekiel 14:13"> There was a famine during the time when the judges ruled<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally “when judges judged,” but this was in an executive role, rather than simply judicial.">a</a></sup>Israel, so a man left Bethlehem in Judah and went to live in exile in the country of Moab, along with his wife and two sons.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Genesis 35:19; Micah 5:2; 1 Samuel 17:21"> His name was Elimelech, and his wife's name Naomi. His sons were called Mahlon and Chilion. They were Ephrathites<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Ephrathah is thought to be an older name for this particular Bethlehem, or a way of specifically identifying it. The two names occur together in Micah 5:2.">b</a></sup>from Bethlehem in Judah. They went to the country of Moab and lived there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Hebrews 12:10–11; PSALMS 34:19; Hebrews 12:6"> However, Elimelech, Naomi's husband, died, and she was left with her two sons.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Matthew 1:5; 1 Kings 11:1–2; Deuteronomy 23:3"> The sons married Moabite women. One was called Orpah, the other was called Ruth. After about ten years,</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Deuteronomy 32:39; PSALMS 89:30–32; Isaiah 49:21"> both Mahlon and Chilion died. Naomi was left alone, without her two sons or her husband.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="PSALMS 132:15; Exodus 3:16; PSALMS 111:5"> So she and her daughters-in-law prepared to leave the country of Moab and return home because she had heard that the Lord had blessed his people there with food.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Kings 8:3; Ruth 1:14; Exodus 18:27"> She left the place where she had been living and with her two daughters-in-law set out on the road back to the land of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Ruth 2:20; Ruth 1:5; Colossians 3:18"> However, as they left, Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, “Each of you, go back to your mother's homes, and may the Lord be as kind to you as you have been to me, and to those who have died.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Ruth 3:1; Acts 20:37; Genesis 29:11"> May the Lord give you a good home with another husband.” She kissed them, and they all started to cry loudly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Zechariah 8:23; PSALMS 16:3; PSALMS 119:63"> “No! We want to go back with you to your people,” they replied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Deuteronomy 25:5; Genesis 38:11"> “Why do you want to go back with me?” Naomi asked. “I'm not able to have any more sons for you to marry.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Genesis 17:17; 1 Timothy 5:9"> Go back home, my daughters, because I'm too old to marry again. Even if I were to sleep with a new husband tonight and had sons,</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Job 19:21; Judges 2:15; PSALMS 32:4"> would you wait for them to grow up? Would you decide you weren't going to marry anyone else? No. The whole situation is more bitter for me than it is for you, for the Lord has turned against me!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“The Lord has turned against me”: literally, “the hand of the Lord has gone against me.”">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Proverbs 18:24; Matthew 16:24; Matthew 10:37"> They started crying loudly again. Then Orpah kissed her mother-in-law goodbye. But Ruth held on tightly to Naomi.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Judges 11:24; Joshua 24:15; Zephaniah 1:6"> “Look, your sister-in-law is going back to her people and her gods. Go back home with her,” said Naomi.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Samuel 15:21; Ruth 2:11–12; 2 Kings 2:2–6"> But Ruth replied, “Please don't keep on telling me to leave you and go back. Where you go, I will go. Where you live, I will live. Your people will be my people. Your God will be my God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Samuel 3:17; 1 Kings 2:23; 2 Kings 6:31"> Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May the Lord punish me harshly if I let anything but death separate us!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Acts 21:14; Ephesians 6:10; Acts 2:42"> When Naomi saw that Ruth was determined to go with her, she stopped telling Ruth to go home.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Matthew 21:10; Isaiah 23:7; Lamentations 2:15"> So the two of them walked on until they reached Bethlehem. When they arrived there, the whole town got excited. “Is this Naomi?”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Not that they didn't recognize her, but that she was returning as a widow in poor circumstances.">d</a></sup>the women asked.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Job 6:4; Hebrews 12:11; PSALMS 73:14"> She said to them, “Don't call me Naomi! Call me Mara,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Naomi means “happy,” while Mara means “bitter.”">e</a></sup>for the Almighty has treated me very bitterly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Samuel 2:7–8; Job 1:21; Job 16:8"> I left here full, but the Lord has brought me home empty. Why call me Naomi when the Lord has condemned me, when the Almighty has brought disaster on me?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Ruth 2:23; 2 Samuel 21:9; Exodus 9:31–32"> This is the way that Naomi returned from Moab with Ruth, the Moabite, her daughter-in-law. They arrived in Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest.</span>
