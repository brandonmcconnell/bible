<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="1 Samuel 18:30; 1 Samuel 18:14–15; Colossians 4:5"> David was successful in doing everything Saul asked him to do, so Saul made him an officer in the army. This pleased everyone, including Saul's other officers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Exodus 15:20; Judges 11:34; PSALMS 68:25"> When the soldiers returned home after David had killed the Philistine, the women of all the towns of Israel came out singing and dancing to meet King Saul, happily celebrating with tambourines and musical instruments.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="1 Samuel 21:11; 1 Samuel 29:5; Exodus 15:21"> As they danced the women sang, “Saul has killed his thousands, and David his tens of thousands.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Samuel 15:28; Proverbs 13:10; James 4:5"> What they were singing made Saul very angry as he didn't think it was right. He said to himself, “They've given David credit for killing tens of thousands, but only thousands to me. All that's left is to give him the kingdom!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Mark 7:22; Genesis 31:2; Matthew 20:15"> From that time on Saul viewed David with suspicion.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Samuel 19:9; 2 Thessalonians 2:11; 1 Kings 18:29"> The following day an evil spirit from God came on Saul with power, and he was ranting<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Ranting”: the word is normally translated “prophesying,” (see for example 10:10when it is applied to Saul), but the main function of a true prophet of God was to deliver messages from God. Saul happened to be holding a spear,</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Samuel 20:33; Isaiah 54:17; Luke 4:30"> and he threw it at David, saying to himself, “I'll pin David to the wall.” But David managed to escape him twice.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Samuel 18:15; 1 Samuel 18:29; 1 Samuel 16:13–14"> Saul was afraid of David, because the Lord was with David, but he had given up on Saul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Samuel 5:2; PSALMS 121:8; 1 Samuel 18:16–17"> So Saul sent David away and made him a commander of a thousand soldiers, leading them out and back as part of the army.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Genesis 39:23; Matthew 28:20; 1 Samuel 16:18"> David was very successful in everything he did, because the Lord was with him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="James 3:17; Colossians 4:5; James 1:5"> When Saul saw how successful David was, he was even more afraid of him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Samuel 18:5; Numbers 27:17; 1 Kings 3:7"> But everyone in Israel and Judah loved David, because of his leadership in the army.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="1 Samuel 17:25; 1 Samuel 25:28; 1 Samuel 18:25"> One day Saul told David, “Here's my oldest daughter Merab. I will give her to you in marriage, but only if you prove to me you're a brave warrior and fight the battles of the Lord.” For Saul was thinking, “I don't need to be the one to kill him—let the Philistines do it!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="2 Samuel 7:18; 1 Samuel 9:21; 1 Samuel 18:23"> “But who am I, and what status does my family have in Israel, for me to become the son-in-law of the king?” David replied.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="David may have been concerned at the cost of providing a dowry, especially as this is a condition of marriage mentioned later in verse 25.">c</a></sup></span>

<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Samuel 18:28"> Meanwhile Saul's daughter Michal had fallen in love with David, and when Saul was told, he was happy about it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Samuel 18:17; Exodus 10:7; Jeremiah 9:8"> “I'll give her to David,” Saul thought. “She can be the bait so the Philistines can trap him.” So Saul said to David, “This is the second time you can become my son-in-law.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="PSALMS 36:1–3; PSALMS 55:21; 2 Samuel 13:28–29"> Saul gave these instructions to his servants, “Talk with David in private and tell him, ‘Look, the king is very happy with you, and all of us love you. Why not become the king's son-in-law?’”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="PSALMS 119:141; Proverbs 14:20; Ecclesiastes 9:15–16"> Saul's servants spoke privately to David, but he replied, “Do you think it's nothing to become the king's son-in-law? I'm a poor man, and I'm not important.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24"> When Saul's servants explained to him what David had said,</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Genesis 34:12; 1 Samuel 18:17; 1 Samuel 14:24"> Saul told them, “Tell David, ‘The only dowry the king wants for the bride is one hundred foreskins of dead Philistines as a way of taking revenge on his enemies.’” Saul's plan was to have David be killed by the Philistines.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="1 Samuel 18:21"> When the servants reported what the king had said back to David, he was happy to become the king's son-in-law. While there was still time,</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 Samuel 3:14; Judges 14:19; 1 Samuel 18:13"> David set off with his men and killed two hundred Philistines, and brought back their foreskins. They counted them all out before the king so that David could become the king's son-in-law. So Saul gave him his daughter Michal in marriage.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="1 Samuel 24:20; 1 Samuel 26:25; Genesis 37:8–11"> Saul realized that the Lord was with David and that his daughter Michal was in love with David,</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 4:4; John 11:53; James 2:19"> and so he became even more afraid of David, and was David's enemy for the rest of his life.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="1 Samuel 18:5; 2 Samuel 11:1; 1 Peter 2:4"> Whenever the Philistine commanders attacked, David was more successful in battle than all of Saul's officers, so that his reputation grew rapidly.</span>
