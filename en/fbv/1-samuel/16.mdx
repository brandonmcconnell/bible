<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Samuel 9:12; 1 Samuel 20:29; Matthew 10:16"> “How can I go and do that?” Samuel asked. “Saul will hear about it and kill me!” The Lord answered, “Take a young cow with you and say, ‘I've come to sacrifice to the Lord.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Samuel 9:16; 1 Samuel 16:12–13; 2 Samuel 15:11"> Invite Jesse to the sacrifice, and I will show you what you have to do. Anoint for me the one I tell you to.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Kings 2:13; 1 Chronicles 12:17–18; 1 Samuel 21:1"> Samuel did what the Lord had told him to and went to Bethlehem. When the town elders met him, they were afraid and asked him, “Do you come in peace?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Joshua 3:5; Leviticus 20:7–8; Exodus 19:10"> “Yes, I come in peace,” he replied. “I have come to sacrifice to the Lord. Purify yourselves and come with me to the sacrifice.” He purified Jesse and his sons and invited them to the sacrifice.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="1 Samuel 17:13; 1 Chronicles 2:13; 1 Samuel 17:22"> When they arrived and Samuel saw Eliab, he said to himself, “This has got to be the Lord's anointed!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="John 7:24; Jeremiah 17:10; Luke 16:15"> But the Lord said to Samuel, “Don't look at his outward appearance or how tall he is, because I have rejected him, for the Lord does not look as human beings do. Human beings only see with their eyes what's on the outside, but the Lord looks at the way people think deep inside.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Samuel 17:13; 1 Chronicles 2:13"> Then Jesse called Abinadab and had him come before Samuel, who said, “The Lord hasn't chosen this one either.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="1 Samuel 17:13; 1 Chronicles 2:13; 2 Samuel 13:3"> Then Jesse had Shammah come forward. But Samuel said, “The Lord hasn't chosen this one either.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Chronicles 2:13–15"> Jesse had seven of his sons come before Samuel, but Samuel told him, “The Lord has not chosen any of these.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="PSALMS 78:70–71; 2 Samuel 7:8; 2 Samuel 13:3"> So he asked Jesse, “Don't you have any more sons?” “Well, there's still the youngest,” Jesse replied, “but he is out looking after the sheep.” “Send for him and bring him here because we will not sit down and eat<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Sit down and eat”: literally, “surround.” This is usually thought to mean to surround a table before sitting down, but it could also mean to “surround” an altar, i.e. the beginning of the sacrificial rituals.">b</a></sup>until he gets here,” Samuel told Jesse.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Lamentations 4:7; PSALMS 89:19–20; Song of Songs 5:10"> So Jesse sent for him and brought him in. He had a red complexion with beautiful eyes, and looked handsome. The Lord said, “Go and anoint him, for he's the one.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="1 Samuel 10:6; Hebrews 1:9; John 3:34"> Samuel took the flask of olive oil and anointed him in the presence of his brothers, and the Spirit of the Lord came on David in power from that day on. Then Samuel left and returned to Ramah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="PSALMS 51:11; 1 Samuel 18:12; Judges 9:23"> The Spirit of the Lord had left Saul, and an evil spirit from the Lord tormented him.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="As in other parts of Scripture, God is sometimes presented as doing something he does not actually prevent. <span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15"> Saul's servants told him, “It's definitely an evil spirit from God that's tormenting you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Kings 3:15; 1 Kings 10:8; 1 Samuel 19:9"> Give us your servants here the order to find someone who is good at playing the harp so that when the evil spirit from God comes on you, he can play and you'll feel much better.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> Saul gave the order to his servants, “Find me someone who is good at playing the harp and bring him to me.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Samuel 3:19; Genesis 39:23; Genesis 39:2"> One of the servants replied, “I know a son of Jesse of Bethlehem who is good at playing the harp. He is a brave man, a good fighter, well-spoken and handsome, and the Lord is with him.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 Samuel 16:11; 1 Samuel 17:15; PSALMS 78:70–72"> Saul sent messengers to Jesse, telling him, “Send me your son David who takes care of the sheep.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Samuel 10:27; Proverbs 18:16; 1 Samuel 17:18"> So Jesse loaded a donkey with bread, a skin of wine, and a young goat and sent them with his son David to Saul.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Genesis 41:46; Proverbs 22:29; PSALMS 146:3"> David came to Saul and began working for him. Saul thought a great deal of him, and David became his armor-bearer.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> Saul sent a message to Jesse, saying, “Please allow David to continue working for me because I am pleased with him.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Samuel 16:14–16; 1 Samuel 18:10–11; Luke 11:24–26"> So whenever the spirit from God came on Saul, David would take his harp and play, and Saul would gain relief and feel better, and the evil spirit would leave him.</span>

## Footnotes

<ol className="fnl">
<li id="fn-a"><a href="#fnref-a" className="fnb">a</a> <span className="fnr">16:1</span> “Flask”: literally, “horn.”</li>
<li id="fn-b"><a href="#fnref-b" className="fnb">b</a> <span className="fnr">16:11</span> “Sit down and eat”: literally, “surround.” This is usually thought to mean to surround a table before sitting down, but it could also mean to “surround” an altar, i.e. the beginning of the sacrificial rituals.</li>
<li id="fn-c"><a href="#fnref-c" className="fnb">c</a> <span className="fnr">16:14</span> As in other parts of Scripture, God is sometimes presented as doing something he does not actually prevent. The removal of the Spirit of the Lord left Saul open to control by another spirit. The way the servants react shows that this was a common view of the time—God is given responsibility for Saul's problems.</li>
