<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Judges 6:27; Micah 7:5; 2 Samuel 1:25–26"> One day Jonathan, son of Saul, said to the young armor-bearer, “Come on, let's cross over to the Philistine garrison on the other side.” But he didn't let his father know his plans.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Samuel 13:15–16; Isaiah 10:28–29"> Saul was staying near Gibeah under a pomegranate tree<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="“A pomegranate tree”: or “the Rock of Rimmon.”">a</a></sup>in Migron. He had about six hundred men with him,</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="1 Samuel 22:20; 1 Samuel 2:28; 1 Samuel 4:21"> including Ahijah, who was wearing an ephod.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="“Ephod”: a priestly garment.">b</a></sup>He was a son of Ichabod's brother Ahitub, son of Phinehas, son of Eli, priest of the Lord in Shiloh. No one realized Jonathan had left.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Samuel 13:23"> Cliffs stood on both sides of the pass that Jonathan planned to cross to reach the Philistine garrison, one called Bozez and the other Seneh.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> The cliff to the north was on the Michmash side, the one to the south on the Geba side.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Matthew 19:26; Romans 8:31; Zechariah 4:6"> Jonathan said to the young armor-bearer, “Come on, let's cross over to the garrison of these heathen<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="“Heathen”: literally, “uncircumcised.”">c</a></sup>men. Maybe the Lord will help us out. It's not a problem for the Lord to win, whether by many or by few.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Samuel 7:3; Zechariah 8:23; 1 Samuel 10:7"> “You choose what you want to do,” the armor-bearer replied. “I'm with you whatever you decide!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Judges 7:9–14"> “Let's go then!” Jonathan said. “We'll cross over in their direction so they can see us.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Judges 6:36–40; Genesis 24:13–14"> If they tell us, ‘Wait there until we come down to you,’ we will wait where we are and we won't go up to them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Genesis 24:14; 1 Samuel 10:7; Judges 7:11"> But if they say, ‘Come on up to us,’ we'll climb up, for that will be the sign that the Lord has handed them over to us.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Samuel 13:6; 1 Samuel 14:22; Judges 6:2"> So both of them let themselves be seen by the Philistine garrison. “Hey, look!” shouted the Philistines. “The Hebrews are coming out of the holes<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="“Holes”: the word is often used to describe burrows where animals live.">d</a></sup>they were hiding in.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="1 Samuel 17:43–44; 2 Samuel 5:24; Judges 7:15"> The men from the garrison called down to Jonathan and his armor-bearer, “Come up here, and we'll teach you a thing or two!” “Follow me up,” Jonathan told his armor-bearer, “for the Lord has handed them over to Israel.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Romans 8:31; Joshua 23:10; Leviticus 26:7–8"> So Jonathan climbed up on his hands and feet, with his armor-bearer right behind him. Jonathan attacked and killed them,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="“Jonathan attacked and killed them”: literally, “they fell before Jonathan.”">e</a></sup>and his armor-bearer followed doing the same.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14"> In this first attack, Jonathan and his armor-bearer killed about twenty men over an area of half an acre.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Samuel 13:17; Genesis 35:5; 2 Samuel 5:24"> Then the Philistines went into a panic, in the camp, in the field, and throughout their army. Even those in the outposts and the groups of raiders became terrified. The earth shook—this was a panic sent from God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Chronicles 20:22–25; Isaiah 19:2; Judges 7:22"> Saul's lookouts at Gibeah in Benjamin saw the Philistine army melting away, scattering in all directions.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> Saul told the soldiers with him, “Do a roll-call and find out who isn't here with us.” When they did the roll-call, they discovered that Jonathan and his armor-bearer weren't there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="1 Samuel 30:7–8; 1 Samuel 5:2; Judges 20:23"> Saul said to Ahijah, “Bring the Ark of God here.” (At the time the Ark of God traveled with the Israelites.)</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 Samuel 14:24; Numbers 27:21; Joshua 9:14"> But as Saul was speaking to the priest, the commotion coming from the Philistine camp grew louder and louder. So Saul told the priest, “Forget it!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="“Forget it!”: literally, “Take away your hand.” The priest was about to try and determine the Lord's will regarding an attack on the Philistines, perhaps by consulting the Urim and the Thummin on the ephod or through the use of the Ark of God in some way. Whatever the case, Saul countermanded his previous order for divine guidance by telling the priest to stop whatever he was about to do.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Judges 7:22; 2 Chronicles 20:23; 1 Samuel 14:16"> Then Saul and all his army assembled and went into battle. They discovered the Philistines were in total disarray, attacking each other with swords.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="1 Samuel 29:4; Judges 7:23"> The Hebrews who had previously sided with the Philistines, and who were with them in their camp, switched sides and joined the Israelites who were with Saul and Jonathan.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="1 Samuel 13:6; 1 Samuel 31:7"> When all the Israelites who had been hiding in the hill country of Ephraim heard that the Philistines were running away, they also joined in chasing after the Philistines to attack them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Exodus 14:30; 1 Samuel 13:5; PSALMS 44:6–8"> On that day the Lord saved Israel, and the battle extended past Beth-aven.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="The Septuagint adds the following at this point: “and the army with Saul numbered around ten thousand men. The battle extended across the hill country of Ephraim.”">g</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Joshua 6:26; Romans 10:2; Judges 1:28"> It was difficult for the men of Israel that day because Saul had ordered the army to take an oath, saying, “Cursed is anyone who eats anything before evening, before I have avenged myself on my enemies.” So no one in the army had eaten anything.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Exodus 3:8; Numbers 13:27; Deuteronomy 9:28"> When they all entered the forest they found honeycomb on the ground.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 9:2"> While they were in the forest they saw the honey running out, but no one picked it up to eat it because they were all afraid of the oath they had taken.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="1 Samuel 14:29; 1 Samuel 14:43; 1 Samuel 30:12"> But Jonathan hadn't heard that his father had ordered the army to take this oath. So he stuck the end of his stick into the honeycomb, picked up a piece to eat, and he felt much better.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="“He felt much better”: literally, “his eyes brightened.” Also in verse 29.">h</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="1 Samuel 14:43; 1 Samuel 14:24"> But one of the soldiers told him, “Your father made the army take a solemn oath, saying, ‘Cursed is anyone who eats anything today!’ That is why the men are exhausted.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="1 Kings 18:18; Joshua 7:25"> “My father has caused a whole lot of trouble for everyone,”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="“Everyone”: literally, “the land.”">i</a></sup>Jonathan replied. “See how well I am because I've eaten a little of this honey.</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Ecclesiastes 9:18"> It would have been so much better if only the army had eaten plenty today from the plunder taken from their enemies! How many more Philistines would have been killed?”</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Joshua 10:12; Joshua 19:42"> After defeating the Philistines that day, killing them from Michmash to Aijalon, the Israelites were totally exhausted.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="1 Samuel 15:19; Leviticus 3:17; Leviticus 17:10–14"> They grabbed the plunder, taking sheep, cattle, and calves, and slaughtered them right there on the ground. But they ate them with the blood.</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Romans 2:1; Matthew 7:5"> Saul was told, “Look, the men are sinning against the Lord by eating meat with the blood.” “Law-breakers!” said Saul. “Roll a large stone over here right now!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34"> Then he said, “Go around the soldiers and tell them, ‘Every man must bring me his cattle or his sheep and slaughter them here, and then eat. Don't sin against the Lord by eating meat with the blood.’” Everyone in the army brought what he had<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="“What he had”: Septuagint reading.">j</a></sup>and slaughtered it there that night.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="1 Samuel 7:17; 1 Samuel 7:12; 2 Timothy 3:5"> So Saul built an altar to the Lord. This was the first altar he had built to the Lord.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="Isaiah 48:1–2; PSALMS 73:28; Malachi 2:7"> Saul said, “Let's go and chase down the Philistines during night and plunder them till dawn, leaving no survivors.” “Do what you think best,” they replied. But the priest said, “Let's ask God first.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="1 Samuel 28:6; 1 Samuel 30:7–8; 1 Samuel 23:9–12"> Saul asked God, “Shall I go down and chase down the Philistines? Will you hand them over to Israel?” But that day God didn't answer him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="Judges 20:2; Joshua 7:14–26; Numbers 24:17"> So Saul gave the order, “All you army commanders, come here so we can investigate what sin has happened today.</span>
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="1 Samuel 14:44; 2 Samuel 12:5; 1 Samuel 14:24"> I swear on the life of the Lord who saves Israel, that even if it's my son Jonathan, he will have to die!” But no one of the whole army said anything.</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="2 Samuel 15:15; 1 Samuel 14:7; 1 Samuel 14:36"> Saul told them all, “You stand over to one side, and I and my son Jonathan will stand on the opposite side.” “Do what you think best,” the army replied.</span>
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="Acts 1:24; Joshua 7:16–18; 1 Samuel 10:20–21"> Saul prayed to the Lord, the God of Israel, “Let the Thummin show.”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-k" id="fnref-k" data-fn="In other words, let the Thummin show who is the guilty party.">k</a></sup>Jonathan and Saul were identified, while everyone else was cleared.</span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42"> Then Saul said, “Cast lots between me and my son Jonathan.” Jonathan was selected.</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43" data-xref="Joshua 7:19; 1 Samuel 14:27; Jonah 1:7–10"> “Tell me what you have done,” Saul asked Jonathan. “I just tasted a little honey with the end of my stick,” Jonathan told him. “Here I am, and I have to die.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44" data-xref="1 Samuel 14:39; Ruth 1:17; 1 Samuel 25:22"> Saul said, “May God punish me very severely if you do not die, Jonathan!”</span>
<span className="v" id="v45" data-v="45" data-xref="Luke 21:18; Acts 27:34; 2 Samuel 14:11"> But the people told Saul, “Does Jonathan have to die, the one who achieved this great victory in Israel? Absolutely not! We swear on the life of the Lord, not a single hair of his head will fall to the ground, for it was with God's help that he achieved this today.” The people saved Jonathan, and he didn't die.</span>
<span className="v" id="v46" data-v="46"> Saul stopped chasing down the Philistines, and the Philistines went to their own country.</span>
<span className="v" id="v47" data-v="47" data-xref="2 Samuel 10:6; 1 Samuel 14:52; 2 Samuel 8:3"> After Saul had made his hold over Israel secure, he fought against all his enemies all around: Moabites, Ammonites, Edomites, the kings of Zobah, and the Philistines. Whatever direction he went he defeated them all.</span>
<span className="v" id="v48" data-v="48" data-xref="Exodus 17:14; 1 Samuel 15:3–7; Deuteronomy 25:19"> He fought bravely, conquering the Amalekites, saving Israel from those who plundered them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v49" data-v="49" data-xref="1 Chronicles 8:33; 1 Samuel 31:2; 2 Samuel 6:20–23"> Saul's sons were Jonathan, Ishvi,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-l" id="fnref-l" data-fn="Also called Ishbosheth.">l</a></sup>and Malchishua. The names of his two daughters were Merab (the firstborn), and Michal (the younger one).</span>
<span className="v" id="v50" data-v="50" data-xref="2 Samuel 2:8; 1 Samuel 17:55; 2 Samuel 3:27"> The name of his wife was Ahinoam, daughter of Ahimaaz. The name of Saul's army commander was Abner, son of Ner, and Ner was Saul's uncle.</span>
<span className="v" id="v51" data-v="51" data-xref="1 Samuel 9:1; 1 Samuel 9:21"> Saul's father Kish and Abner's father Ner were sons of Abiel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v52" data-v="52" data-xref="1 Samuel 8:11; 1 Samuel 8:1"> Throughout Saul's lifetime there was constant war with the Philistines. Saul recruited into his army every strong warrior and every brave fighter that he met.</span>
