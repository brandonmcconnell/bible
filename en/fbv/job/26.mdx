<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1"> Job replied,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Isaiah 35:3–4; Job 4:3–4; Isaiah 41:5–7"> “How helpful you have been to this feeble man that I am. How supportive you have been to the weak.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="PSALMS 71:15–18; Job 32:11–13; Job 33:3"> What good advice you have given to this ignorant man, demonstrating you have so much wisdom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Kings 22:23–24; Job 32:18; Ecclesiastes 12:7"> Who helped you speak these words? Who inspired you to say such things?<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Clearly in these verses Job is being ironic. In addition some commentators see 26:1-4as Job interrupting Bildad who continues as speaker of 26:5-14.">a</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="PSALMS 88:10; Genesis 6:4; Job 41:1–34"> The dead tremble, those beneath the waters.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="PSALMS 139:8; Proverbs 15:11; Hebrews 4:13"> Sheol lies naked before God, Abaddon is uncovered.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Sheol and Abaddon are names for the underworld, the place of the dead. The idea in this verse is that nothing is hidden from God.">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Job 9:8; PSALMS 104:2–5; Isaiah 40:22"> He stretches the northern sky over empty space; he hangs the world on nothing.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Proverbs 30:4; Jeremiah 10:13; Isaiah 5:6"> He gathers the rain in his storm clouds which do not break under the weight.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="PSALMS 97:2; Job 22:14; Habakkuk 3:3–5"> He veils his throne; covering it with his clouds.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Proverbs 8:29; Job 38:8–11; Jeremiah 5:22"> On the surface of the waters he set a boundary; he set a limit dividing light from darkness.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="There are echoes of the Genesis account of creation here.">c</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="1 Samuel 2:8; PSALMS 18:7; Job 15:15"> The pillars of heaven tremble; they shake with fear at his rebuke.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="According to the ancients, the sky was supported by pillars (identified with certain mountains).">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Isaiah 51:15; Jeremiah 31:35; PSALMS 89:9–10"> He calmed the sea with his power; because he knew what to do, he crushed Rahab.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Rahab was a legendary sea-monster, and may refer to some creation stories.">e</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Isaiah 27:1; PSALMS 104:30; PSALMS 74:13–14"> The breath of his voice made the heavens beautiful; with his hand he pierced the gliding serpent.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="See Isaiah 27:1.">f</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Isaiah 40:26–29; PSALMS 145:3; Romans 11:33"> This is just a little of all he does—what we hear of him is hardly a whisper, so who can understand his thunderous power?”</span>
