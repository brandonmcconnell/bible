## Azariah Reigns in Judah

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Chronicles 26:3–4; 2 Kings 14:21; 2 Chronicles 26:1"> In the twenty-seventh year of Jeroboam’s reign over Israel, Azariah<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Azariah is also called Uzziah; also in verses 6, 7, 8, 17, 23, and 27; see 2 Chronicles 26:1.">a</a></sup>son of Amaziah became king of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Chronicles 26:3–4"> He was sixteen years old when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem fifty-two years. His mother’s name was Jecoliah; she was from Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Kings 14:3–4; 2 Kings 12:2–3; 2 Chronicles 26:4"> And he did what was right in the eyes of the LORD, just as his father Amaziah had done.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Kings 15:35; 2 Kings 14:4; 2 Kings 12:3"> Nevertheless, the high places were not taken away; the people continued sacrificing and burning incense there.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Leviticus 13:46; Numbers 12:14; 2 Kings 7:3"> And the LORD afflicted the king with leprosy<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Leprosy was a term used for various skin diseases; see Leviticus 13.">b</a></sup>until the day he died, so that he lived in a separate house while his son Jotham had charge of the palace and governed the people of the land.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Chronicles 26:5–15; 2 Kings 14:18"> As for the rest of the acts of Azariah, along with all his accomplishments, are they not written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah?</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Chronicles 26:23; Isaiah 6:1"> And Azariah rested with his fathers and was buried near them<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Literally with his fathers; see 2 Chronicles 26:23.">c</a></sup>in the City of David. And his son Jotham reigned in his place.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Zechariah Reigns in Israel

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Kings 14:16–17; 2 Kings 14:29–1; 2 Kings 14:21"> In the thirty-eighth year of Azariah’s reign over Judah, Zechariah son of Jeroboam became king of Israel, and he reigned in Samaria six months.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="2 Kings 14:24; 2 Kings 13:2; 2 Kings 13:11"> And he did evil in the sight of the LORD, as his fathers had done. He did not turn away from the sins that Jeroboam son of Nebat had caused Israel to commit.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 Kings 15:28; 2 Kings 15:14; 2 Kings 9:24"> Then Shallum son of Jabesh conspired against Zechariah, struck him down and killed him in front of the people,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Hebrew; some LXX manuscripts in Ibleam">d</a></sup>and reigned in his place.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="2 Kings 14:28"> As for the rest of the acts of Zechariah, they are indeed written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Kings 10:30; Mark 13:31; 2 Kings 13:13"> So the word of the LORD spoken to Jehu was fulfilled: “Four generations of your sons will sit on the throne of Israel.”</span>
</div>
</div>

## Shallum Reigns in Israel

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Kings 15:1; Matthew 1:8–9; 2 Kings 15:8"> In the thirty-ninth year of Uzziah’s<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Uzziah is also called Azariah; also in verses 32 and 34; see verse 1.">e</a></sup>reign over Judah, Shallum son of Jabesh became king, and he reigned in Samaria one full month.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="1 Kings 14:17; 1 Kings 16:17; 1 Kings 16:15"> Then Menahem son of Gadi went up from Tirzah to Samaria, struck down and killed Shallum son of Jabesh, and reigned in his place.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="1 Kings 14:19; 1 Kings 14:29; 2 Kings 15:11"> As for the rest of the acts of Shallum, along with the conspiracy he led, they are indeed written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Israel.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Kings 8:12; 1 Kings 4:24; Hosea 13:16"> At that time Menahem, starting from Tirzah, attacked Tiphsah and everyone in its vicinity, because they would not open their gates. So he attacked Tiphsah and ripped open all the pregnant women.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Menahem Reigns in Israel

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> In the thirty-ninth year of Azariah’s reign over Judah, Menahem son of Gadi became king of Israel, and he reigned in Samaria ten years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18"> And he did evil in the sight of the LORD, and throughout his reign he did not turn away from the sins that Jeroboam son of Nebat had caused Israel to commit.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Kings 14:5; 1 Chronicles 5:25–26; 2 Kings 18:16"> Then Pul<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Pul is another name for Tiglath-pileser; see verse 29.">f</a></sup>king of Assyria invaded the land, and Menahem gave Pul a thousand talents of silver<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="1,000 talents is approximately 37.7 tons or 34.2 metric tons of silver.">g</a></sup>in order to gain his support and strengthen his own grip on the kingdom.</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="2 Kings 18:14–17; 2 Kings 23:35; 2 Kings 17:3–4"> Menahem exacted this money from each of the wealthy men of Israel—fifty shekels of silver<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="50 shekels is approximately 1.26 pounds or 569.8 grams of silver.">h</a></sup>from each man—to give to the king of Assyria. So the king of Assyria withdrew and did not remain in the land.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21"> As for the rest of the acts of Menahem, along with all his accomplishments, are they not written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Israel?</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> And Menahem rested with his fathers, and his son Pekahiah reigned in his place.</span>
</div>
</div>

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Kings 15:25; 1 Kings 22:51; Job 20:5"> In the fiftieth year of Azariah’s reign over Judah, Pekahiah son of Menahem became king of Israel and reigned in Samaria two years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="2 Kings 15:9; 2 Kings 15:18"> And he did evil in the sight of the LORD and did not turn away from the sins that Jeroboam son of Nebat had caused Israel to commit.</span>

</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> As for the rest of the acts of Pekahiah, along with all his accomplishments, they are indeed written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Israel.</span>

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Isaiah 7:4; Isaiah 7:1; 2 Kings 15:23"> In the fifty-second year of Azariah’s reign over Judah, Pekah son of Remaliah became king of Israel, and he reigned in Samaria twenty years.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 Kings 15:18; 2 Kings 13:6; 2 Kings 15:9"> And he did evil in the sight of the LORD and did not turn away from the sins that Jeroboam son of Nebat had caused Israel to commit.</span>

</div>
<div className="bp">

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="1 Chronicles 3:12; 2 Kings 15:7; 2 Chronicles 27:1–9"> In the second year of the reign of Pekah son of Remaliah over Israel, Jotham son of Uzziah became king of Judah.</span>

<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="2 Chronicles 26:4–5; 2 Kings 15:3–4; 2 Chronicles 27:2"> And he did what was right in the eyes of the LORD, just as his father Uzziah had done.</span>

<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="2 Kings 12:3; 2 Chronicles 23:20; 2 Chronicles 27:3–9"> Nevertheless, the high places were not taken away; the people continued sacrificing and burning incense there.<br />Jotham rebuilt the Upper Gate of the house of the LORD.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="2 Chronicles 27:4–9; 2 Kings 15:6–7"> As for the rest of the acts of Jotham, along with his accomplishments, are they not written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah?</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="2 Kings 16:5; Isaiah 7:1; Luke 21:28"> (In those days the LORD began to send Rezin king of Aram and Pekah son of Remaliah against Judah.)</span>
