## Ahaz Reigns in Judah

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Isaiah 1:1; Isaiah 7:1; 2 Kings 15:38"> In the seventeenth year of Pekah son of Remaliah, Ahaz son of Jotham became king of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Chronicles 28:1–4; 2 Kings 15:34; 1 Kings 11:4–8"> Ahaz was twenty years old when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem sixteen years. And unlike David his father, he did not do what was right in the eyes of the LORD his God.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Deuteronomy 12:31; Leviticus 18:21; 2 Kings 21:2"> Instead, he walked in the ways of the kings of Israel and even sacrificed his son in the fire,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Literally made his son pass through the fire">a</a></sup>according to the abominations of the nations that the LORD had driven out before the Israelites.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Deuteronomy 12:2; 1 Kings 14:23; Jeremiah 17:2"> And he sacrificed and burned incense on the high places, on the hills, and under every green tree.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Kings 15:37; Isaiah 9:6–7; Isaiah 8:9–10"> Then Rezin king of Aram and Pekah son of Remaliah king of Israel came up to wage war against Jerusalem. They besieged Ahaz but could not overcome him.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="2 Kings 14:22; Deuteronomy 2:8; 1 Kings 9:26"> At that time Rezin king of Aram recovered Elath<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Elath is a variant of Eloth; see 1 Kings 9:26.">b</a></sup>for Aram,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Or Syria">c</a></sup>drove out the men of Judah, and sent the Edomites into Elath, where they live to this day.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Kings 15:29; Lamentations 4:17; Hosea 14:3"> So Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria, saying, “I am your servant and your son. Come up and save me from the hands of the kings of Aram and Israel, who are rising up against me.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="2 Kings 12:17–18; 2 Chronicles 28:20–21; 2 Chronicles 16:2"> Ahaz also took the silver and gold found in the house of the LORD and in the treasuries of the king’s palace, and he sent it as a gift to the king of Assyria.</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Amos 9:7; Isaiah 22:6; Amos 1:3–5"> So the king of Assyria responded to him, marched up to Damascus, and captured it. He took its people to Kir as captives and put Rezin to death.</span>
</div>
</div>

## The Idolatry of Ahaz

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Romans 12:2; Ezekiel 23:16–17; Isaiah 8:2"> Then King Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria. On seeing the altar in Damascus, King Ahaz sent Uriah the priest a model of the altar and complete plans for its construction.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Hosea 4:6; 2 Chronicles 26:17–18; Hosea 5:11"> And Uriah the priest built the altar according to all the instructions King Ahaz had sent from Damascus, and he completed it before King Ahaz returned.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Chronicles 26:16–19; 2 Chronicles 28:25; 1 Kings 13:1"> When the king came back from Damascus and saw the altar, he approached it and presented offerings on it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Leviticus 1:1–3"> He offered his ascension offering and his grain offering, poured out his drink offering, and splattered the blood of his well-being offerings on the altar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:1; Exodus 40:6; Exodus 40:29"> He also took the bronze altar that stood before the LORD from the front of the temple (between the new altar and the house of the LORD) and he put it on the north side of the new altar.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Exodus 29:39–41; 1 Kings 8:64; 2 Chronicles 29:21–24"> Then King Ahaz commanded Uriah the priest, “Offer on the great altar the morning ascension offering, the evening grain offering, and the king’s ascension offering and grain offering, as well as the ascension offerings, grain offerings, and drink offerings of all the people of the land. Splatter on the altar all the blood of the ascension offerings and sacrifices. But I will use the bronze altar to seek guidance.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Acts 4:19; Acts 5:29; Jude 1:11"> So Uriah the priest did just as King Ahaz had commanded.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Chronicles 4:14–15; Jeremiah 52:20; 2 Chronicles 28:24"> King Ahaz also cut off the frames of the movable stands and removed the bronze basin from each of them. He took down the Sea from the bronze oxen that were under it and put it on a stone base.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="2 Kings 11:5; 1 Kings 10:5; Ezekiel 46:2"> And on account of the king of Assyria, he removed the Sabbath canopy<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Or the base of his throne; see also LXX.">d</a></sup>they had built in the temple and closed the royal entryway outside the house of the LORD.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="2 Kings 15:36; 2 Chronicles 28:26; 2 Kings 20:20–21"> As for the rest of the acts of Ahaz, along with his accomplishments, are they not written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah?</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Matthew 1:9; Isaiah 1:1; 1 Chronicles 3:13"> And Ahaz rested with his fathers and was buried with them in the City of David, and his son Hezekiah reigned in his place.</span>
</div>
</div>

</div>
