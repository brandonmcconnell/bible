## The Dreams of Pharaoh

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Genesis 20:3; Genesis 29:14; Exodus 4:9"> After two full years had passed, Pharaoh had a dream: He was standing beside the Nile,</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Isaiah 19:6"> when seven cows, sleek and well-fed, came up from the river and began to graze among the reeds.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Genesis 41:20–21; Genesis 41:4"> After them, seven other cows, sickly and thin, came up from the Nile and stood beside the well-fed cows on the bank of the river.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="1 Kings 3:15"> And the cows that were sickly and thin devoured the seven sleek, well-fed cows.<br />Then Pharaoh woke up,</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Deuteronomy 32:14"> but he fell back asleep and dreamed a second time: Seven heads of grain, plump and ripe, came up on one stalk.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Ezekiel 17:10; Hosea 13:15; Ezekiel 19:12"> After them, seven other heads of grain sprouted, thin and scorched by the east wind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Genesis 37:5; Genesis 20:3"> And the thin heads of grain swallowed up the seven plump, ripe ones. Then Pharaoh awoke and realized it was a dream.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Daniel 4:7; Exodus 7:22; Matthew 2:1"> In the morning his spirit was troubled, so he summoned all the magicians and wise men of Egypt. Pharaoh told them his dreams, but no one could interpret them for him.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Genesis 40:14; Genesis 40:23; Genesis 40:1–3"> Then the chief cupbearer said to Pharaoh, “Today I recall my failures.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Genesis 39:20; Genesis 40:2–3; Genesis 37:36"> Pharaoh was once angry with his servants, and he put me and the chief baker in the custody of the captain of the guard.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Genesis 40:5–8"> One night both the chief baker and I had dreams, and each dream had its own meaning.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Genesis 40:12–19; Genesis 37:36; Genesis 39:1"> Now a young Hebrew was there with us, a servant of the captain of the guard. We told him our dreams and he interpreted them for us individually.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Ezekiel 43:3; Genesis 40:20–22; Genesis 40:12"> And it happened to us just as he had interpreted: I was restored to my position, and the other man was hanged.”</span>
</div>
</div>

## Joseph Interprets Pharaoh’s Dreams

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Daniel 2:25; Psalms 113:7–8; Psalms 105:19–22"> So Pharaoh sent for Joseph, who was quickly brought out of the dungeon. After he had shaved and changed his clothes, he went in before Pharaoh.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Daniel 5:16; Daniel 5:12; Psalms 25:14"> Pharaoh said to Joseph, “I had a dream, and no one can interpret it. But I have heard it said of you that when you hear a dream you can interpret it.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Corinthians 3:5; Genesis 40:8; Daniel 2:47"> “I myself cannot do it,” Joseph replied, “but God will give Pharaoh a sound answer.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17"> Then Pharaoh said to Joseph: “In my dream I was standing on the bank of the Nile,</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Jeremiah 24:8; Jeremiah 24:1–3; Jeremiah 24:5"> when seven cows, well-fed and sleek, came up from the river and began to graze among the reeds.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19"> After them, seven other cows—sickly, ugly, and thin—came up. I have never seen such ugly cows in all the land of Egypt!</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20"> Then the thin, ugly cows devoured the seven well-fed cows that were there first.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Ezekiel 3:3; Psalms 37:19; Revelation 10:9–10"> When they had devoured them, however, no one could tell that they had done so; their appearance was as ugly as it had been before. Then I awoke.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22"> In my dream I also saw seven heads of grain, plump and ripe, growing on a single stalk.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Psalms 129:6–7; Hosea 13:15; 2 Kings 19:26"> After them, seven other heads of grain sprouted—withered, thin, and scorched by the east wind.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Daniel 4:7; Genesis 41:8; Exodus 8:19"> And the thin heads of grain swallowed the seven plump ones.<br />I told this dream to the magicians, but no one could explain it to me.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Daniel 2:45; Revelation 4:1; Ephesians 1:17"> At this, Joseph said to Pharaoh, “The dreams of Pharaoh are one and the same. God has revealed to Pharaoh what He is about to do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Genesis 41:47; Genesis 41:2; Genesis 41:53"> The seven good cows are seven years, and the seven ripe heads of grain are seven years. The dreams have the same meaning.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="2 Kings 8:1; 2 Samuel 24:19"> Moreover, the seven thin, ugly cows that came up after them are seven years, and so are the seven worthless heads of grain scorched by the east wind—they are seven years of famine.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="Genesis 41:25; Genesis 41:16"> It is just as I said to Pharaoh: God has shown Pharaoh what He is about to do.</span>
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Genesis 41:26; Genesis 41:46–47; Genesis 41:49"> Behold, seven years of great abundance are coming throughout the land of Egypt,</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Genesis 47:13; Genesis 41:54; James 5:17"> but seven years of famine will follow them. Then all the abundance in the land of Egypt will be forgotten, and the famine will devastate the land.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Isaiah 24:20; 1 Samuel 5:6"> The abundance in the land will not be remembered, since the famine that follows it will be so severe.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Numbers 23:19; Isaiah 46:10–11; Isaiah 14:24–27"> Moreover, because the dream was given to Pharaoh in two versions, the matter has been decreed by God, and He will carry it out shortly.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Genesis 41:39; Acts 6:3; Deuteronomy 1:13"> Now, therefore, Pharaoh should look for a discerning and wise man and set him over the land of Egypt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Proverbs 22:3; Luke 16:5; Proverbs 6:6–8"> Let Pharaoh take action and appoint commissioners over the land to take a fifth of the harvest<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="See LXX; MT a fifth from the land">a</a></sup>of Egypt during the seven years of abundance.</span>
<span className="v" id="v35" data-v="35" data-xref="Genesis 45:6–7; Genesis 41:56; Exodus 4:13"> Under the authority of Pharaoh, let them collect all the excess food from these good years, that they may come and lay up the grain to be preserved as food in the cities.</span>
<span className="v" id="v36" data-v="36" data-xref="Genesis 47:13–25"> This food will be a reserve for the land during the seven years of famine to come upon the land of Egypt. Then the country will not perish in the famine.”</span>
</div>
</div>

## Joseph Given Charge of Egypt

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v37" data-v="37" data-xref="Acts 7:10; 1 Kings 21:2; 2 Samuel 3:36"> This proposal pleased Pharaoh and all his officials.</span>
<span className="v" id="v38" data-v="38" data-xref="Job 32:8; Daniel 5:14; Numbers 27:18"> So Pharaoh asked them, “Can we find anyone like this man, in whom the Spirit of God<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Or the spirit of the gods">b</a></sup>abides?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v39" data-v="39" data-xref="Genesis 41:33; Genesis 41:28; Genesis 41:25"> Then Pharaoh said to Joseph, “Since God has made all this known to you, there is no one as discerning and wise as you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v40" data-v="40" data-xref="Proverbs 22:29; Acts 7:10; Psalms 105:21–22"> You shall be in charge of my house, and all my people are to obey your commands. Only with regard to the throne will I be greater than you.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v41" data-v="41" data-xref="Matthew 28:18; Philippians 2:9–11; Genesis 42:6"> Pharaoh also told Joseph, “I hereby place you over all the land of Egypt.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v42" data-v="42" data-xref="Daniel 5:29; Daniel 5:7; Esther 3:10"> Then Pharaoh removed the signet ring from his finger, put it on Joseph’s finger, clothed him in garments of fine linen, and placed a gold chain around his neck.</span>
<span className="v" id="v43" data-v="43" data-xref="Acts 7:10; Genesis 45:8; Genesis 45:26"> He had Joseph ride in his second chariot, with men calling out before him, “Bow the knee!”<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="That is, “Abrek,” probably an Egyptian word that sounds similar to the Hebrew for kneel">c</a></sup>So he placed him over all the land of Egypt.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v44" data-v="44" data-xref="Psalms 105:22; Exodus 11:7"> And Pharaoh declared to Joseph, “I am Pharaoh, but without your permission, no one in all the land of Egypt shall lift his hand or foot.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v45" data-v="45" data-xref="Genesis 46:20; Ezekiel 30:17; Exodus 2:16"> Pharaoh gave Joseph the name Zaphenath-paneah,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Zaphenath-paneah probably means Revealer of Mysteries or God speaks and lives.">d</a></sup>and he gave him Asenath daughter of Potiphera, priest of On,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="That is, Heliopolis, as in LXX; also in verse 50">e</a></sup>to be his wife. And Joseph took charge of all the land of Egypt.</span>
</div>
</div>

## The Seven Years of Plenty

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v46" data-v="46" data-xref="Daniel 1:19; 1 Kings 12:6; 1 Samuel 16:21"> Now Joseph was thirty years old when he entered the service of Pharaoh king of Egypt. And Joseph left Pharaoh’s presence and traveled throughout the land of Egypt.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v47" data-v="47" data-xref="Genesis 26:12; Psalms 72:16"> During the seven years of abundance, the land brought forth bountifully.</span>
<span className="v" id="v48" data-v="48" data-xref="Genesis 41:34–36; Genesis 47:21"> During those seven years, Joseph collected all the excess food in the land of Egypt and stored it in the cities. In every city he laid up the food from the fields around it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v49" data-v="49" data-xref="Psalms 78:27; Judges 6:5; Genesis 22:17"> So Joseph stored up grain in such abundance, like the sand of the sea, that he stopped keeping track of it; for it was beyond measure.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v50" data-v="50" data-xref="Genesis 46:20; Genesis 48:5; 2 Samuel 8:18"> Before the years of famine arrived, two sons were born to Joseph by Asenath daughter of Potiphera, priest of On.</span>
<span className="v" id="v51" data-v="51" data-xref="Psalms 30:5; Proverbs 31:7; Deuteronomy 33:17"> Joseph named the firstborn Manasseh,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Manasseh sounds like the Hebrew for making to forget.">f</a></sup>saying, “God has made me forget all my hardship and all my father’s household.”</span>
<span className="v" id="v52" data-v="52" data-xref="Genesis 49:22; Genesis 50:23; Genesis 17:6"> And the second son he named Ephraim,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Ephraim sounds like the Hebrew for making fruitful or twice fruitful.">g</a></sup>saying, “God has made me fruitful in the land of my affliction.”</span>
</div>
</div>

## The Famine Begins

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v53" data-v="53" data-xref="Genesis 41:29–31; Psalms 73:20; Luke 16:25"> When the seven years of abundance in the land of Egypt came to an end,</span>
<span className="v" id="v54" data-v="54" data-xref="Genesis 45:11; Acts 7:11; Psalms 105:16"> the seven years of famine began, just as Joseph had said. And although there was famine in every country, there was food throughout the land of Egypt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v55" data-v="55" data-xref="Matthew 17:5; Matthew 3:17; Jeremiah 14:1–6"> When extreme hunger came to all the land of Egypt and the people cried out to Pharaoh for food, he told all the Egyptians, “Go to Joseph and do whatever he tells you.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v56" data-v="56" data-xref="Genesis 42:6; Zechariah 5:3; Luke 21:35"> When the famine had spread over all the land, Joseph opened up all the storehouses and sold grain to the Egyptians; for the famine was severe in the land of Egypt.</span>
<span className="v" id="v57" data-v="57" data-xref="Genesis 41:54; Genesis 42:5; Genesis 41:56"> And every nation came to Joseph in Egypt to buy grain, because the famine was severe over all the earth.</span>
</div>
</div>
