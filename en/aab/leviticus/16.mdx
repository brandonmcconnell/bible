## The Day of Purification

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Leviticus 10:1–2"> Now the LORD spoke to Moses after the death of two of Aaron’s sons when they approached the presence of the LORD.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Exodus 30:10; 1 Kings 8:10–12; Hebrews 10:19–20"> And the LORD said to Moses: “Tell your brother Aaron not to enter freely into the Most Holy Place<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Or the Holy Place; also in verses 16, 17, 20, 23, and 27">a</a></sup>behind the veil in front of the throne seat<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Or throne seat; here and throughout this chapter">b</a></sup>on the ark, or else he will die, because I appear in the cloud above the throne seat.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Leviticus 4:3; Hebrews 9:24–25; Hebrews 9:7"> This is how Aaron is to enter the Holy Place: with a young bull for a purification offering and a ram for an ascension offering.</span>
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Exodus 28:39–43; Ezekiel 44:17–18; Exodus 30:20"> He is to wear the sacred linen tunic, with linen undergarments. He must tie a linen sash around him and put on the linen turban. These are holy garments, and he must bathe himself with water before he wears them.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Chronicles 29:21; Leviticus 8:2; Ezra 6:17"> And he shall take from the congregation of Israel two male goats for a purification offering<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="The two goats together are designated one purification offering, with two operations: the blood of the slaughtered goat purges the sanctuary (verses 15-19), and the live goat carries the impurities away outside the camp (verses 21-22). The cleansing rite of Lev 14:4-7 has the same two-part shape: one bird killed, one released alive">c</a></sup>and one ram for an ascension offering.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Leviticus 9:7; Hebrews 9:7; Hebrews 7:27"> Aaron is to present the bull for his purification offering and effect purgation for himself and his household.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Romans 12:1; Leviticus 4:4; Matthew 16:21"> Then he shall take the two goats and present them before the LORD at the entrance to the Tent of Meeting.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Acts 1:23–26; Jonah 1:7; Proverbs 16:33"> After Aaron casts lots for the two goats, one for the LORD and the other for the Azazel,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Heb. la-azazel, ‘for Azazel’; similarly in verses 10 and 26. The second goat is traditionally called the ‘scapegoat’ (from Tyndale’s ‘escape goat,’ 1530), but in modern English ‘scapegoat’ has come to mean someone unfairly blamed, which this goat is not. A closer plain-English name is the ‘tote-goat,’ the goat that totes, or carries, the people’s sin away into the wilderness (see note on verse 10). The AAB keeps the Hebrew name, Azazel.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Acts 4:27–28; Acts 2:23"> he shall present the goat chosen by lot for the LORD and sacrifice it as a purification offering.</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="1 John 2:2; Leviticus 16:21–22; Hebrews 9:23–24"> But the goat chosen by lot as the Azazel shall be presented alive before the LORD to make expiation by sending it into the wilderness as the Azazel.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Two goats are used on the Day of Purification, and the ritual runs on life against death. Blood carries life (Lev 17:11), and it purges the sanctuary, and with it the camp, of the death-pollution that the people’s sin and mortality leave behind. The first goat is killed and its blood wipes the sanctuary clean. The second is not killed; alive, it carries the people’s sins out of the camp and into the wilderness (Lev 16:21-22; see note on Lev 16:5). Carrying sin away like this is what ‘expiation’ means. The wilderness is the wild, lifeless land outside the camp, in Scripture the haunt of wild spirits and goat-demons (Lev 17:7; Isa 13:21; 34:14), far from where God dwells. The goat is sent ‘to Azazel,’ whom the Second Temple book of 1 Enoch names a leading rebel among the heavenly beings, bound in the desert and blamed for leading humanity into sin (1 Enoch 10). His exact identity does not change the picture. If Azazel is Satan under another name, the sin goes straight to the chief rebel. If Azazel is one of the heavenly beings who followed Satan into rebellion, then he took up Satan’s own revolt and shares his condemnation to death, so it still goes into Satan’s domain, carried there through one of his own. Either way the goat sends the people’s death-pollution out into the wilderness so that all is properly sorted: life stays in the camp with God, and the death-pollution is driven out to the realm where death and rebellion belong. This cleansing covers the people’s unintentional sins and impurities, not deliberate, high-handed sin, which has no sacrifice and which God alone forgives (Num 15:30-31; see note on Lev 23:27).">e</a></sup></span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Leviticus 16:6; Hebrews 7:27; Hebrews 9:7"> When Aaron presents the bull for his purification offering and effects purgation for himself and his household, he is to slaughter the bull for his own purification offering.</span>
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="Leviticus 10:1; Exodus 30:34–38; Revelation 8:3–4"> Then he must take a censer full of burning coals from the altar before the LORD, and two handfuls of finely ground fragrant incense, and take them inside the veil.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Exodus 25:21; Exodus 30:1; Exodus 30:7–8"> He is to put the incense on the fire before the LORD, and the cloud of incense will cover the throne seat above the Testimony,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="The Testimony refers to the stone tablets in the ark of the covenant inscribed with the Ten Commandments.">f</a></sup>so that he will not die.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Hebrews 9:25; Leviticus 4:5–6; Hebrews 9:13"> And he is to take some of the bull’s blood and sprinkle it with his finger on the east side of the throne seat; then he shall sprinkle some of it with his finger seven times before the throne seat.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Hebrews 9:7; Hebrews 2:17; Hebrews 9:3"> Aaron shall then slaughter the goat for the purification offering for the people and bring its blood behind the veil, and with its blood he must do as he did with the bull’s blood: He is to sprinkle it against the throne seat and in front of it.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="Hebrews 9:22–23; Leviticus 16:18; Ezekiel 45:18–19"> So he shall effect purgation for the Most Holy Place because of the impurities and rebellious acts of the Israelites in regard to all their sins. He is to do the same for the Tent of Meeting which abides among them in the midst of their impurities.</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Isaiah 53:6; Luke 1:10; Exodus 34:3"> No one may be in the Tent of Meeting from the time Aaron goes in to effect purgation in the Most Holy Place until he leaves, after he has effected purgation for himself, his household, and the whole assembly of Israel.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Leviticus 4:7; Hebrews 5:7–8; Leviticus 16:16"> Then he shall go out to the altar that is before the LORD and effect purgation for it. He is to take some of the bull’s blood and some of the goat’s blood and put it on all the horns of the altar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Zechariah 13:1; Ezekiel 43:18–22"> He is to sprinkle some of the blood on it with his finger seven times to cleanse it and consecrate it from the uncleanness of the Israelites.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Leviticus 16:16; Ezekiel 45:20; Colossians 1:20"> When Aaron has finished purifying the Most Holy Place, the Tent of Meeting, and the altar, he is to bring forward the live goat.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Isaiah 53:6; 2 Corinthians 5:21; Ezra 10:1"> Then he is to lay both hands on the head of the live goat<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Both hands signify transfer of sin, used only here; the one hand laid on ordinary offerings marks ownership, not transfer (Lev 1:4). The goat that carries sin away is precisely the one not sacrificed: it is not slaughtered and its blood is never offered. The slaughtered goat that purges the sanctuary carries no sin. Sin transfer and sacrifice are mutually exclusive operations in this chapter.">g</a></sup>and confess over it all the iniquities and rebellious acts of the Israelites in regard to all their sins. He is to put them on the goat’s head and send it away into the wilderness by the hand of a man appointed for the task.</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Hebrews 9:28; Isaiah 53:11–12; John 1:29"> The goat will carry on itself all their iniquities into a solitary place, and the man will release it into the wilderness.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="Ezekiel 44:19; Ezekiel 42:14; Leviticus 16:4"> Then Aaron is to enter the Tent of Meeting, take off the linen garments he put on before entering the Most Holy Place, and leave them there.</span>
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="Leviticus 16:3–5; Hebrews 10:19–22; Exodus 28:4–14"> He is to bathe himself with water in a holy place and put on his own clothes. Then he must go out and sacrifice his ascension offering and the people’s ascension offering to effect purgation for himself and for the people.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="Exodus 29:13; Leviticus 4:8–10; Leviticus 16:6"> He is also to burn the fat of the purification offering on the altar.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26" data-xref="Leviticus 16:10; Hebrews 7:19; Numbers 19:21"> The man who released the goat as the Azazel must wash his clothes and bathe himself with water; afterward he may reenter the camp.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Leviticus 4:21; Leviticus 6:30; Leviticus 4:11–12"> The bull for the purification offering and the goat for the purification offering, whose blood was brought into the Most Holy Place to effect purgation, must be taken outside the camp; and their hides, flesh, and dung must be burned up.</span>
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28"> The one who burns them must wash his clothes and bathe himself with water, and afterward he may reenter the camp.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Isaiah 58:3; Numbers 29:7; Exodus 20:10"> This is to be a permanent statute for you: On the tenth day of the seventh month, you shall humble yourselves<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-h" id="fnref-h" data-fn="Or afflict your souls or deny yourselves; also in verse 31">h</a></sup>and not do any work—whether the native or the foreigner who resides among you—</span>
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Jeremiah 33:8; Psalms 51:2; Ephesians 5:26"> because on this day purgation will be made for you to cleanse you,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-i" id="fnref-i" data-fn="This chapter is the rite of the Day of Purification (yom hakkippurim, Lev 23:27), traditionally 'Day of Atonement.' The verse states the day’s logic plainly: purgation is cleansing, in modern terms decontamination. What is cleansed is the death-contamination that the people’s mortality and sin deposit on God’s house; the purgative is blood because 'the life is in the blood' (Lev 17:11), life applied against death. The day clears only ritual impurity and inadvertent sin (Num 15:27-28); it does not address deliberate, high-handed sin (Num 15:30-31), cleanse the polluted land (purged by justice or by the land’s enforced sabbath rest, Num 35:33; Lev 26:34-35), or lift the guilt of deliberate sin from the offender. See notes on Leviticus 1:4 and 23:27.">i</a></sup>and you will be clean from all your sins before the LORD.</span>
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="Leviticus 23:32; Isaiah 58:5; Leviticus 25:4"> It is a Sabbath of complete rest for you, that you may humble yourselves; it is a permanent statute.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Leviticus 16:4; Exodus 29:29–30; Leviticus 4:5"> The priest who is anointed and ordained to succeed his father as high priest shall effect purgation. He will put on the sacred linen garments</span>
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="Leviticus 16:16; Leviticus 16:24; Leviticus 16:6"> and effect purgation for the Most Holy Place,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-j" id="fnref-j" data-fn="Or the Holy Sanctuary">j</a></sup>the Tent of Meeting, and the altar, and for the priests and all the people of the assembly.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="Hebrews 9:7; Hebrews 9:25; Exodus 30:10"> This is to be a permanent statute for you, to effect purgation once a year for the Israelites because of all their sins.”<br />And all this was done as the LORD had commanded Moses.</span>
</div>
