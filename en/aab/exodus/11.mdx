## The Plague on the Firstborn Foretold

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="Genesis 15:14; Leviticus 26:21; Exodus 3:20"> Then the LORD said to Moses, “I will bring upon Pharaoh and Egypt one more plague. After that, he will allow you to leave this place. And when he sends you away as a bride, he will surely drive you out from here.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Heb. kallah, ‘bride,’ spelled the same as kalah, ‘completely,’ which most translations read here. The word belongs to the sending rather than the driving out, and ‘send you away completely’ is awkward, which is why the line has long been treated as difficult. Shalach is the verb for sending a daughter off in marriage, and the goods she leaves with are her shilluchim, her send-off gift, as when Pharaoh gave Gezer to his daughter on her marriage (1 Kgs 9:16). Israel’s silver and gold follow at once (v. 2; 12:35-36). The same thing had already happened to Sarai, taken by a Pharaoh, released after plagues, and sent away with wealth (Gen 12:14-20). The same verb also sends a wife away in divorce (Deut 24:1), and ‘drive out’ is garash, the root behind ‘divorced woman’ (Lev 21:7, 14). Pharaoh dismisses her and sends her off with her portion in one act. God then takes her for Himself. ‘I will take you as My own people’ uses the verb for taking a wife (Exod 6:7), He brings her to Himself (Exod 19:4), and at Sinai He binds her by covenant, which is why He is a jealous God (Exod 20:5; see note there). The prophets read the Exodus this way. Jeremiah recalls ‘your love as a bride, how you followed Me in the wilderness’ (Jer 2:2), Ezekiel says ‘I pledged Myself to you, entered into a covenant with you, and you became Mine’ (Ezek 16:8), and both then call Israel’s idolatry adultery (Jer 3:8-9; Ezek 16:32).">a</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="Exodus 3:22; Exodus 12:35–36; Haggai 2:8"> Now announce to the people that men and women alike should ask their neighbors for articles of silver and gold.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Exodus 3:21; Exodus 12:36; Revelation 3:9"> And the LORD gave the people favor in the sight of the Egyptians. Moreover, Moses himself was highly regarded in Egypt by Pharaoh’s officials and by the people.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Exodus 12:29; Psalms 60:10; Job 34:20"> So Moses declared, “This is what the LORD says: ‘About midnight I will go throughout Egypt,</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="Psalms 78:51; Exodus 12:12; Psalms 105:36"> and every firstborn son in the land of Egypt will die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sits on his throne, to the firstborn of the servant girl behind the hand mill, as well as the firstborn of all the cattle.</span>
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Exodus 12:30; Amos 5:17; Zephaniah 1:10"> Then a great cry will go out over all the land of Egypt. Such an outcry has never been heard before and will never be heard again.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Joshua 10:21; Exodus 10:23; Exodus 8:22"> But among all the Israelites, not even a dog will snarl at man or beast.’<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Some translators close this quotation at the end of the verse.">b</a></sup><br />Then you will know that the LORD makes a distinction between Egypt and Israel.</span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Exodus 12:31–33; Ezekiel 3:14; Daniel 3:19"> And all these officials of yours will come and bow before me, saying, ‘Go, you and all the people who follow you!’ After that, I will depart.”<br />And hot with anger, Moses left Pharaoh’s presence.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Exodus 7:3–4; Exodus 3:19; Exodus 10:1"> The LORD said to Moses, “Pharaoh will not listen to you, so that My wonders may be multiplied in the land of Egypt.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Exodus 4:21; Exodus 10:20; Exodus 10:27"> Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh, but the LORD hardened<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Or stiffened or strengthened">c</a></sup>Pharaoh’s heart so that he would not let the Israelites go out of his land.</span>
</div>
