## Sennacherib Threatens Jerusalem

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Chronicles 32:1; 2 Kings 18:13; 2 Kings 18:17"> In the fourteenth year of Hezekiah’s reign, Sennacherib king of Assyria attacked and captured all the fortified cities of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="2 Chronicles 32:9–23; Isaiah 7:3; 2 Kings 18:17–37"> And the king of Assyria sent the Rabshakeh,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="Hebrew Rabshakeh is the title of a high-ranking Assyrian military officer; here and throughout chapters 36 and 37, as well as 2 Kings 18 and 19">a</a></sup>with a great army, from Lachish to King Hezekiah at Jerusalem. And he stopped by the aqueduct of the upper pool, on the road to the Launderer’s Field.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="2 Samuel 8:16–17; Isaiah 22:15–21; 2 Samuel 20:24–25"> Then Eliakim son of Hilkiah the palace administrator, Shebna the scribe, and Joah son of Asaph the recorder, went out to him.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="2 Kings 18:19–37; Jude 1:16; Proverbs 16:18"> The Rabshakeh said to them, “Tell Hezekiah that this is what the great king, the king of Assyria, says: What is the basis of this confidence of yours?</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5" data-xref="2 Kings 18:7; Jeremiah 52:3; Proverbs 21:30–31"> You claim to have<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Literally You speak; see DSS and 2 Kings 18:20; MT I speak.">b</a></sup>a strategy and strength for war, but these are empty words. In whom are you now trusting, that you have rebelled against me?</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Ezekiel 29:6–7; Jeremiah 37:5–8; 2 Kings 18:21"> Look now, you are trusting in Egypt, that splintered reed of a staff that will pierce the hand of anyone who leans on it. Such is Pharaoh king of Egypt to all who trust in him.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="2 Kings 18:4–5; Psalms 42:10–11; Psalms 42:5"> But if you say to me, ‘We trust in the LORD our God,’ is He not the One whose high places and altars Hezekiah has removed, saying to Judah and Jerusalem, ‘You must worship before this altar’?</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="1 Samuel 17:40–43; 1 Kings 20:10; Nehemiah 4:2–5"> Now, therefore, make a bargain with my master, the king of Assyria. I will give you two thousand horses—if you can put riders on them!</span>
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Isaiah 10:8; Isaiah 30:16–17; Isaiah 36:6"> For how can you repel a single officer among the least of my master’s servants when you depend on Egypt for chariots and horsemen?</span>
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="2 Kings 18:25; Isaiah 10:5–7; 1 Kings 13:18"> So now, was it apart from the LORD that I have come up against this land to destroy it? The LORD Himself said to me, ‘Go up against this land and destroy it.’”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ezra 4:7; Daniel 2:4; 2 Kings 18:26–27"> Then Eliakim, Shebna, and Joah said to the Rabshakeh, “Please speak to your servants in Aramaic, since we understand it. Do not speak to us in Hebrew<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Or in the dialect of Judah; also in verse 13">c</a></sup>in the hearing of the people on the wall.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Kings 18:27; Leviticus 26:29; Lamentations 4:9–10"> But the Rabshakeh replied, “Has my master sent me to speak these words only to you and your master, and not to the men sitting on the wall, who are destined with you to eat their own dung and drink their own urine?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="2 Chronicles 32:18; Psalms 73:8–9; 2 Kings 18:28–32"> Then the Rabshakeh stood and called out loudly in Hebrew: “Hear the words of the great king, the king of Assyria!</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="2 Thessalonians 2:4; Revelation 13:5–6; Daniel 6:20"> This is what the king says: Do not let Hezekiah deceive you, for he cannot deliver you.</span>
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Isaiah 37:23–24; Matthew 27:43; Psalms 71:9–11"> Do not let Hezekiah persuade you to trust in the LORD when he says, ‘The LORD will surely deliver us; this city will not be given into the hand of the king of Assyria.’</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="1 Kings 4:25; Zechariah 3:10; Micah 4:4"> Do not listen to Hezekiah, for this is what the king of Assyria says: Make peace with me<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="Or Make a blessing with me">d</a></sup>and come out to me. Then every one of you will eat from his own vine and his own fig tree, and drink water from his own cistern,</span>
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="2 Kings 18:32; Deuteronomy 8:7–9; Job 20:17"> until I come and take you away to a land like your own—a land of grain and new wine, a land of bread and vineyards.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Isaiah 37:17–18; Isaiah 36:15; Isaiah 37:10"> Do not let Hezekiah mislead you when he says, ‘The LORD will deliver us.’ Has the god of any nation ever delivered his land from the hand of the king of Assyria?</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="Isaiah 10:9–11; Jeremiah 49:23; 2 Samuel 8:9"> Where are the gods of Hamath and Arpad? Where are the gods of Sepharvaim? Have they delivered Samaria from my hand?</span>
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="Psalms 50:21; 2 Chronicles 32:19; Isaiah 37:23–29"> Who among all the gods of these lands has delivered his land from my hand? How then can the LORD deliver Jerusalem from my hand?”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Proverbs 26:4; Psalms 38:13–15; Matthew 7:6"> But the people remained silent and did not answer a word, for Hezekiah had commanded, “Do not answer him.”</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Isaiah 36:3; Isaiah 33:7; Matthew 26:65"> Then Hilkiah’s son Eliakim the palace administrator, Shebna the scribe, and Asaph’s son Joah the recorder came to Hezekiah with their clothes torn, and they relayed to him the words of the Rabshakeh.</span>
</div>
</div>

</div>
