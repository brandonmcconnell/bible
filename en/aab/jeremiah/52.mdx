## The Fall of Jerusalem Recounted

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v1" data-v="1" data-xref="2 Kings 24:18; Joshua 10:29; 2 Chronicles 36:11"> Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem eleven years. His mother’s name was Hamutal daughter of Jeremiah; she was from Libnah.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v2" data-v="2" data-xref="1 Kings 14:22; 2 Kings 24:19–20; Ezekiel 17:16–20"> And Zedekiah did evil in the sight of the LORD, just as Jehoiakim had done.</span>
<span className="v" id="v3" data-v="3" data-xref="Isaiah 3:4–5; 2 Chronicles 36:13; Isaiah 3:1"> For because of the anger of the LORD, all this happened in Jerusalem and Judah, until He finally banished them from His presence.<br />And Zedekiah also rebelled against the king of Babylon.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v4" data-v="4" data-xref="Jeremiah 39:1; Zechariah 8:19; Ezekiel 24:1–2"> So in the ninth year of Zedekiah’s reign, on the tenth day of the tenth month, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon marched against Jerusalem with his entire army. They encamped outside the city and built a siege wall all around it.</span>
<span className="v" id="v5" data-v="5"> And the city was kept under siege until King Zedekiah’s eleventh year.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v6" data-v="6" data-xref="Jeremiah 39:2; Isaiah 3:1; 2 Kings 25:3"> By the ninth day of the fourth month, the famine in the city was so severe that the people of the land had no food.</span>
<span className="v" id="v7" data-v="7" data-xref="Jeremiah 39:4–7; Jeremiah 51:32; 2 Kings 25:4"> Then the city was breached; and though the Chaldeans<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-a" id="fnref-a" data-fn="That is, the Babylonians; similarly in verses 8, 9, 14, and 17">a</a></sup>had surrounded the city, all the men of war fled the city by night by way of the gate between the two walls near the king’s garden.<br />They headed toward the Arabah,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-b" id="fnref-b" data-fn="Or the Jordan Valley">b</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v8" data-v="8" data-xref="Jeremiah 34:21; Jeremiah 32:4; Jeremiah 38:23"> but the army of the Chaldeans pursued the king and overtook Zedekiah in the plains of Jericho, and his whole army deserted him.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v9" data-v="9" data-xref="Jeremiah 39:5; Numbers 13:21; 2 Kings 25:6"> The Chaldeans seized the king and brought him up to the king of Babylon at Riblah in the land of Hamath, where he pronounced judgment on Zedekiah.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v10" data-v="10" data-xref="Jeremiah 22:30; 2 Kings 25:7; Genesis 21:16"> There at Riblah the king of Babylon slaughtered the sons of Zedekiah before his eyes, and he also killed all the officials of Judah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v11" data-v="11" data-xref="Ezekiel 12:13; Jeremiah 34:3–5"> Then he put out Zedekiah’s eyes, bound him with bronze shackles, and took him to Babylon, where he kept him in custody until his dying day.</span>
</div>
</div>

## The Temple Destroyed

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v12" data-v="12" data-xref="2 Kings 25:8; Jeremiah 39:9; Jeremiah 52:29"> On the tenth day of the fifth month, in the nineteenth year of Nebuchadnezzar’s reign over Babylon, Nebuzaradan captain of the guard, a servant of the king of Babylon, entered Jerusalem.</span>
<span className="v" id="v13" data-v="13" data-xref="Psalms 79:1; Micah 3:12; 2 Chronicles 36:19"> He burned down the house of the LORD, the royal palace, and all the houses of Jerusalem—every significant building.</span>
<span className="v" id="v14" data-v="14" data-xref="Nehemiah 1:3; 2 Kings 25:10"> And the whole army of the Chaldeans under the captain of the guard broke down all the walls around Jerusalem.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v15" data-v="15" data-xref="Jeremiah 15:1–2; Zechariah 14:2"> Then Nebuzaradan captain of the guard carried into exile some of the poorest people and those who remained in the city, along with the deserters who had defected to the king of Babylon and the rest of the craftsmen.</span>
<span className="v" id="v16" data-v="16" data-xref="2 Kings 25:12; Jeremiah 39:9–10; Jeremiah 40:5–7"> But Nebuzaradan captain of the guard left behind some of the poorest of the land to tend the vineyards and fields.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v17" data-v="17" data-xref="Jeremiah 27:19–22; Jeremiah 52:21–23; 2 Chronicles 4:12–15"> Moreover, the Chaldeans broke up the bronze pillars and stands and the bronze Sea in the house of the LORD, and they carried all the bronze to Babylon.</span>
<span className="v" id="v18" data-v="18" data-xref="Exodus 27:3; Numbers 4:14; 1 Kings 7:45"> They also took away the pots, shovels, wick trimmers, sprinkling bowls, dishes, and all the articles of bronze used in the temple service.</span>
<span className="v" id="v19" data-v="19" data-xref="1 Kings 7:49–50; 2 Chronicles 4:6–22; Leviticus 26:12"> The captain of the guard also took away the basins, censers, sprinkling bowls, pots, lampstands, pans, and drink offering bowls—anything made of pure gold or fine silver.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v20" data-v="20" data-xref="1 Kings 7:47; 2 Kings 25:16; 2 Chronicles 4:18"> As for the two pillars, the Sea, the twelve bronze bulls under it, and the movable stands that King Solomon had made for the house of the LORD, the weight of the bronze from all these articles was beyond measure.</span>
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="2 Kings 25:17; 1 Kings 7:15–21; 2 Chronicles 3:15–17"> Each pillar was eighteen cubits tall and twelve cubits in circumference;<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Each pillar was approximately 27 feet high and 18 feet in circumference (8.2 meters high and 5.5 meters in circumference).">c</a></sup>each was hollow, four fingers thick.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="4 fingers is approximately 2.9 inches or 7.4 centimeters.">d</a></sup></span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="Exodus 28:25; 2 Kings 25:17; 2 Chronicles 3:15"> The bronze capital atop one pillar was five cubits high,<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="5 cubits is approximately 7.5 feet or 2.3 meters.">e</a></sup>with a network of bronze pomegranates all around. The second pillar, with its pomegranates, was similar.</span>
<span className="v" id="v23" data-v="23" data-xref="1 Kings 7:20"> Each capital had ninety-six pomegranates on the sides, and a total of a hundred pomegranates were above the surrounding network.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Captives Carried to Babylon

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v24" data-v="24" data-xref="2 Kings 25:18; Jeremiah 29:25; Jeremiah 35:4"> The captain of the guard also took away Seraiah the chief priest, Zephaniah the priest of second rank, and the three doorkeepers.</span>
<span className="v" id="v25" data-v="25" data-xref="2 Kings 25:19; Esther 1:14; Matthew 18:10"> Of those still in the city, he took a court official who had been appointed over the men of war, as well as seven trusted royal advisers. He also took the scribe of the captain of the army, who had enlisted the people of the land, and sixty men who were found in the city.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v26" data-v="26"> Nebuzaradan captain of the guard took them and brought them to the king of Babylon at Riblah.</span>
<span className="v" id="v27" data-v="27" data-xref="Ezekiel 33:28; Isaiah 32:13–14; Isaiah 6:11–12"> There at Riblah in the land of Hamath, the king of Babylon struck them down and put them to death. So Judah was taken into exile, away from its own land.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v28" data-v="28" data-xref="2 Kings 24:12–16; 2 Kings 24:2–3; Daniel 1:1–3"> These are the people Nebuchadnezzar carried away:<br />in the seventh year, 3,023 Jews;</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v29" data-v="29" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:12; Genesis 12:5; 2 Kings 25:11"> in Nebuchadnezzar’s eighteenth year, 832 people from Jerusalem;</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v30" data-v="30" data-xref="Jeremiah 52:15; Jeremiah 6:9"> in Nebuchadnezzar’s twenty-third year, Nebuzaradan captain of the guard carried away 745 Jews.<br />So in all, 4,600 people were taken away.</span>
</div>
</div>

## Jehoiachin Released from Prison

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v31" data-v="31" data-xref="2 Kings 25:27–30; Psalms 3:3; Genesis 40:13"> On the twenty-fifth day of the twelfth month of the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin king of Judah, in the first year of the reign of Evil-merodach king of Babylon, he pardoned<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Literally he lifted up the head of">f</a></sup>Jehoiachin king of Judah and released him from prison.</span>
<span className="v" id="v32" data-v="32" data-xref="Proverbs 12:25; Daniel 5:18–19; Daniel 2:37"> And he spoke kindly to Jehoiachin and set his throne above the thrones of the other kings who were with him in Babylon.</span>
</div>
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v33" data-v="33" data-xref="2 Samuel 9:7; 1 Kings 2:7; Genesis 41:14"> So Jehoiachin changed out of his prison clothes, and he dined regularly at the king’s table for the rest of his life.</span>
<span className="v" id="v34" data-v="34" data-xref="2 Samuel 9:10; Luke 11:3; Matthew 6:11"> And the king of Babylon provided Jehoiachin a daily portion for the rest of his life, until the day of his death.</span>
</div>
</div>
