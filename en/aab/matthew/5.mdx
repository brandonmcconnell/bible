## Anger and Reconciliation

<div className="bt">
<div className="bp">
<span className="v" id="v21" data-v="21" data-xref="Deuteronomy 5:17; Exodus 20:13; Numbers 35:30–34"> You have heard that it was said to the ancients, ‘Do not murder’<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-c" id="fnref-c" data-fn="Exodus 20:13; Deuteronomy 5:17">c</a></sup>and ‘Anyone who murders will be subject to judgment.’</span>
<span className="v" id="v22" data-v="22" data-xref="1 John 3:14–15; 1 John 4:20–21; 1 John 2:9"> But I tell you that anyone who is angry with his brother<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-d" id="fnref-d" data-fn="BYZ and TR include without cause.">d</a></sup>will be subject to judgment. Again, anyone who says to his brother, ‘Raca,’<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-e" id="fnref-e" data-fn="Raca is an Aramaic expression of contempt.">e</a></sup>will be subject to the Sanhedrin.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-f" id="fnref-f" data-fn="Or the Council">f</a></sup>But anyone who says, ‘You fool!’ will be subject to the fire of Gehenna.<sup className="fn"><a href="#fn-g" id="fnref-g" data-fn="Gk. geenna, from the Hebrew ge hinnom, the Valley of Hinnom on the edge of Jerusalem (Josh 15:8). Children were burned there to Molech (2 Kgs 23:10; Jer 7:31), and Jeremiah renamed it the Valley of Slaughter, a place of unburied corpses (Jer 7:32-33). Isaiah ends his book with the same picture: those who go out look on the corpses of the rebels, whose worm never dies and whose fire is never quenched (Isa 66:24). These are dead bodies, not living people. The undying worm and the unquenched fire are the destruction of the dead, and Jesus takes up those words for Gehenna (Mark 9:48). Gehenna is the place of final judgment and death, into which the whole person, body and life, is thrown (Matt 5:29-30; 10:28): the second death (Rev 20:14; 21:8). The dead now wait in Hades (Luke 16:23; Rev 20:13). Commonly rendered ‘hell,’ a word that in modern English suggests a present realm of torment where the dead already are; the AAB keeps the name Jesus used.">g</a></sup></span>
</div>
